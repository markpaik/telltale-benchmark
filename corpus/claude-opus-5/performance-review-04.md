## Section 2: Performance Against Established Goals

### Goal 1 — Reduce unplanned downtime on Presses 1 through 4 to 35 hours per month or below

**Result: 29 hours per month current run rate, from a 46-hour baseline. Exceeds.**

At the start of the period, unplanned downtime across Rick's four presses averaged 46 hours per month. As of the June close, the trailing three-month average is 29 hours. That is a 37 percent reduction and it is eleven hours per month below the goal.

Breaking out the trajectory, since the shape matters: the July-through-March average was 44 hours. The April-through-June average was 29. The improvement is concentrated entirely in the period following the PIP.

The reduction came from three things I can identify. First, Rick converted six recurring reactive repairs into scheduled PM tasks, which moved that work out of unplanned and into planned. There is no way to score this as a met goal. A recordable occurred on February 9. Since February 17: 47 of 47 observed lockout applications compliant. Zero procedural findings. Rick requested and completed LOTO recertification on February 24, ahead of the PIP requirement. He has since served on the team that rewrote the die-change procedure to require second-person energy verification — a procedure that now applies to every mechanic in this plant on all three shifts. I want that noted precisely: the corrective action arising from his injury has been strengthened by his own participation, and the plant is safer for it.

**Rating for this goal: 2 — Needs Improvement for the period as a whole.** I am not adjusting it upward. A recordable is a recordable. ### Goal 4 — Support quality improvement and customer corrective action

**Result: Identified and corrected the root cause of the audit-flagged extrusion defect. Significantly exceeds.**

The June customer audit at the Phoenix racking integrator produced a corrective action driven in part by a dimensional defect on extruded racking rail — a periodic surface and dimensional variation that had been intermittently present since approximately March and had been chased by quality, engineering, and process without resolution for roughly ten weeks.

Rick identified the cause. Working from the periodicity of the defect against ram position, he traced it to a worn container liner seal on Press 2 producing intermittent pressure loss at a specific point in the stroke. He proposed it, tested it on a controlled run on June 18, confirmed it, and executed the repair over the weekend of June 20–21.

The defect has not recurred in the six weeks since. The corrective action response submitted to the customer on July 6 cites this repair as the root cause and containment. Susan Marchetti, Quality Manager, wrote in her input to this review: *"Rick found in four days what my department and process engineering did not find in ten weeks. I had three engineers on it. He solved it from the shop floor with a stopwatch and a ram position readout. Whatever else is in his file, that needs to be in it too."*

I am quoting her in full because it is the single most important thing in this document. That defect threatened a $22 million annual account. It is closed because of Rick Dombrowski.
