Rosalind,

Short version now, formal memo by Friday.

The city may award the contract as bid to the lowest responsive, responsible bidder and subsequently negotiate a deductive change order. The legal risk arises if the post-award changes are so substantial that they effectively create a different project than the one bid — the concern being that other bidders, or contractors who chose not to bid, might have competed differently for the reduced scope. Courts and the case law on this look at materiality: percentage of contract value, whether the change alters the essential identity of the work, and whether there's evidence of bad faith or bid manipulation.

A $3.1M reduction on an $18.6M contract is about 17%, which is at the high end of what I'm comfortable with but defensible, particularly because (a) the deferred digester is a discrete, severable component that doesn't change the nature of the project — it remains a nutrient removal upgrade; (b) the deductions apply proportionally to work any bidder would have priced similarly, so relative standing among the three bidders would almost certainly be unchanged; and (c) we have a documented, legitimate public purpose — a state compliance deadline with accruing penalties — for proceeding rather than rebidding. I would want Priya's firm to provide a short technical memo confirming point (b) for the record.

Two conditions. First, the change order should be negotiated and executed promptly after award, and the council resolution should reference it explicitly, so no one can claim it was concealed. Second, do not negotiate scope or price with Carraway before award. Award the bid as submitted, then negotiate. The sequence matters.

On Councilmember Toussaint's question about recourse against Two Rivers on the estimate: the contract's standard of care provision holds them to the care ordinarily exercised by similar professionals, and cost opinions carry an express disclaimer that the engineer has no control over market conditions. Given what every owner on the Front Range has experienced in the last two years, I do not see a viable claim, and pursuing one would poison a relationship we need through 2028. I'll address it in the memo so the question is answered on the record.
