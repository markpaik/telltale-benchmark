# Proposal to the Board of Directors
## Vista Alta Community Development Corporation
### Acquisition and Conversion of the Palm Grove Inn to Permanent Supportive Housing

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors, Vista Alta Community Development Corporation
**Prepared by:** Ines Barrantes, Executive Director; Trevor Lindgren, Real Estate Director; Marcus Delahoussaye, Supportive Services Director
**Date of Board Meeting:** October 9, 2026
**Action Requested:** Authorization to execute purchase agreement, accept conditional funding commitments, and proceed to closing

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Staff requests that the Board authorize Vista Alta Community Development Corporation ("Vista Alta") to acquire the 96-room Palm Grove Inn at 4180 Foothill Boulevard, San Bernardino, and convert it into 88 units of permanent supportive housing (PSH) for individuals experiencing chronic homelessness, at a total development cost of $19.4 million.

This is a decision under real time pressure. The seller's purchase agreement requires a nonrefundable $600,000 deposit upon Board authorization and signing, with closing no later than January 15, 2027. The $8.6 million county housing award that anchors the financing is reserved for this project only through March 31, 2027. If the Board does not authorize the purchase agreement on October 9, both the agreement and the county award lapse, and the county has indicated the funds will be redirected to another sponsor.

The proposal below sets out what is being purchased and for whom; the full $19.4 million sources-and-uses budget and the extent to which it draws on Vista Alta's own reserves; the fourteen-month construction and lease-up schedule; the staffing and services model, including a $620,000 annual services gap after year two; the alternatives staff considered and rejected; the principal risks in entitlement, cost, and services funding, with mitigations for each; and the specific authorization the Board is asked to grant. Treasurer Wan-Chen Liu's caution about the 2023 Loma Verde overrun, and community opposition expressed at two town halls, are addressed directly in Section 7.

---

## 2. The Site and the Population to Be Housed

### 2.1 The Property

The Palm Grove Inn is a 96-room, two-story motel built in 1974 on a 2.1-acre parcel at 4180 Foothill Boulevard. The building is of masonry and wood-frame construction, with exterior corridors, a central office/lobby building, and a surface parking lot. It has operated as an extended-stay motel for the past six years under absentee ownership, with declining occupancy and code enforcement history including two abatement actions in 2024.

Vista Alta proposes to convert the 96 existing rooms into 88 units of permanent supportive housing through combination of adjoining rooms to create larger single-occupancy units with private kitchenettes and bathrooms, along with the following common-area improvements:

- Full seismic and structural upgrade to current code
- New electrical, plumbing, and fire/life-safety systems throughout
- Conversion of the ground-floor lobby into a 24-hour front desk and reception area
- Conversion of four ground-floor rooms into offices for case management staff, a nurse's office, and a group/community room
- New on-site laundry facility
- Landscaped courtyard in the existing parking footprint, with reduced surface parking (site is on two bus lines)
- Site security improvements including perimeter fencing, cameras, and controlled entry

The site sits within a half-mile of a full-service grocery store, a federally qualified health center, and a light-rail stop, and is zoned for the proposed use as of right under the city's 2021 permanent supportive housing streamlining ordinance, which is discussed further in Section 7.1.

### 2.2 The Population

The 88 units will house adults who meet HUD's definition of chronic homelessness — individuals with a disabling condition (mental illness, substance use disorder, or chronic physical health condition) who have been continuously homeless for at least one year, or who have had four or more episodes of homelessness in the past three years. Referrals will come exclusively through the county's Coordinated Entry System, prioritized by vulnerability score, consistent with Vista Alta's existing PSH portfolio at three other sites.

This population profile is consistent with the county's own targeting priorities for its Permanent Local Housing Allocation funds, which is the source of the $8.6 million award described below. All 88 units will carry project-based rental subsidy (a mix of county-funded operating subsidy and Section 8 project-based vouchers already committed by the housing authority, outside this budget), so tenants will pay no more than 30 percent of income toward rent.

### 3.3 Reserve Exposure

This is the number that should hold the Board's attention. Vista Alta's unrestricted organizational reserve currently stands at 41 days of operating cash — roughly $1.3 million against an $11.4 million annual budget. The proposed project draws $1.2 million of that reserve, which would leave the organization with approximately three days of unrestricted cash on hand at the moment of the largest capital commitment in its history.

Three additional exposures compound this:

1. **The $600,000 deposit is nonrefundable upon signing**, not upon closing. If any of the three not-yet-fully-committed sources (tax credit equity, the bank loan, or timely LIHTC award) fails to materialize before the January 15 closing, Vista Alta has no contractual right to recover the deposit, and would need to either self-fund the gap from reserves it does not have, or forfeit the deposit and the county award along with it.
2. **The reserve draw is a permanent use of cash**, not a bridge loan to itself. Staff has not identified a repayment source or timeline for restoring the $1.2 million to the organizational reserve. Absent Board direction, this reserve depletion is indefinite.
3. **The $1.15 million contingency (9% blended)** is thinner than the 12–15% contingency more commonly carried on acquisition-rehabilitation of a 1970s motel structure with unknown conditions behind walls. Treasurer Liu's reference to Loma Verde, discussed in Section 7.2, is directly relevant here.

Staff does not raise this to argue against the project, but to be explicit: the Board is being asked to accept a period of materially reduced organizational liquidity, with limited room for error, in exchange for securing an $8.6 million award and 88 units of housing that would otherwise not be built.

### 4.1 Relocation

The Palm Grove Inn currently houses an estimated 40–55 long-term motel occupants who will be treated as tenants under the state's relocation assistance law given their length of stay. The $340,000 relocation budget assumes relocation benefits, temporary housing assistance, and the services of a relocation consultant already under contract with Vista Alta on a prior project. This process must be substantially complete before construction mobilization and is scheduled concurrently with entitlement processing in November–December. Any delay in relocation is the most likely near-term threat to the January 15 closing date, and is addressed further in Section 7.

## 5. Services Model and Staffing

### 5.1 Staffing Plan

Supportive Services Director Marcus Delahoussaye proposes an 11-person on-site staffing model:

| Position | FTE | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Site/Program Manager | 1.0 | Overall site operations, staff supervision |
| Case Managers | 4.4 (1:20 ratio) | Housing stability, benefits, treatment linkage |
| 24-hour Front Desk Coverage | 3.5 | Security, tenant support, incident response |
| Part-time Nurse | 0.5 | Health screening, medication support, care coordination |
| Maintenance Technician | 1.0 | Building upkeep, unit turnover |
| Administrative Support | 0.6 | Compliance reporting, file management |
| **Total** | **11.0** | |

The 1:20 case management ratio is consistent with the county's PSH service standard and with staffing at Vista Alta's existing three PSH sites. The 24-hour front desk model — three shifts, seven days a week — reflects lessons learned from those sites regarding after-hours incident management and is treated as a non-negotiable element of the operating model by both Vista Alta staff and the county's PSH funding guidelines.

### 5.2 Services Funding

Annual supportive services cost is projected at $1.4 million. The state's Medi-Cal Community Supports pathway (via managed care plan billing for housing-related case management and community-based care coordination) is projected to cover $780,000 of this amount, based on enrollment and billing rates achieved at Vista Alta's comparable sites.

**The remaining $620,000 has no committed funding source beyond year two.** The current plan relies on a combination of county mental health realignment funds and philanthropic grants that are approved on a year-to-year basis and are not contractually guaranteed. This is a structural, ongoing operating risk rather than a one-time development cost risk, and is addressed in Section 7.3.

### 5.3 Services Approach

The services model follows a Housing First framework consistent with the rest of Vista Alta's PSH portfolio: housing is not contingent on treatment participation or sobriety. Case managers conduct individualized service planning, connect tenants to county behavioral health and substance use treatment, coordinate with the part-time nurse on health needs, and support benefits establishment (SSI, Medi-Cal, CalFresh). The front desk functions as both a security and a light-touch engagement point, given that many chronically homeless tenants have had adverse experiences with more institutional service settings.

## 6. Alternatives Considered

Staff evaluated three alternatives before recommending the Palm Grove Inn acquisition.

### 6.1 New Construction on the City-Owned Parcel (Baseline Street)

The city has offered Vista Alta a long-term ground lease on a vacant 1.4-acre parcel on Baseline Street for new PSH construction. Staff obtained a preliminary feasibility estimate:

- Estimated total development cost: $31–34 million for 88 units of new construction, or roughly $365,000–$385,000 per unit — 65 to 75 percent more than the motel conversion.
- Estimated timeline to occupancy: 42–48 months from today, versus approximately 22 months for the Palm Grove Inn conversion, due to entitlement processing, design development, and new-construction permitting.
- The city's ground lease terms had not been finalized at the time of this analysis, and the parcel would still require a rezone, since it is not currently covered by the streamlining ordinance that applies to the Palm Grove Inn site (see Section 7.1).
- The $8.6 million county award, reserved specifically for this acquisition, would not be available on the same terms for a new-construction project with a multi-year longer timeline; county staff have indicated informally that the award is tied to this specific transaction's speed to occupancy.

New construction remains a viable long-term option for Vista Alta's pipeline and staff recommends continuing to explore the Baseline Street site as a separate future project, but it is not a substitute for the opportunity before the Board on October 9, given cost, timeline, and the loss of the county award.

### 6.2 Passing on the Site

The Board could decline to pursue the Palm Grove Inn. Under this alternative:

- The $8.6 million county award is redirected to another sponsor, likely outside San Bernardino, per county staff's informal indication.
- The Palm Grove Inn most likely returns to the market as a motel or is purchased by another operator without PSH conversion; it has been a persistent source of code enforcement activity and neighborhood concern independent of this transaction.
- Vista Alta preserves its full organizational reserve and avoids the execution and cost-overrun risks discussed in Section 7.
- Vista Alta forgoes the addition of 88 units of PSH to its portfolio and its associated mission impact, at a moment when the county's chronic homelessness count has grown for three consecutive years.

Staff's assessment is that passing is a legitimate and defensible choice given the reserve exposure described in Section 3.3, but that it should be a deliberate Board decision made with full information, not a default outcome of running out the clock on the October 9 deadline.

### 6.3 Renegotiating Deposit Terms or Timeline

Staff explored asking the seller for a refundable deposit or an extended closing date. The seller, who has three other motel-to-market-rate-apartment offers pending, declined both requests and indicated the nonrefundable $600,000 deposit and January 15 closing date are firm. This alternative is noted for completeness but is not available to the Board as a negotiated middle path.

## 7. Risks and Mitigations

### 7.1 Entitlement Risk

**Risk:** Although the site qualifies for streamlined, by-right approval under the city's 2021 PSH ordinance, two community town halls hosted by the council district representative have surfaced organized neighborhood opposition. While the ordinance limits the city's discretion to deny or condition the project on grounds related to use, opponents could pursue delay through CEQA challenge, code enforcement disputes tied to the relocation process, or political pressure on the building permit timeline even where legal discretion is limited.

**Mitigation:** Staff has retained land use counsel experienced with the ordinance's litigation history, which to date has withstood challenge in two other jurisdictions. Vista Alta will hold a community information session (distinct from the council district's town halls) prior to permit submission to address specific concerns raised — primarily around parking reduction and 24-hour operations — and will commit to the security and front-desk staffing plan in Section 5 as a direct response to safety concerns raised at the town halls. Staff recommends the Board authorize up to $75,000 in supplemental legal fees, within the existing soft-cost contingency, for entitlement defense if needed.

### 7.2 Cost and Schedule Risk

**Risk:** The Board has directly relevant recent experience here. The 2023 Loma Verde rehabilitation, a comparable-scale acquisition-rehabilitation project, finished eleven months late and $1.8 million over its original budget, driven primarily by unforeseen structural conditions discovered after wall demolition and by construction-period interest accumulating over the extended schedule. The Palm Grove Inn is an older, lower-quality asset than Loma Verde was at the time of its acquisition, increasing the likelihood of comparable surprises.

**Mitigation:** Staff has commissioned a more extensive pre-acquisition physical needs assessment than was conducted for Loma Verde, including selective destructive testing behind walls in a sample of units, completed in September 2026 and available to the Board on request. The $1.15 million contingency (9%) is nonetheless thinner than the roughly 13% contingency that, in hindsight, would have absorbed the Loma Verde overrun. Staff recommends the Board consider two specific actions: (1) directing staff to negotiate with the tax credit investor and bank lender to increase the contingency line to 12% by reallocating from the deferred developer fee, which would reduce the fee from $950,000 to approximately $700,000; and (2) requiring monthly cost-to-complete reporting to the Board (rather than quarterly) throughout construction, with a defined threshold — 5% of hard cost — that triggers mandatory Board notification and, if applicable, a request for supplemental authorization rather than staff proceeding unilaterally as occurred for a period during Loma Verde.

### 7.3 Services Funding Risk

**Risk:** The $620,000 annual gap in services funding beyond year two is not a hypothetical — it is a documented, current absence of committed funding for roughly 44 percent of the ongoing services budget. If unresolved, this would force Vista Alta to either reduce staffing below the 1:20 case management ratio and 24-hour front desk model described in Section 5, or subsidize services from other organizational funds, straining the same reserve base already reduced by this transaction.

**Mitigation:** Staff proposes three concurrent actions, none of which fully closes the gap on its own: (1) pursuing county mental health realignment funds through the FY2028 budget cycle, for which Vista Alta's other PSH sites have a successful application history but no guarantee; (2) building a philanthropic campaign specifically for services endowment, targeting $3–4 million in gifts that would generate approximately $150,000–200,000 annually if invested conservatively, closing roughly a third of the gap; and (3) if neither materializes by month 18 of operations, presenting the Board with a formal staffing reduction plan rather than allowing services to be informally cut. Staff is explicit that this risk is not resolved by this proposal and will require continued Board attention after October 9, independent of the development decision.

### 7.4 Financing Contingency Risk

**Risk:** As shown in Section 3.2, only the county award and the CDBG funds are firm commitments as of this meeting. The tax credit equity is a letter of intent contingent on a competitive 9% credit award still to be applied for, and the bank loan is a term sheet, not a binding commitment. Closing is scheduled for January 15, 2027 — before the LIHTC award is expected to be finalized.

**Mitigation:** This is the sharpest edge of the reserve exposure discussed in Section 3.3. Staff recommends the Board authorize execution of the purchase agreement and deposit payment, but direct staff to structure the acquisition closing, to the extent the seller will permit, so that the bank construction loan and a binding tax credit equity commitment (even if not final funding) are both secured as closing conditions. If the seller will not agree to closing conditions of this kind — and staff's current read, given competing offers, is that this is unlikely — the Board should understand that authorizing the purchase agreement on October 9 carries real exposure to forfeiting the $600,000 deposit if LIHTC or the bank loan does not come through by January 15, in addition to the reserve depletion described above.

## 8. Summary of Risk Against the Alternative of Inaction

It is worth stating plainly what happens under each path. If the Board authorizes the project and everything proceeds as planned, Vista Alta adds 88 units of permanent supportive housing, secures the $8.6 million county award, and returns to a healthier reserve position once the deferred developer fee and tax credit equity fund at stabilization in 2028. If the Board authorizes the project and either the entitlement, cost, or financing risks materialize, Vista Alta faces reserve depletion compounded by deposit forfeiture, cost overrun, or both, at an organization already operating at 41 days of cash. If the Board declines to authorize the project, Vista Alta preserves its financial position but loses the county award, the site likely reverts to non-PSH use, and 88 units of housing that the county has prioritized funding for do not get built in San Bernardino.

There is no option before the Board that eliminates risk. The purpose of this proposal is to make the specific risks, and the specific reserve exposure, explicit enough that the Board's decision — whichever way it goes — is made with full information rather than by default.

## Appendix F: Budget Sensitivity Analysis

Staff modeled three scenarios against the base budget in Section 3.2, to give the Board a sense of the range of outcomes rather than a single point estimate.

**Scenario 1 — Base Case (as presented in Section 3.2):** Total development cost of $19,400,000, contingency of $1,150,000 (9%), all sources fund as projected, construction completes on the 14-month schedule.

**Scenario 2 — Loma Verde-Comparable Overrun:** Applying the same percentage overrun experienced at Loma Verde (roughly 16% of hard cost, and an 11-month schedule extension) to this project's $8.9 million hard cost estimate yields an overrun of approximately $1.42 million, against a contingency of $1.15 million — a shortfall of roughly $270,000, before accounting for the additional construction-period interest an 11-month extension would generate on the $3.5 million bank loan, estimated at an additional $160,000–190,000. Under this scenario, total additional exposure is approximately $430,000–460,000 beyond the budgeted contingency, which would need to be absorbed through some combination of further reserve draw, increased deferred developer fee, or a request for supplemental county or philanthropic funds not currently identified.

**Scenario 3 — Contingency Increased to 12% (per Section 7.2 mitigation):** If staff succeeds in negotiating the contingency increase to approximately $1.42 million by reducing the deferred developer fee to approximately $700,000, the Loma Verde-comparable overrun scenario above would be fully absorbed by contingency alone, though the schedule extension's interest cost would still require an additional source. This scenario is the basis for staff's recommendation in Section 7.2 and is the assumption reflected in Appendix D's draft resolution language.

Staff emphasizes that Scenario 2 is not a worst case — a physical needs assessment conducted with destructive testing, as described in Appendix A, is intended precisely to reduce the likelihood of the kind of surprise condition that drove the Loma Verde overrun, and the Palm Grove Inn assessment did not identify structural issues of the magnitude found at Loma Verde. But staff considers it the most useful comparison available to the Board because it is drawn from the organization's own recent experience rather than an industry benchmark.
