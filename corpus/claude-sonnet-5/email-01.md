Marisol,

Here's the first pass. I built three packages against the four buildings you named.

Ponderosa: 317 students, 44% utilization
Juniper Flat: 402 students, 61% utilization
Sawyer Creek: 289 students, 51% utilization
Deschutes View: 511 students, 88% utilization

Combined capacity across the four buildings is roughly 2,527 seats for 1,519 students district-wide in these zones, so we are carrying a lot of empty square footage no matter how you slice it. Deschutes View is the outlier — it's nearly full and can't absorb much of anything without portables or a second bell schedule.

Package A — close Sawyer Creek only, redistribute to Ponderosa and Juniper Flat. Annual savings $1.9 million. One-time moving/boundary cost $600,000.

Package B — close Ponderosa only, redistribute to Juniper Flat and Sawyer Creek. Annual savings $2.7 million. One-time cost $750,000.

Package C — close both Ponderosa and Sawyer Creek, consolidate everything into Juniper Flat and Deschutes View with boundary work at both. Annual savings $4.6 million. One-time cost $1.1 million.

None of these close the full $8.4 million by itself — that was never the plan. This sits alongside the levy renewal ask and the transportation route redesign Roberta and I have been discussing separately. But even the smallest package is real money, and Package C gets us more than half the gap in one action.

I'll have building-by-building operating cost breakdowns by the 9th for the working session.

Grant, Marisol,

I'm not saying Ponderosa is untouchable. I'm saying if the recommendation that comes out of this process is "close the building with the lowest utilization," we will have built a policy on the one number that is easiest to defend and hardest to explain to the families who actually live it.

Package B — close Ponderosa only. Operating savings $2.7 million, revised to about $2.62 million. One-time cost $750,000. Add Roberta's $60,000 a year in transportation. Forfeit the $2.3 million grant and repay the $310,000 already advanced. Net first-year impact: negative $370,000. That's not a typo — in year one, closing Ponderosa costs us money once the clawback and moving costs are counted. It becomes net positive starting year two, at roughly $1.81 million annually, but the first-year hole is real and I don't think it survives contact with the board's April 20 discussion.

Package C — close both Ponderosa and Sawyer Creek. Operating savings $4.6 million, revised to about $4.46 million. One-time cost $1.1 million. Add both transportation figures, roughly $240,000 a year combined. Forfeit grant and repay clawback, same $2.61 million hit as Package B. Net first-year impact: roughly $520,000 positive — better than Package B alone because the larger operating savings absorb the grant loss faster, but still far below the $4.6 million headline number Curt is going to be looking at.

Denise, to your point about Curt: yes, Package C still nets positive in year one and clears $3.6 million annually from year two on. It is not fiscally irrational to take it. What it costs is the roof and HVAC at Ponderosa, which we would then need to fund from the general fund or capital reserves at some point regardless, likely at a higher price than $2.3 million by the time we got to it, plus whatever credibility cost comes from walking back a signed state award five months after accepting it.

My recommendation, for what it's worth before this goes to Marisol: Package A. It's the smallest number on paper and it's the one I would have argued against in my March 4 memo. But it's the only package where the numbers, the grant, and what Hana heard in four rooms full of families all point the same direction.

Curt,

Fair question and I'd rather answer it in writing than have it feel unaddressed at the table.

You're right on the pure five-year arithmetic — Package C wins on paper if you assume the grant condition is just a number to be absorbed rather than a real constraint. Two things make me land differently.

First, the $2.3 million isn't ours to reallocate freely even if we forfeit it — it's earmarked capital funding for a specific roof and HVAC system that needs replacing regardless of what building configuration we run. If we walk away from the grant, we don't save that $2.3 million, we defer it, and we'll be back in front of the board in three to five years asking for the same roof paid for entirely out of general fund reserves instead, at a cost that's more likely to be $3 million than $2.3 million given construction inflation. That's not a wash, it's a worse deal spread over time.

Second, and this is less a spreadsheet point and more a judgment call I'm asking the board to make with me: we applied for this grant, in writing, representing that Ponderosa would remain in service. Walking it back five months later to fund a different consolidation package is the kind of thing that shows up when we go to the state for the next grant, or to voters for the levy renewal Marisol and I have both been counting on to cover the rest of the $8.4 million. I can't put a dollar figure on that risk, but I don't think it's zero, and I don't think it's small.

On Deschutes View — you're right that poverty there isn't zero, but at 31 percent it's roughly half Ponderosa's rate and well below Sawyer Creek's 54 percent, so the comparison doesn't move me toward Package C, it actually reinforces why Deschutes View was never a candidate for closure in any of the three packages to begin with.

I don't think this is a case where the fiscally maximal option and the fiscally correct option are the same thing once you count everything. Happy to walk the full model with you one-on-one before the 20th if that's useful.
