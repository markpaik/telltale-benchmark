# Arkansas River Valley Manufacturing Consortium
## Skilled Trades Pipeline Fund Application
### Submitted to the Dorsey-Vachon Foundation

**Applicant:** Arkansas River Valley Manufacturing Consortium, Fort Smith, Arkansas
**Requested Amount:** $1,800,000 over three years (dollar-for-dollar match required)
**Submission Deadline:** March 20

---

## Statement of Need

The Arkansas River Valley Manufacturing Consortium exists because the region's manufacturers cannot find enough trained workers, and the region's workers cannot find enough pathways into skilled trades. Both halves of that sentence have grown truer, not less true, over the nine years the Consortium has operated its training center in Fort Smith, and both halves define the need this proposal addresses.

On the labor supply side, 41 percent of adults in Sebastian County hold no postsecondary credential of any kind — no certificate, no apprenticeship completion, no associate or bachelor's degree. This is not a population without work history or without capacity; it is a population that has been passed over by a training infrastructure built around four-year degrees and located, in most cases, outside the reach of people working hourly jobs, raising children, or managing the logistics of a criminal record. Roughly 1,300 people return to Sebastian County and the surrounding river valley each year from state custody, most within driving distance of the Consortium's Fort Smith training center, almost none of them connected to a credentialing pathway that would make them competitive for the wage growth manufacturing offers.

On the labor demand side, the Consortium's 34 member manufacturers currently report 310 unfilled production openings — positions in CNC machining, industrial maintenance, and welding that sit vacant not for lack of pay but for lack of qualified applicants. These are not entry-level positions in the sense of requiring no skill; they require measurable, credentialed competency, and member firms have told the Consortium directly and repeatedly that they will hire every qualified graduate the training center produces. The constraint is not employer demand. The constraint is throughput, retention through completion, and the wraparound supports that determine whether a trainee finishes the program and shows up for the job on the other side of it.

The Consortium has a nine-year record against this need. Since 2017, the training center has trained 1,240 people in CNC machining, industrial maintenance, and welding. That record includes a period of real success: in 2022, 79 percent of graduates were placed in manufacturing employment within 90 days of completion, a placement rate that matched or exceeded regional workforce board benchmarks and justified continued member investment in the training center. It also includes a period of real decline. By 2024, that 90-day placement rate had fallen to 58 percent — a 21-point drop that this application does not minimize, explain away, or bury in the middle of a budget table. We address it directly in the Project Description and Evaluation Plan sections below, because the Foundation deserves a straight account of what happened and what changes as a result of this grant.

The need, then, has three layers. First, a regional population — disproportionately including justice-impacted adults — is locked out of credentialed employment despite documented, willing employer demand. Second, the Consortium's own capacity to convert training into placement has weakened over the past two years in ways directly traceable to the loss of a single employer partner, and that capacity gap must be repaired, not papered over, if new investment is to produce different results than the trend line predicts. Third, the region's justice-involved reentry population — 1,300 people a year — represents both the deepest unmet need and the largest untapped supply of workers for the 310 open positions member firms report, provided training is paired with the case management, transportation, and stability supports this population specifically requires and that the Consortium's current $2.6 million operating budget does not fully fund.

This proposal asks the Dorsey-Vachon Foundation to fund the repair of that capacity gap and the deliberate expansion into reentry population, at a scale — 480 trainees over three years — that would represent the largest single expansion of the training center's throughput since its founding.

## Project Description

The Skilled Trades Pipeline project will train 480 people over three years in CNC machining, industrial maintenance, and welding at the Consortium's Fort Smith training center, with three structural changes designed specifically to address the causes of the 2022–2024 placement decline and to reach the reentry population the region's demographics demand.

**Paid work experience.** Every trainee will move through a paid work-experience placement with a member manufacturer during the final phase of training, rather than completing coursework and only then entering a job search. This design directly answers the central lesson of the placement decline described below: trainees who have already worked inside a member firm's production floor, on that firm's equipment, alongside that firm's supervisors, convert to full-time hires at dramatically higher rates than trainees whose only connection to an employer is a resume handed off at graduation. Training Director Jamal Whitfield has restructured the CNC, maintenance, and welding curricula so that the paid work-experience block sits at weeks nine through twelve of each twelve-week cohort, timed so trainees are placed at a partner floor while still enrolled and still receiving instructional support if problems arise.

**Transportation and childcare stipends.** The training center's lease location, chosen originally for proximity to member manufacturing sites, is not proximate to public transit, and neither the 2022 cohort nor the 2024 cohort budget included transportation support. Exit interviews conducted by Employer Engagement Manager Rosa Delgadillo with 2024 non-completers identified transportation and childcare as the two most commonly cited reasons for dropping out before finishing a credential — ahead of curriculum difficulty, ahead of pay expectations, ahead of every other factor combined. This project funds a stipend, administered on a sliding scale tied to household income and distance from the training center, covering the twelve-week enrollment period plus the paid work-experience block. Childcare stipends are similarly structured and will be administered in coordination with the reentry case management partner described below for trainees with dependent children.

**Reentry cohort.** One of every three cohorts across the three-year grant period — roughly 160 of the 480 total trainees — will be recruited, screened, and supported as a dedicated reentry cohort, built around the population of approximately 1,300 people returning annually to the region from state custody. This is not an add-on population folded into general cohorts; it is a structured cohort with its own recruitment pipeline, its own onboarding timeline calibrated to supervision and documentation requirements specific to reentry, and its own case management relationship, described below, running in parallel with instructional time from week one.

**Partner roles.** Three partner organizations carry defined responsibilities under this project, and none of them is a signatory in name only.

The **University of Arkansas at Fort Smith** provides credit articulation, so that hours completed in the Consortium's CNC machining, industrial maintenance, and welding curricula transfer into UAFS's applied technology associate degree pathway. This matters for two reasons beyond the credential itself: it gives graduates a documented, transcript-based credential recognized outside the region if they relocate, and it gives the Consortium a defensible basis for the "credential attainment" metric the Foundation requires in reporting, since UAFS's registrar — not the Consortium's own staff — issues the transcript record.

The **state workforce center** provides referral volume, particularly for the general (non-reentry) cohorts, and administers WIOA-eligible funding determinations for trainees who qualify, which offsets a portion of per-trainee cost and provides an independent verification source for enrollment eligibility.

**River Valley Second Chance Coalition**, the region's reentry nonprofit, provides case management for the reentry cohort specifically — coordinating with parole and probation supervision schedules, connecting trainees to housing and documentation services outside the scope of workforce training, and serving as the first point of contact when a reentry trainee's attendance or performance signals a problem that transportation stipends and childcare support alone will not solve. This partnership is structured as a subcontract with defined caseload ratios, not an informal referral relationship, because Executive Director Margarethe Ostrander and the board concluded, after 2024, that reentry-specific supports cannot be layered onto general case management capacity as an afterthought and expect to hold.

**Addressing the placement decline directly.** The 21-point drop in 90-day placement between 2022 and 2024 followed the closure of Talbert Cordage, a founding member firm that had absorbed a disproportionate share of training center graduates directly into its own production lines and that had also provided a $250,000 annual match commitment supporting the training center's operating budget. Talbert Cordage's closure removed both the single largest employer sink for graduates and a quarter-million dollars of the funding base that supported instructor staffing and equipment maintenance in the training center's core programs. The Consortium's 2023–2024 response — broadening outreach to additional member firms without restructuring the placement pipeline itself — was insufficient, and placement fell before that broadening produced comparable absorption capacity. This project's paid work-experience structure is the direct corrective: rather than depending on any single employer to absorb graduates at the volume Talbert Cordage once did, every one of the Consortium's 34 member firms will host work-experience placements, distributing placement risk across the full member base rather than concentrating it in one firm whose closure can no longer take down the pipeline with it.

## Goals and Measurable Objectives

**Goal 1: Restore and exceed pre-2022 placement performance.**
- Objective 1.1: Achieve 90-day placement of 75 percent and 180-day placement of 82 percent across all cohorts by the end of Year 2, and sustain those rates through Year 3.
- Objective 1.2: Achieve median wage at placement of at least $19.50 per hour and median wage at twelve months of at least $22.00 per hour, reflecting both entry wages and the wage progression member firms report for retained CNC, maintenance, and welding staff.

**Goal 2: Expand throughput to 480 trainees over three years without repeating the concentration risk that produced the 2024 decline.**
- Objective 2.1: Enroll and complete 160 trainees per year (480 over three years), with no single member firm accounting for more than 15 percent of total work-experience placements in any program year.
- Objective 2.2: Achieve credential attainment (UAFS-articulated credit and program completion certificate) for at least 85 percent of enrolled trainees.

**Goal 3: Build a functioning reentry pathway at meaningful scale.**
- Objective 3.1: Enroll 160 reentry-cohort trainees over three years (roughly one-third of total enrollment), drawn from the population of approximately 1,300 annual returning residents.
- Objective 3.2: Achieve 90-day placement of at least 65 percent for the reentry cohort specifically — set deliberately below the general-cohort target of 75 percent in recognition of documented barriers this population faces, but tracked and reported separately rather than folded into an aggregate number that would obscure whether the reentry-specific model is working.

**Goal 4: Demonstrate employer value and retention.**
- Objective 4.1: Achieve 12-month employer retention of at least 70 percent among placed graduates.
- Objective 4.2: Reduce member firms' reported unfilled production openings from 310 to no more than 220 by the end of Year 3, tracked through an annual member firm survey administered by Rosa Delgadillo's office.

**Goal 5: Control and report cost per placement transparently.**
- Objective 5.1: Achieve a blended cost per placement (grant funds plus match, divided by verified 90-day placements) of no more than $6,500, reported annually and cumulatively.

## Evaluation Plan

The evaluation plan is built around the exact metrics the Dorsey-Vachon Foundation requires — credential attainment, placement at 90 and 180 days, median wage at placement and at twelve months, employer retention, and cost per placement — and around one additional discipline the Consortium is imposing on itself as a direct response to 2024: every metric will be reported disaggregated by cohort type (general versus reentry) and, where relevant, by member firm, so that aggregate numbers cannot mask a concentration problem the way the pre-2024 reporting structure did.

**Data sources and verification.** Credential attainment will be verified through UAFS registrar records, not self-report, satisfying the Foundation's requirement with an independent third-party source. Placement at 90 and 180 days will be verified through a combination of employer confirmation (collected by Rosa Delgadillo's office through direct employer contact, not trainee self-report) and Arkansas Department of Commerce wage record matching where available, cross-checked against state workforce center placement data for trainees who entered through that referral channel. Wage data at placement and at twelve months will be drawn from employer-reported payroll confirmation, with the twelve-month figure collected through a structured follow-up contact protocol assigned to Training Director Jamal Whitfield's staff. Employer retention at twelve months will be tracked through the same employer confirmation process used for placement verification, with a defined follow-up call at month twelve for every placed graduate.

**Cost per placement** will be calculated annually by dividing total program expenditure (grant funds and match combined, excluding partner subcontract costs attributable solely to case management for non-placed reentry participants who exit before completion) by the number of verified 90-day placements for that program year, and reported cumulatively across the three-year grant period.

**Reentry-specific tracking.** Because Goal 3 sets a deliberately different placement target for the reentry cohort, the evaluation plan tracks reentry-cohort outcomes as a standing item in every quarterly report, not as a special study conducted separately from core reporting. River Valley Second Chance Coalition case managers will log engagement touchpoints (contact frequency, barrier types identified, referrals made) on a shared basis with Consortium staff, allowing analyst Tomasz Wierzbicki to correlate case management intensity with completion and placement outcomes across the three-year period — data the Consortium did not previously collect and that will inform whether the reentry cohort model should be expanded, held steady, or restructured at the end of the grant period.

**Quarterly and annual reporting.** Wierzbicki will produce a quarterly dashboard covering enrollment, completion, and interim placement indicators, and an annual report covering the full required metric set, submitted to the Foundation and reviewed by the Consortium's board. The annual report will explicitly compare current-year placement and wage figures against both the 2022 peak and the 2024 trough, so that progress or shortfall against the Consortium's own history — not only against the grant's own targets — remains visible to the Foundation and to the board across the full grant term.

**Course correction protocol.** If any 90-day placement figure falls more than 10 percentage points below the Objective 1.1 target in any given quarter, Employer Engagement Manager Rosa Delgadillo will conduct structured exit interviews with that quarter's non-placed graduates within thirty days, following the same protocol used to diagnose the 2024 decline, and will report findings and proposed corrective action to the Executive Director and board chair within sixty days of the shortfall being identified. This protocol did not exist during the 2022–2024 period; its absence is one reason the decline was not addressed until placement had already fallen 21 points.

## Organizational Capacity

The Arkansas River Valley Manufacturing Consortium has operated as the training arm of 34 member manufacturers since its founding, with a current staff of nine and an annual operating budget of $2.6 million. Executive Director Margarethe Ostrander has led the organization through its full nine-year history, including both the 2022 placement peak and the 2024 decline, and has direct, first-hand knowledge of what changed between those two years — knowledge reflected throughout this application rather than reconstructed after the fact for grant-writing purposes.

Training Director Jamal Whitfield holds direct responsibility for curriculum design and instructional delivery across the CNC machining, industrial maintenance, and welding programs, and has already begun the curriculum restructuring described in the Project Description — repositioning the paid work-experience block into weeks nine through twelve of each cohort — independent of whether this grant is funded, because the Consortium's board has already directed that change as a response to 2024 regardless of the outcome of this application. This grant funds the stipend and staffing capacity to execute that redesign at the scale of 480 trainees rather than the smaller scale the current $2.6 million budget alone can support.

Employer Engagement Manager Rosa Delgadillo manages the relationship between the training center and all 34 member firms, including the work-experience placement coordination central to this project's design, and has already conducted the exit-interview diagnostic work that identified transportation and childcare as the leading causes of 2024 non-completion — work that directly informs the stipend design in this proposal rather than a supposition offered without evidence.

Board chair Sung-Min Park and analyst Tomasz Wierzbicki bring governance oversight and data capacity respectively. Park has led board-level review of the placement decline, including the member-dues sustainability questions addressed in the following section, and Wierzbicki's role — newly formalized in the Consortium's staffing structure as part of the organizational response to 2024 — is to ensure that outcome data is tracked with enough granularity that a decline of the kind the organization experienced between 2022 and 2024 would be visible in real time rather than only in a subsequent grant application.

The training center itself — the physical facility housing CNC, welding, and industrial maintenance equipment — operates under a lease running through 2028, giving this three-year grant period a facility horizon that comfortably outlasts the grant term and removes facility uncertainty as a risk factor for the project's duration. The Consortium's board and staff are aware that the 2028 lease expiration falls within the sustainability planning horizon discussed below, and address it directly in that section rather than leaving it as an unstated risk.

Since 2017, the training center has trained 1,240 people across its three core programs, giving the organization nine years of instructional operating history — a track record of program delivery that predates and will outlast the specific placement challenges of 2022–2024, and that reflects capacity to deliver training at volume even during the years when placement conversion weakened. The organization's core capacity to train has not been in question; this application is built on the premise that the capacity to convert training into sustained employment is the specific, addressable gap this grant closes.

## Sustainability

Two questions define sustainability for this project, and this application answers both directly rather than assuming the Foundation will not ask them.

**How does the program continue when member dues cover only 30 percent of operating cost?** The Consortium's $2.6 million annual operating budget has historically depended on a mix of member dues, employer match commitments tied to specific training cohorts, state workforce funding channeled through referral partnerships, and, until its closure, Talbert Cordage's $250,000 annual contribution. Member dues alone cover 30 percent of operating cost, a proportion that has not changed materially in recent years and that board chair Sung-Min Park and analyst Tomasz Wierzbicki have reviewed as part of the organization's post-2024 financial planning. The remaining 70 percent has historically come from the combination of employer match, state funding, and grant support — a structure that is not unusual for workforce training intermediaries, but one that concentrated too much risk in a single employer relationship prior to 2024, exactly as this proposal's project design is built to avoid going forward.

This grant's structure directly strengthens that sustainability model rather than creating a new dependency the Consortium cannot sustain past Year 3. The dollar-for-dollar match requirement means every Foundation dollar is matched by Consortium-secured funding, and the Consortium's plan is to secure that match by distributing work-experience host commitments — and associated per-trainee employer contributions — across all 34 member firms rather than concentrating them, replacing the single $250,000 Talbert Cordage commitment with a broader base of smaller commitments from a larger number of firms. Rosa Delgadillo's employer engagement plan for Year 1 includes securing signed work-experience and match commitments from at least 20 of the 34 member firms before the first reentry and general cohorts begin, specifically so that no single firm's future closure can reproduce the 2024 shock.

Beyond the grant period, the credential attainment infrastructure built through the UAFS articulation partnership and the improved placement and retention data this project generates are themselves sustainability assets: a training center that can document 75 percent placement, transcript-verified credentials, and 70 percent employer retention is positioned to attract continued state workforce funding, additional employer match, and future foundation support in a way that the 58 percent 2024 figures were not. Sustainability, in other words, is not a separate line item pursued after the grant ends; it is the direct output of the outcome improvement this grant funds.

**What happens at the 2028 lease expiration?** The training center's lease runs through 2028, meaning Year 3 of this grant (year three of a three-year term beginning upon award) will conclude before the lease question must be resolved, but the board is aware that facility continuity beyond 2028 is a planning question that begins now rather than in 2027. Executive Director Margarethe Ostrander and the board have begun preliminary discussion of lease renewal terms with the current landlord and identified, as a contingency, potential co-location with UAFS's applied technology facilities given the credit articulation partnership this project establishes — a contingency that would not exist without the partnership this grant funds. This application does not ask the Foundation to fund facility planning beyond the grant term, but the organizational capacity built through this project — stronger employer relationships, a functioning UAFS partnership, and improved outcome data — directly strengthens the Consortium's negotiating position for whatever facility arrangement follows 2028.

**Continuation of the reentry cohort model.** The reentry cohort represents the most significant new cost center in this proposal, given the case management subcontract with River Valley Second Chance Coalition. Should this three-year grant period demonstrate the 65 percent placement target for reentry participants, the Consortium's sustainability plan includes seeking continuation funding through the state's justice reinvestment or reentry-specific workforce funding streams, for which a documented three-year outcome record — the record this grant makes possible — is typically a prerequisite for state funders. Absent that continuation funding, the Consortium's board has committed to maintaining the reentry cohort at a reduced scale funded through general operating budget rather than eliminating it outright, given the scale of regional need (1,300 annual returning residents) the cohort addresses.

## Budget Narrative

The total three-year project budget is $3,600,000, composed of $1,800,000 in requested Dorsey-Vachon Foundation funds and $1,800,000 in Consortium-secured match, satisfying the Foundation's dollar-for-dollar match requirement in full.

**Personnel ($1,440,000 over three years).** This includes a proportional share of Training Director Jamal Whitfield's salary dedicated to curriculum delivery and cohort instruction across the expanded 480-trainee scale; a proportional share of Employer Engagement Manager Rosa Delgadillo's salary dedicated to work-experience placement coordination and the exit-interview and course-correction protocol described in the Evaluation Plan; a proportional share of analyst Tomasz Wierzbicki's salary dedicated to the quarterly and annual reporting structure required by the Foundation; and one new instructional support position, funded entirely by this grant, dedicated to the reentry cohort's specific onboarding and instructional pacing needs.

**Participant Support — Transportation and Childcare Stipends ($720,000 over three years, $1,500 average per trainee across 480 trainees).** This is the single largest new cost category in the project and the most direct response to the transportation and childcare barriers Rosa Delgadillo's exit interviews identified as the leading causes of 2024 non-completion. Stipends are administered on a sliding scale and disbursed biweekly across the twelve-week instructional period plus the paid work-experience block, verified through attendance records to ensure funds support active participation rather than enrollment alone.

**Paid Work Experience Wages ($900,000 over three years).** This covers the wage cost of the four-week paid work-experience placement for all 480 trainees, calculated at the placement wage rate described in Objective 1.2, split between grant funds and the employer match contributions Rosa Delgadillo is securing from the 34 member firms as described in the Sustainability section. Employer contributions to this line are the primary vehicle through which the dollar-for-dollar match requirement is met, distributed across at least 20 member firms rather than concentrated in one, directly addressing the concentration risk exposed by Talbert Cordage's closure.

**Partner Subcontracts ($360,000 over three years).** This funds the River Valley Second Chance Coalition case management subcontract at a defined caseload ratio for the 160 reentry-cohort trainees over the grant period, and a smaller administrative fee to UAFS for credit articulation processing and registrar-verified credential reporting supporting the Evaluation Plan's data verification requirements. The state workforce center's referral role is provided under existing intergovernmental agreement at no direct cost to this budget.

**Equipment and Materials ($120,000 over three years).** This covers consumable materials for CNC machining, welding, and industrial maintenance instruction at the expanded 480-trainee scale (roughly 100 additional trainees per year above recent historical throughput), and incremental equipment maintenance costs, but does not include capital equipment replacement, which remains funded through the Consortium's base $2.6 million operating budget and member dues.

**Evaluation and Reporting ($60,000 over three years).** This covers data verification costs, including wage record matching fees and the structured twelve-month follow-up contact protocol for retention tracking, ensuring the credential attainment, placement, wage, retention, and cost-per-placement metrics required by the Foundation are reported with third-party verification rather than self-report alone.

**Indirect Costs.** Indirect costs are calculated at the Consortium's federally negotiated (or foundation-approved de minimis) rate applied to the direct cost base above, covering a proportional share of facility costs under the lease running through 2028, financial management, and general administrative oversight by Executive Director Margarethe Ostrander's office.

This budget structure reflects the project design principle running through every section of this application: risk that was concentrated in a single employer relationship prior to 2024 is, in this proposal, distributed across personnel, employer commitments, and partner organizations in a way intended to make the Consortium's placement outcomes resilient to the loss of any single partner in a way the 2022–2024 model was not.

## Implementation Timeline

**Year 1 (Months 1–12).** The first six months are dedicated to the infrastructure this project depends on rather than to enrollment volume for its own sake. Rosa Delgadillo begins immediately upon award with the employer commitment campaign described in the Sustainability section, targeting signed work-experience host agreements and match commitments from at least 20 of the 34 member firms before the first cohort begins instruction. This sequencing is deliberate: the Consortium will not repeat the 2023–2024 pattern of enrolling trainees against placement capacity that has not yet been secured. In parallel, Jamal Whitfield finalizes the restructured curriculum placing paid work experience at weeks nine through twelve, and completes staff training for the new reentry-cohort instructional support position. The UAFS articulation agreement, currently in draft form, is finalized and signed by month three, and the River Valley Second Chance Coalition subcontract is executed by month two, with case manager hiring and training complete by month four.

The first general cohort of 53 trainees begins in month five, the first reentry cohort of 27 trainees begins in month seven, offset deliberately so that early operational issues surface in the general cohort, where they are easier to diagnose, before the reentry cohort — carrying the added complexity of parole and probation coordination — begins. By the end of Year 1, the Consortium expects 160 total trainees enrolled across both cohort types, consistent with Objective 2.1, with the first 90-day placement data available in month eight and reported to the Foundation in the Year 1 annual report.

**Year 2 (Months 13–24).** With infrastructure in place, Year 2 focuses on steady-state delivery and the first full test of the course-correction protocol described in the Evaluation Plan. Four cohorts run across the year — three general, one reentry — maintaining the roughly two-to-one ratio that produces 160 reentry trainees across the full three-year term. Tomasz Wierzbicki's quarterly dashboards are fully operational by this point, and the Year 2 annual report is the first to include a full twelve-month wage and retention data set from Year 1 placements, satisfying the Foundation's requirement for wage-at-twelve-months reporting for the first time in the grant cycle. Any cohort falling short of the 10-point placement threshold triggers the exit-interview and corrective-action protocol within the timeframes specified in the Evaluation Plan.

**Year 3 (Months 25–36).** The final year completes enrollment toward the 480-trainee total and shifts organizational attention toward the sustainability questions addressed above: employer commitment renewal conversations for a potential continuation beyond the grant period, preliminary engagement with state reentry and justice reinvestment funding streams to support continuation of the reentry cohort, and early discussion with the training center's landlord regarding lease terms beyond 2028. The final annual report, submitted at the close of Year 3, presents cumulative three-year data across all required metrics — credential attainment, 90- and 180-day placement, median wage at placement and twelve months, employer retention, and cost per placement — compared explicitly against both the 2022 peak and the 2024 trough that motivated this proposal, giving the Foundation and the Consortium's board a complete before-and-after account of whether this investment closed the gap it was designed to close.

## Risk Factors and Mitigation

The Consortium's board, in reviewing this application prior to submission, required that specific risks be named rather than implied, given the organization's recent experience with an unaddressed risk — employer concentration — producing a measurable decline in outcomes.

**Employer concentration risk.** As described throughout this application, the 2024 placement decline traces directly to over-reliance on a single member firm. This project's distributed work-experience model, requiring commitments from at least 20 of 34 member firms with no single firm exceeding 15 percent of placements, is the direct mitigation, and Rosa Delgadillo's Year 1 employer commitment campaign is structured as a precondition for cohort launch rather than a parallel activity.

**Reentry cohort barriers beyond training capacity.** Case managers at River Valley Second Chance Coalition report that housing instability and documentation barriers (identification, licensing eligibility for certain welding and maintenance certifications) can prevent otherwise qualified reentry trainees from completing or converting training into employment regardless of instructional quality. The subcontract with River Valley Second Chance Coalition is scoped specifically to address these barriers as they arise, and the deliberately lower 65 percent placement target for the reentry cohort reflects a realistic accounting of this risk rather than an assumption that stipends and case management eliminate it entirely.

**Instructor capacity strain.** Scaling to 160 trainees per year, up from recent historical throughput, places real demand on Jamal Whitfield's instructional staff. The new reentry-cohort instructional support position funded by this grant is the primary mitigation, and the Year 1 timeline's deliberate staggering of general and reentry cohort start dates is designed in part to prevent simultaneous peak instructional demand during the period when staff are still adjusting to the redesigned curriculum.

**Data verification gaps.** The Consortium's pre-2024 reporting relied more heavily on trainee self-report than the verification protocol described in the Evaluation Plan. Wierzbicki's formalized role and the employer-confirmation and wage-record-matching procedures described above are the direct response, and the Year 1 timeline builds in a full quarter for these verification systems to be operational before the first cohort's 90-day placement window closes.

## Conclusion and Certification

This application asks the Dorsey-Vachon Foundation to fund a specific, evidence-based correction to a specific, documented problem: a training organization with nine years of instructional history and 1,240 graduates whose placement outcomes declined sharply following the loss of a single employer relationship, proposing a structurally different model — distributed employer risk, paid work experience, targeted stipends, and a dedicated reentry pathway — designed so that no single partner's exit can again produce the outcome the Consortium experienced between 2022 and 2024. Executive Director Margarethe Ostrander, Training Director Jamal Whitfield, Employer Engagement Manager Rosa Delgadillo, board chair Sung-Min Park, and analyst Tomasz Wierzbicki have reviewed this proposal in full and certify that the outcome data, partner commitments, and budget figures presented here reflect the Consortium's actual operating history and current planning, not aspirational figures constructed for the purposes of this application.

We welcome the Foundation's questions and are prepared to provide additional documentation — member firm commitment letters, the UAFS articulation agreement, the River Valley Second Chance Coalition subcontract terms, and detailed 2022–2024 placement data by cohort and employer — upon request.
