## 1. PURPOSE

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the required methods, responsibilities, and documentation for two related compliance programs operated by the City of Harlow Springs Water Division:

1. **Lead and copper tap sampling** under the federal Lead and Copper Rule as adopted by Oregon Administrative Rules Chapter 333, Division 61, including site tier classification, sampling pool maintenance, sample kit distribution, first-draw collection, chain of custody, laboratory submission, result review, 90th percentile calculation, reporting, sample invalidation, and substitution.

2. **Service line material inventory verification**, including the field and records-based methods used to classify service lines of unknown material and the documentation standard required to retire an "unknown" classification.

This SOP also defines the City's response to an action level exceedance, including the 24-hour public notification requirement, public education delivery, and the triggers for service line replacement.

This SOP is issued in direct response to the Oregon Health Authority (OHA) compliance advisory dated January 2026, which identified three deficiencies in the City's 2025 monitoring period: (a) 11 of 30 tap samples were collected from sites that did not meet the highest available tier criteria; (b) two chain-of-custody forms were missing from the compliance file; and (c) 1,240 of the City's 9,800 service lines remained classified as "unknown" in the material inventory. The advisory requires this written procedure to be in place by **September 1, 2026**, and requires the unknown service line count to be reduced **below 400 by June 30, 2027**.

The controlling performance standard of this SOP is simple: every sample must come from a correctly tiered site, every sample must have a complete chain of custody, and every unknown service line must be retired only with documented evidence.

## 2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:

- All lead and copper compliance tap sampling conducted by or on behalf of the City of Harlow Springs public water system (28,400 residents; 9,800 service connections; two groundwater wells and one surface water intake).
- All personnel who classify sampling sites, distribute or retrieve sample kits, handle samples, complete chain-of-custody documentation, review laboratory results, or report to OHA.
- All service line material verification work performed by the Meter Services Crew, including pothole and hydrant-vacuum (hydro-vac) excavations, and all inventory updates entered by the Records Coordinator.
- The contracted laboratory, Rogue Basin Analytical Labs (Medford, Oregon), to the extent its handling, analysis, and reporting obligations are described here and in the laboratory services agreement.

This SOP does **not** cover corrosion control treatment operation, source water monitoring, water quality parameter monitoring at entry points, or distribution system flushing, which are addressed in separate Water Division SOPs.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Action Level (AL):** The concentration of lead (0.010 mg/L) or copper (1.3 mg/L) which, if exceeded at the 90th percentile of tap samples, triggers mandatory response actions. The action level is not a health-based standard for individual taps; it is a system-wide performance trigger.

**Chain of Custody (COC):** The unbroken documented record of sample possession from the moment of collection (or resident hand-off) through laboratory receipt, signed at every transfer.

**Cityworks:** The City's computerized maintenance management system used to generate, assign, and close work orders for kit distribution, kit retrieval, and material verification excavations.

**DWDO (Drinking Water Data Online):** The Oregon Health Authority's electronic portal through which the City submits compliance monitoring results, the service line inventory, and required reports.

**First-Draw Sample:** A one-liter tap sample collected from a cold-water kitchen or bathroom tap that has been motionless (unused) for a minimum of six hours, collected before any other water use at the site that day.

**Fifth-Liter Sample:** Where required for sites served by a lead service line, the fifth sequential liter drawn from the tap, representing water that has been standing in the service line itself.

**GRR (Galvanized Requiring Replacement):** A galvanized service line that is or ever was downstream of a lead service line, or downstream of a line of unknown material.

**Lead Service Line (LSL):** A service line made of lead, in whole or in part. A lead gooseneck, pigtail, or connector alone does not classify a line as an LSL but must be recorded separately.

**90th Percentile Value:** For 30 samples, the average of the 27th and 28th highest results when all results are ranked from lowest to highest concentration. See Section 6.5.4 for the calculation method used by this system.

**Non-Lead:** A service line conclusively documented as copper, plastic (PVC, HDPE, PEX), or ductile/cast iron, on both the system-owned and customer-owned portions.

**Sampling Pool:** The maintained list of at least 60 eligible, tier-classified sites from which the 30 monitoring-period sites are drawn each period.

**Tier Criteria:** The site prioritization hierarchy defined in OAR 333-061 and 40 CFR 141.86, requiring samples to be drawn from the highest-risk sites available. See Section 6.1.2.

**Unknown Service Line:** A service line for which the material of either the system-owned or customer-owned portion is not documented to the evidentiary standard in Section 6.7.5. A line remains "unknown" until both portions are verified.

## 4. RESPONSIBILITIES

**Water Quality Supervisor (Elena Mirzoyan) — SOP Owner.** Maintains the sampling pool and tier classifications; approves every monitoring-period site list before kits go out; reviews all laboratory results within two business days of receipt; performs and documents the 90th percentile calculation; approves all sample invalidations and site substitutions in writing; certifies all DWDO submissions; initiates exceedance response; trains all staff on this SOP annually and documents training; owns the annual review of this document.

**Public Works Director (Omar Haddad) — Approver.** Approves this SOP and all revisions; is notified within four hours of any confirmed action level exceedance; approves the public notification content before release; authorizes service line replacement expenditures; signs annual inventory certification to OHA.

**Utility Operators.** Distribute and retrieve sample kits per Cityworks work orders; verify site eligibility at distribution (Section 6.2.4); complete the field portion of every COC form; deliver samples and COCs to the laboratory shipment point; report any resident refusal, vacancy, or site condition problem to the Water Quality Supervisor the same day.

**Meter Services Crew.** Perform pothole and hydro-vac excavations for material verification per Cityworks work orders; photograph and document exposed pipe per Section 6.7.4; restore excavation sites; submit verification records to the Records Coordinator within two business days of the excavation.

**Records Coordinator (Sylvia Koval).** Maintains the service line material inventory and updates DWDO; maintains the master COC file and performs the completeness audit in Section 6.4.6; maintains the sampling pool database; retains all records per Section 7; flags any missing COC to the Water Quality Supervisor within 24 hours of discovery.

**Rogue Basin Analytical Labs (Medford, OR) — Contract Laboratory.** ORELAP-accredited for lead and copper by EPA Method 200.8 (or equivalent approved method); signs COC on receipt and returns a receipt confirmation within one business day; reports results electronically within ten business days of receipt; flags any result exceeding the lead action level to the Water Quality Supervisor by phone **and** email on the day of data validation; retains sample bottles for 60 days after reporting.

No person may deviate from this SOP without written approval of the Water Quality Supervisor. Deviations affecting compliance data require concurrence from OHA before the data are used.

## 6. PROCEDURAL STEPS

### 6.1 Site Tier Classification and Sampling Pool Maintenance

**6.1.1 Pool size.** The City shall maintain a sampling pool of no fewer than 60 classified, resident-confirmed sites — twice the required 30 — so that refusals, vacancies, and invalidations can be covered without using lower-tier sites (this is the direct corrective action for the 2025 deficiency).

**6.1.2 Tier hierarchy.** Every site in the pool shall be classified using the following hierarchy. Samples must be drawn from the highest tier available; a lower-tier site may be used only when the higher tier is exhausted, and the exhaustion must be documented in the pool database.

- **Tier 1:** Single-family residences served by a lead service line, or containing lead pipes or copper pipes with lead solder installed before the state lead ban effective date, prioritizing sites served by an LSL.
- **Tier 2:** Buildings and multi-family residences meeting the Tier 1 plumbing/service line criteria, used only if Tier 1 sites are insufficient.
- **Tier 3:** Single-family residences with galvanized service lines or with copper pipes with lead solder of unverified installation date.
- **Tier 4:** Representative sites throughout the distribution system, used only if Tiers 1–3 cannot supply 30 sites, with written justification retained in the compliance file.

**6.1.3 Classification evidence.** No site enters the pool, and no site retains its tier, without at least one of the following on file: (a) a verified service line inventory record per Section 6.7.5; (b) a plumbing permit or building record identifying interior pipe material and solder date; or (c) a documented in-home plumbing assessment by an operator (scratch/magnet test on accessible pipe, photographed). "Owner says so" is not sufficient by itself; it must be corroborated by one of the above.

**6.1.4 Annual re-tiering (March).** Each March, the Water Quality Supervisor shall re-run every pooled site against the current service line inventory. Any site whose service line has been replaced, whose material verification has changed its classification, or whose ownership/occupancy has changed shall be re-tiered or removed. The re-tiering worksheet, showing prior tier, new tier, and evidence citation for every site, shall be signed and dated by the Supervisor and filed.

**6.1.5 Consistency with inventory.** The sampling pool database (maintained by the Records Coordinator) shall carry the inventory line ID for every site. Any inventory change entered under Section 6.7 automatically flags the linked pool site for Supervisor review within five business days.

**6.1.6 Site list approval.** By April 30, the Supervisor shall approve the monitoring-period list of 30 primary sites and 15 ranked alternates (all alternates at the same or higher tier than the primary they may replace, wherever possible). The approved list, with tier and evidence citation for each site, is the only authorized source of sampling sites for the period.

### 6.2 Sample Kit Distribution and Resident Instruction

**6.2.1 Kit contents.** Each of the 30 kits contains: one laboratory-supplied wide-mouth 1-liter HDPE sample bottle (pre-labeled with site ID and kit number by the Records Coordinator; **no preservative in the bottle** — acidification occurs at the laboratory); the resident instruction sheet (Form WD-014-A); the sample information tag; a partially pre-filled COC form; a sealable transport bag; and the Water Quality Supervisor's direct phone number for questions.

**6.2.2 Distribution work orders.** By May 25, the Records Coordinator generates a Cityworks work order for each of the 30 sites, assigned to Utility Operators, with the site address, tier, resident contact, and target distribution date. Operators close each work order in Cityworks the same day the kit is delivered, recording delivery method (hand-delivered with instruction walkthrough preferred; door-hanger with kit only if resident is unavailable after two attempts).

**6.2.3 Resident instructions (Form WD-014-A).** The instruction sheet shall state, in plain language and in both English and Spanish:

1. Use the **cold water kitchen tap** (or bathroom tap if no kitchen tap), with any point-of-use filter or aerator screen **left in place as normally used** — do not remove or clean the aerator before sampling.
2. Water must sit **unused for at least 6 hours** (but residents are advised not to exceed 18 hours of stagnation). Overnight is easiest: no water use anywhere in the home after the evening — no toilets, no ice maker, no irrigation drawing through the house.
3. First thing in the morning, **before any other water use**, place the open bottle under the tap, open the cold tap to a gentle flow, and fill to the 1-liter line without letting the bottle overflow. Do not rinse the bottle. Do not remove water if overfilled slightly.
4. Cap the bottle tightly, complete the tag (date, time of last water use the night before, time of collection, name), place bottle and tag in the bag, and call the number on the sheet or leave the kit at the designated pickup spot.
5. Do not sample if the home was vacant more than 5 days immediately prior, if there was plumbing work in the past week, or if a water outage or main break affected the street in the past 72 hours — call the Water Division instead.

**6.2.4 Eligibility check at distribution.** At delivery, the Operator confirms with the resident: the tap to be used, that no plumbing work or point-of-entry treatment changes have occurred since the site was approved, and that the home is occupied. If any answer disqualifies the site, the Operator does **not** leave the kit, notes the reason in Cityworks, and notifies the Supervisor the same day so an approved alternate is activated (Section 6.6).

**6.2.5 Reminder contact.** If a kit is outstanding 14 days after delivery, the Records Coordinator flags it and the Operator makes a reminder contact (phone, then door visit). A kit outstanding at 30 days triggers substitution review under Section 6.6.

### 6.3 Collection Window and First-Draw Conditions

**6.3.1 Window.** All compliance samples shall be collected between **June 1 and September 30**. No sample collected outside this window shall be submitted as compliance data. Kit distribution shall be paced so that at least 20 of 30 samples are collected by July 31, leaving buffer time for substitutions.

**6.3.2 First-draw verification.** A sample is a valid first-draw sample only if the tag shows: stagnation of ≥ 6 hours (calculated from "time of last use" to "time of collection"); collection from the approved cold-water tap; and no interior plumbing disturbance in the prior week. On kit retrieval, the Operator reviews the tag **before leaving the site** and, if the stagnation time is short, the tag is incomplete, or the fill level is materially below 1 liter, asks the resident to re-sample with a replacement bottle rather than submitting a defective sample.

**6.3.3 Fifth-liter sites.** For any pooled site confirmed to be served by a lead service line where a fifth-liter sample is required under the current rule, the kit shall contain five sequentially numbered 1-liter bottles and modified instructions (Form WD-014-A5): fill bottles 1 through 5 in order without shutting off the tap between bottles. All five bottles are submitted; the fifth liter is the compliance value for lead at that site as directed by OHA. The Supervisor shall confirm with OHA before each monitoring period which sites require fifth-liter collection.

**6.3.4 Operator-collected samples.** If a resident is unable to collect, an Operator may collect the sample by appointment, arriving before the household's first water use, following the identical first-draw procedure, and noting "operator-collected" on the COC. Operator collection does not change any other requirement.

**6.3.5 Holding conditions.** Samples require no refrigeration and no field preservation. Holding time from collection to laboratory acidification shall not exceed **14 days**; the City's internal standard is delivery to Rogue Basin within **5 business days** of collection.

### 6.4 Chain of Custody and Laboratory Submission

*This section is a direct corrective action for the two missing COC forms cited by OHA. The rule is absolute: a sample without a complete COC does not exist for compliance purposes.*

**6.4.1 COC form (Form WD-014-C).** Every sample bottle travels with a COC form bearing: site ID and kit number; address; sample date and time (from the resident tag); collector name (resident or operator); stagnation start/end times; analysis requested (Pb/Cu, EPA 200.8); and a signature block for every transfer of possession.

**6.4.2 Field custody.** On retrieving a kit, the Operator: (a) verifies the tag against the COC and transcribes tag data onto the COC; (b) signs the "Relinquished by resident / Received by" line with date and time; (c) seals the bottle bag with a numbered tamper-evident seal and records the seal number on the COC; (d) photographs the completed COC with a phone and attaches the photo to the Cityworks work order the same day. **The photo requirement means no COC can be "missing" — a legible image exists in Cityworks from the day of retrieval.**

**6.4.3 Custody storage.** Samples awaiting shipment are stored in the locked sample cabinet in the Water Division office. Any hand-off between staff is signed on the COC. No sample is left unattended outside the locked cabinet.

**6.4.4 Laboratory submission.** Samples ship or are hand-delivered to Rogue Basin Analytical Labs, Medford, in batches at least weekly during the window. The transmitting staff member signs the "Relinquished to lab/courier" line; the original COC travels with the samples; a copy (or the Cityworks photo) stays with the Records Coordinator. Rogue Basin signs receipt and returns a signed receipt confirmation with intact seal numbers within one business day.

**6.4.5 Broken custody.** If a seal is broken, a signature block is missing, or possession cannot be documented for any interval, the Operator or Coordinator who discovers it notifies the Supervisor immediately. The Supervisor determines whether the sample is compromised; a compromised sample is invalidated under Section 6.6 and the site is re-sampled or substituted. The incident and disposition are documented on Form WD-014-D (Deviation/Invalidation Report).

**6.4.6 Weekly COC audit.** Every Friday during the collection window, the Records Coordinator reconciles: kits distributed vs. kits retrieved vs. COCs on file vs. lab receipt confirmations. Any gap is reported to the Supervisor the same day. The signed weekly reconciliation sheet is filed in the compliance file. At the close of the window, the Coordinator certifies in writing that a complete COC (field copy, lab receipt, and Cityworks photo) exists for all 30 compliance samples before the 90th percentile calculation is performed.

### 6.5 Result Review and Reporting

**6.5.1 Receipt.** Rogue Basin reports results electronically through its laboratory system within ten business days of sample receipt. The Records Coordinator logs each result into the compliance tracking spreadsheet on the day received and links it to the site ID, COC, and Cityworks work order.

**6.5.2 Supervisor review (within 2 business days of receipt).** The Water Quality Supervisor reviews each result for: correct site ID and match to COC; method and reporting limit (lead RL ≤ 0.001 mg/L); qualifiers or flags; and plausibility (a non-detect at a known LSL site, or a result > 0.050 mg/L anywhere, triggers a call to the lab to confirm no transcription or dilution error before the value is accepted). Review is documented by initial and date on the tracking spreadsheet.

**6.5.3 Individual high results.** Any single-site lead result above 0.010 mg/L — regardless of the eventual 90th percentile — triggers, within three business days: written notification to that resident of their result with health information and flushing/filter guidance (this consumer notice is required for **all** sampled residents within 30 days regardless of result; high results get the accelerated 3-day standard), and Supervisor review of that site's service line and plumbing records.

**6.5.4 90th percentile calculation.** After all 30 valid results are in and the COC certification (6.4.6) is complete, the Supervisor calculates the 90th percentile:

1. Rank all 30 results from lowest to highest concentration (non-detects entered at zero for ranking, per OHA convention, unless OHA directs use of the reporting limit).
2. Multiply 30 × 0.9 = 27. Because 27 is a whole number, the 90th percentile is the **average of the 27th and 28th ranked results**.
3. Compare the value to the lead action level of **0.010 mg/L** and the copper action level of 1.3 mg/L.
4. Document the ranked table, the calculation, and the comparison on Form WD-014-E, signed and dated by the Supervisor, with a copy to the Director.

**6.5.5 Reporting to OHA.** By **October 10**, the Supervisor submits through **Drinking Water Data Online (DWDO)**: all individual results (if not already transmitted electronically by the laboratory), the 90th percentile values, the site list with tier certifications, and any invalidation/substitution documentation. The Supervisor prints or saves the DWDO submission confirmation to the compliance file. If the laboratory transmits results directly to DWDO, the Supervisor verifies within five business days that every result posted matches the City's tracking spreadsheet.

**6.5.6 Consumer notice of results.** Within 30 days of receiving results, every participating household receives its individual result, the action level for comparison, health effects language, and steps to reduce exposure, using the OHA template. Proof of delivery (mail log) is certified to OHA within three months per rule.

### 6.6 Sample Invalidation and Substitution

**6.6.1 Grounds for invalidation.** A sample may be invalidated only for: documented improper collection (stagnation < 6 hours, wrong tap, flushed line, sampling after plumbing work); broken or undocumented chain of custody (6.4.5); laboratory error acknowledged in writing by Rogue Basin; damaged bottle or lost sample; or a site condition that made the sample non-representative (vacancy during stagnation, point-of-entry treatment installed without notice). **A sample may never be invalidated because the result is high.** OHA concurrence is required before any reported result is invalidated.

**6.6.2 Invalidation record.** Every invalidation is documented on Form WD-014-D stating the ground, the evidence, the Supervisor's written approval, and OHA concurrence where applicable. Invalidated results are retained in the file, marked "INVALIDATED — not used in compliance calculation," never deleted.

**6.6.3 Substitution for refusals and vacancies.** When a resident refuses to participate, a home is vacant, a kit is unreturned at 30 days, or a sample is invalidated:

1. The Operator records the reason in Cityworks and notifies the Supervisor the same day.
2. The Supervisor activates the **highest-ranked available alternate of the same or higher tier** from the approved April list. A lower-tier alternate may be used only if all same-tier alternates are exhausted, and the exhaustion is documented on Form WD-014-D.
3. The Records Coordinator issues a new Cityworks distribution work order within two business days; the alternate site follows the full procedure from Section 6.2.
4. A refused or chronically vacant site is flagged in the pool database; after two consecutive periods of unavailability it is removed from the pool and replaced during the March review.

**6.6.4 Timing guard.** No substitution may push collection past September 30. Any site not sampled by **September 10** is escalated to the Supervisor for immediate substitution so the alternate has time to complete a valid first draw within the window.

### 6.7 Service Line Material Verification and Retirement of Unknowns

**6.7.1 Compliance target.** The inventory carried **1,240 unknown lines** as of January 2026. OHA requires fewer than **400 unknowns by June 30, 2027**. The Division's working target is fewer than 350, requiring at least **890 retirements** over the program. The Supervisor shall set and track a monthly retirement target (minimum **85 lines per month** beginning September 2026) on a dashboard reviewed with the Director monthly. Field verification capacity is supplemented by records review, which typically retires 30–40% of unknowns without excavation.

**6.7.2 Prioritization.** Unknowns are worked in this order: (1) lines serving schools, licensed child-care facilities, and buildings occupied by sensitive populations; (2) lines in construction-era neighborhoods most likely to contain lead (pre-1960 plats); (3) lines linked to sampling pool sites; (4) all others, batched geographically to minimize crew travel.

**6.7.3 Verification methods (in order of preference/cost).**

- **(a) Records review.** The Records Coordinator examines tap cards, plumbing permits, meter installation and replacement records, main installation dates, and prior work orders. A records determination may classify a line **non-lead** only when at least **two independent records** agree on material and there is no conflicting record, per current OHA guidance on records-based classification; a single record supports a "likely" flag for field confirmation, not retirement.
- **(b) Visual inspection at existing access.** During meter replacement, curb-stop repair, or any excavation already open, the crew inspects and documents the exposed line at the meter setter (system side) and at the customer connection (customer side).
- **(c) In-home entry point inspection.** With owner consent, an operator inspects and scratch-tests the pipe where it enters the building (customer-side evidence).
- **(d) Pothole excavation.** Hand-dug or mechanical pothole (~12" × 18") at the curb stop or property line exposing the line on both sides of the meter.
- **(e) Hydro-vac excavation.** Vacuum excavation used where utilities are congested, pavement restoration must be minimized, or two exposure points are needed (one near the main tap, one near the meter/property line). Hydro-vac is the default method for arterial streets and any location with marked conflicting utilities.

**6.7.4 Field verification steps (pothole/hydro-vac).**

1. Records Coordinator generates a Cityworks work order per address, batched by block; utility locates requested (call 811) at least two business days before excavation.
2. Crew excavates to expose the service line; **both the system-owned side and the customer-owned side must be observed** — an excavation at the meter setter satisfies this when both pipes entering the setter are exposed.
3. For each exposed pipe: scratch-test the surface (lead scratches shiny gray and is soft; copper scratches to bright metal; galvanized attracts a magnet; plastic is self-evident); apply the magnet test; photograph the pipe with the scratch visible, a scale reference, and the address/meter number visible or slated in the photo.
4. Complete Form WD-014-F (Material Verification Record): address, line ID, date, crew, method, system-side material, customer-side material, presence of any lead gooseneck/connector, photos attached, crew signature.
5. Restore the excavation to City standards; close the Cityworks work order with Form WD-014-F and photos attached within two business days.

**6.7.5 Evidentiary standard to retire an unknown.** An unknown may be reclassified only when **both** portions of the line are documented by: field observation with photos (methods b–e), **or** a qualifying two-record records determination (method a), **or** a documented full replacement. The Records Coordinator enters the new classification (Lead, GRR, Non-Lead, or — for a lead connector only — the connector flag) in the inventory with the evidence reference and the date. No line is retired on prediction, neighborhood inference, or a single uncorroborated record.

**6.7.6 Discovered lead.** If any excavation reveals lead pipe or a lead connector: the crew photographs and documents it, does **not** disturb or partially remove it, and notifies the Supervisor the same day. The Supervisor notifies the resident in writing within three business days with health guidance and filter information, adds the line to the replacement plan (Section 6.8.6), and evaluates whether the site should be added to the sampling pool as Tier 1.

**6.7.7 Inventory reporting.** The Records Coordinator submits the updated inventory to OHA through DWDO quarterly (by January 15, April 15, July 15, October 15), and the publicly accessible inventory on the City website is refreshed on the same dates. The Director signs the annual inventory certification. Progress against the < 400 target is reported to OHA with each quarterly submission.

### 6.8 Action Level Exceedance Response

**6.8.1 Confirmation.** If the 90th percentile lead value exceeds 0.010 mg/L (or copper exceeds 1.3 mg/L), the Supervisor immediately re-verifies the ranked data, COC completeness, and calculation, then notifies the Public Works Director **within four hours** and OHA's Drinking Water Services regional office **the same day** by phone and DWDO/email.

**6.8.2 24-hour public notification (lead).** Within **24 hours of the confirmed lead exceedance**, the City shall deliver public notification using OHA-approved template language, stating the 90th percentile value, health effects, exposure-reduction steps (flushing, cold water for cooking/formula, certified filters), and City contact information. Delivery channels, executed in parallel: City website banner; City social media accounts; press release to local media; the reverse-911/utility alert system; and notices at City Hall and the library. The Director approves the content before release; if the Director is unreachable at hour 20, the Supervisor releases the OHA pre-approved template without further edits. Proof of each delivery channel is filed and certified to OHA within ten days.

**6.8.3 Public education program.** Within 60 days of the exceedance (or sooner if OHA directs), the City delivers the full public education program per OAR 333-061: mailed materials to all bill-paying customers and posted materials for non-billed consumers; outreach to the county health department, schools, child-care facilities, and healthcare providers; and repeat delivery per rule for as long as the exceedance stands. The Supervisor documents each delivery and certifies completion to OHA.

**6.8.4 Follow-up monitoring.** The system moves to the accelerated monitoring schedule directed by OHA (standard six-month monitoring with two 30-sample rounds per year until two consecutive periods are at or below the action level), plus any required water quality parameter and source water monitoring.

**6.8.5 Corrosion control.** The Supervisor coordinates with OHA on corrosion control treatment review — evaluation of existing treatment performance at the two wells and surface intake, and a corrosion control study or re-optimization if directed. This work proceeds under a separate project plan approved by the Director.

**6.8.6 Service line replacement triggers.** Full lead service line replacement (never partial replacement initiated by the City) is triggered by:

1. **Discovery:** any LSL or GRR identified through the inventory program enters the replacement plan regardless of sampling results, on the mandatory replacement schedule in the current rule as directed by OHA;
2. **Exceedance acceleration:** upon a lead action level exceedance, replacement proceeds at the annual rate required by OHA (planning basis: the applicable mandatory annual percentage of the LSL/GRR total), prioritized by Section 6.7.2 criteria;
3. **Opportunity:** any LSL or GRR encountered during main replacement, meter work, or emergency repair is replaced concurrently where feasible; emergency partial disturbance of an LSL triggers resident notification, filter provision, and post-work flushing/sampling per OHA guidance.

Each replacement is documented in Cityworks, the inventory is updated within ten business days, and the resident receives post-replacement flushing instructions, a certified filter with six months of cartridges, and a follow-up tap sample offer at three to six months.

**6.8.7 After-action review.** Within 90 days of any exceedance, the Supervisor and Director conduct a documented after-action review covering data quality, notification timeliness, and program gaps, with resulting revisions to this SOP processed per the revision procedure.
