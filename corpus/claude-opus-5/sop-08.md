## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure establishes how FRWC carries out its responsibilities as a pass-through entity under 2 CFR Part 200. It exists to ensure that every dollar FRWC passes to another organization is placed under a written agreement containing all required terms, is preceded by a documented risk assessment, is monitored at a frequency and depth matched to assessed risk, and is supported by an audit report where one is required.

The procedure responds directly to the material weakness in subrecipient monitoring reported in the FY2025 single audit and to the corrective action plans requested by the federal awarding agency and the state pass-through entity. Continuation of the $12.8 million grant is conditioned on an approved written monitoring procedure being in place by October 1, 2026.

Specific objectives:

- Correctly distinguish subrecipients from contractors before funds are committed.
- Complete and document a pre-award risk assessment for every subrecipient, every budget period, with no exceptions.
- Issue subawards containing all data elements and terms required by 2 CFR 200.332.
- Assign each subrecipient a risk tier that drives a defined, scheduled monitoring plan.
- Obtain, review, and act on every required single audit report within nine months of the subrecipient's fiscal year end.
- Track findings to closure and recover disallowed costs.
- Maintain records sufficient for an auditor or federal reviewer to reconstruct every monitoring decision.

---

## 2. SCOPE

**In scope.** All federal and state pass-through funds that FRWC redistributes to other organizations for the purpose of carrying out part of a federal program. This currently comprises approximately $46.2 million annually across 31 subrecipient organizations operating in 14 counties. The procedure covers the full lifecycle: determination, pre-award assessment, agreement issuance, monitoring, audit review, findings resolution, cost recovery, closeout, and record retention.

**Out of scope.**

- Procurement of goods and services for FRWC's own use, governed by GC-SOP-002 (Procurement).
- Payments to individual program participants (stipends, supportive services, incentive payments), governed by PR-SOP-011.
- FRWC's own single audit, governed by FN-SOP-008.
- Contracts funded entirely with unrestricted or philanthropic revenue.
- Interagency agreements with no transfer of federal funds.

**Authority.** Where this procedure and a specific award term conflict, the award term controls, and the Director of Grants Compliance documents the conflict and the resolution in the award file. Where this procedure is more restrictive than the Uniform Guidance, this procedure controls.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Contractor.** An entity receiving a payment from FRWC to provide goods or services within normal business operations, offered to many purchasers, in a competitive environment, where the goods or services are ancillary to the federal program and the entity is not subject to federal program compliance requirements. Determination criteria at 2 CFR 200.331.

**Corrective Action Log (CAL).** The controlled register in which every subrecipient finding, its corrective action, owner, due date, and closure evidence are recorded.

**Desk Review.** A remote examination of subrecipient documents, reports, and financial data performed without travel.

**Disallowed Cost.** A charge that FRWC determines does not meet the allowability standards of 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart E, the terms of the subaward, or the approved subrecipient budget.

**Going Concern.** A condition in which an auditor expresses substantial doubt about an entity's ability to continue operating for twelve months beyond the financial statement date.

**High-Risk Subrecipient.** A subrecipient scoring 60 or more points on the Risk Assessment Worksheet, or designated high-risk by the Director of Grants Compliance regardless of score.

**Monitoring Tool.** The standardized instrument (GC-FRM-014) used to document desk reviews and on-site monitoring, including the expenditure sampling worksheet.

**On-Site Monitoring.** A visit to a subrecipient's place of business to examine records, observe service delivery, and interview staff. Includes a documented virtual visit only where approved in advance under §7.6.

**Pass-Through Entity.** A non-federal entity that provides a subaward to a subrecipient to carry out part of a federal program. FRWC is a pass-through entity.

**Questioned Cost.** A cost that appears to be unallowable, unsupported, or unnecessary, identified by an auditor or monitor, pending final determination.

**Risk Assessment Worksheet (RAW).** Form GC-FRM-012, the scored instrument used to evaluate subrecipient risk before award and at each renewal.

**Single Audit.** The audit required by 2 CFR 200.501 of a non-federal entity that expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards during its fiscal year.

**Subaward.** An award provided by FRWC to a subrecipient for the subrecipient to carry out part of a federal award. Does not include payments to a contractor or to a program beneficiary.

**Subrecipient.** An entity receiving a subaward from FRWC to carry out part of a federal program, whose performance is measured against program objectives, who has responsibility for programmatic decision making, and who must comply with federal program requirements. Criteria at 2 CFR 200.331.

**Suspected Fraud.** A reasonable indication that a subrecipient or its employee has knowingly made a false statement, submitted a false claim, misappropriated funds, or falsified records.

## 6. PROCEDURE — PRE-AWARD

### 6.1 Contractor versus Subrecipient Determination

**6.1.1** Before any funding instrument is drafted, the assigned Program Officer opens a Determination Memo (GC-FRM-010) in the grants management system (GMS) and records the entity name, proposed dollar amount, funding source, CFDA/Assistance Listing number, and a one-paragraph description of what the entity will do.

**6.1.2** The Program Officer answers all eight questions on the memo. Answering "yes" to any of items (a) through (e) indicates a **subrecipient**:

(a) Does the entity determine who is eligible to receive federal assistance?
(b) Does the entity have its own performance measured against the objectives of the federal program?
(c) Does the entity have responsibility for programmatic decision making?
(d) Is the entity responsible for adhering to applicable federal program requirements?
(e) Does the entity use the funds to carry out a program of the organization rather than to provide goods or services to FRWC?

Answering "yes" to any of items (f) through (h) indicates a **contractor**:

(f) Does the entity provide the goods or services within its normal business operations?
(g) Does the entity provide similar goods or services to many different purchasers?
(h) Does the entity operate in a competitive environment, providing goods or services ancillary to the federal program?

**6.1.3** Where indicators point in both directions, the Program Officer applies substance over form: the label used in the proposal, the funding mechanism, and the entity's tax status are not determinative. The Program Officer writes a reasoned conclusion of at least 150 words addressing the conflicting indicators.

**6.1.4** The Program Officer routes the memo to the Director of Grants Compliance, who approves, rejects, or reclassifies within **five business days**. The Director's decision is final and is recorded in the GMS determination field.

**6.1.5** No purchase order, contract, or subaward may be issued until the determination is approved. The Contract Analyst confirms an approved determination exists before opening any award record.

**6.1.6** If a determination is reclassified from contractor to subrecipient after funds have been disbursed, the Director of Grants Compliance opens a CAL entry, and the Contract Analyst issues a replacement subaward agreement retroactive to the original start date within 20 business days.

### 6.2 Eligibility and Exclusion Screening

**6.2.1** The Contract Analyst verifies, and documents in the GMS Eligibility tab, each of the following for every prospective subrecipient, and repeats the verification at each renewal:

| Check | Source | Evidence Retained |
|---|---|---|
| Active Unique Entity Identifier registration | SAM.gov | Screenshot with date, UEI, expiration date |
| No active exclusion | SAM.gov exclusions | Screenshot showing "No active exclusion records," dated |
| Legal name and address match | SAM.gov and IRS records | Comparison note |
| Tax-exempt status or corporate registration in good standing | Colorado Secretary of State | Certificate of good standing |
| No delinquent federal debt | Entity certification in application | Signed certification |
| Principals not excluded | SAM.gov exclusions search on each named principal | Screenshots |

**6.2.2** Exclusion checks are valid for **30 calendar days**. If the subaward is not executed within 30 days, the Contract Analyst reruns the check.

**6.2.3** If an active exclusion is found, the Contract Analyst notifies the Director of Grants Compliance the same business day. No subaward is issued. The Director notifies the Program Officer and documents the disqualification.

**6.2.4** If an exclusion appears on an existing subrecipient during a renewal check, the Director of Grants Compliance instructs Finance to place an immediate payment hold, notifies the CEO within one business day, and notifies the federal awarding agency and state pass-through within five business days.

### 6.3 Pre-Award Risk Assessment

**6.3.1** A Risk Assessment Worksheet (GC-FRM-012) is completed for **every subrecipient before every subaward or subaward renewal, without exception**. There is no dollar threshold below which the assessment is waived. The FY2025 finding that 9 of 31 subrecipients lacked a documented pre-award risk assessment is addressed by this rule and by the system control in §6.3.9.

**6.3.2** The Program Officer completes Sections A and B; the Fiscal Monitor completes Sections C and D. Both sign and date. Target completion is **20 business days before the intended subaward start date**.

**6.3.3** Documents the subrecipient must supply before the RAW can be completed. The Program Officer requests these in writing with a 10-business-day deadline:

1. Two most recent audited financial statements, or reviewed/compiled statements if the entity is not audited.
2. Two most recent single audit reporting packages, if applicable.
3. Current fiscal year board-approved operating budget.
4. Most recent interim financial statements (not older than 90 days).
5. Written accounting policies and procedures.
6. Written procurement policy.
7. Written personnel policy including timekeeping and leave.
8. Current negotiated indirect cost rate agreement, or a written statement electing the de minimis rate.
9. Organizational chart with names in key fiscal and program positions.
10. List of all federal awards received in the prior and current fiscal years with amounts.
11. Board roster with terms and a conflict-of-interest policy.
12. IRS Form 990 most recent filing, if applicable.
13. Certificates of insurance.
14. Any federal or state monitoring reports, sanctions, or debarment actions in the prior three years.

**6.3.4** Scoring. The RAW scores nine factors. Maximum 100 points. Higher scores indicate higher risk.

| # | Factor | Max Pts | Scoring Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prior experience with same or similar federal program | 15 | 0 = 3+ years successful; 5 = 1–2 years; 10 = same program elsewhere only; 15 = no prior federal program experience |
| 2 | Single audit results | 15 | 0 = unmodified, no findings; 5 = unmodified with non-material findings; 10 = significant deficiency or repeat finding; 15 = material weakness, modified opinion, or no audit obtained when required |
| 3 | Financial condition | 15 | 0 = 90+ days unrestricted operating reserve, positive 3-yr net; 5 = 45–89 days; 10 = under 45 days or 2 consecutive deficit years; 15 = going-concern doubt, default, or negative net assets |
| 4 | New personnel or systems | 10 | 0 = stable 2+ years; 5 = one key position or system changed in 12 months; 10 = fiscal director or accounting system changed in 12 months |
| 5 | Subaward amount and share of entity budget | 10 | 0 = under $250K and under 20% of budget; 5 = $250K–$1M or 20–50%; 10 = over $1M or over 50% |
| 6 | Prior FRWC monitoring results | 15 | 0 = no findings in 2 cycles; 5 = minor findings closed on time; 10 = findings closed late or repeated; 15 = open findings, questioned costs, or unresponsive to corrective action |
| 7 | Internal control and policy adequacy | 10 | 0 = complete written policies covering all required areas; 5 = policies present with gaps; 10 = missing procurement, timekeeping, or cash management policy |
| 8 | Program complexity and participant volume | 5 | 0 = single service, under 100 participants; 3 = multi-service; 5 = multi-site, multi-funding-stream, 500+ participants |
| 9 | Reporting timeliness in prior period | 5 | 0 = all on time; 3 = 1–2 late; 5 = 3+ late or chronically incomplete |

**6.3.5** Automatic high-risk triggers. Regardless of total score, the subrecipient is designated High risk if any of the following is true:

- Going-concern doubt in the most recent audit.
- A required single audit was not obtained or not submitted to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse.
- A material weakness in the most recent single audit.
- Open FRWC or federal findings unresolved beyond their due date.
- Questioned costs of $50,000 or more identified in the prior 24 months.
- A change in Executive Director or Chief Financial/Fiscal Officer within the prior six months.
- First-time subrecipient of FRWC with no prior federal award experience.

**6.3.6** Risk tiers.

| Tier | Score | Portfolio Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | 60–100 or automatic trigger | ~6–8 of 31 |
| **Moderate** | 30–59 | ~12–15 of 31 |
| **Low** | 0–29 | ~8–12 of 31 |

**6.3.7** The Fiscal Monitor drafts a Risk Determination summary of no more than one page: score by factor, tier, triggers applied, the two highest risks, and recommended specific conditions.

**6.3.8** The Director of Grants Compliance approves the tier within five business days, or overrides it with written rationale. Overrides in either direction are permitted and must be justified; a downward override may not be applied where an automatic trigger under §6.3.5 exists.

**6.3.9** **System control.** The GMS is configured so that the subaward record cannot advance to "Ready to Issue" status unless (a) an approved Determination Memo, (b) a dated exclusion check within 30 days, and (c) an approved RAW with an assigned tier are all attached. The Contract Analyst cannot override this control. Only the Director of Grants Compliance may grant a documented exception, and only where funds must be obligated to avoid lapse; the exception expires in 15 business days.

### 6.4 Specific Conditions

**6.4.1** For High-risk subrecipients and any subrecipient with an automatic trigger, the Director of Grants Compliance selects one or more specific conditions and states them in the subaward:

(a) Reimbursement only, no advances.
(b) Detailed transaction-level backup with every invoice.
(c) Monthly rather than quarterly financial reporting.
(d) Withholding of 10% of the subaward until performance conditions are met.
(e) Prior written approval for equipment, travel, subcontracts, or budget realignment above 5% of any line.
(f) Additional on-site visits beyond the baseline.
(g) Required technical assistance or training with a completion date.
(h) A named third-party fiscal agent for cash disbursement.

**6.4.2** Conditions are stated with the reason imposed, the actions required to remove them, and the timeline for reconsideration, in accordance with 2 CFR 200.208. The Program Officer notifies the subrecipient in writing at least 10 business days before the subaward start date.

## 7. PROCEDURE — SUBAWARD ISSUANCE AND MONITORING

### 7.1 Subaward Agreement Content

**7.1.1** The Contract Analyst assembles the subaward from template GC-TPL-003 and verifies every element below is present and populated. A missing element blocks issuance.

**Required identification data (2 CFR 200.332(a)):**

| # | Element |
|---|---|
| 1 | Subrecipient legal name matching SAM.gov |
| 2 | Subrecipient Unique Entity Identifier |
| 3 | Federal Award Identification Number (FAIN) |
| 4 | Federal award date of the FRWC prime award |
| 5 | Subaward period of performance start and end dates |
| 6 | Subaward budget period start and end dates |
| 7 | Amount of federal funds obligated by this action |
| 8 | Total amount of federal funds obligated to the subrecipient to date |
| 9 | Total amount of the federal award committed to the subrecipient |
| 10 | Federal award project description |
| 11 | Name of the federal awarding agency, FRWC as pass-through entity, and FRWC contact |
| 12 | Assistance Listing number and title |
| 13 | Identification of whether the award is research and development |
| 14 | Indirect cost rate for the federal award |

**Required terms:**

15. All applicable requirements of the federal award, including FRWC's prime award terms flowed down.
16. Applicable Uniform Guidance requirements, cited.
17. Any additional FRWC-imposed requirements necessary to meet FRWC's own compliance obligations, including performance reporting.
18. Access by FRWC, the federal agency, and auditors to the subrecipient's records and financial statements as necessary.
19. Appropriate terms and conditions for closeout.
20. Single audit requirement statement, including the $1,000,000 threshold and the obligation to submit the reporting package to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse.
21. Right of FRWC to impose specific conditions, withhold payment, suspend, or terminate.
22. Disallowed cost repayment obligation and FRWC's right of offset.
23. Records retention of three years from submission of the final expenditure report.
24. Mandatory disclosure of violations of federal criminal law involving fraud, bribery, or gratuity.
25. Prohibition on use of covered telecommunications equipment.
26. Nondiscrimination, whistleblower, trafficking, and lobbying certifications.
27. Conflict-of-interest disclosure obligation.
28. Program income treatment.
29. Equipment and property title and disposition.
30. Approved budget by cost category, attached as an exhibit.
31. Statement of work with measurable performance targets.

**7.1.2 Indirect cost treatment.** The Contract Analyst determines and states one of the following in the subaward, with documentation attached:

(a) **Federally negotiated rate.** If the subrecipient holds a current negotiated indirect cost rate agreement, FRWC applies that rate. The Contract Analyst attaches the agreement and states the rate, base, and expiration.

(b) **De minimis rate.** If the subrecipient has no negotiated rate, it may elect the de minimis rate applied to modified total direct costs. FRWC may not require an entity to accept a lower rate in this circumstance. The Contract Analyst attaches the subrecipient's written election.

(c) **Negotiated rate with FRWC.** FRWC may negotiate a rate with the subrecipient. The negotiation memo, signed by the Director of Grants Compliance and the subrecipient, is attached.

(d) **No indirect costs.** Only where the subrecipient declines in writing.

The Contract Analyst confirms the stated base excludes capital expenditures, participant support costs, subawards beyond the first $25,000 of each, and other items excluded from modified total direct costs.

**7.1.3** Signature order: subrecipient authorized official, then Director of Grants Compliance. Subawards above $1,000,000 also require CEO signature.

**7.1.4** The Contract Analyst records execution in the GMS within two business days and notifies Finance, which opens the payment record. **No reimbursement may be processed against an unexecuted subaward.**

**7.1.5** Subaward reporting submissions are filed by the Contract Analyst by the end of the month following the month of obligation for each reportable action, and are reconciled quarterly against the subaward register.

**7.1.6** Modifications follow the same content verification. Any change in scope, period, amount, or key personnel requires a written modification. Verbal approvals are prohibited.

### 7.2 The Annual Monitoring Plan

**7.2.1** By **September 15** each year, the Director of Grants Compliance issues the Annual Subrecipient Monitoring Plan for the coming year, covering all 31 subrecipients. The plan lists for each subrecipient: risk tier, assigned Program Officer and Fiscal Monitor, scheduled desk review months, scheduled on-site visit month, audit report due date, open findings, and specific conditions.

**7.2.2** Baseline monitoring by tier.

| Activity | High | Moderate | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desk review of financial report | Monthly | Quarterly | Semiannual |
| Desk review of performance report | Quarterly | Quarterly | Semiannual |
| On-site monitoring visit | Annual, plus a second focused visit | Annual | Once every two years, and never more than 36 months apart |
| Expenditure sample size | 40 transactions or 20% of dollars, whichever is greater | 25 transactions | 15 transactions |
| Documented contact with Program Officer | Monthly | Quarterly | Semiannual |
| Audit report review | Within 30 days of receipt | Within 45 days | Within 60 days |

**7.2.3** **No subrecipient may go more than 36 months without an on-site visit.** The GMS generates an exception report on the first business day of each month listing any subrecipient whose last visit exceeds 24 months. The Director of Grants Compliance schedules the visit within 60 days of appearing on that report. This control addresses the FY2025 finding that 4 subrecipients received no site visit in three years.

**7.2.4** The Director of Grants Compliance distributes the plan to the CEO, CFO, all Program Officers, all Fiscal Monitors, and the Contract Analysts. The plan is a controlled document; changes require the Director's written approval and are logged.

**7.2.5** Plan execution is reviewed at the monthly Subrecipient Oversight Meeting. Any activity more than 30 days behind schedule requires a written recovery date.

### 7.3 Desk Reviews

**7.3.1** The Fiscal Monitor confirms receipt of the subrecipient's financial report within five business days of its due date. If not received, the Program Officer sends a written reminder; if still not received after 10 business days, the Fiscal Monitor requests a payment hold.

**7.3.2** Desk review steps, documented on the Monitoring Tool (GC-FRM-014), Section 1:

1. Confirm the report is signed by an authorized official.
2. Compare reported cumulative expenditures to the approved budget by cost category. Flag any category exceeding 100% of budget, or exceeding 10% variance where prior approval is required.
3. Compare cumulative expenditures to the elapsed portion of the period of performance. Flag spending below 60% or above 140% of the straight-line pace.
4. Recalculate the indirect cost charge against the approved rate and base. Flag any variance over $500.
5. Reconcile the amount requested to the amount recorded in the FRWC accounting system.
6. Confirm cumulative draws do not exceed the total obligated amount.
7. Review the performance report against the statement of work targets. Flag any measure below 80% of the year-to-date target.
8. Confirm participant counts on the performance report reconcile to the case management extract within 2%.
9. Check whether any prior-period questioned cost has been credited.
10. Confirm no charges appear in categories not in the approved budget. **Staff time and vehicle or equipment leases receive specific attention at this step**, given the FY2025 questioned costs of $412,000 arising from exactly these charges outside an approved budget.
11. Record all flags, the explanation obtained, and the disposition.

**7.3.3** For every flag, the Fiscal Monitor issues a written inquiry with a five-business-day response deadline. Unresolved flags after 10 business days become findings entered on the CAL.

**7.3.4** For High-risk subrecipients, the Fiscal Monitor additionally selects three transactions per month for source-document testing: general ledger detail, invoice or timesheet, proof of payment, and evidence of receipt or delivery.

**7.3.5** The Fiscal Monitor completes the desk review within 15 business days of receiving a complete report and routes the Monitoring Tool to the Director of Grants Compliance for signature.

### 7.4 On-Site Monitoring

**7.4.1 Scheduling.** The Program Officer issues a written engagement letter at least **15 business days** before the visit stating dates, participants, records to be available, the sample transaction list, staff to be interviewed, and facilities to be toured. Unannounced visits require CEO approval and are used only where records may be at risk.

**7.4.2 Pre-visit preparation.** The Fiscal Monitor assembles a working file containing the executed subaward and modifications, approved budget, all financial and performance reports for the period, prior monitoring reports and open findings, the most recent single audit and management letter, the current RAW, and the accounting system draw history.

**7.4.3 Expenditure sampling.** The Fiscal Monitor documents the sample on the Monitoring Tool, Section 3:

1. Obtain a general ledger detail export for the review period, reconciled to the sum of reported expenditures. Document the reconciliation.
2. Stratify the population: (a) personnel, (b) fringe, (c) contractual, (d) equipment and leases, (e) participant costs, (f) travel, (g) other direct, (h) indirect.
3. Select **judgmentally**: every transaction above $10,000; every transaction in a category with prior findings; every equipment or vehicle lease payment; all transactions in any category not in the approved budget.
4. Select **randomly** the remainder of the required sample count (§7.2.2) using the GMS random selection function, retaining the seed and the selection log.
5. For each selected personnel charge, test: an approved time record signed by employee and supervisor covering the actual period; allocation among funding sources supported by the time record; a rate agreeing to an authorized personnel action; the position appearing in the approved budget; and payroll register agreement.
6. For each selected non-personnel charge, test: invoice or contract; evidence of receipt; proof of payment; approval by an authorized person; correct cost category; allocability to the program; and presence in the approved budget.
7. For each lease, test: an executed lease agreement; that the lease was in the approved budget; the allocation basis if shared; and lease-versus-purchase analysis for items over $50,000.
8. Compute the error rate in dollars and in transaction count. Where the dollar error rate exceeds 5%, expand the sample by 50% in the affected stratum.
9. Where the expanded sample confirms a systemic error, project the error rate across the stratum and state the projection in the report as an estimated exposure, clearly labeled as an estimate.

**7.4.4 Internal control and compliance testing.** The Fiscal Monitor and Program Officer jointly test: segregation of duties in cash disbursement; bank reconciliations for three months; procurement files for two purchases above the subrecipient's small purchase threshold; the current audit engagement status; equipment inventory against records for five items; participant eligibility files for a sample of 10 participants; personnel files for I-9, background check, and job description for three employees; and the conflict-of-interest disclosure file.

**7.4.5 Exit conference.** Held before departure with the subrecipient's Executive Director and fiscal lead. The Program Officer presents each preliminary finding, the criteria, the condition, the dollar effect, and the response deadline. Minutes are taken and signed by both parties. No finding is added after the exit conference without written notice and an opportunity to respond.

**7.4.6 Report.** The Fiscal Monitor drafts the monitoring report within **15 business days** of the visit. The Director of Grants Compliance reviews and signs within 10 business days. The report is issued to the subrecipient within **30 business days** of the last day of fieldwork.

Report contents: scope and period; procedures performed; sample size and method; findings, each with criteria, condition, cause, effect, dollar amount, and recommendation; observations not rising to findings; questioned costs by category; corrective action due dates; and a statement of appeal rights.

**7.4.7 Finding classification.**

| Class | Definition | CAP Due | FRWC Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Fraud indicators, unallowable costs over $25,000, no accounting records, or loss of legal status | 10 business days | Immediate payment hold; CEO notified; funder notified |
| **Major** | Material noncompliance, questioned costs $5,000–$25,000, missing required policy, repeat finding | 20 business days | Specific conditions; tier raised to High |
| **Minor** | Isolated documentation gaps, questioned costs under $5,000, late reports | 30 business days | Technical assistance |
| **Observation** | Practice improvement, no noncompliance | None | Noted in report |

### 7.5 Audit Report Collection and Review

**7.5.1 Annual determination.** Each October, the Fiscal Monitor obtains from every subrecipient a signed Federal Expenditure Certification (GC-FRM-016) stating total federal awards expended in its most recent fiscal year from all sources, the fiscal year end, and whether it meets or exceeds the **$1,000,000 single audit threshold**. A subrecipient's failure to return the certification within 20 business days results in a payment hold.

**7.5.2 Audit tracking register.** The Fiscal Monitor maintains a register listing, for all 31 subrecipients: fiscal year end, audit required yes/no, threshold certification date, audit due date (**nine months after fiscal year end**), date reporting package received, Federal Audit Clearinghouse submission confirmation, opinion type, findings affecting FRWC funds, management decision due date, and management decision issue date. The register is reviewed at every monthly Subrecipient Oversight Meeting. This register and its monthly review address the FY2025 finding that required audit reports were missing for 6 subrecipients.

**7.5.3 Follow-up schedule.** For every subrecipient with an audit due:

| Timing | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 60 days before due date | Written reminder with due date and submission instructions | Fiscal Monitor |
| 15 days before due date | Second reminder; confirm auditor engaged and fieldwork status | Fiscal Monitor |
| Due date +1 day | Notice of delinquency; payment hold on new reimbursements | Director of Grants Compliance |
| Due date +30 days | Written notice that continued delinquency may result in suspension; copy to subrecipient board chair | Director of Grants Compliance |
| Due date +60 days | Suspension of subaward; notification to federal awarding agency and state pass-through | CEO |

**7.5.4 Review procedure.** Within the tier timeframe at §7.2.2, the Fiscal Monitor documents on the Audit Review Checklist (GC-FRM-017):

1. Report covers the correct fiscal year and the entity is the correct legal entity.
2. Federal Audit Clearinghouse submission confirmed.
3. Opinion on financial statements; note any modification.
4. Opinion on compliance for each major program.
5. Internal control report: material weaknesses and significant deficiencies.
6. Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards — confirm FRWC pass-through funds appear with the correct FAIN and Assistance Listing number, and that the amount agrees to FRWC accounting records within 2% or is reconciled.
7. Schedule of findings and questioned costs: identify every finding, whether it touches FRWC funds or crosscutting controls.
8. Prior-year finding status: identify repeats.
9. Going-concern language and significant subsequent events.
10. Management letter comments.
11. Auditor's peer review status where the opinion is modified or a material weakness is reported.

**7.5.5 Management decision.** For every finding affecting FRWC funds, the Director of Grants Compliance issues a written management decision **within six months of receipt** of the audit reporting package. The decision states whether the finding is sustained, the reason, the amount of any disallowed cost, required corrective action, the repayment or offset method, and appeal rights. A copy goes to the CAL and to the audit register.

**7.5.6 Crosscutting findings.** A finding not specific to FRWC funds but indicating a control deficiency that could affect them (for example, payroll allocation or procurement) is treated as if it affected FRWC funds and receives a management decision.

**7.5.7 Below-threshold subrecipients.** See §8.1.

### 7.6 Virtual Monitoring Exception

**7.6.1** A virtual visit may substitute for an on-site visit only where: the subrecipient is Low risk; the last on-site visit was within 24 months; there are no open findings; and the Director of Grants Compliance approves in writing before the engagement letter is issued. Virtual visits may not substitute in two consecutive cycles. All sampling and testing requirements apply, with documents provided through the secure portal and interviews conducted by video with cameras on.

## 8. PROCEDURE — EXCEPTIONS AND SPECIAL SITUATIONS

### 8.1 Exception: Subrecipient Below the Single Audit Threshold

**8.1.1** A subrecipient expending less than $1,000,000 in federal awards in its fiscal year is exempt from the single audit requirement. **This does not reduce FRWC's monitoring obligation**, and FRWC still requires access to records.

**8.1.2** The Fiscal Monitor obtains and retains the signed Federal Expenditure Certification (§7.5.1) as the basis for the exemption, and independently corroborates it against the subrecipient's audited or unaudited financial statements and the FRWC draw history. Where the certified figure is within 10% of the threshold, the Fiscal Monitor recalculates using the subrecipient's schedule of federal revenue and documents the recalculation.

**8.1.3** In place of an audit report, the Fiscal Monitor obtains **at least one** of the following annually, in this order of preference: audited financial statements without a single audit; a reviewed financial statement; a program-specific audit; or an agreed-upon-procedures engagement over the FRWC subaward performed by an independent CPA and paid from the subaward as a direct cost where allowable.

**8.1.4** Where none of the above is obtainable, the Fiscal Monitor performs an **enhanced desk review** annually consisting of: a full-year general ledger export reconciled to reported expenditures; source-document testing of 25 transactions; three months of bank reconciliations; the payroll register for one quarter tied to time records; and confirmation that no other federal funding pushes the entity over the threshold.

**8.1.5** The exemption is documented in the GMS Audit tab with the certification, the corroboration note, the alternative procedure selected, and the Director of Grants Compliance's approval. **An exemption is never assumed from silence.**

### 8.2 Exception: Going-Concern Doubt

**8.2.1** Upon identifying going-concern language in an audit, a lender default, a missed payroll, or negative net assets, the Fiscal Monitor notifies the Director of Grants Compliance **the same business day**.

**8.2.2** Within two business days the Director of Grants Compliance:

1. Designates the subrecipient High risk (automatic trigger, §6.3.5).
2. Suspends any advance payments; converts to reimbursement only.
3. Requires 13-week rolling cash flow projections, submitted weekly.
4. Requires monthly board minutes and the board's written turnaround plan.
5. Requires 10 business days' prior written notice of any layoff, office closure, or program suspension.
6. Instructs the Program Officer to prepare a **participant continuity plan** identifying how many participants are enrolled, which other subrecipients could absorb them, and the transfer timeline.
7. Requires an inventory of FRWC-funded equipment with location and serial numbers within 10 business days.
8. Notifies the CEO, who notifies the federal awarding agency and state pass-through within 10 business days.

**8.2.3** The Director of Grants Compliance and CFO jointly assess unrecoverable exposure: unliquidated obligations, advances not yet expended, and equipment at risk. Where exposure exceeds $250,000, the CEO decides within 15 business days whether to suspend or terminate for convenience.

**8.2.4** Where the subrecipient continues, a written continuation memorandum records the conditions, the exposure limit, and a mandatory 60-day reassessment. Continuation without a signed memorandum is prohibited.

**8.2.5** Where the subrecipient ceases operations, the Program Officer executes the continuity plan, the Fiscal Monitor performs an immediate closeout under §9, and the Contract Analyst secures records under §9.5 before facilities are vacated.

### 8.3 Exception: Mid-Year Change in Key Personnel

**8.3.1** Every subaward names key personnel — at minimum the Executive Director, the fiscal officer, and the program director — and requires written notice to FRWC **within 10 business days** of any departure, replacement, or vacancy.

**8.3.2** Upon notice, or upon FRWC learning of a change from any source, the Program Officer logs it in the GMS Key Personnel tab and notifies the Fiscal Monitor and Director of Grants Compliance within two business days.

**8.3.3** Response by position:

| Position vacated | Required response |
|---|---|
| **Fiscal officer / Chief Financial Officer** | Rescore RAW factor 4 (10 points). Interim desk review within 30 days including one month of source-document testing. Verify who now signs reimbursement requests and obtain an updated signature authorization. Confirm bank account signatories changed. Confirm accounting system access removed for the departed employee. |
| **Executive Director** | Obtain board resolution naming the interim leader. Confirm continued authority to execute agreements. Rescore RAW factor 4. Program Officer meets the interim leader within 20 business days. |
| **Program director** | Program Officer conducts a performance check-in within 20 business days; confirm participant service continuity; confirm data entry continuing. |
| **Two or more key positions within 90 days** | Automatic High-risk designation; unscheduled on-site visit within 60 days. |

**8.3.4** Where the rescored RAW moves the subrecipient into a higher tier, the Director of Grants Compliance issues a **revised monitoring plan for that subrecipient within 10 business days** and notifies the subrecipient in writing of the new monitoring frequency and any specific conditions.

**8.3.5** Where a fiscal officer vacancy remains unfilled for more than 60 days, the Director of Grants Compliance requires either a contracted fiscal service provider or a payment hold until the position is filled.

**8.3.6** A change in key personnel requires a subaward modification only where the position is named in the agreement text; otherwise the Key Personnel tab entry, with the subrecipient's written notice attached, is the record.

### 8.4 Exception: Suspected Fraud

**8.4.1** Any FRWC employee who suspects fraud involving subaward funds reports it **the same business day** to the Director of Grants Compliance, or to the CEO where the Director may be involved. Indicators include altered or duplicate documents, ghost employees or participants, payments to entities owned by subrecipient staff, missing records after a request, cash withdrawals, timesheets signed after the fact in identical handwriting, and refusal of access.

**8.4.2 Prohibited actions.** The reporting employee and all others must **not**: confront the suspected individual; announce the suspicion to the subrecipient; request additional records in a way that signals an investigation; delete or alter any file or message; or discuss the matter outside the response team.

**8.4.3** Within one business day the Director of Grants Compliance:

1. Opens a confidential file outside the GMS, access-restricted to the response team.
2. Preserves all evidence in hand: originals secured, electronic files copied with hash values, email and portal logs preserved. Issues a litigation-hold instruction to IT.
3. Convenes the response team: CEO, CFO, Director of Grants Compliance, and legal counsel.
4. Instructs Finance to hold all payments to the subrecipient. Finance implements within one business day.

**8.4.4 Mandatory disclosure.** Where there is credible evidence of a violation of federal criminal law involving fraud, bribery, or gratuity, or a violation of the civil False Claims Act, the CEO makes written disclosure to the **federal awarding agency and its Office of Inspector General**, with a copy to the state pass-through entity, **in a timely manner** and in no case later than **10 business days** after the response team determines the evidence is credible. Legal counsel reviews the disclosure before transmittal. **The disclosure is not delayed pending completion of FRWC's own inquiry.**

**8.4.5 Investigation.** FRWC conducts only the fact-gathering necessary to determine credibility and dollar exposure. Once a referral is made, FRWC coordinates all further steps with the OIG and does not conduct interviews of subrecipient staff without OIG concurrence.

**8.4.6 Notification of the subrecipient.** The subrecipient is notified only after the response team, with legal counsel, determines notification will not compromise the investigation, and only in the form counsel approves. Where the suspicion concerns a single employee rather than the organization, the subrecipient's board chair is normally notified.

**8.4.7 Concurrent actions.** Suspension or termination, cost disallowance, and referral for suspension and debarment proceed in parallel with any criminal or civil matter and are not deferred.

**8.4.8 Records.** The confidential file is retained for the longer of three years after final resolution or the period directed by the OIG, and is excluded from routine records destruction. The CAL contains only a reference number and status.

### 8.5 Other Exceptions

**8.5.1 Emergency obligation.** Where the prime award period requires obligation before a RAW can be completed, the Director of Grants Compliance may approve a 15-business-day conditional subaward limited to 10% of the planned amount, reimbursement only, with the RAW completed before any further obligation. Requires CEO countersignature.

**8.5.2 Subrecipient refuses access.** Refusal to provide records or access is a Critical finding. Payment hold same day; written demand citing the access provision; funder notified within 10 business days if unresolved in 15.

**8.5.3 Lower-tier subawards.** A subrecipient may issue a lower-tier subaward only with FRWC's prior written approval, and remains fully responsible for monitoring it. FRWC tests the subrecipient's monitoring of its lower tiers during on-site visits.

## 9. PROCEDURE — FINDINGS, RECOVERY, AND CLOSEOUT

### 9.1 Corrective Action Log

**9.1.1** Every finding from any source — FRWC monitoring, single audit, federal or state review, self-disclosure, complaint — is entered on the CAL within **three business days** of identification by the Fiscal Monitor.

**9.1.2** CAL fields: finding ID; subrecipient; source and date; classification; criteria citation; condition; cause; dollar effect; questioned cost amount; corrective action required; subrecipient owner; FRWC owner; CAP due date; CAP received date; CAP approval date; corrective action due date; verification method; verification date; closure date; repeat indicator; and disallowed cost disposition.

**9.1.3** No finding is removed from the CAL. Closure is recorded; the record persists.

### 9.2 Corrective Action Plans

**9.2.1** The Program Officer transmits findings with the CAP deadline from the table at §7.4.7. An acceptable CAP states, for each finding: the root cause; the specific action; the responsible person by name and title; the completion date; how recurrence will be prevented; and the evidence that will be submitted.

**9.2.2** The Program Officer and Fiscal Monitor review the CAP within **10 business days**. A CAP that restates the finding, blames staff turnover without a systemic remedy, or lacks dates is rejected with written reasons and a 10-business-day resubmission deadline.

**9.2.3** The Director of Grants Compliance approves each CAP in writing. Approval does not resolve questioned costs, which follow §9.3.

**9.2.4** **Verification is mandatory.** The Fiscal Monitor verifies each completed action with evidence — a revised policy with an adoption date, training rosters, corrected records, a sample retest — and records the method and date. **A subrecipient's assertion of completion is not sufficient evidence of closure.**

**9.2.5** A CAP action past due by 30 days escalates to the Director of Grants Compliance, who imposes specific conditions or a payment hold. Past due by 60 days escalates to the CEO for a suspension decision.

**9.2.6** A repeat finding is automatically classified one level more severe and triggers automatic High-risk designation.

### 9.3 Questioned and Disallowed Cost Recovery

**9.3.1** Questioned costs are stated in the monitoring report by category with the supporting calculation and the criteria violated. The subrecipient has **30 calendar days** to submit additional documentation or a written rebuttal.

**9.3.2** The Fiscal Monitor evaluates the response and recommends: allowed with documentation; disallowed; or partially disallowed. The Director of Grants Compliance issues a written **management decision within 30 calendar days** of the response deadline, stating the amount disallowed, the criteria, the basis for rejecting contrary arguments, the repayment amount and due date, and appeal rights.

**9.3.3** Repayment options, in order of FRWC preference:

1. **Cash repayment** within 30 calendar days of the management decision.
2. **Offset** against the next reimbursement, or successive reimbursements where the amount exceeds one month's draw.
3. **Written repayment agreement** of up to 12 months, requiring CFO approval, and prohibited where the entity has going-concern doubt or is in its final subaward year.
4. **Substitution of non-federal funds** for the disallowed charge, with evidence of the substituting source.

Repayment from federal funds of any award is prohibited.

**9.3.4** Finance records a receivable upon issuance of the management decision and reports the aging monthly to the Director of Grants Compliance and CFO.

**9.3.5** Where the disallowed cost arises from a category-level pattern — for example, staff time and vehicle leases charged outside an approved budget, as in the FY2025 $412,000 matter — the Fiscal Monitor tests the full period of performance for that category, not only the sampled months, before finalizing the amount.

**9.3.6** FRWC remains accountable to the federal awarding agency for disallowed costs whether or not it recovers them from the subrecipient. Where recovery appears unlikely, the CFO and Director of Grants Compliance jointly notify the federal awarding agency and state pass-through, and the CFO determines whether the cost must be repaid from FRWC non-federal funds.

**9.3.7** Write-off of an uncollected amount requires: documented collection efforts; a legal-counsel assessment; CFO approval above $10,000; CEO approval above $50,000; and notification to the federal awarding agency. Write-off does not close the CAL entry.

**9.3.8** Where a subrecipient has repeated or material disallowed costs, the Director of Grants Compliance evaluates referral for suspension and debarment and documents the decision either way.

### 9.4 Closeout

**9.4.1** Ninety days before the subaward end date, the Contract Analyst issues a closeout notice listing all deliverables and deadlines.

**9.4.2** The subrecipient submits within **60 calendar days** of the period-of-performance end date: the final expenditure report; the final performance report; a final indirect cost calculation; an equipment inventory; a program income report; a subcontractor closeout certification; a records-retention certification naming the custodian and location; and a release-of-claims certification.

**9.4.3** The Fiscal Monitor reconciles the final expenditure report to the FRWC accounting system and the total obligated amount; recalculates final indirect costs; confirms no expenditures after the end date except allowable pre-existing obligations; and confirms all questioned costs resolved and all CAP actions verified.

**9.4.4** The Program Officer confirms final performance against targets, participant records transferred or retained appropriately, and equipment disposition instructions issued.

**9.4.5** Any unobligated balance is deobligated by the Contract Analyst and any excess payment recovered under §9.3.3.

**9.4.6** The Director of Grants Compliance signs the Closeout Certification (GC-FRM-021) only when every item in §§9.4.2–9.4.5 is complete. Where an audit or finding remains open, closeout is recorded as **administratively closed with open items**, and the CAL entry stays active.

**9.4.7** Final closeout occurs within **one year** of receipt of all required final reports. FRWC's final performance assessment is recorded in the GMS for use in future risk assessment and any federal past-performance reporting.

### 9.5 Record Retention

**9.5.1** Retention is **three years from the date FRWC submits the final expenditure report** on the prime award to the federal awarding agency, not from the subaward end date. The Contract Analyst records the calculated destruction-eligible date in the GMS at closeout.

**9.5.2** Retention extends where: litigation, claim, or audit is started before the period expires (until resolution and final action); the federal awarding agency notifies FRWC in writing of an extension; records relate to real property or equipment (three years from final disposition); or indirect cost proposals are involved (per 2 CFR 200.334).

**9.5.3** No record subject to a litigation hold under §8.4.3 is destroyed. The Director of Grants Compliance maintains the hold list and reviews it before any destruction cycle.

**9.5.4** Records are retained electronically in the GMS as the system of record. Paper originals of executed agreements and single audit reporting packages are retained in the Grants Compliance secure file room. Destruction requires a Records Destruction Authorization signed by the Director of Grants Compliance, with a log of what was destroyed and when.

## 10. MONITORING OF THIS PROCEDURE

**10.1** The Director of Grants Compliance reports monthly to the CEO on: RAWs completed versus subawards issued (target 100%); on-site visits completed versus planned; subrecipients exceeding 24 months since last visit (target zero); audit reports outstanding past due date (target zero); management decisions issued within six months (target 100%); open findings by age; and disallowed cost receivable aging.

**10.2** Quarterly, the Director of Grants Compliance issues the Subrecipient Portfolio Risk Report to the CEO and Board Audit Committee, showing tier distribution across all 31 subrecipients, tier changes, and portfolio-level themes.

**10.3** Annually, before the review under the Document Control block, the Director of Grants Compliance performs a self-assessment: a sample of five subaward files tested for all §7.1.1 elements; five RAWs tested for completeness and approval; three monitoring reports traced to CAL entries and closure evidence; and the audit register reconciled to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse. Results and remediation are reported to the CEO and Board Audit Committee.
