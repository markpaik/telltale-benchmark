## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the required method for reprocessing flexible gastrointestinal endoscopes (gastroscopes and colonoscopes) at Klamath Valley Regional Medical Center (KVRMC) from the moment a procedure ends through bedside precleaning, transport, leak testing, manual cleaning, cleaning verification, automated high-level disinfection, drying, storage, and release for the next patient use. It defines documentation requirements that create an unbroken record tying every endoscope to every patient and every technician who handled it, establishes quarterly water quality testing, defines initial and annual competency requirements, and defines the required response to exceptions including failed leak tests, failed cleaning verification tests, delayed reprocessing, loaner endoscopes, after-hours emergency cases, and endoscopes implicated in an infection investigation.

This procedure exists to prevent patient-to-patient transmission of infectious organisms via flexible endoscopes and to satisfy the plan of correction commitments made to The Joint Commission and the Oregon Health Authority following the May 2026 survey findings.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all flexible gastrointestinal endoscopes in the KVRMC inventory (currently 22 gastroscopes and colonoscopes), all loaner or evaluation endoscopes brought into the facility, and all personnel who handle endoscopes at any point between patient use and return to patient-ready storage, including procedure room nurses, transport staff, and reprocessing technicians.

This procedure applies at all hours, including nights, weekends, and holidays. There is no circumstance under which any step of this procedure may be abbreviated or skipped for an emergency case; Section 6.15 defines the only approved after-hours pathway.

This procedure does not apply to rigid endoscopes, bronchoscopes, or endoscope accessories that are single-use or that are reprocessed by steam sterilization; those are covered by separate procedures listed in Section 9.

## 3. Definitions

**AER (Automated Endoscope Reprocessor):** A machine that performs cleaning-assist, high-level disinfection, and rinsing cycles on flexible endoscopes according to a validated program.

**ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) Test:** A rapid cleaning verification test that detects organic residue by measuring ATP in a sample collected from an endoscope channel or surface, reported in relative light units (RLU).

**Bedside Precleaning:** The wiping and channel flushing performed in the procedure room immediately after the endoscope is removed from the patient, before transport.

**Borescope:** A thin, flexible camera inserted into endoscope channels to visually inspect internal surfaces for damage, debris, or fluid.

**Delayed Reprocessing:** Any situation in which manual cleaning does not begin within 60 minutes of the end of the procedure (or within the shorter time specified in the manufacturer's IFU, whichever is stricter).

**Hang Time:** The maximum period a properly reprocessed, dried endoscope may remain in storage before it must be reprocessed again prior to patient use. At KVRMC the hang time is **7 days**, established by the facility risk assessment dated June 15, 2026 and consistent with AAMI ST91.

**High-Level Disinfection (HLD):** A process that destroys all microorganisms except high numbers of bacterial spores, accomplished at KVRMC by the AER using an FDA-cleared high-level disinfectant.

**IFU (Instructions for Use):** The endoscope, AER, chemistry, or accessory manufacturer's validated written instructions. Where this procedure and an IFU conflict, the more stringent requirement applies; the technician must notify the Manager before proceeding if the conflict is unclear.

**Leak Test:** A pressurized test of the endoscope's internal integrity performed before immersion, per the endoscope IFU.

**MEC (Minimum Effective Concentration):** The lowest concentration at which a high-level disinfectant achieves its labeled performance, verified by test strip before each AER cycle where required by the chemistry IFU.

**MRC/MRE (Minimum Recommended Concentration test strip):** The chemistry manufacturer's test strip used to verify MEC.

**Point-of-Use Treatment:** Synonym for bedside precleaning as used in AAMI ST91.

**Protein Residue Test:** A cleaning verification swab test that detects residual protein on endoscope surfaces or in channels; a color change indicates failure.

**Reprocessing Cycle Record:** The complete documentation set for one reprocessing event of one endoscope, generated primarily through barcode scans in the scope tracking system.

**Scope Tracking System:** The electronic system (currently iRIScope, hereafter "the tracking system") that records, via barcode scan, every handoff, process step, technician identity, patient association, and test result for every endoscope.

**Sequestration:** Removal of an endoscope from service with a red "DO NOT USE" tag, entry of out-of-service status in the tracking system, and physical placement in the locked quarantine cabinet in the reprocessing room.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Bedside Precleaning (Point-of-Use Treatment)

Performed by the procedure room nurse or trained technician **immediately** upon removal of the endoscope from the patient, before the endoscope is disconnected from the processor.

1. Don gloves and eye protection if not already worn.
2. Wipe the insertion tube from control body to distal tip with a lint-free cloth or sponge saturated with freshly prepared enzymatic detergent solution. Discard the wipe.
3. Place the distal tip into the container of detergent solution. Suction detergent through the biopsy/suction channel for **at least 30 seconds** or until the return solution runs visibly clear, whichever is longer, alternating suctioning solution and air per the endoscope IFU.
4. Depress the air/water valve and flush the air/water channel per the endoscope IFU (use the model-specific cleaning adapter where the IFU requires it).
5. If the model has an auxiliary water or elevator channel, flush per the IFU using the model-specific adapter. Elevator channels: actuate the elevator up and down during flushing.
6. Detach the endoscope from the light source/processor. Attach the water-resistant cap if the model requires one (verify against the IFU photo card at the workstation — attaching the wrong cap or omitting a required cap will flood the endoscope).
7. Scan the endoscope barcode and the patient label into the tracking system at the procedure room station. Confirm the tracking system displays **"Precleaned — [time]"** with the nurse's ID. Record the procedure end time; this timestamp starts the 60-minute reprocessing clock (Section 6.14).
8. Place the endoscope loosely coiled (coils no smaller than the IFU minimum, typically ≥ 25 cm diameter) in a contaminated-transport container. Close the lid. Do not place valves, caps, or accessories loose on top of the insertion tube; place them in the container's accessory basket. Single-use valves are discarded in the procedure room.

### 6.2 Transport

1. Transport staff scan the container's endoscope barcode at pickup. The tracking system records transporter ID and pickup time.
2. Transport the closed, labeled container directly to the decontamination room by the designated soiled route. Do not stop, stack containers, or transport clean and contaminated endoscopes together.
3. Deliver to the decontamination pass-through window. Scan at delivery. Target elapsed time from procedure end to decontamination intake: **≤ 15 minutes**.
4. The receiving technician scans intake, which stamps the intake time. If the time from procedure end already exceeds 60 minutes, or will foreseeably exceed it before manual cleaning begins, the technician follows Section 6.14 (Delayed Reprocessing) — do not simply proceed.

### 6.3 Decontamination Setup and Visual Inspection at Intake

1. Don full PPE (Section 5).
2. Retrieve the model-specific IFU and open it to the cleaning section. Verify brush sizes and adapters against the IFU table.
3. Visually inspect the endoscope exterior under lighted magnification for cuts, bite marks, dents, distal-end damage, loose parts, or fluid under the lens. Any damage found: stop, sequester per Section 6.13 steps 4–7, and document.

### 6.4 Leak Testing

Performed on **every endoscope, after every use, before immersion.** No exceptions.

1. Confirm the water-resistant cap (if required) is attached and seated.
2. Attach the leak tester to the venting connector per the endoscope IFU. Pressurize to the IFU-specified pressure. Confirm the bending section visibly expands (dry check).
3. With the endoscope pressurized, fully immerse it in clear water (no detergent — bubbles must be visible). Keep the endoscope pressurized and immersed for the IFU-specified observation period, **minimum 30 seconds**, while:
   a. Angulating the bending section fully in all directions using the control knobs.
   b. Depressing valve cylinders.
   c. Observing the entire endoscope — control body, insertion tube, bending section, distal tip, and universal cord — for a continuous stream of bubbles. (Isolated bubbles releasing from surface texture on first immersion are normal; a steady stream from one point is a leak.)
4. **Pass:** Remove the endoscope from the water while pressurized, depressurize per the IFU, disconnect the tester, and scan **"Leak Test — Pass"** in the tracking system. Proceed to Section 6.5.
5. **Fail:** Follow Section 6.13 (Failed Leak Test). Do not immerse further and do not proceed to manual cleaning.

### 6.5 Manual Cleaning

Manual cleaning must begin within **60 minutes** of the procedure end time (or sooner if the IFU is stricter). Manual cleaning is required before every AER cycle; the AER's wash phase does not replace it.

1. Fill the sink to the marked line with water at the detergent IFU temperature range (verify with thermometer; record temperature in the tracking system prompt). Add detergent measured with the calibrated pump to the IFU concentration. Detergent solution is **single use — one endoscope, then drain.**
2. Fully immerse the endoscope. All subsequent cleaning steps occur below the water surface to prevent aerosolization.
3. Wash all external surfaces with a soft lint-free cloth or sponge. Clean the distal end, including the air/water nozzle and (if present) elevator recess, with the IFU-specified soft brush; for elevator models, brush the recess with the elevator raised, lowered, and in between, minimum 10 strokes per position or per IFU.
4. Remove all removable valves and buttons. Brush and flush valve cylinders and openings with the IFU-specified brushes and adapters. Reusable valves are cleaned and reprocessed with (and stay paired to) their endoscope.
5. Brush each accessible channel (suction/biopsy channel, both directions where the IFU directs) with the exact brush size specified in the IFU:
   a. Pass the brush through the full channel length until it exits the distal end.
   b. Clean debris from the brush bristles under the water surface before withdrawing.
   c. Repeat until **no debris is visible on the brush bristles for one full pass, with a minimum of three passes** per channel segment.
   d. Brush the channel openings at the control body.
6. Attach the model-specific all-channel irrigation adapter. Flush every channel (including air/water and auxiliary channels not accessible by brush) with detergent solution at the IFU volume and contact time. Soak the fully immersed endoscope for the detergent IFU contact time (**timer required; do not estimate**).
7. Drain the sink. Fill the rinse sink with fresh utility water. Rinse all external surfaces and flush all channels with the IFU-specified rinse volume (minimum 3× flush volume unless the IFU specifies more).
8. Purge all channels with instrument air to remove rinse water.
9. Inspect under lighted magnification: exterior for cleanliness and damage; distal end for debris in the nozzle or elevator recess. If debris remains, repeat steps 1–8. Two failed visual inspections: sequester per Section 6.13 steps 4–7 and notify the Manager.
10. Scan **"Manual Clean — Complete"** in the tracking system with the timer values prompted (detergent temperature, soak time).

### 6.6 Cleaning Verification Testing

Cleaning verification confirms manual cleaning was effective **before** high-level disinfection. HLD is not a substitute for cleaning; a soiled endoscope cannot be reliably disinfected.

**6.6.1 Sampling frequency**
- **ATP testing: every endoscope, every reprocessing cycle**, sampled after manual cleaning and before the AER. (This frequency exceeds the AAMI ST91 minimum and is required under the KVRMC plan of correction; it may be reduced only by written revision of this SOP approved by the VP Quality after 12 consecutive months of failure rate < 1%.)
- **Protein residue testing:** each endoscope at least **monthly** per the rotation schedule posted in the decontamination room, and additionally after any repair return, after any ATP failure retest, and on all endoscopes tested during an infection investigation.

**6.6.2 ATP test method**
1. Using aseptic technique per the kit IFU, flush the biopsy/suction channel with the kit's sterile sampling solution, collect the effluent at the distal end, and swab per kit instructions. For elevator models, additionally swab the elevator recess.
2. Insert the swab into the luminometer and read RLU.
3. **Pass:** RLU **< 200** (or the kit manufacturer's validated benchmark, whichever is lower). Scan the result into the tracking system (**"ATP — Pass — [RLU value]"**) and proceed to Section 6.7.
4. **Fail:** RLU ≥ 200. Follow Section 6.13.2 (Failed Residue Test).

**6.6.3 Protein residue test method**
1. Sample the biopsy channel per the kit IFU and develop per kit instructions.
2. **Pass** (no color change at or below threshold): scan the result and proceed. **Fail:** follow Section 6.13.2.

### 6.7 Automated Cycle (High-Level Disinfection in the AER)

1. Verify the AER's daily setup is complete: disinfectant within its labeled reuse life and expiry, self-disinfection cycle current, and printer/data link functioning. If not, complete daily setup first per the AER IFU.
2. Test the disinfectant with the MEC test strip. Record the result in the tracking system. **If the MEC test fails, discard and replace the disinfectant before running any cycle.** MEC strips must themselves be within expiry and quality-checked per the strip IFU on each new bottle opening.
3. Select the AER connector set validated for the exact endoscope model (connector chart posted on each AER). Connect every channel per the AER IFU; verify no kinks and that the endoscope is fully seated below the fluid line. Place valves and buttons in the basket. **Wrong or missing connectors mean unprocessed channels — verify against the chart every time.**
4. Scan the endoscope barcode, the AER unit barcode, and the technician badge at the AER. Select the validated cycle for the model.
5. Start the cycle. Do not open the AER during the cycle. If the cycle aborts or alarms for any reason, the entire cycle must be repeated from the start after resolving the fault; a partial cycle counts as no cycle.
6. At cycle completion, verify the printed/electronic cycle record shows: cycle complete without alarm, disinfectant temperature and contact time within the validated range, and final rinse complete. Attach/associate the cycle record to the endoscope's tracking record.
7. If the AER performs a terminal alcohol flush and air purge, confirm it ran; if not, perform a 70–90% isopropyl alcohol flush of all channels followed by instrument-air purge **only if the endoscope IFU permits alcohol** (check the model card — some IFUs prohibit it).
8. Remove the endoscope with clean, freshly donned gloves. From this point the endoscope is handled only with clean gloves and only in the clean area.
9. Scan **"HLD — Complete"** in the tracking system.

### 6.8 Drying

Drying is a critical infection-prevention step. Moisture remaining in channels supports growth of waterborne organisms including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

1. Purge all channels with instrument air using the drying adapter until no visible moisture exits the distal end, minimum per the endoscope IFU.
2. Dry all external surfaces with a clean, low-lint cloth. Dry valve cylinders and ports.
3. Place the endoscope in the drying cabinet with all channels connected to the cabinet's forced-air manifold. Minimum active drying time: **10 minutes of forced filtered air through all channels**, or longer per the cabinet and endoscope IFUs. The cabinet timer must complete before release to storage.
4. After drying, perform a borescope spot-check of the suction/biopsy channel on at least **two endoscopes per day** (rotating schedule) and on **every endoscope returning from repair or loan**, checking for retained moisture, debris, or channel damage. Findings of damage or debris: sequester per Section 6.13. Findings of moisture only: repeat drying and recheck.
5. Scan **"Drying — Complete"** in the tracking system.

### 6.9 Storage and Hang Time

1. Store endoscopes only in the designated clean storage cabinets (drying/storage cabinets with continuous HEPA-filtered air, or approved ventilated cabinets), hanging vertically or in the cabinet's validated horizontal cradle, with valves and buttons detached and stored with (but not attached to) their endoscope in a labeled basket.
2. Endoscopes must not touch each other or the cabinet floor. Distal tips hang free.
3. Scan the endoscope into its storage location. The tracking system records the storage start time and calculates the **7-day hang time expiration**, displayed on the cabinet tag.
4. Before dispensing any endoscope for patient use, the dispensing staff member scans the endoscope; the tracking system will **block release** if the hang time has expired, if any process step is missing, or if the endoscope is sequestered. A blocked endoscope goes to reprocessing (full cycle from Section 6.4) before use — never override the block. Overrides do not exist in the system by design.
5. Any endoscope stored past 7 days is fully reprocessed (leak test through drying) before patient use.
6. Storage cabinets are cleaned and disinfected weekly and whenever visibly soiled; cleaning is logged on the cabinet log.

### 6.10 Documentation and Traceability

Every reprocessing cycle record must contain, via barcode scans and system prompts, all of the following. A cycle record missing any element is treated as a failed cycle and the endoscope is reprocessed.

1. Endoscope unique ID (serial number barcode).
2. Patient identifier for the preceding procedure (scanned in the procedure room, Section 6.1).
3. Procedure end time (start of the 60-minute clock).
4. Identity of the nurse performing bedside precleaning.
5. Transport pickup/delivery times and transporter ID.
6. Leak test result and technician ID.
7. Manual cleaning completion, detergent temperature, soak time, and technician ID.
8. Cleaning verification result(s) (ATP value; protein result when performed) and technician ID.
9. AER unit ID, cycle number, MEC result, cycle parameters/printout, and technician ID.
10. Drying completion time and technician ID.
11. Storage location, storage start time, and hang time expiration.
12. Dispensing scan linking the endoscope to the **next** patient at time of use.

The Manager audits 10% of cycle records monthly for completeness and audits the tracking system's exception queue (missed scans, out-of-sequence scans) **daily**. Any out-of-sequence or missing scan triggers immediate investigation; if the endoscope's status cannot be positively verified, it is reprocessed before use.

Records retention: reprocessing cycle records, AER printouts, verification results, water testing results, and competency records are retained for **a minimum of 7 years** (or longer if required by an open investigation or litigation hold).

### 6.11 Competency: Initial and Annual

1. **Initial competency (before any independent reprocessing):**
   a. Completion of the department orientation curriculum, including AAMI ST91 overview, this SOP, and the IFU for every endoscope model in inventory.
   b. Supervised performance of a **minimum of 10 complete reprocessing cycles** observed by the Department Educator or a designated preceptor using the model-specific competency checklist, with 100% of critical steps performed correctly on the final 3 consecutive observations.
   c. Written examination score ≥ 85%.
   d. Demonstrated leak test failure response, ATP failure response, and AER alarm response using scenario drills.
   e. Certification (CER, CRCST with endoscope module, or equivalent) obtained within 12 months of hire; documented pursuit begins at hire.
2. **Annual competency:** every reprocessing technician, procedure room nurse (bedside precleaning module only), and transport staff member (transport module only) completes an observed competency assessment on the applicable checklist plus written exam each year, and additionally whenever: a new endoscope model or AER enters service, an IFU changes materially, the individual is implicated in a verification failure attributed to technique, or the individual has not performed reprocessing for more than 90 days.
3. The Educator maintains the competency matrix. The tracking system blocks technician badge scans at reprocessing steps for any individual whose competency has lapsed.
4. Failed competency: the individual is removed from independent reprocessing, retrained, and reassessed; two consecutive failures are escalated to the Manager and Human Resources.

### 6.12 Quarterly Water Testing

1. In the first month of each calendar quarter, Biomedical Engineering collects water samples per the AER IFU and AAMI ST108 from: (a) the final rinse water of each AER, (b) the utility water supply at each manual cleaning sink, and (c) any point-of-use filter outlets.
2. Samples are tested by the contracted laboratory for heterotrophic plate count and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*; final rinse water must meet the AER manufacturer's and AAMI ST108 quality criteria (target: HPC ≤ 10 CFU/mL final rinse; no *P. aeruginosa* detected).
3. AER water filters are replaced per the AER IFU schedule and immediately after any failed water result.
4. **Failed water result:** the affected AER is removed from service immediately, filters replaced, lines disinfected per the AER IFU, and the AER retested. It returns to service only after a passing result, released jointly by the Manager and Infection Preventionist. Endoscopes processed in that AER since the last passing test are identified from the tracking system and reprocessed in the other unit before use; the Infection Preventionist assesses whether patient notification is warranted.
5. Results are reported to the Infection Preventionist within 24 hours of receipt and to the Infection Control Committee quarterly.

### 6.13 Exception Path: Failed Leak Test

1. Depressurize per the IFU. Do **not** immerse the endoscope further and do not run it through the AER.
2. Wipe external surfaces with detergent solution and then disinfectant wipe per the endoscope IFU's leaking-scope instructions (do not force fluid into channels).
3. Repeat the leak test once to rule out tester or connection error. If it passes on retest with a verified tester, document both results and proceed to Section 6.5.
4. Confirmed failure: attach a red "DO NOT USE" tag stating "FAILED LEAK TEST — CONTAMINATED," scan **"Sequestered — Leak Test Failure"** in the tracking system (this blocks all future dispensing scans), and place the endoscope in the locked quarantine cabinet.
5. Notify the Manager (or House Supervisor after hours) the same shift.
6. Biomedical Engineering coordinates shipment to the manufacturer/authorized repair vendor per the vendor's contaminated-device shipping instructions.
7. On return from repair, the endoscope is treated as a loaner return: full inspection, borescope examination, and complete reprocessing per Section 6.16 steps 3–5 before entering storage.

### 6.13.2 Exception Path: Failed Residue Test (ATP or Protein)

1. A failed cleaning verification test means manual cleaning was inadequate. Do not proceed to the AER.
2. Repeat the **entire** manual cleaning process (Section 6.5, steps 1–9) with fresh detergent solution and fresh brushes.
3. Retest with **both** ATP and protein residue tests.
4. **Second pass:** document both failure and passing results, proceed to Section 6.7, and complete a verification failure report in the tracking system. The Educator provides just-in-time retraining to the involved technician within 5 business days if technique is implicated.
5. **Second failure:** sequester the endoscope per Section 6.13 steps 4–5 (tag: "FAILED CLEANING VERIFICATION ×2"), perform a borescope inspection of all accessible channels, and notify the Manager and Infection Preventionist. The endoscope goes to the repair vendor for evaluation if the borescope shows damage or if a third supervised cleaning attempt also fails.
6. The Manager reviews all verification failures weekly for trends by endoscope, technician, or model, and reports monthly to the Infection Control Committee.

### 6.14 Exception Path: Delayed Reprocessing

1. If manual cleaning cannot begin within 60 minutes of procedure end (or the IFU limit if shorter), the endoscope is a delayed-reprocessing case. Common causes: after-hours cases, transport delay, decontamination backlog.
2. Follow the endoscope IFU's delayed reprocessing instructions exactly. Where the IFU specifies extended soak times, additional flushing volumes, or a specific delayed-reprocessing protocol, those replace the standard Section 6.5 parameters. Where the IFU has no delayed protocol, perform an extended detergent soak of **60 minutes** fully immersed with all channels filled with detergent, then complete Section 6.5 in full.
3. Scan **"Delayed Reprocessing"** in the tracking system with the elapsed time; the system requires entry of the reason.
4. Cleaning verification (ATP and protein, both) is mandatory on every delayed-reprocessing endoscope.
5. Delays exceeding the IFU's maximum allowable delay (or **8 hours** where the IFU is silent): notify the Manager; the endoscope may require vendor evaluation per the IFU.
6. The Manager reviews delayed-reprocessing entries weekly; three or more delays in a week trigger a workflow review.

### 6.15 Exception Path: After-Hours Emergency Case

1. The on-call reprocessing technician list is posted in the GI lab and with the House Supervisor. **Only staff with current documented reprocessing competency may reprocess an endoscope — at any hour.** Procedure nurses without reprocessing competency may perform only bedside precleaning.
2. For an emergency case, a patient-ready endoscope is dispensed from storage by scan as usual (Section 6.9, step 4).
3. After the case: the procedure nurse performs bedside precleaning (Section 6.1) and the on-call technician is called in to perform full reprocessing. If the technician cannot begin manual cleaning within 60 minutes, Section 6.14 applies — bedside precleaning still occurs immediately, and the endoscope is held in the closed transport container in the decontamination room, tracked as delayed.
4. There is no "quick turn" or abbreviated cycle. If a second emergency requires an endoscope and none is patient-ready, the House Supervisor arranges transfer or an alternate patient-ready endoscope; an incompletely processed endoscope is never used.
5. All after-hours cycles are flagged by the tracking system and audited by the Manager the next business day.

### 6.16 Exception Path: Loaner Endoscopes

1. All loaner, evaluation, and repair-return endoscopes enter through the Manager. No loaner may be delivered directly to the GI lab.
2. Intake: verify the endoscope model has a validated AER connector set and current IFU on site, register the endoscope in the tracking system with a temporary barcode, and record vendor documentation of prior reprocessing.
3. Regardless of vendor documentation, every incoming loaner or repair return receives, before first use: exterior inspection, **borescope inspection of all accessible channels**, leak test, full manual cleaning, ATP **and** protein verification, AER cycle, and drying per Sections 6.3–6.8.
4. Loaners are stored, tracked, dispensed, and reprocessed identically to owned inventory for the duration of the loan.
5. Departure: the endoscope is fully reprocessed, its tracking record is closed and archived, and the shipping documentation notes the final reprocessing date. A copy of the complete usage/reprocessing history for the loan period is retained per Section 6.10.

### 6.17 Exception Path: Endoscope Implicated in an Infection Investigation

1. Upon notification (from the Infection Preventionist, a clinician, microbiology, or public health) that an endoscope is potentially linked to a patient infection or a positive surveillance culture:
   a. **Immediately sequester** the endoscope: red tag "INFECTION INVESTIGATION — DO NOT USE OR REPROCESS," locked quarantine cabinet, tracking system sequestration status. **Do not reprocess the endoscope** — its current state is evidence.
   b. Notify, the same day: the Manager, the Infection Preventionist, and the VP Quality (Dr. Escobar). The VP Quality directs the investigation.
2. The Infection Preventionist coordinates microbiologic sampling of the endoscope (channel flush-brush cultures per CDC/duodenoscope sampling methodology adapted to the model) before any further reprocessing, in conjunction with the microbiology laboratory and, where indicated, the Oregon Health Authority.
3. The Manager pulls the endoscope's complete tracking history: every patient exposure, every reprocessing cycle, every technician, every verification result, and every AER cycle record, and preserves originals under the investigation file.
4. Biomedical Engineering performs borescope inspection (documented with images) and reviews the endoscope's repair history.
5. The investigation team (VP Quality, Infection Preventionist, Manager, GI medical director) determines: patient lookback and notification scope, regulatory reporting obligations (Oregon Health Authority; FDA MedWatch/manufacturer device report where a device defect is suspected), and whether related endoscopes or an AER require sequestration or culturing.
6. The endoscope returns to service only after: culture clearance or vendor repair with post-repair reprocessing per Section 6.16, written release signed by **both** the Infection Preventionist and the VP Quality, and entry of the release in the tracking system.
7. A root cause analysis is completed within 30 days and corrective actions tracked through the Quality department.
