# Rural Readers Initiative Grant Application
## Kings Basin Unified School District

---

## Statement of Need

Kings Basin Unified School District serves 6,420 students across eleven schools in Hanford, California, a rural community in California's Central Valley where agricultural employment patterns, seasonal migration, and multigenerational poverty intersect with one of the most urgent literacy crises in the region. Districtwide, only 24 percent of third-grade students are proficient in English language arts. This figure represents not a momentary dip but a structural pattern that has held largely steady across recent years despite the district's ongoing investment in curriculum adoption, professional development days, and intervention blocks.

The population Kings Basin serves requires particular attention to how literacy instruction is designed and delivered. Seventy-four percent of students are Hispanic or Latino, and many enter kindergarten as dual language learners. Nine percent of families speak Punjabi at home, reflecting a significant Sikh Punjabi community whose children often arrive at school navigating three linguistic environments simultaneously: home language, English instruction, and in many cases exposure to Spanish through peer and community networks. Thirty-six percent of the district's students are classified as English learners, a rate that shapes every aspect of how early literacy must be taught, assessed, and supported. Twelve percent of students are eligible for migrant education services, meaning their families move for agricultural work, often disrupting instructional continuity mid-year and complicating efforts to build sustained reading skill over time. Eighty-two percent of students qualify for free or reduced-price meals, placing Kings Basin among the most economically disadvantaged districts in the state and limiting the informal literacy supports—books at home, tutoring, summer enrichment—that better-resourced families can provide.

Third-grade reading proficiency is widely recognized as a hinge point in a child's academic trajectory. Students who are not reading proficiently by the end of third grade are markedly more likely to struggle across every subsequent subject, more likely to be retained, more likely to disengage from school, and more likely to leave without a diploma. For a district where fewer than one in four third graders currently meets this benchmark, the stakes are not abstract. They are measured in the widening gap between what these students can do and what the state's standards expect, and in the compounding disadvantage that follows a child who cannot yet read fluently into fourth grade, fifth grade, and beyond.

Kings Basin has direct evidence that this trajectory can be changed. From 2019 to 2023, the district operated a literacy coaching model at three school sites. At those three schools, third-grade ELA proficiency rose from 21 percent to 34 percent over the life of the program—a thirteen-percentage-point gain achieved through sustained, job-embedded coaching rather than one-time training events. During the same period, proficiency across the rest of the district remained flat. This is the clearest evidence available to the district that its literacy challenge is not primarily a challenge of curriculum materials or student capability, but a challenge of instructional support infrastructure: teachers in Kings Basin's highest-need schools do not have consistent access to the kind of expert, ongoing, classroom-embedded coaching that allows evidence-based literacy practice to take root and improve outcomes.

The coaching model was discontinued at the end of the 2023 school year when its funding source expired, and its benefits have not been extended to the remaining eight schools in the district. The three schools where coaching operated have begun to see their gains plateau without continued support, and the eight schools that never received the intervention continue to post proficiency rates well below the districtwide average. Without a source of sustained investment, Kings Basin will not be able to close this gap. The Rural Readers Initiative gives the district the opportunity to do what it already knows works, at the scale its students actually need.

The need, in short, is threefold: to restore and expand a proven coaching model to schools that have never had access to it; to prevent erosion of the gains already achieved at the original three sites; and to build the kind of sustained, district-owned infrastructure—supervision, pipeline, community partnership—that ensures literacy support does not depend on any single grant cycle or any single staff member.

## Project Description

The Rural Readers Initiative at Kings Basin Unified School District will extend and strengthen the literacy coaching model that produced measurable third-grade proficiency gains from 2019 to 2023, expanding it from three schools to a total of seven schools serving the highest concentrations of English learners, migrant-eligible students, and economically disadvantaged families across the district. The project is designed by Superintendent Priyanka Raghunathan and Director of Curriculum Elena Vasquez-Ortiz, who led the original coaching effort and have direct institutional knowledge of both what made it effective and what caused it to falter when funding lapsed.

**The Coaching Model.** At its core, the project places full-time literacy coaches inside participating schools, embedded in classrooms rather than delivering instruction from a central office. Coaches work side by side with K-3 teachers through a cycle of joint planning, co-teaching, observation, and structured feedback focused on foundational reading skills: phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension. Unlike one-time professional development, this model is built on repetition and relationship—coaches return to the same classrooms week after week, allowing instructional practice to be adjusted in real time based on what students are actually demonstrating. The 2019–2023 data show this approach working precisely where sporadic training has not: teachers at coached schools reported greater confidence in delivering explicit phonics instruction, and the proficiency gains at those three sites bore this out.

The expansion under this grant will bring the model to four additional schools, chosen based on the lowest current third-grade proficiency rates and highest concentrations of English learners and migrant-eligible students districtwide. The three original coaching schools will remain in the program to protect against the erosion already being observed since the prior coaching support ended, and to test whether a second phase of coaching—now focused on deepening practice among veteran-coached teachers and supporting new hires at those sites—can extend gains further.

**Partner Roles.** Three partner organizations are integral to the design and were selected because each addresses a distinct structural gap that undermined the durability of the district's prior coaching effort.

The Kings County Office of Education will provide coach supervision. Under the prior initiative, coaching quality varied because supervision was handled internally by curriculum staff already stretched across other duties, and when the coaching lead resigned in 2023, there was no clear succession structure in place. Under this grant, the county office will assign a dedicated literacy coordinator to supervise, evaluate, and provide ongoing professional development to Kings Basin's coaching staff, building a layer of oversight that is external to any single district position and therefore more resilient to staff turnover.

West Hills Community College will operate a paraeducator-to-teacher pathway, allowing bilingual paraeducators already working in Kings Basin classrooms—many of whom are trusted, long-tenured members of the community and fluent in Spanish or Punjabi—to pursue teaching credentials with coursework tied directly to the literacy coaching curriculum. This partnership is designed to solve two problems simultaneously: it builds a pipeline of future teachers who already understand the community and the coaching model, and it addresses the district's persistent difficulty recruiting credentialed bilingual teachers from outside the region.

The Kings County Library system will operate a summer reading program aligned with the coaching model's instructional framework, ensuring that literacy gains made during the school year are not lost over the summer months—a risk made more acute in Kings Basin by the number of migrant-eligible families who may be away from the district for part of the summer and by the general research on summer learning loss among low-income student populations. The library will host reading events, provide take-home book bags aligned to students' instructional levels, and coordinate with participating schools to identify and invite students who most need continued literacy support.

**Addressing Prior Performance Concerns.** The district recognizes that this application will be evaluated in the context of two issues in its recent grants history, and addresses both directly and transparently.

First, a prior $600,000 award from the Delaney Trust for a similar coaching effort lapsed in 2024 with only $410,000 drawn down. That shortfall occurred because the coaching lead who had been the sole point of accountability for the program resigned mid-grant, and the district did not have a supervisory structure in place to sustain implementation in her absence. This experience directly informed the design of the current proposal: coach supervision is now assigned to the county office of education rather than resting on a single internal position, creating redundancy that did not previously exist. Grants Manager DeAndre Booker has also built a staffing contingency plan into the current design, including cross-training of a second instructional lead at the district level who can step into a supervisory role if a coaching lead departs.

Second, the district's FY2024 single audit identified a finding related to time-and-effort certification for staff paid from federal funds. Assistant Superintendent of Business Kaori Tanabe has led a corrective action process that includes revised timekeeping protocols, quarterly reconciliation reviews, and staff training on documentation requirements, and the district anticipates full resolution of the finding prior to any funds being drawn under this grant. The district discloses this finding because it bears directly on fiscal stewardship of grant-funded staff positions, and it commits to applying the corrected time-and-effort protocols to every coaching and pathway position funded under this award from day one.

Together, these design choices are meant to ensure that the Rural Readers Initiative does not repeat the vulnerabilities that disrupted the district's prior coaching effort, while building on the instructional model that is already proven to work.

## Goals and Measurable Objectives

The Rural Readers Initiative is organized around one overarching goal: to significantly and sustainably increase third-grade English language arts proficiency across Kings Basin Unified School District by extending a coaching model with a demonstrated track record of success to the schools that most need it. This goal is supported by four measurable objectives aligned to the outcomes the Aldous Renwick Foundation requires the district to report annually.

**Objective 1: Third-Grade Proficiency.** Increase third-grade ELA proficiency at the seven participating schools from a current baseline average of approximately 22 percent to 35 percent by the end of the four-year grant period, an improvement trajectory consistent with the thirteen-point gain achieved at the original three coaching sites between 2019 and 2023. Interim targets will be set at approximately three percentage points of growth per year, recognizing that gains are unlikely to be perfectly linear and that new coaching sites may show slower initial movement than sites where coaching resumes.

**Objective 2: DIBELS Composite Growth.** Increase the percentage of K-3 students meeting or exceeding benchmark on DIBELS composite scores at participating schools by 15 percentage points over the grant period, with coaches using DIBELS progress-monitoring data as the primary formative tool guiding weekly coaching cycles. Because DIBELS is administered multiple times per year, this measure will also serve as the district's earliest indicator of whether coaching is producing within-year movement, allowing for mid-course adjustment well before annual state assessment results are available.

**Objective 3: Chronic Absenteeism.** Reduce chronic absenteeism among K-3 students at participating schools by five percentage points over the grant period. This objective reflects the district's understanding that literacy instruction cannot improve outcomes for students who are not present to receive it, and that the summer library partnership and family engagement components of this project are designed in part to strengthen student and family connection to school.

**Objective 4: Teacher Retention.** Increase year-over-year teacher retention at participating schools by eight percentage points over the grant period, and maintain retention above the districtwide average throughout the grant. The prior coaching model's exit interviews and informal feedback suggested that teachers who received sustained coaching support reported greater instructional confidence and job satisfaction; this objective tests whether that pattern holds at scale and treats teacher retention as both an outcome in its own right and a leading indicator of program stability.

**Objective 5: Cost Per Student Served.** Maintain a cost per student served that decreases over the grant period as the program matures and start-up costs are absorbed, targeting a per-student cost in year four that is at least 20 percent lower than the year one figure, reflecting efficiencies gained through the paraeducator pipeline reducing long-term recruitment costs and coaching capacity being built internally rather than purchased externally on a recurring basis.

Each objective has been set with reference either to the district's own 2019–2023 outcomes or to documented baseline data from the four new expansion sites, and each will be reported annually to the foundation alongside narrative context explaining any variance from target, consistent with the reporting expectations described in the funding announcement.

## Evaluation Plan

Evaluation of the Rural Readers Initiative will be conducted using a mixed-methods design combining the quantitative outcome measures specified by the foundation with implementation and fidelity data collected throughout each program year, allowing the district to distinguish between outcomes driven by the coaching model itself and outcomes affected by external factors.

**Data Collection.** Third-grade ELA proficiency will be measured using California's statewide summative assessment, disaggregated by participating school and, where sample sizes allow, by English learner status and migrant education eligibility, to ensure the district can determine whether the intervention is closing gaps for the subgroups it is specifically designed to serve. DIBELS composite scores will be collected at the three standard benchmark periods each year (fall, winter, spring) for all K-3 students at participating schools, providing a within-year growth measure that complements the once-a-year state assessment data. Chronic absenteeism will be tracked using the district's existing student information system, drawing on the same attendance-code definitions the state uses for its chronic absenteeism accountability metric, and will be reported monthly to the project team to allow early intervention with individual students and families. Teacher retention will be tracked through the district's human resources system, measuring the percentage of teachers at each participating school who return the following school year, with exit interview data collected from any departing teacher to identify whether coaching-related factors influenced the decision to stay or leave. Cost per student served will be calculated annually by Grants Manager DeAndre Booker and Assistant Superintendent Kaori Tanabe by dividing total program expenditure by the number of students directly served by coaching and pathway activities at participating sites.

**Fidelity Monitoring.** Because the district's own experience shows that this coaching model's effectiveness depends heavily on consistent implementation, the evaluation plan places particular emphasis on fidelity monitoring. The county office of education's assigned literacy coordinator will conduct structured classroom observations at each participating school on a monthly basis, using a coaching fidelity rubric adapted from the tool used during the 2019–2023 program, and will document coaching dosage (frequency and duration of coaching cycles per teacher) to allow the evaluation team to correlate dosage with outcome data at the classroom level. This fidelity data will also serve as an early warning system: if dosage or observed coaching quality begins to decline at any site, district leadership will be alerted well before that decline shows up in annual outcome data, allowing for corrective action such as reallocating coaching time or providing additional coach training.

**Analysis and Reporting.** Elena Vasquez-Ortiz will lead an annual evaluation report compiling all five required measures, alongside fidelity data and qualitative feedback gathered through teacher surveys and family focus groups conducted at each participating school. This report will be submitted to the Aldous Renwick Foundation on the schedule specified in the grant agreement and will include year-over-year comparisons against both the objectives set out above and the historical 2019–2023 baseline. Where outcomes diverge meaningfully from target, the report will include a root-cause analysis and a specific adjustment plan, consistent with the district's stated commitment to transparency about both what is working and what requires correction.

An external evaluator will be contracted in year one to design the DIBELS and proficiency data protocols in a manner that supports rigorous, defensible comparison across sites and years, and to conduct an independent mid-point review at the end of year two assessing both outcome trends and implementation fidelity. This external review is intended to give the foundation additional confidence in the objectivity of reported results, separate from the district's internal reporting.

## Organizational Capacity

Kings Basin Unified School District brings both direct prior experience with this exact intervention and a leadership team positioned to ensure its successful implementation, while also having taken specific, documented steps to address the two operational weaknesses that affected past grant performance.

**Leadership Team.** Superintendent Priyanka Raghunathan has led the district since before the original 2019–2023 coaching initiative and championed its adoption at the time; she brings direct institutional memory of both its design and the reasons it succeeded, and will serve as executive sponsor for this grant, ensuring the project maintains visibility and priority at the cabinet level throughout its four-year duration. Director of Curriculum Elena Vasquez-Ortiz co-designed the original coaching model and will serve as project director for the Rural Readers Initiative, overseeing coach hiring, training, and day-to-day program management, and leading the evaluation reporting process described above. Assistant Superintendent of Business Kaori Tanabe will oversee all fiscal management of grant funds, including the match, and has direct oversight of the corrective action plan addressing the FY2024 single audit finding on time-and-effort certification, ensuring that documentation practices for all grant-funded coaching and pathway positions meet federal and foundation requirements from the outset. Grants Manager DeAndre Booker will manage compliance, reporting timelines, and coordination with all three external partners, and has developed the staffing contingency plan intended to prevent the kind of implementation gap that led to the lapse of the district's prior Delaney Trust award.

**Track Record.** The district's clearest evidence of capacity is the outcome of its own prior coaching effort: a thirteen-percentage-point increase in third-grade proficiency at three schools over four years, achieved with a smaller and less well-supervised structure than what this grant proposes. This is not a hypothetical model being proposed for the first time; it is a model with a documented result, led by the same senior staff who are proposing to extend it, now supported by a stronger supervisory and succession structure than existed previously.

**Partner Capacity.** The Kings County Office of Education already provides a range of instructional support services to Kings Basin and other districts in the county and has experience supervising school-based instructional coaches in other contexts, giving it directly relevant expertise to bring to this partnership. West Hills Community College operates existing teacher pathway programs and has experience building cohort-based coursework for working paraeducators, and district leadership has already had preliminary conversations with the college's education department chair about aligning coursework to the coaching model's instructional framework. The Kings County Library system has operated summer reading programming in the region for years and has existing relationships with several of the participating school sites, providing a foundation of trust and familiarity that the project can build on rather than construct from scratch.

**Addressing Past Weaknesses Directly.** The district acknowledges that an evaluator reviewing its recent history will see two flags: the lapsed Delaney Trust award and the FY2024 audit finding. Rather than minimize these, the district has built structural responses to both directly into this proposal's design, described in the Project Description above, and commits to reporting on the status of both the audit corrective action and the coaching succession plan as part of its annual reporting to the foundation, in addition to the five outcome measures the foundation requires. The district believes that transparent acknowledgment of past shortfalls, paired with specific structural fixes, is a stronger indicator of organizational capacity than a record with no history of difficulty at all.

## Sustainability

The Rural Readers Initiative is designed from the outset with a plan for continuation beyond the four years of Aldous Renwick Foundation funding, addressing directly the reality that grant-funded coaching positions often disappear when the grant that created them ends.

The most significant sustainability commitment is that Kings Basin has already secured supplemental state funding, committed through 2030, that will support core coaching positions beyond the conclusion of this grant. This state funding stream was not designed to fully replicate the scope of the Rural Readers Initiative on its own; rather, it is structured to sustain the coaching positions and supervisory infrastructure this grant will build out, so that the model does not depend indefinitely on foundation support to survive. In practical terms, this means that the coaching positions funded by this grant in years one through four will not need to be eliminated in year five; the state funding already committed will carry the core instructional coaching structure forward through at least 2030, six years beyond the conclusion of this grant.

The West Hills Community College paraeducator-to-teacher pathway is itself a sustainability mechanism, not simply a program component. By building a pipeline of credentialed teachers drawn from the district's own paraeducator workforce, the district reduces its long-term dependence on external recruitment and lowers the cost of maintaining a stable, well-trained teaching staff at participating schools over time—directly supporting the cost-per-student-served objective described above, since credential candidates who complete the pathway are more likely to remain in the district long-term than teachers recruited from outside the region.

The partnership structure itself is designed for durability. Coach supervision resting with the county office of education, rather than solely within district administration, means that a change in district staffing does not create the same vulnerability that led to the lapse of the district's prior Delaney Trust award. Should a coaching lead depart during or after this grant period, the county office supervisory structure and Grants Manager Booker's staffing contingency plan are both designed to ensure continuity rather than program lapse.

The district also views the improved fiscal management practices developed in response to the FY2024 audit finding as a sustainability asset. The revised time-and-effort certification and reconciliation protocols developed under Assistant Superintendent Tanabe's oversight will apply not only to this grant but to all future federally and philanthropically funded positions the district manages, strengthening the district's capacity to responsibly steward future funding streams, including potential future philanthropic support beyond this grant's conclusion.

Finally, district leadership intends to use the evaluation data generated under this grant to make the case, both to its own governing board and to other potential funders, for permanent inclusion of literacy coaching positions within the district's general fund budget as state and philanthropic funding sources evolve. Superintendent Raghunathan has indicated her intention to bring proficiency and DIBELS growth data to the board annually throughout the grant period specifically to build the case for this longer-term structural commitment, so that by the time state supplemental funding and this grant's four-year term both approach their limits, the district has built both the evidence base and the institutional will to sustain the model through its own core budget.

## Budget Narrative

The Rural Readers Initiative requests $600,000 annually from the Aldous Renwick Foundation across four years, for a total request of $2,400,000, matched by a 25 percent cash contribution from Kings Basin Unified School District, bringing total program investment to $3,000,000 over the grant period, or $750,000 annually.

**Personnel (approximately 68 percent of annual budget, $510,000).** The largest budget category funds coaching staff at the seven participating schools. Four full-time literacy coaches will be funded to serve the four new expansion sites, and continued partial coaching support will be provided at the three original sites to prevent erosion of prior gains, at a blended average cost of approximately $95,000 per position including salary and benefits, totaling approximately $410,000. The county office of education's dedicated literacy coordinator position, providing supervision and fidelity monitoring across all seven sites, is budgeted at approximately $100,000 annually, split between grant funds and district match. This personnel line also includes a stipend structure for the second instructional lead cross-trained as part of the district's staffing contingency plan, ensuring supervisory continuity is not dependent on any single position.

**Partner Contracts (approximately 15 percent, $112,500).** This category includes the contracted cost of the West Hills Community College paraeducator-to-teacher pathway coursework, approximately $62,500 annually, covering tuition subsidies for a cohort of paraeducators pursuing credentials and instructional coordination between the college and the district's coaching curriculum. The remaining $50,000 funds the Kings County Library summer reading program, including staffing, book bag materials aligned to students' instructional levels, and family literacy events held at participating school sites and library branches over the summer months.

**Assessment and Evaluation (approximately 8 percent, $60,000).** This line funds DIBELS assessment materials and administration support, the external evaluator contracted in year one to design data protocols and conduct the year-two midpoint review, and data management support to allow disaggregated reporting by school, English learner status, and migrant education eligibility as described in the Evaluation Plan.

**Professional Development (approximately 5 percent, $37,500).** This category supports initial and ongoing training for coaches, including alignment with the district's existing curriculum and specific training in supporting English learners and students receiving migrant education services, reflecting the demographic composition of the district's student population.

**Administration and Indirect Costs (approximately 4 percent, $30,000).** This line covers a portion of Grants Manager DeAndre Booker's time dedicated to compliance monitoring and foundation reporting, and Assistant Superintendent Kaori Tanabe's oversight of time-and-effort certification and fiscal reconciliation for all grant-funded positions, consistent with the corrective action plan addressing the FY2024 single audit finding. The district has intentionally kept this line modest to maximize direct investment in coaching and partner activities, while still ensuring the fiscal oversight capacity necessary to prevent recurrence of prior compliance issues.

**Match.** The district's required 25 percent cash match, totaling $600,000 over four years ($150,000 annually), will be drawn from the state supplemental funding already committed through 2030, described in the Sustainability section above. Using this committed state revenue stream to fund the match, rather than identifying a separate one-time source, directly demonstrates the sustainability plan in practice: the same funding that will carry coaching positions forward after this grant concludes is being used to co-invest in those positions during the grant period itself. Assistant Superintendent Tanabe will track match expenditures separately within the district's financial system to ensure clean documentation for audit purposes, applying the same reconciliation protocols developed in response to the FY2024 finding.

The district respectfully requests that the Aldous Renwick Foundation consider this budget as reflecting not a new, untested program, but the responsible scaling of a model with four years of prior evidence, supported by a fiscal management structure strengthened specifically in response to the district's own past shortcomings.

Year one carries a higher assessment and evaluation cost to fund the external evaluator's initial protocol design work, and a higher professional development cost to fund intensive initial training for the four new coaches and the county office's literacy coordinator before the school year begins. These costs decline in years two through four as the evaluation infrastructure matures and professional development shifts from initial training to ongoing refinement, allowing personnel investment to grow modestly each year in line with standard salary step increases and the anticipated addition of coaching hours at the three original sites as the paraeducator pathway begins producing credentialed candidates.

**Budget Justification by Objective.** Each budget line maps directly to one or more of the measurable objectives described above. Personnel spending on coaching and supervision is the primary driver of Objectives 1 and 2, third-grade proficiency and DIBELS composite growth, since coaching dosage and fidelity are the mechanisms through which instructional practice changes. The library partnership and a portion of the personnel line supporting family engagement activities at participating schools are the primary drivers of Objective 3, chronic absenteeism, on the premise that students and families with stronger relationships to school-based and community literacy activities are more likely to maintain regular attendance. The professional development line and the structural investment in county office supervision are the primary levers for Objective 4, teacher retention, reflecting the district's experience that teachers who feel adequately supported and coached are more likely to remain in their positions. Objective 5, cost per student served, will be tracked against the full annual total divided by the approximately 2,800 K-3 students enrolled at the seven participating schools, and is expected to improve over the grant period as the West Hills pathway begins reducing external recruitment costs and as initial evaluation infrastructure costs, concentrated in year one, are not repeated in later years.

**Budget Controls.** All expenditures under this grant will be processed through the district's standard purchase order and payroll systems, with grant-funded positions flagged for the enhanced time-and-effort documentation protocols developed under Assistant Superintendent Tanabe's corrective action plan. Grants Manager Booker will produce quarterly budget-to-actual reports for internal review by the Superintendent's cabinet, in addition to the annual financial reporting required by the foundation, to ensure that any variance between planned and actual spending is identified and addressed well before it could affect program implementation. This quarterly internal review process did not exist during the district's prior Delaney Trust award and is included here specifically to close that gap.

The district is prepared to provide any additional financial documentation, staffing plans, or partner agreements the Aldous Renwick Foundation requires to support review of this application, and looks forward to the opportunity to extend a coaching model it has already seen work to the students across Kings Basin who need it most.
