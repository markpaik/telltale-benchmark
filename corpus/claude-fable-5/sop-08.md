## 2. Purpose

This standard operating procedure (SOP) establishes the mandatory process by which Front Range Workforce Collaborative (FRWC) determines the nature of its funding relationships, assesses and tiers subrecipient risk, issues compliant subaward agreements, monitors subrecipient performance and expenditures, collects and reviews audit reports, tracks findings through corrective action, recovers disallowed costs, and closes out subawards.

This SOP responds directly to the FY2025 single audit material weakness in subrecipient monitoring and satisfies the corrective action plan commitments made to the federal awarding agency and the state pass-through entity. It implements the pass-through entity requirements of the Uniform Guidance, principally 2 CFR 200.331 through 200.333 and 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart F, including the $1,000,000 single audit threshold. Approval and consistent execution of this SOP is a condition of continuation of FRWC's $12.8 million workforce grant effective October 1, 2026.

No subaward may be issued, amended, paid, or closed out except in accordance with this SOP. Deviations require written approval as described in Section 7.5.

---

## 3. Scope

This SOP applies to:

- All federal and state pass-through funds that FRWC awards to any legal entity to carry out part of a federal program, currently approximately $46.2 million annually to 31 subrecipient organizations delivering job training across 14 Colorado counties.
- All FRWC staff who touch the subaward lifecycle: grants compliance staff, program officers, contract analysts, fiscal monitors, and finance staff.
- All systems used in the subaward lifecycle: the grants management system (GMS), the accounting system, SAM.gov exclusion checks, FSRS/SAM.gov subaward reporting, the pre-award risk assessment worksheet, the monitoring tool, and the corrective action log.

This SOP does **not** apply to procurement contracts for goods and services (see Section 6.1 for how that determination is made and documented; contracts are governed by FRWC's Procurement Policy). It does not apply to FRWC's own direct-delivery program operations.

## 4. Definitions

**Contractor.** An entity that receives a contract to provide goods or services for FRWC's own use and creates a procurement relationship, per 2 CFR 200.1 and 200.331.

**Corrective Action Log.** The single authoritative register, maintained in the GMS, of every monitoring finding, audit finding, questioned cost, and required corrective action for every subrecipient, with status and due dates.

**Desk Review.** A remote examination of a subrecipient's financial and programmatic records, reports, and supporting documentation, performed without an on-site visit.

**Disallowed Cost.** A charge to a subaward that FRWC or an oversight agency determines to be unallowable under the terms of the subaward, the approved budget, or 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart E.

**Fiscal Monitor.** FRWC staff responsible for testing subrecipient expenditures against approved budgets, cost principles, and supporting documentation.

**Pass-Through Entity (PTE).** A recipient or subrecipient that provides a subaward to a subrecipient to carry out part of a federal program. FRWC is a PTE.

**Questioned Cost.** A cost identified during monitoring or audit that may be disallowed because of a possible violation of statute, regulation, or award terms; inadequate documentation; or unreasonableness.

**Risk Tier.** The classification (Tier 1 – Low, Tier 2 – Moderate, Tier 3 – High) assigned to each subrecipient annually and at pre-award, which determines the intensity of monitoring.

**Single Audit.** The audit required by 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart F for a non-federal entity that expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards during its fiscal year (for fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024).

**Subaward.** An award provided by a PTE to a subrecipient to carry out part of a federal award.

**Subrecipient.** A non-federal entity that receives a subaward to carry out part of a federal program, with performance measured against the program's objectives, per 2 CFR 200.1 and 200.331.

**UEI.** Unique Entity Identifier assigned in SAM.gov, required for every subrecipient before a subaward may be issued (2 CFR 25.300).

## 5. Responsibilities

**Chief Executive Officer (Dr. Tanya Ruelas).** Approves this SOP and all revisions. Approves any suspension or termination of a subaward, any referral of suspected fraud to an Office of Inspector General, and any write-off of an uncollected disallowed cost. Signs corrective action plan submissions to awarding agencies.

**Director of Grants Compliance (Solomon Kirui).** Owns this SOP. Approves all contractor/subrecipient determinations, risk tier assignments, annual monitoring plans, monitoring reports, findings, corrective action closures, and cost disallowance determinations. Certifies quarterly to the CEO that monitoring is on schedule. Serves as the point of contact for federal and state monitors.

**Program Officers.** Manage day-to-day subrecipient relationships. Draft the programmatic portion of pre-award risk assessments, participate in site visits, review programmatic performance reports, and confirm programmatic corrective actions are implemented. Program officers may not close their own findings.

**Contract Analysts.** Complete eligibility screening (SAM.gov exclusion checks, UEI verification), prepare subaward agreements and amendments containing all required data elements, obtain signatures, submit FFATA subaward reports, and maintain the official subaward file in the GMS.

**Fiscal Monitors.** Execute desk reviews and on-site expenditure testing per the monitoring plan, document results in the monitoring tool, draft fiscal findings, and verify fiscal corrective actions.

**Finance Team.** Processes reimbursement requests only after the checks in Section 6.5.2 are complete, executes payment holds and offsets directed by the Director of Grants Compliance, records receivables for disallowed costs, and reconciles subaward expenditures in the accounting system monthly.

**Segregation of duties.** The staff member who manages a subrecipient relationship (program officer) may not perform that subrecipient's fiscal monitoring, approve its findings closures, or process its payments.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Contractor Versus Subrecipient Determination

**6.1.1** Before any funding instrument is drafted, the requesting program officer completes the Contractor/Subrecipient Determination Checklist in the GMS. The checklist applies the characteristics in 2 CFR 200.331 based on the substance of the relationship, not the label of the agreement:

An entity is likely a **subrecipient** when it: (a) determines who is eligible to receive program assistance; (b) has its performance measured against whether the federal program's objectives are met; (c) has responsibility for programmatic decision-making; (d) must adhere to applicable federal program requirements; and (e) uses the federal funds to carry out a program for a public purpose, rather than to provide goods or services for FRWC's benefit.

An entity is likely a **contractor** when it: (a) provides goods or services within its normal business operations; (b) provides similar goods or services to many different purchasers; (c) operates in a competitive environment; (d) provides goods or services ancillary to the operation of the federal program; and (e) is not subject to the program's compliance requirements as a result of the agreement.

**6.1.2** The program officer records, for each characteristic, whether it is present and a one- to two-sentence factual basis. A conclusory checklist without factual bases will be returned.

**6.1.3** The Director of Grants Compliance reviews and approves or corrects the determination within 5 business days. The signed determination is filed in the GMS entity record. If the determination is **contractor**, the request is routed to the Procurement Policy and exits this SOP. If **subrecipient**, proceed to Section 6.2.

**6.1.4** Determinations are re-performed whenever the scope of a relationship materially changes, and at least once per funding cycle for renewed relationships.

### 6.2 Pre-Award Risk Assessment and Eligibility Screening

No subaward or renewal may be executed without a documented pre-award risk assessment and eligibility screening completed within the 90 days preceding execution. This requirement applies to every subrecipient, every award period, without exception; the FY2025 audit found 9 of 31 subrecipients lacking this documentation, and the GMS now blocks subaward routing until the risk assessment record is approved.

**6.2.1 Eligibility screening (contract analyst).** The contract analyst:

1. Verifies the entity has an active UEI registration in SAM.gov and prints/saves the SAM.gov record to the GMS entity file.
2. Performs a SAM.gov exclusions check on the entity and its principals. Saves a dated screenshot or PDF of the search result to the GMS. An excluded entity may not receive a subaward; the contract analyst notifies the Director of Grants Compliance the same day.
3. Checks FAC (Federal Audit Clearinghouse) for the entity's most recent single audit submission and notes the fiscal year, opinion, and any findings.
4. Confirms the entity is not on the state's debarment or high-risk list.
5. Records completion in the GMS eligibility screen. Screening is repeated at every renewal, at every amendment adding funds, and annually for multi-year subawards.

**6.2.2 Risk assessment worksheet (program officer and fiscal monitor jointly).** Using the standard risk assessment worksheet in the GMS, the assessors score the entity on the factors in 2 CFR 200.332(b):

1. **Prior experience** with the same or similar subawards (new subrecipient = higher risk).
2. **Results of previous audits**, including single audit findings, going-concern language, and material weaknesses.
3. **New or substantially changed personnel or systems** (executive director, fiscal director, or accounting system changes within 12 months).
4. **Results of prior FRWC monitoring**, including open corrective actions and prior questioned or disallowed costs.
5. **Federal oversight**, including whether the subrecipient is monitored directly by a federal or state agency.
6. **Financial stability**, based on the most recent audited financial statements: operating reserves, current ratio, debt, and any going-concern doubt.
7. **Internal control environment**, based on the subrecipient's completed Financial Management Questionnaire (segregation of duties, written policies, timesheet practices, procurement standards).
8. **Award size and complexity**, including subaward amount relative to the entity's total budget and whether the entity further subawards funds.

**6.2.3** Each factor is scored 1 (low) to 3 (high) with a written basis. The worksheet computes a total score that maps to a preliminary risk tier (Section 6.4). The Director of Grants Compliance may override the computed tier upward with a documented rationale; downward overrides require CEO concurrence.

**6.2.4** The Director of Grants Compliance approves the completed worksheet in the GMS. Only then may the contract analyst generate a subaward agreement.

### 6.3 Subaward Issuance and Required Terms

**6.3.1** The contract analyst prepares every subaward on the FRWC standard subaward template, which contains all data elements required by 2 CFR 200.332(a). Before routing for signature, the analyst completes the Subaward Issuance Checklist confirming the agreement states:

1. Subrecipient legal name (matching SAM.gov) and UEI.
2. Federal Award Identification Number (FAIN) and federal award date of the prime award.
3. Subaward period of performance and budget period.
4. Amount of federal funds obligated by this action; total obligated to date; total approved amount of the subaward.
5. Federal award project description.
6. Name of the federal awarding agency, name of the pass-through entity (and, where applicable, the state pass-through), and contact information for FRWC's awarding official.
7. Assistance Listing number and title, and the dollar amount made available under each listing.
8. Whether the award is research and development (typically no for FRWC programs).
9. **Indirect cost rate:** the subrecipient's federally negotiated indirect cost rate agreement (NICRA) rate; or, if the subrecipient has no NICRA, either the de minimis rate of up to 15% of modified total direct costs per 2 CFR 200.414(f) or a rate negotiated between FRWC and the subrecipient. The agreement attaches the NICRA or the subrecipient's written de minimis election. Contract analysts may not issue a subaward with an unsupported indirect rate.
10. All requirements FRWC imposes so that the federal award is used in accordance with statutes, regulations, and prime award terms, including flow-down of the prime award's terms and conditions.
11. Any additional requirements imposed based on the risk assessment (e.g., cost-reimbursement only, monthly reporting, prior approval for equipment or budget line shifts above 10%).
12. The subrecipient's obligation to permit FRWC, the awarding agencies, and the Comptroller General access to records and financial statements per 2 CFR 200.332(a)(5).
13. Audit requirements: the subrecipient must obtain a single audit if it expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in its fiscal year, submit it to the FAC within the earlier of 30 days after receipt or 9 months after fiscal year end, and provide the reporting package to FRWC per Section 6.6.
14. Closeout terms and record retention obligations (Section 6.9), and terms for termination and remedies for noncompliance consistent with 2 CFR 200.339–200.343.
15. The approved line-item budget as an attachment. **Costs are allowable only within the approved budget**; the agreement states the budget modification thresholds requiring prior written FRWC approval. (This term directly addresses the $412,000 questioned-cost finding, where staff time and vehicle leases were charged outside an approved budget.)

**6.3.2** Signature routing: subrecipient authorized official signs first; the Director of Grants Compliance signs for FRWC on subawards up to $500,000; the CEO signs subawards above $500,000. Fully executed agreements are filed in the GMS within 3 business days.

**6.3.3 FFATA reporting.** For each subaward action of $30,000 or more, the contract analyst submits the required subaward report in the federal subaward reporting system by the end of the month following the month of obligation, and saves the submission confirmation to the GMS.

**6.3.4 Amendments.** All changes to amount, period, scope, budget, or key terms are executed as written amendments using the same checklist, screening refresh, and signature thresholds.

### 6.4 Risk Tiering and the Annual Monitoring Plan

**6.4.1 Tier definitions and minimum monitoring.** Every active subrecipient carries a current risk tier, assigned at pre-award and reassessed each August using the same worksheet:

| Tier | Typical Profile | Minimum Monitoring Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 – Low** | Clean audits, no open findings, stable staffing, ≥3 years satisfactory FRWC history | Annual desk review; on-site visit at least once every **2 years**; quarterly financial/programmatic report review; expenditure sample of 10 transactions or 5% of reimbursement lines at desk review |
| **Tier 2 – Moderate** | New subrecipient, minor prior findings (closed), award >25% of entity budget, or personnel change in last 12 months | Semiannual desk review; on-site visit **annually**; monthly report review; expenditure sample of 20 transactions or 10% at each review |
| **Tier 3 – High** | Material weakness or significant deficiency in audit, open corrective actions, questioned/disallowed costs in prior 2 years, going-concern language, or first-time subrecipient of >$500,000 | Quarterly desk review; on-site visits **twice annually**; monthly report review; reimbursement documentation review of 100% of requests before payment; expenditure sample of 40 transactions or 15% at each review; monthly check-in call documented in GMS |

**6.4.2** Under no circumstance may any subrecipient go longer than **24 months** without an on-site visit. The GMS produces a monthly "site visit aging" report to the Director of Grants Compliance; any subrecipient at 18 months without a visit is flagged and scheduled immediately. (This control addresses the FY2025 finding that 4 subrecipients had no site visit in three years.)

**6.4.3 Annual monitoring plan.** By September 15 each year, the Director of Grants Compliance publishes the annual monitoring plan in the GMS, listing for each of the subrecipients: risk tier, assigned fiscal monitor and program officer, scheduled desk review months, scheduled site visit dates, and sample sizes. The CEO receives the plan and quarterly status reports showing completed versus scheduled events. Any monitoring event more than 30 days behind schedule requires a written recovery plan to the CEO.

**6.4.4** A subrecipient's tier is raised immediately (not at annual reassessment) upon any of: a new audit finding rated significant deficiency or worse, questioned costs identified by any monitor, a key personnel change (Section 7.3), going-concern doubt (Section 7.2), or missed reports in two consecutive periods.

### 6.5 Desk Reviews and Reimbursement Controls

**6.5.1 Scheduled desk reviews.** For each desk review on the monitoring plan, the assigned fiscal monitor:

1. Sends the subrecipient a document request at least 15 business days before the review date: general ledger detail for the subaward for the period, payroll registers and timesheets for sampled staff, invoices and proof of payment for sampled non-payroll costs, bank reconciliation for the operating account, current budget-versus-actual report, and updated policies if changed.
2. Selects the expenditure sample per the tier table in 6.4.1, drawn from reimbursement request line items using the monitoring tool's random selector, plus judgmental additions for any line the monitor deems higher risk (e.g., leases, consultant costs, staff time allocated across funders).
3. Tests each sampled item for: allowability under 2 CFR Part 200 Subpart E; conformity to the **approved budget line**; adequacy of source documentation; correct period; correct allocation methodology for shared costs; proof of payment; and, for payroll, timesheets signed by employee and supervisor reflecting actual hours by funding source.
4. Reconciles total reimbursements paid (accounting system) to the subrecipient's general ledger for the subaward.
5. Reviews programmatic reports with the program officer for enrollment, training completions, and placement outcomes versus targets.
6. Documents all work in the monitoring tool within 10 business days of completing testing, records any findings (Section 6.7), and issues a desk review report signed by the Director of Grants Compliance to the subrecipient within 20 business days.

**6.5.2 Reimbursement controls (finance team, continuous).** Before releasing any reimbursement, finance confirms in the GMS: (a) the subaward is fully executed and within its period of performance; (b) cumulative payments will not exceed the obligated amount; (c) the request maps to approved budget lines and no line is overspent beyond the approved threshold; (d) required reports are current; and (e) for Tier 3 subrecipients, the fiscal monitor has approved the supporting documentation for 100% of the request. Requests failing any check are held and the program officer notified the same day.

### 6.6 On-Site Monitoring

**6.6.1 Scheduling and scope.** On-site visits follow the annual monitoring plan. The fiscal monitor and program officer conduct visits jointly. The subrecipient receives written notice and a document/agenda letter at least 20 business days in advance (except unannounced visits authorized by the Director of Grants Compliance for suspected fraud, Section 7.4).

**6.6.2 On-site protocol.** At minimum the team:

1. Holds an entrance conference with the subrecipient's executive and fiscal leads.
2. Performs expenditure testing per the tier sample sizes on original source documents, including tracing sampled costs from the reimbursement request to the general ledger to invoices/timesheets to proof of payment.
3. Tests payroll allocation: for at least 3 employees charged to the subaward, verifies personnel records, timesheets, and that charged effort reflects actual work on the program.
4. Physically verifies any equipment purchased with subaward funds against the equipment inventory (tag, location, condition, use).
5. Tests participant files (minimum 10) for eligibility documentation, enrollment, service records, and outcome support.
6. Reviews internal controls in operation: segregation of duties in cash disbursements, approval chains, and procurement files for any subaward-funded procurement.
7. Reviews status of all open corrective actions on site.
8. Holds an exit conference summarizing preliminary observations; the monitor's exit notes are signed by both parties.

**6.6.3 Reporting.** The monitoring team completes the monitoring tool and drafts the site visit report within 15 business days. The Director of Grants Compliance approves and issues the report within 25 business days of the visit. Reports classify each issue as an observation (no response required), a finding (corrective action required), or questioned costs (Section 6.8), and state response deadlines.

### 6.7 Collection and Review of Audit Reports

**6.7.1 Applicability tracking.** At subaward execution and each January, the contract analyst records in the GMS each subrecipient's fiscal year end and whether the subrecipient expects to expend $1,000,000 or more in total federal awards (from all sources) in that fiscal year, based on a written certification the subrecipient must submit annually. Subrecipients at or above the threshold are flagged "single audit required."

**6.7.2 Collection.** For each flagged subrecipient, the GMS generates a due date of 9 months after fiscal year end. The contract analyst:

1. Sends a reminder 60 days before the due date.
2. Verifies submission on the Federal Audit Clearinghouse and obtains the full reporting package (financial statements, SEFA, auditor's reports, schedule of findings and questioned costs, summary schedule of prior audit findings, and any corrective action plan).
3. If the report is not received within 30 days after the due date, escalates to the Director of Grants Compliance, who places the subrecipient on payment hold and issues a written demand with a 30-day cure period. Continued failure is grounds for remedies under 2 CFR 200.339, up to termination. (This control addresses the FY2025 finding of 6 missing audit reports; the GMS audit-tracking dashboard is reviewed by the Director of Grants Compliance monthly and no flagged subrecipient may show "not received" past 30 days without an open escalation.)

**6.7.3 Review.** Within 30 days of receipt, the fiscal monitor completes the Audit Report Review Checklist: opinion type; going-concern language; findings related to FRWC's federal awards; questioned costs; status of prior findings; and adequacy of the subrecipient's corrective action plan. Per 2 CFR 200.332(d)(2) and (e), FRWC issues a written management decision on any audit finding pertaining to FRWC's subaward within **6 months** of the FAC acceptance date, entered in the corrective action log with required actions and deadlines.

**6.7.4 Below-threshold subrecipients.** See Section 7.1 for the alternative review that substitutes for a single audit.

### 6.8 Findings and Corrective Action Tracking

**6.8.1 Single log.** Every finding — from desk reviews, site visits, audit report reviews, awarding agency monitoring, or self-disclosure — is entered in the corrective action log in the GMS within 5 business days of report issuance, with: subrecipient, source, condition, criteria (citation), cause, effect, questioned cost amount if any, required corrective action, responsible party, due date, and status.

**6.8.2 Subrecipient response.** Subrecipients must submit a written corrective action plan within **30 calendar days** of the report. The plan must state the specific action, responsible person, and completion date for each finding. The Director of Grants Compliance accepts or rejects the plan within 10 business days; rejected plans are returned with required revisions and a 15-day resubmission deadline.

**6.8.3 Verification and closure.** A finding may be closed only when the fiscal monitor (fiscal findings) or program officer (programmatic findings) obtains and files evidence that the corrective action was implemented — e.g., revised policy adopted by the board, corrected timesheet practices verified in a follow-up sample, repayment received. Closure requires the Director of Grants Compliance's approval in the log. Program officers may not close findings they identified or that arose from their own portfolio's fiscal testing.

**6.8.4 Escalation.** Findings open past their due date trigger: at 30 days overdue, written notice to the subrecipient's executive director; at 60 days, payment hold and Tier 3 designation; at 90 days, referral to the CEO for remedies under 2 CFR 200.339 (additional conditions, withholding, disallowance, suspension, or termination). The Director of Grants Compliance reports open-findings aging to the CEO quarterly.

### 6.9 Disallowed Cost Recovery

**6.9.1 Determination.** Questioned costs from any source are evaluated by the fiscal monitor, who prepares a disallowance memo stating the amount, the specific criterion violated (regulation, budget term, or agreement clause), and the evidence. The Director of Grants Compliance issues the written disallowance determination to the subrecipient, which states the amount due, the basis, the repayment deadline (30 calendar days), and the subrecipient's right to submit additional documentation within 15 calendar days for reconsideration.

**6.9.2 Recovery methods**, in order of preference:

1. **Cash repayment** by check or ACH within 30 days, recorded as a receivable and applied against the federal award in the accounting system.
2. **Offset** against pending or future reimbursement requests under the same subaward, documented on the remittance advice, where cash repayment is not received on time.
3. **Repayment plan** not exceeding 12 months, approved by the CEO, only where immediate recovery would jeopardize participant services and the subrecipient is otherwise compliant.

**6.9.3** Amounts unrecovered after 90 days are referred to the CEO, who may direct legal action, termination, and reporting to the awarding agencies. Any write-off requires CEO approval, notification to the federal awarding agency and state pass-through, and may require FRWC to repay the prime award from non-federal funds. All recoveries and their disposition are reported to awarding agencies as required by the prime award terms and reflected in federal financial reports by the finance team.

**6.9.4** The finance team reconciles the disallowed-cost receivable ledger monthly and reports balances to the Director of Grants Compliance.

### 6.10 Closeout and Record Retention

**6.10.1** Within 30 days after a subaward's end date, the contract analyst opens a closeout record in the GMS. The subrecipient must submit, no later than **90 calendar days** after the end date (per subaward terms consistent with 2 CFR 200.344): the final financial report, final programmatic report, final reimbursement request, equipment and unused supplies inventory, and a release/certification that all costs claimed are allowable and supported.

**6.10.2** The fiscal monitor reconciles total payments to the final financial report and the accounting system; resolves any variance; confirms all findings are closed or transferred to the recovery process; and confirms disposition instructions for equipment and residual supplies per 2 CFR 200.313–200.314. Unexpended advances or overpayments are recovered per Section 6.9.2.

**6.10.3** The Director of Grants Compliance approves closeout in the GMS; finance deobligates any remaining balance. Closeout is completed within 120 days of the subaward end date; FRWC's own prime-award closeout reporting reflects subaward closeouts within the timeframes of 2 CFR 200.344.

**6.10.4 Record retention.** FRWC retains all subaward records — determinations, risk assessments, screenings, agreements, monitoring workpapers, reports, corrective action logs, and financial records — for a minimum of **3 years from the date FRWC submits its final expenditure report** for the prime award, per 2 CFR 200.334, and longer if litigation, claim, audit, or awarding-agency direction requires. Subaward agreements impose the equivalent retention obligation on subrecipients, with FRWC and federal access rights per 2 CFR 200.337. Closeout does not terminate FRWC's right to disallow costs and recover funds based on later audit or review (2 CFR 200.345).

## 7. Exceptions and Special Situations

### 7.1 Subrecipient Below the Single Audit Threshold

A subrecipient expending less than $1,000,000 in total federal awards in its fiscal year is exempt from the single audit requirement (2 CFR 200.501) but **not** from FRWC monitoring. For each below-threshold subrecipient, FRWC substitutes an **annual limited-scope fiscal review**: the subrecipient submits its most recent financial statements (audited, reviewed, or compiled), its year-end general ledger for the subaward, and its annual federal expenditure certification. The fiscal monitor performs a desk review with the Tier 2 expenditure sample at minimum, regardless of the subrecipient's assigned tier, and documents the review in the monitoring tool. The threshold certification is re-verified annually; a subrecipient crossing the threshold is immediately flagged "single audit required" under Section 6.7.

### 7.2 Subrecipient with Going-Concern Doubt

Upon identification of substantial doubt about a subrecipient's ability to continue as a going concern (from auditor's report, financial statement review, or credible information), the Director of Grants Compliance, within 5 business days: (a) reassigns the subrecipient to Tier 3; (b) converts payments to **reimbursement-only with 100% documentation review** (no advances); (c) requires monthly financial statements including cash position and payables aging; (d) directs the program officer to prepare a participant continuity plan identifying alternative providers should services stop; and (e) notifies the CEO in writing. The Director of Grants Compliance decides within 30 days, with CEO concurrence, whether to continue the subaward with conditions, shorten its term, or begin orderly transition, and notifies the state pass-through if wind-down is likely.

### 7.3 Mid-Year Change in Key Personnel

When a subrecipient's executive director, fiscal director/CFO, or program director for the funded program changes mid-award, the subrecipient must notify FRWC within 10 business days (a subaward term). The program officer then: (a) obtains the successor's résumé and confirms qualifications against the subaward scope; (b) schedules an orientation call within 30 days covering subaward terms, budget controls, reporting, and this SOP's requirements; and (c) requests a risk reassessment. The fiscal monitor performs an off-cycle desk review with the next tier's sample size within 90 days of the change. If both the executive and fiscal leads change within 6 months, the subrecipient is assigned Tier 3 for at least two quarters.

### 7.4 Suspected Fraud

Any FRWC staff member who suspects fraud, falsified documentation, misappropriation, or conflict-of-interest violations at a subrecipient reports it to the Director of Grants Compliance **the same business day**, in writing, and does not alert the subrecipient. The Director of Grants Compliance immediately: (a) notifies the CEO; (b) places a payment hold; (c) preserves all relevant records and freezes routine correspondence that could compromise an investigation; and (d) within 3 business days, with the CEO, determines whether the facts require disclosure to the federal awarding agency's Office of Inspector General and the state pass-through under 2 CFR 200.113 (mandatory disclosure of credible evidence of violations of federal criminal law involving fraud, bribery, or gratuity). The CEO approves any referral, unannounced site visit, or engagement of forensic accountants or legal counsel. Routine monitoring of the subrecipient is suspended or modified only at the direction of the Director of Grants Compliance to avoid interfering with an investigation. No employee may negotiate resolution of suspected fraud without CEO approval; whistleblower protections under 41 U.S.C. 4712 are honored and communicated.

### 7.5 Other Deviations

Any other deviation from this SOP requires advance written approval from the Director of Grants Compliance, documented in the GMS with the rationale and duration; deviations affecting award conditions or payment require CEO approval. No deviation may waive a requirement of 2 CFR Part 200 or the prime award.
