# Executive Summary: Final-Mile Network — Atlanta/Charlotte

**Program Review Office | Cumberland Freight Group**
**Prepared by Marcus Oyelaran, Director of Program Review**
**July 30, 2025**

**Distribution:** Executive Committee, c/o Priya Raghunathan, Chief Operating Officer

## Finding and Recommendation

The final-mile delivery network serving the Atlanta and Charlotte metros was reviewed for the full fiscal 2025 cycle, from July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2025. The evidence shows a program that has outperformed its plan on every dimension the Program Review Office tracks: volume, unit cost, and service. The recommendation to the Executive Committee is to reaffirm the network's current funding, authorize planning work for expansion into one or two adjacent metros in fiscal 2026, and lock in the carrier and facility agreements that produced this year's cost performance before they come up for renewal. No corrective action is warranted. The open question for the Committee is not whether to continue funding this program but how much additional capital to make available for growth, since the network is now operating meaningfully beyond the scale it was built to handle.

## What the Evidence Shows

The network moved 2.4 million parcels against a plan of 1.9 million, a volume overshoot of roughly 26 percent. Overshoots of this size are usually accompanied by cost creep, as networks lean on spot capacity, overtime, and rush facility expansions to absorb unplanned demand. That did not happen here. Cost per stop came in at $4.18 against a target of $4.75, a favorable variance of 57 cents per stop. Applied across the full 2.4 million stops delivered, that variance represents on the order of $1.37 million in cost avoidance relative to the target the network was built to hit, achieved while carrying 500,000 more parcels than planned. Service held at 97.2 percent on-time, a level consistent with what the network delivered at its planned volume, which indicates the additional throughput was absorbed without degrading the customer experience that justified the investment in the first place.

Taken together, these three figures point to the same conclusion from three different angles: the network has structural slack it was not expected to have, the operating model transfers well to volume above plan, and the facilities, routing, and labor arrangements underlying the $4.18 figure are worth protecting rather than renegotiating from a position of urgency. The Program Review Office's read is that this performance is not a one-time favorable variance driven by mix or seasonality. The full-year duration of the review period, spanning both peak and non-peak months, and the consistency of the on-time figure against plan-level performance, both argue for a durable operating advantage rather than a temporary one.

The one caution the Committee should weigh is that a network running 26 percent over its designed volume is closer to its practical ceiling than the plan assumed. The favorable cost per stop was achieved at this volume; it was not tested at higher volume, and there is no evidence in this review about where the cost curve bends if growth continues at the pace seen this year. That is the basis for the expansion-planning recommendation below rather than an open-ended commitment to just add volume to the existing footprint.

## What This Asks of the Executive Committee

Three actions follow directly from the evidence:

- **Reaffirm current-year funding without reduction.** The network's performance does not support any budget trim in the next cycle; doing so would risk the service level that is driving the volume in the first place.
- **Authorize expansion scoping for fiscal 2026.** The Program Review Office recommends the Committee direct a feasibility study, to be completed before the fiscal 2026 planning cycle, on extending the Atlanta/Charlotte operating model to one or two adjacent metros with comparable density and carrier availability.
- **Direct early renewal conversations on facility and carrier agreements.** Several of the arrangements underlying the $4.18 cost-per-stop figure are the kind that typically firm up in price at renewal. Locking multi-year terms now, from a position where Cumberland is the stronger counterparty given the volume it is delivering, protects the cost structure the Committee is being asked to fund forward.

## Disposition of Committed Capital

No capital in this program is at risk, in question, or recommended for reallocation. The facilities, equipment, and labor commitments already in place for this network should continue unchanged into fiscal 2026 pending the outcome of the expansion feasibility study. The Program Review Office is not recommending incremental capital release at this time; the expansion study itself is a planning exercise to be funded from existing Program Review and network-planning budgets, with any capital ask for physical expansion to come back to the Committee as a separate item once the study is complete. In short: protect what is funded, study what might come next, and treat this program as a template rather than a subject of concern.

# Executive Summary: Refrigerated Pilot — Nashville

**Program Review Office | Cumberland Freight Group**
**Prepared by Marcus Oyelaran, Director of Program Review**
**July 30, 2025**

**Distribution:** Executive Committee, c/o Priya Raghunathan, Chief Operating Officer

## Finding and Recommendation

The refrigerated trailer pilot launched out of Nashville in September 2024 on $6.8 million of capital was reviewed for the ten months of operating history available through June 30, 2025. The evidence shows a pilot that has not approached commercial viability, is losing money at a rate that will not self-correct, and has lost the customer base it was built around. The recommendation to the Executive Committee is to end the pilot on a defined timeline, recover what capital can be recovered from the equipment and facility investment, and require a documented root-cause finding on the loss of anchor shippers before any refrigerated service concept is brought back to the Committee in any market.

## What the Evidence Shows

The pilot was underwritten on the assumption that 70 percent trailer utilization was the breakeven point. Ten months in, utilization stands at 41 percent, a gap of 29 points against the number the business case depended on. This is not a shortfall that improving execution closes on its own; it is a shortfall between the volume the pilot needs and the volume the market for it in Nashville has actually produced. The financial result follows directly: the pilot has lost $2.3 million against $6.8 million of invested capital, meaning roughly a third of the original capital commitment has already been consumed by operating losses in under a year, separate from the capital tied up in the trailers and dock modifications themselves.

The customer evidence is the most difficult to work around. The pilot was built with three anchor shippers whose committed volume was the basis for the 70 percent breakeven assumption. Two of those three are gone. A refrigerated network built to serve three anchor accounts cannot be expected to hit its utilization target serving one, and there is no indication in the review period of replacement volume materializing to offset the loss. When a pilot loses two-thirds of its anchor customer base within its first year of operation, the standard the Program Review Office applies is not whether the remaining relationship can be salvaged, but whether the original commercial premise still exists. Here, it does not.

It is worth being direct with the Committee about what this combination of facts means: this is not a program that needs more time to mature. Utilization pilots typically show a ramp curve toward breakeven over their first 12 to 18 months. This pilot is ten months in with utilization moving in the wrong direction relative to its customer base, not the right one. Continued operation without a change in the customer roster would mean funding further losses against a business case that no longer exists in the form it was approved under.

## What This Asks of the Executive Committee

The Program Review Office recommends the Committee take the following action at the August 14 budget lock:

- **Approve a wind-down of the pilot, effective no later than the end of Q1 fiscal 2026,** with an orderly exit from the remaining anchor relationship and any spot business currently moving through the pilot.
- **Decline any further capital commitment to the pilot** pending the root-cause review described below. No incremental funding should be authorized to "stabilize" utilization while that review is outstanding.
- **Direct a formal review of why two of three anchor shippers exited,** to be completed within 45 days of the wind-down decision, with findings reported back to the Committee before any future refrigerated concept — in Nashville or elsewhere — is proposed for capital.

## Disposition of Committed Capital

Of the $6.8 million capitalized at launch, $2.3 million has already been consumed in operating losses and is not recoverable. The Program Review Office recommends the Committee direct Finance to assess the remaining equipment — the refrigerated trailers and any dock modifications made at the Nashville facility — for redeployment value rather than write-off. Trailers acquired or leased for the pilot should be evaluated first for reassignment into the core dry-van or existing cold-chain fleet, where they retain value even if the standalone pilot does not continue; only equipment with no redeployment path should be treated as a loss. Any portion of the original $6.8 million not yet obligated to vendors or lessors as of the wind-down decision should be returned to the general capital pool ahead of the August 14 lock rather than held against this program. The Program Review Office does not recommend holding capital in reserve for a relaunch; any future refrigerated concept should be evaluated and capitalized on its own merits once the root-cause review is complete.

# Executive Summary: Driver Academy

**Program Review Office | Cumberland Freight Group**
**Prepared by Marcus Oyelaran, Director of Program Review**
**July 30, 2025**

**Distribution:** Executive Committee, c/o Priya Raghunathan, Chief Operating Officer

## Finding and Recommendation

The Driver Academy, run by Talent Director Yolanda Brissett, was reviewed for its fiscal 2025 graduating cohort of 218 drivers, trained at a cost of $9,400 per graduate. The evidence shows a genuine and welcome improvement in fleet-wide retention, but one that arrived in the same quarter as a company-wide pay increase, which makes it impossible from the data available to state how much of the gain the Academy is actually responsible for. The evidence also shows a specific, unresolved failure: retention among Academy graduates in the Birmingham lane has not moved at all. The recommendation to the Executive Committee is to continue the Academy into fiscal 2026, but on a conditional basis — funding should be renewed at current scale only alongside a controlled comparison that isolates the Academy's effect from the pay increase, and alongside a specific corrective plan for Birmingham with its own success metric and reporting timeline.

## What the Evidence Shows

Twelve-month retention across the fleet rose from 54 percent to 63 percent over the review period, a nine-point improvement that, if attributable to the Academy, would represent a strong return on the $2,049,200 spent training this year's 218 graduates. The difficulty is timing: a company-wide pay increase took effect in the same quarter as this improvement. Pay is one of the best-documented drivers of retention in this industry, and an increase applied to the entire driver population, not just Academy graduates, would be expected to move the fleet-wide retention number regardless of what the Academy did or did not do. The Program Review Office has no basis in the current data to separate the two effects. It would be equally wrong to credit the Academy with the full nine-point gain and to assume the Academy contributed nothing; the honest position is that the current evidence cannot support either claim, and the Committee should not renew funding on the assumption that this year's number will repeat next year absent a further pay action.

The Birmingham lane result is the sharper piece of evidence in this review, because it does not depend on separating out the pay increase. Graduate turnover in Birmingham held at 48 percent across the same period in which fleet-wide retention improved. Whatever combination of Academy training and pay increase produced the fleet-wide gain, it did not reach Birmingham. A program that is meant to improve retention broadly, but that shows no measurable effect in a lane where the underlying turnover problem is worst, has identified its own priority for the coming year. It also suggests the Birmingham problem may not be a training problem at all — it may be route economics, dispatch conditions, or local labor competition that no amount of academy instruction addresses, which is a distinct question the Committee should ask Talent to run down directly rather than assume more training solves it.

## What This Asks of the Executive Committee

- **Approve conditional renewal of the Academy at current scale for fiscal 2026**, contingent on the two items below rather than as an unconditional continuation.
- **Direct Talent to design and run a controlled comparison** — Academy graduates against a matched set of non-Academy new hires, both populations subject to the same pay increase — with interim results reported to the Committee within two quarters, so that the fiscal 2027 funding decision rests on evidence that isolates the Academy's actual contribution.
- **Require a Birmingham-specific corrective plan from Yolanda Brissett within 60 days**, including a hypothesis for why turnover has not moved there, a proposed intervention distinct from continued training volume, and a target retention figure against which the next review will measure it.

## Disposition of Committed Capital

The $2,049,200 spent training this year's 218 graduates is a sunk cost tied to drivers now in the fleet; it is not recoverable and is not in question. The decision before the Committee is entirely forward-looking: whether to commit a comparable amount for the fiscal 2026 cohort. The Program Review Office recommends that renewal be approved in principle at the August 14 lock, but that a portion of the fiscal 2026 Academy budget — sufficient to cover the controlled comparison study and the Birmingham intervention — be ring-fenced and released only against the deliverables above, rather than folded into per-graduate training spend on the same terms as this year.

# Executive Summary: Olive Branch Automation Retrofit

**Program Review Office | Cumberland Freight Group**
**Prepared by Marcus Oyelaran, Director of Program Review**
**July 30, 2025**

**Distribution:** Executive Committee, c/o Priya Raghunathan, Chief Operating Officer

## Finding and Recommendation

The automation retrofit at the Olive Branch facility, sponsored by VP of Engineering Hasan Demirci, was reviewed for the period from its March 2024 approval through June 30, 2025. The evidence shows a project that is well over its approved budget, eight months behind its original schedule, and not yet complete — but also a project whose core technology is working: the live zone is already delivering a 31 percent gain in picks per labor hour. The recommendation to the Executive Committee is not to halt the project. The productivity evidence from the live zone is strong enough to justify finishing it. The recommendation is instead to approve the additional capital needed to complete the remaining scope, but only alongside a revised project structure — a new total cost estimate, milestone-gated funding releases, and an accountability review of the program management that allowed a 38 percent cost overrun to develop before it was escalated.

## What the Evidence Shows

The project was approved at $14.2 million. To date, $19.6 million has been spent, an overrun of $5.4 million, or 38 percent above the approved figure — and the project is not finished. The original schedule called for completion in March 2025; the current forecast is November 2025, an eight-month slip. Cost overruns and schedule slips of this size, occurring together on a project that has not yet reached completion, are the pattern the Program Review Office treats most seriously, because it means the $19.6 million spent to date is not the final number. Absent a revised estimate and tighter controls, there is no basis in this review to assume the project finishes at or near its current spend; the same conditions that produced the overrun through June 2025 remain in place going into the final phase.

Set against that, the operational evidence from the portion of the retrofit already live is genuinely strong. Picks per labor hour in the live zone are up 31 percent, which is a material, measurable productivity gain of the kind automation investments are supposed to produce and often do not. This is the fact that should anchor the Committee's decision: the technology itself is delivering, in the zone where it has actually been deployed. The problem this review identifies is not that the retrofit does not work. The problem is that the project has been managed in a way that allowed cost and schedule to drift 38 percent and eight months, respectively, without the kind of gated escalation that should have brought this to the Committee's attention well before this review cycle. Those are separate failures — one of engineering, one of program management — and they call for separate responses.

## What This Asks of the Executive Committee

- **Approve continuation of the project through to the revised November 2025 completion date**, on the strength of the live-zone productivity result, rather than treating the overrun as grounds to stop.
- **Require Hasan Demirci to submit a revised total project cost estimate**, covering the remaining scope through November 2025, before any additional capital is released. The Program Review Office does not have confidence that $19.6 million represents the true cost to completion and recommends the Committee not authorize an open-ended additional allocation without this figure.
- **Direct that remaining funding be released in milestone-gated tranches** tied to specific completion markers between now and November 2025, rather than as a single supplemental appropriation, so that any further slippage is visible to the Committee before it compounds.
- **Commission a separate, short program-management review** — distinct from this cost and schedule review — to determine how a 38 percent overrun and an eight-month slip accumulated before being brought forward, so that the same pattern does not recur on the next capital project of this size.

## Disposition of Committed Capital

The $19.6 million spent to date is committed and largely irreversible; the equipment and integration work already in the live zone is producing the productivity gain cited above and should not be disturbed. The open question is the capital still required to complete the remaining scope through the revised November 2025 date. The Program Review Office recommends the Committee authorize this additional capital in principle at the August 14 lock, but withhold the specific dollar figure and release schedule until the revised total cost estimate is delivered, and structure whatever is approved as milestone-gated rather than lump-sum. This protects the demonstrated value of the work already done while addressing directly the control failure that allowed this project to run 38 percent over budget before reaching this review.

## Closing Note to the Executive Committee

The four summaries above represent the complete output of the fiscal 2025 Program Review cycle. Each was prepared independently, on its own evidence, and each stands as a standalone record for the Committee's file regardless of the disposition reached on the other three. Read together, they ask the Committee for four different kinds of action in the same meeting: reaffirmation without change, termination with recovery, conditional renewal pending further evidence, and continuation under tightened controls. The Program Review Office has deliberately not attempted to net these against one another into a single portfolio recommendation, because the four programs do not share a common objective, a common sponsor, or a common capital source, and forcing them into one combined ask would obscure the distinct decision each one requires.

Two items cut across all four and are worth stating plainly before the August 14 lock. First, in every program where this review found a problem — the Nashville pilot's customer attrition, the Birmingham retention gap, the Olive Branch overrun — the underlying cause was known internally before it reached this review. Each has surfaced through routine reporting channels for at least two quarters. The recommendations above address the specific programs; the Program Review Office would ask the Committee separately whether the reporting thresholds that allow issues of this size to reach a annual review before escalation are set correctly, or whether they should be tightened for fiscal 2026. Second, the strongest result in this cycle, the final-mile network's performance, and the weakest, the refrigerated pilot's collapse, both originated from the same planning function within the same fiscal year. The Program Review Office has no finding on why one business case held and the other did not, but recommends the Committee ask Network Planning directly, rather than treat the two outcomes as unrelated.

Formal certification of these findings, and the underlying work papers supporting each figure cited above, are available to any Committee member on request ahead of the August 14 meeting.
