## 3. Environmental Report — Fourth Exceedance and Restart of Penalty Accrual

Ms. Fasthorse reported that on Monday, April 6, 2026, at 2:14 p.m., the continuous opacity monitor on Baghouse 2 recorded a six-minute average opacity of 24 percent, exceeding the 20 percent limit. This was the fourth exceedance since the events giving rise to the consent order and the first since the order was entered. The root cause was preliminarily identified as a failed pulse-jet solenoid valve on compartment 6, which allowed a bank of bags to blind and forced flow through torn bags in compartment 3. The event was reported to the Department of Natural Resources within the 24-hour deadline on April 7.

Ms. Jelinek advised that under Paragraph 14 of the consent order, the exceedance restarted stipulated penalty accrual at $2,500 per day, beginning April 6, and that accrual would continue until the company demonstrated thirty consecutive days of compliant operation on the affected unit or the department accepted a corrective action closure. She estimated exposure of $75,000 to $112,500 if the corrective action ran thirty to forty-five days, and recommended a prompt written corrective action submittal.

Mr. Trombley reported that the failed solenoid had been replaced April 7, that compartment 3 bags would be replaced by April 15, and that the Baghouse 4 change-out remained scheduled for April 10–12. Ms. Katsaros directed that a formal root cause analysis be completed and reviewed with counsel before submittal to the department.

Ms. Fasthorse moved that the committee authorize submittal of a corrective action plan for the April 6 exceedance to the department by April 20, 2026, following legal review. Mr. Trombley seconded. The motion carried 5–0.

## 5. Safety Report — Pouring Floor Near-Miss Investigation

Ms. Fasthorse presented the completed investigation of the May 4 pouring-floor near-miss (Item 26-20). Findings: the monorail hoist's variable-frequency drive braking parameter had been reset to factory default during an April control-board replacement, producing an abrupt stop and ladle swing; the change was not captured under the management-of-change procedure; and the stanchion struck was within the posted ladle travel path. The report made four recommendations: (1) restore and lock hoist deceleration parameters and add them to the preventive maintenance checklist; (2) apply the management-of-change procedure to all drive and control parameter changes; (3) re-mark and physically guard the ladle travel corridor; and (4) retrain all pouring-floor and maintenance personnel by July 31, 2026.

Mr. Trombley confirmed that recommendation (1) was completed May 22. Ms. Fasthorse moved that the committee accept the investigation report and adopt all four recommendations with the completion dates shown in the action items below. Mr. Balogun seconded. The motion carried 6–0.
