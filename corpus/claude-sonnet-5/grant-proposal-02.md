# Rural Maternal Health Access Program
## Application Narrative
### Platte Meridian Community Health

---

## Statement of Need

Platte Meridian Community Health (PMCH) is a federally qualified health center operating six sites across central Nebraska, serving 28,400 patients through the work of 214 staff on a $41 million annual operating budget. Our patient population reflects the demographic reality of rural central Nebraska's changing economy: 62 percent of patients identify as Hispanic or Latino, and 11 percent as Somali or Sudanese, communities drawn to the region by meatpacking and agricultural processing employment. Thirty-eight percent of our patients are best served in a language other than English, requiring interpretation across Spanish, Somali, and Arabic in addition to English-language care. Forty-four percent of patients live at or below 138 percent of the federal poverty level, and 27 percent are uninsured. These are not abstractions to us; they describe the daily conditions under which our clinicians and community health workers try to get pregnant patients into care early, keep them in care, and deliver them safely.

The need this application addresses is acute and worsening. Since 2021, the number of prenatal patients served across our six sites has nearly doubled, from 610 to 1,180 annually, a 93 percent increase driven by population growth, immigration and refugee resettlement patterns, and the collapse of obstetric access elsewhere in our service area. Despite this growth in volume, only 51 percent of our prenatal patients enter care in the first trimester, compared to a Nebraska state average of 74 percent. That 23-point gap is the central problem this proposal is built to close. First-trimester entry to care is not a bureaucratic milestone; it is the window in which gestational diabetes and hypertensive disorders are caught early, in which genetic and anatomic screening can be offered with enough time to act on results, and in which patients with limited English proficiency or unstable housing can be connected to the community health worker support that keeps them engaged through delivery. A patient who enters care in the second or third trimester has already lost the chance for early risk stratification, and our data show that late entry correlates directly with the low birth weight and severe maternal morbidity outcomes the Trust asks us to move.

The crisis in access has a second, more recent driver. In 2023, Meadowlark Regional Medical Center closed its obstetrics unit, a decision made on hospital-system financial grounds rather than community need. Meadowlark had been the delivery hospital for roughly 40 percent of our prenatal patients. Since its closure, the nearest facility offering labor and delivery services is 38 miles from our largest site, a distance that becomes a genuine safety hazard in a central Nebraska winter, for a patient in active labor, or for a family without a reliable second vehicle. Thirty-eight miles is also, for many of our patients, a two-hour round trip on unpaved county roads to attend a single prenatal visit, which helps explain both our late entry-to-care numbers and our postpartum visit completion rates, which lag the state average as well. The obstetric desert created by Meadowlark's closure did not simply reduce delivery capacity; it collapsed the surrounding referral and prenatal-support ecosystem that rural patients depend on.

We had intended to address the delivery gap in partnership with Prairie Women's Health, a physician group that had committed in principle to serving as our delivery partner for midwifery-led births requiring hospital transfer. In June of this year, following Prairie Women's Health's sale to an outside investor group, that commitment was withdrawn. We describe this development in full in the Organizational Capacity section below, because the Trust deserves an honest account of where our partner arrangements stand, not a proposal built on an agreement that no longer exists. We raise it here because it is itself evidence of the need: even organizations positioned to solve rural obstetric access are vulnerable to consolidation and investor decisions made far from central Nebraska, and community health centers like PMCH are often the only stable, mission-anchored institution left standing when private delivery infrastructure contracts.

The consequence of these converging pressures, population growth, late entry to care, linguistic and economic barriers, and the loss of local delivery capacity, is a maternal health system under visible strain. We are not requesting funds to build a program from nothing. We are requesting funds to scale and stabilize a response that our own patients have made necessary by their growing numbers, and that the closure of Meadowlark's obstetrics unit has made urgent. The Trust's Rural Maternal Health Access Program is designed for precisely this circumstance: a federally qualified health center with demonstrated need, a growing prenatal population, and a specific, credible plan to move first-trimester entry, postpartum completion, severe maternal morbidity, low birth weight, and cost per prenatal episode in a five-year window.

## Project Description

PMCH proposes a five-year Rural Maternal Health Access Initiative built around four interlocking components: midwifery-led prenatal and postpartum care, an expanded community health worker workforce trained through a local community college pipeline, a contracted doula benefit for patients who choose it, and extended evening clinic hours at three of our six sites. These components were designed together, by CEO Oluwaseun Adebayo and Director of Women's Health Yesenia Padilla, specifically to attack the first-trimester entry gap and the postpartum follow-up gap that our data show are the two most correctable drivers of our poor outcomes relative to state averages.

**Midwifery-Led Care.** We will hire two certified nurse-midwives, one based at our largest site and one who will rotate among our other five sites on a fixed weekly schedule, prioritizing the two sites farthest from any delivery hospital. Midwives will provide the full scope of prenatal care, labor support in coordination with delivery partners, and postpartum care, with physician backup from Dr. Karin Lindholm, our Chief Medical Officer, and our existing family medicine physicians who hold obstetric privileges. The midwifery model is central to closing the first-trimester gap because midwives can be scheduled with same-week and same-day prenatal intake appointments in a way our current physician-only capacity cannot sustain, and because midwifery-led continuity of care is independently associated in the literature with higher postpartum visit completion, one of the Trust's five scored measures.

**Community Health Workers.** We will hire and train four new community health workers (CHWs), bringing our total maternal health CHW staff to six. These positions will be trained through a certificate partnership with Central Community College, whose CHW curriculum includes maternal-health-specific modules we have worked with the college to develop over the past year in anticipation of this proposal. CHWs will be assigned by language and community: we anticipate two Spanish-speaking CHWs, one Somali-speaking CHW, and one CHW cross-trained in both outreach logistics and Arabic-language support for our Sudanese patients, reflecting the 38 percent of our patients best served in a language other than English. CHWs will conduct outreach at WIC offices, churches, meatpacking plant break rooms during shift changes, and resettlement agency intake, all identified through our current CHWs' informal but consistent field reporting as the highest-yield points of contact with pregnant patients before they have entered any care system. Once patients are enrolled, CHWs will conduct home visits, arrange transportation, accompany patients to the 38-mile trip for delivery when needed, and provide the culturally specific follow-up that our data indicate is the strongest predictor of postpartum visit completion within twelve weeks.

**Doula Contract.** We will contract with a regional doula collective to offer doula support to prenatal patients who choose it, at no direct cost to the patient. Doula support will be coordinated by the CHW assigned to each patient and will be available in Spanish, Somali, and English, with interpretation arranged for Arabic-speaking patients as needed. We are proposing a contracted model rather than employed doulas in year one because it allows us to scale coverage against actual demand without the fixed cost of underutilized employed staff, while preserving the option to bring the function in-house in later years if utilization and outcomes justify it.

**Evening Hours.** We will extend clinic hours to two evenings per week at three sites, chosen because they serve the highest concentrations of patients working meatpacking and agricultural shifts that make daytime prenatal visits difficult to attend without lost wages. Evening hours will be staffed by the midwives and by rotating physician and nursing staff, supported by CHWs who will use evening hours as a scheduling anchor for outreach follow-up.

**Delivery Partnership.** As described in the Organizational Capacity section, our original delivery partner, Prairie Women's Health, withdrew its commitment in June 2026 following its sale to an investor group. We are addressing this directly rather than presenting the proposal as though that arrangement still holds. Dr. Lindholm and Ms. Padilla have opened conversations with two alternative delivery partners: the regional hospital 38 miles from our largest site, which retains a functioning labor and delivery unit, and a second, newer relationship with a certified nurse-midwife-friendly hospital system 52 miles in the opposite direction that has expressed interest in a formal transfer and collaborative practice agreement. Neither agreement is finalized. We describe below, in Organizational Capacity, exactly what is committed, what is in negotiation, and the timeline on which we expect resolution, because we believe the Trust's investment should be evaluated against the honest state of these relationships rather than an idealized one.

**Integration.** These four components are designed to function as a single intake-to-postpartum pathway rather than four independent services. A patient's first contact is typically a CHW, at outreach or at intake; the CHW's job is to get that patient to a first-trimester visit with a midwife or physician as fast as possible, arrange doula support if desired, and remain the patient's point of continuity through delivery, coordinating transportation to whichever delivery partner applies, and following up through the twelfth postpartum week. Evening hours exist to remove the single largest logistical barrier CHWs report hearing from patients who miss or delay appointments: the conflict between clinic hours and shift work. Each component is built to move one or more of the Trust's five scored measures, and we return to that mapping explicitly in the Goals and Measurable Objectives section.

We are requesting $3.75 million over five years, phased to front-load staffing and training costs in years one and two and to shift emphasis toward sustainability and outcome consolidation in years four and five, detailed fully in the Budget Narrative.

## Goals and Measurable Objectives

Our overarching goal is to bring PMCH's prenatal care outcomes into line with Nebraska state averages within the five-year grant period, measured against the five indicators the Trust has identified as its scoring criteria. We present our objectives against each of those five measures directly.

**Goal 1: Increase first-trimester entry to prenatal care.**
Objective 1.1: Increase the percentage of prenatal patients entering care in the first trimester from the current 51 percent to 62 percent by the end of year two, and to 70 percent by the end of year five, closing roughly three-quarters of the current 23-point gap against the state average of 74 percent.
Objective 1.2: Increase CHW-initiated prenatal intake contacts (patients identified and scheduled by a CHW before self-referral) from an estimated 90 per year currently to 350 per year by year three.

**Goal 2: Increase postpartum visit completion within twelve weeks.**
Objective 2.1: Increase the percentage of prenatal patients completing a postpartum visit within twelve weeks of delivery from our current baseline of 58 percent to 75 percent by year three and 80 percent by year five.
Objective 2.2: Achieve CHW-documented postpartum contact (home visit, phone, or in-person) with at least 85 percent of enrolled patients within two weeks of delivery by year two, as a leading indicator for the twelve-week visit measure.

**Goal 3: Reduce severe maternal morbidity.**
Objective 3.1: Reduce our severe maternal morbidity rate, currently estimated at 210 per 10,000 delivery hospitalizations based on the most recent three years of available hospital discharge data, to at or below the Nebraska average of 155 per 10,000 by year five.
Objective 3.2: Achieve documented risk screening (hypertensive disorder, gestational diabetes, hemorrhage risk) at every first-trimester and third-trimester visit for 95 percent of enrolled patients by year two.

**Goal 4: Reduce low birth weight rate.**
Objective 4.1: Reduce the low birth weight rate among PMCH prenatal patients from the current 9.4 percent to 7.5 percent by year five, moving toward the state rate of 6.8 percent.
Objective 4.2: Increase the percentage of patients receiving at least eight prenatal visits (the ACOG-recommended minimum absent complicating factors) from 61 percent currently to 80 percent by year four.

**Goal 5: Reduce cost per prenatal episode.**
Objective 5.1: Reduce average cost per prenatal episode (defined as total direct cost of prenatal, delivery-coordination, and postpartum care divided by number of patients completing the episode) by 15 percent by year five, driven by earlier entry to care, reduced emergency department utilization for preventable prenatal complications, and reduced low birth weight-associated neonatal costs.
Objective 5.2: Reduce prenatal-related emergency department visits among enrolled patients by 20 percent by year four, as the primary near-term driver of cost per episode.

Each objective is tied to a specific data source and reporting cadence described fully in the Evaluation Plan, and each is assigned to a named accountable staff member: Ms. Padilla for clinical and CHW-outcome objectives, Dr. Lindholm for morbidity and screening objectives, and CFO Bao Tran and Grants Director Elias Mwangi for the cost-per-episode measure.

## Evaluation Plan

PMCH will evaluate this initiative using a mixed quantitative and qualitative design built around the Trust's five scored measures, with quarterly internal review and annual reporting to the Trust.

**Data Sources.** Clinical outcome data (first-trimester entry, visit completion, screening documentation, birth weight, delivery complications) will be drawn from our electronic health record, which we upgraded in 2024 to support structured prenatal care pathways and which already captures gestational age at first visit and postpartum visit completion as discrete, reportable fields. Severe maternal morbidity data will be drawn from delivery hospital discharge summaries obtained through data-sharing agreements we will formalize with whichever delivery partner or partners are finalized, supplemented by CHW-documented delivery outcomes for patients who deliver outside a formal partner relationship. Cost data will be developed by CFO Bao Tran's office using a per-episode costing methodology that allocates direct clinical staff time, CHW time, doula contract costs, and delivery-coordination costs against completed prenatal episodes, reconciled quarterly against the general ledger.

**Design.** We will use a pre-post design comparing each cohort year against our 2021–2025 baseline, with the understanding that a randomized or controlled design is neither ethical nor feasible in a single-site rural FQHC context where all eligible patients should receive the intervention. To strengthen causal inference, we will track a dose-response relationship internally: patients receiving full-pathway services (CHW enrollment, midwifery care, and doula support where elected) compared against patients receiving partial services, to help us understand which components are driving outcome change and to inform the staffing decisions we describe in Sustainability.

**Cadence and Responsibility.** Ms. Padilla will convene a quarterly outcomes review with the two midwives, the CHW team lead, and Dr. Lindholm, examining progress against each measurable objective and flagging any site or population subgroup falling behind target, with particular attention to whether Somali- and Arabic-speaking patients are progressing at the same rate as Spanish-speaking and English-speaking patients, given known disparities in outreach penetration among newer resettlement communities. Dr. Lindholm will lead an annual clinical quality review examining severe maternal morbidity and screening documentation in the context of our broader clinical quality program, the same infrastructure through which we are closing the sliding fee documentation condition described below. Mr. Mwangi will compile an annual report to the Trust each December, aligned to the Trust's reporting requirements, combining outcome data, narrative progress against goals, budget-to-actual analysis, and a revised sustainability projection.

**External Validation.** We will benchmark our first-trimester entry, postpartum completion, and low birth weight figures annually against Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services vital statistics and against the Uniform Data System (UDS) reporting we already submit as an FQHC, so that our progress is verifiable against sources independent of our own record-keeping. We will also submit to an external chart audit in year three, conducted by an outside quality consultant, to validate the accuracy of our EHR-derived measures before reporting them to the Trust for the year-three and year-five benchmarks that carry the greatest weight in our goal structure.

**Course Correction.** If quarterly review shows an objective falling more than 10 percent behind its interim target, Ms. Padilla and Dr. Lindholm will bring a corrective plan to CEO Adebayo within 30 days, which may include reallocating CHW outreach effort, adjusting evening hour scheduling, or renegotiating doula contract terms. We will document these adjustments in our annual report to the Trust rather than treating them as failures to be concealed; a five-year rural maternal health initiative that never requires mid-course correction is not one we would find credible ourselves.

## Organizational Capacity

Platte Meridian Community Health has operated as a federally qualified health center for eighteen years and currently serves 28,400 patients across six sites with 214 staff and a $41 million operating budget. We bring to this proposal an existing women's health infrastructure, under Director of Women's Health Yesenia Padilla, that has grown prenatal enrollment from 610 to 1,180 patients since 2021 without the benefit of the staffing this grant would provide, using existing family medicine physicians, two current CHWs, and a patchwork of transportation and interpretation support. That growth, achieved under strain, is the strongest evidence we can offer that our organization has the demand, the community trust, and the operational discipline to deploy new resources effectively rather than merely absorb them.

**Leadership.** CEO Oluwaseun Adebayo has led PMCH for six years, prior experience includes FQHC operations in a comparably rural Midwestern setting, and he will serve as the ultimate accountable executive for this grant, with direct oversight of Mr. Mwangi's grants management office. Ms. Padilla, a family nurse practitioner with a background in maternal-child health program design, has led the Women's Health department for four years and is the architect of the clinical model described in the Project Description. Dr. Karin Lindholm, Chief Medical Officer, provides physician oversight for all clinical operations, including obstetric backup, credentialing, and quality assurance.

**The 2025 HRSA Site Visit.** In the interest of full transparency with the Trust, Dr. Lindholm reports the following regarding our most recent HRSA operational site visit, conducted in the fall of 2025. The visit produced two conditions. The first concerned credentialing documentation gaps for two contracted providers whose privileging files lacked complete primary source verification records at the time of review; this condition has been fully resolved, with corrected files submitted to HRSA and confirmed closed in writing in the first quarter of 2026. Our credentialing process now includes a standing quarterly audit, led by Dr. Lindholm's office, specifically to prevent recurrence. The second condition concerned sliding fee discount program documentation, specifically incomplete retention of income verification records for a sample of sliding fee patients reviewed during the visit. This condition remains open. We have submitted a corrective action plan to HRSA that includes a revised documentation checklist, staff retraining completed in the second quarter of 2026, and a retrospective audit of the prior twelve months of sliding fee files, which is underway and on track for completion in the fourth quarter of 2026. We expect to submit closure documentation to HRSA by year end. We report this to the Trust not because it is required but because a funder evaluating a five-year, $3.75 million commitment should know exactly where our compliance posture stands, and because the same quality infrastructure now correcting this condition, structured quarterly review, named accountability, and documented corrective action, is the infrastructure that will govern this grant's evaluation plan.

**Delivery Partnership Status.** Our proposal was originally developed around a delivery partnership with Prairie Women's Health, an independent obstetric group with whom Ms. Padilla and Dr. Lindholm had reached an informal agreement in principle to provide labor and delivery services and midwifery collaborative practice arrangements for PMCH patients. In June 2026, following Prairie Women's Health's sale to an outside investor group, that group's new ownership withdrew the commitment, citing a shift toward a narrower, higher-margin service line. We regard this as a serious setback, and we are not minimizing it. In its place, Dr. Lindholm and Ms. Padilla have opened active discussions with two alternative partners. The first is the regional hospital 38 miles from our largest site, which operates the nearest functioning labor and delivery unit and has expressed interest in a collaborative practice agreement for our incoming midwives; we expect a term sheet by the fourth quarter of 2026. The second is a hospital system 52 miles from our southernmost site, which has an existing history of certified nurse-midwife collaborative practice and has indicated willingness to formalize a transfer and consultation agreement; early conversations began in July 2026. Neither agreement is executed as of this application. We are requesting that the Trust evaluate our organizational capacity on the basis of our eighteen-year operating history, our demonstrated prenatal enrollment growth, our existing physician obstetric backup capacity through Dr. Lindholm's office, and the concrete, dated progress of these two partnership conversations, rather than on a partnership that no longer exists. We commit to providing the Trust with written notice and an executed collaborative practice or transfer agreement, or a revised operational plan if neither materializes, no later than the end of year one of the grant period, well before midwifery hiring is complete and delivery volume under this program becomes significant.

**Facilities and Systems.** All six sites are equipped for prenatal care delivery, with ultrasound capacity at three sites and interpretation services, in-person and via contracted telephonic interpretation, available in Spanish, Somali, Arabic, and eleven other languages across our patient population. Our 2024 EHR upgrade supports the structured data capture this evaluation plan depends on. Our finance office, led by CFO Bao Tran, has managed federal 330 grant funding, state perinatal grant funding, and private foundation funding concurrently for the past four years without an audit finding, and Grants Director Elias Mwangi has managed FQHC grant compliance and reporting for seven years.

## Sustainability

The Trust's five-year investment is intended to build capacity that persists after the grant period ends, and we have designed this initiative, and this section, around that expectation rather than around simply spending down an award. CFO Bao Tran and Grants Director Elias Mwangi have modeled sustainability separately for each component of the program, because each has a different post-grant funding pathway.

**Midwives.** The two midwife positions are the most straightforwardly sustainable component, because midwifery care generates billable clinical revenue at rates comparable to physician primary care visits under Medicaid and commercial reimbursement, and Nebraska Medicaid reimburses certified nurse-midwife services at 100 percent of the physician fee schedule for the relevant CPT codes. Mr. Tran's modeling projects that by year three, midwife-generated clinical revenue, combined with our existing FQHC Prospective Payment System (PPS) encounter rate, will cover approximately 70 percent of fully loaded midwife compensation and benefits, with the remaining gap closeable through the visit volume growth we expect from the first-trimester entry gains themselves; more patients entering care earlier means more billable encounters per completed pregnancy.

**Community Health Workers.** The CHW positions are the component the Trust has specifically asked us to address, because CHW services are not independently billable under Nebraska Medicaid in the same way clinical encounters are, and grant-funded CHW positions are a well-documented sustainability failure point for maternal health programs nationally. Our plan addresses this on three tracks. First, Nebraska Medicaid adopted a state plan amendment in 2024 permitting reimbursement for CHW services delivered under the supervision of a billing provider and tied to a documented care plan; we are structuring our CHW documentation and supervision model now, in year one, specifically to meet these billing requirements, and Mr. Mwangi has engaged our Medicaid managed care organization contacts to confirm claims pathway details. We project this will cover an estimated 30 to 40 percent of CHW program cost by year four. Second, we are building CHW cost into our FQHC PPS wraparound rate negotiation with our state Medicaid agency, arguing, on the strength of the outcome data this grant will generate, that CHW-supported prenatal care is a cost-effective covered service; this is a longer and less certain pathway but one several other FQHCs nationally have successfully pursued using outcome data of exactly this kind. Third, we are incorporating CHW cost into our 330 grant base funding request in our next federal FQHC grant renewal cycle, positioning the program's demonstrated outcomes, first-trimester entry, postpartum completion, and cost per episode, as the evidence base for that request. We project that between billing revenue, PPS rate adjustment, and 330 base funding, we can sustain four of the six CHW positions at full grant-period staffing levels by year six, with the remaining two positions dependent on continued philanthropic or state perinatal grant support, which we will pursue actively beginning in year four so that any gap is identified and addressed before, not after, grant funds are exhausted.

**Doula Contract.** We will evaluate doula utilization and outcome contribution annually beginning in year two. If utilization and outcome data justify continuation, Mr. Mwangi will pursue Medicaid doula reimbursement, which Nebraska has signaled intent to adopt but has not yet implemented as of this application; if that pathway materializes during the grant period, as several neighboring states have already done, doula services become self-sustaining on the same basis as midwifery care. If it does not materialize, we will right-size the contract to a level fundable through our existing community benefit and philanthropic budget, which currently supports smaller-scale contracted services elsewhere in the organization.

**Evening Hours.** Evening hours carry marginal staffing cost above existing salaried clinical staff and are the least resource-intensive component to sustain; we project full sustainability through existing PPS encounter revenue by year three, since evening appointments generate the same billable encounter as daytime appointments.

**Governance.** CEO Adebayo will bring an annual sustainability review to the PMCH Board of Directors beginning in year two, formalizing the Medicaid billing, PPS rate, and 330 base funding progress described above, so that sustainability planning is a standing governance function rather than a year-five scramble.

## Budget Narrative

We request $3.75 million over five years, structured to front-load workforce build-out in years one and two while preserving funding through year five for continued direct service, evaluation, and the sustainability transition described above. The following narrative describes major budget categories; a full line-item budget is attached as a separate document per Trust guidelines.

**Personnel (approximately $2.55 million, 68 percent of total).** This category funds the fully loaded salary and benefits of two certified nurse-midwives (estimated combined annual cost $340,000, phased in during year one), four new community health workers (estimated combined annual cost $220,000 once fully staffed, hired in a staggered schedule across year one to allow concurrent enrollment in the Central Community College CHW certificate program), and a 0.5 FTE program coordinator housed in Ms. Padilla's office to manage scheduling integration across midwifery, CHW, doula, and evening-hour components. Personnel costs grow modestly in years two through five with standard cost-of-living adjustment and step increases, and are partially offset beginning in year three by the clinical revenue described in the Sustainability section, which we have netted against the grant request in years four and five to avoid requesting Trust funds for cost already covered by billing revenue.

**Training (approximately $180,000, 5 percent of total).** This funds CHW tuition and materials at Central Community College for four new hires across a staggered year-one and year-two cohort structure, continuing education for the midwives specific to rural collaborative practice credentialing, and an annual half-day training for all women's health staff on culturally specific outreach for our Somali and Sudanese patient populations, developed in consultation with community liaisons.

**Contracted Services (approximately $410,000, 11 percent of total).** This funds the doula collective contract, structured as a per-patient-encounter rate rather than a flat retainer to control cost against actual utilization, contracted telephonic interpretation supplementing our in-person capacity for evening hours and CHW field work, and the year-three external chart audit described in the Evaluation Plan.

**Transportation (approximately $190,000, 5 percent of total).** Given the 38-mile distance to the nearest delivery hospital and the additional 52-mile distance to our second prospective delivery partner, transportation support is not incidental to this program but core to it. This funds a program-dedicated vehicle and mileage reimbursement structure for CHW-accompanied delivery transport, and a patient transportation assistance fund, administered by CHWs, for prenatal visit and postpartum visit travel, targeted specifically at the sites farthest from any delivery facility.

**Evaluation and Data (approximately $195,000, 5 percent of total).** This funds EHR reporting module enhancements to support the structured data capture described in the Evaluation Plan, data-sharing agreement development with delivery partners, and staff time for the quarterly and annual review process led by Ms. Padilla, Dr. Lindholm, and Mr. Mwangi.

**Equipment and Site Costs (approximately $95,000, 2.5 percent of total).** This funds modest exam room and evening-hours facility adjustments at the three sites extending hours, including additional intake and waiting area furnishing to accommodate evening patient flow separate from daytime scheduling.

**Indirect Costs (approximately $130,000, 3.5 percent of total).** Calculated at PMCH's federally negotiated indirect cost rate, applied to direct program costs, covering a proportional share of finance, human resources, and facilities administration under CFO Tran's office.

Mr. Mwangi's office will manage all grant funds through a dedicated program budget code, reconciled monthly against actual expenditure and reported to CEO Adebayo and the Board quarterly, with the annual report to the Trust each December including a full budget-to-actual reconciliation alongside the outcome data described in the Evaluation Plan. We have deliberately built modest revenue offsets into years three through five, rather than requesting flat annual funding, because we believe the Trust's investment should be evaluated on its trajectory toward sustainability, not merely its capacity to sustain five years of direct grant dependence.
