## 1. Executive Summary

Fairmount Software Group enters the second half of fiscal 2026 with $146 million of annual recurring revenue spread across six vertical software businesses, $310 million of outstanding debt, and a net leverage ratio of 4.61 times Adjusted EBITDA against a credit agreement ceiling of 4.75 times. The portfolio is performing unevenly. Three businesses — DentalPath, StoreVault, and VetLedger — are compounding at healthy rates and either meeting or exceeding underwriting expectations. Two businesses — RxCounter and Everlight — are contracting or facing structural threats that require near-term capital and management attention. CivicDesk sits in between: strong bookings growth masking a services delivery model that is currently unprofitable and operationally strained.

The covenant cushion is real but thin. At $9.0 million of headroom against the 4.75x ceiling, a single quarter of EBITDA softness — combined with the capital commitments this review recommends, including the $4.2 million Everlight rewrite and continued investment in the VetLedger billing migration — will compress that cushion materially. This review lays out the results and the specific problem at each business, the consolidated financial position against the covenant, a recommendation on the Everlight platform rewrite, a status report on the VetLedger integration, and management's view of the second half.

The headline for the board and the lender group is this: the portfolio-level growth story remains intact — blended ARR growth across the six businesses is running at approximately 10 percent — but two of the six units require decisions in the next two quarters that will consume cash, management bandwidth, and covenant headroom simultaneously. This review is organized to give both audiences what they need to evaluate those decisions.

## 3. Business Unit Reviews

### 3.1 DentalPath — Practice Management Software

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ARR | $38.0M |
| YoY Growth | 6% |
| Gross Retention | 91% |
| Segment | Largest business by revenue |

DentalPath remains Fairmount's largest business by revenue and its most mature. Six percent growth is down from 11 percent in fiscal 2024 and 9 percent in fiscal 2025, and the deceleration is now a two-year trend rather than a single soft quarter. Two factors are driving it. First, DentalPath's customer base skews toward smaller, independent dental practices that are increasingly being acquired by dental service organizations (DSOs) running their own standardized software stacks; when a DentalPath customer is absorbed into a DSO, Fairmount typically loses the account within twelve to eighteen months regardless of product satisfaction. Second, gross retention has slipped from 94 percent two years ago to 91 percent today, and exit interviews point to a price increase implemented in the second half of fiscal 2025 that was not accompanied by a corresponding increase in perceived product value — several churned customers cited cloud-based competitors offering comparable functionality at lower price points with more modern interfaces.

DentalPath's problem, in short, is not product quality but market structure and pricing discipline: consolidation in its end market is shrinking the addressable base of independent practices faster than the sales team can replace lost accounts, and the last price action damaged retention more than it helped revenue. The product team has begun a DSO-specific enterprise offering, expected to enter limited release in the fourth quarter, but it will not materially affect fiscal 2026 results.

### 3.2 RxCounter — Pharmacy Point-of-Sale

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ARR | $27.0M |
| YoY Growth | 0% |
| Customers, start of period | 4,100 |
| Customers lost to competitor | 340 |
| Net customer loss rate | 8.3% |

RxCounter is flat year over year, and flat is the good news relative to what the underlying customer trend implies. The business lost 340 of its 4,100 independent pharmacy customers over the trailing twelve months, essentially all of them to a single competitor that bundles point-of-sale software with wholesaler drug ordering — a workflow RxCounter does not offer natively. Independent pharmacies operate on thin margins and a bundled offering that reduces vendor count and potentially improves wholesaler pricing terms is proving difficult for RxCounter's sales and retention teams to counter on product merits alone. Revenue has held flat only because average revenue per remaining customer has increased through modest price increases and add-on module attach, masking a customer count decline that, left unaddressed, will show up as revenue contraction within two to three quarters as the price increases lap themselves.

This is RxCounter's central problem: it is losing the account, not the seat. A pharmacy that switches point-of-sale vendors switches entirely; there is no partial-churn dynamic to manage. Management has evaluated three responses: (a) build native wholesaler ordering integration, estimated at $2.8 million and nine months of engineering time; (b) pursue a partnership or reseller arrangement with an existing wholesaler ordering platform, which could be live within a single quarter but carries revenue-share economics that would compress RxCounter's margin; or (c) accept continued share loss and manage the business for cash generation rather than growth. No decision has been finalized; the product and corporate development teams will bring a recommendation to the September board meeting. In the interim, RxCounter's sales team has been directed to prioritize retention conversations with the largest 500 accounts, which represent approximately 40 percent of segment ARR.

### 3.3 CivicDesk — Municipal Permitting Software

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ARR | $22.0M |
| YoY Growth | 19% |
| Implementation backlog | 14 months |
| Services gross margin | (4%) |

CivicDesk is the fastest-growing business in the portfolio on a bookings basis and the most operationally strained. Nineteen percent growth reflects genuine market demand — municipalities are replacing legacy permitting systems at an accelerating pace, driven in part by state-level digitization mandates — but CivicDesk's implementation organization has not scaled with sales. New customers now wait an average of fourteen months between contract signature and go-live, up from six months eighteen months ago. During that wait, CivicDesk recognizes some subscription revenue but bears ongoing services cost without the customer reaching a productive state, and the services organization as a whole is running at a negative 4 percent gross margin — meaning every implementation currently in progress is losing money before it goes live.

The root cause is straightforward: CivicDesk's implementations are configuration-heavy, requiring detailed mapping of each municipality's specific permitting workflows, fee schedules, and inspection sequences, and the company has hired implementation consultants more slowly than it has signed new logos. The backlog is now a competitive liability in its own right — prospective customers are beginning to ask about the fourteen-month wait during the sales process, and at least two competitive losses this year were attributed directly to implementation timeline concerns rather than product or price.

Management's plan, already underway, is to add twelve implementation consultants in the third quarter (versus a prior plan of six), funded partly by slowing new sales hiring in the same period — effectively trading near-term bookings growth for a return to positive services margin and a shorter backlog. The product team is also building a self-service configuration toolkit intended to reduce the consultant hours required per implementation by an estimated 20 percent, targeted for release in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Until backlog normalizes, CivicDesk's reported growth rate should be read with the caveat that a meaningful share of it is not yet productive revenue in the sense that matters for cash generation.

### 3.4 StoreVault — Self-Storage Management Software

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ARR | $31.0M |
| YoY Growth | 11% |
| Embedded payments attach rate | 34% of tenants |
| Attach rate, prior year | 24% of tenants |

StoreVault is the portfolio's clearest execution success story this fiscal year. Eleven percent growth is in line with plan, and the standout metric is the embedded payments attach rate, which has risen from 24 percent to 34 percent of tenants over the trailing twelve months. Embedded payments — StoreVault processes tenant rent payments directly rather than routing them through a third-party payment processor selected by the storage facility operator — carry materially higher margin than the core subscription license and represent the single largest source of upside to the original StoreVault acquisition case.

StoreVault's problem is one of concentration and capacity rather than growth. All embedded payments volume currently routes through a single third-party payment processing partner under a contract that expires in the first quarter of fiscal 2027; the partner has indicated pricing will increase upon renewal, and StoreVault has not yet completed a competitive process to evaluate alternatives, creating renewal-timing risk against a growing and increasingly important revenue stream. Separately, the customer success and onboarding team that drives payments attach has not scaled proportionally with the growth in attach rate, and the team lead has flagged early signs of the same implementation-queue dynamic CivicDesk is experiencing, though at a much smaller scale — new customer payments onboarding now takes five weeks versus three weeks a year ago. Management is treating this as an early warning rather than a crisis: a processor RFP is underway with a decision targeted for the fourth quarter, and two additional onboarding specialists have been approved for hire in the third quarter.

## 4. The Everlight Rewrite Decision

Everlight presents the review's clearest case where inaction has a defined and approaching cost. The product runs on a third-party application framework whose vendor has formally announced end of support in March 2027 — just under eight months from the date of this review. After that date, the framework will receive no security patches, and Everlight's product will be running on infrastructure the company cannot responsibly represent as secure to funeral home customers who store sensitive, often legally regulated, records of the deceased and next of kin. This is not a theoretical risk: two of Everlight's larger customers have already asked about the company's security roadmap in the context of state-level data protection requirements applicable to funeral records.

The engineering estimate to rewrite Everlight onto a currently supported framework is $4.2 million, developed by the product organization based on a component-by-component assessment of the existing codebase. That estimate was built before Warren Sikes's departure in April, and it carries meaningful execution risk as a result. Sikes was not only the founder but the primary architect of Everlight's original implementation, and he left with two of the four engineers who had worked on the codebase for more than two years. The remaining engineering team — two developers with an average tenure under Fairmount of fourteen months — has less institutional knowledge of the system than the estimate assumed. Aaron Kleinfeld's assessment is that the $4.2 million figure should be treated as a floor rather than a point estimate, with a realistic range of $4.2 million to $5.6 million once the cost of onboarding replacement or contract engineers unfamiliar with the codebase is included, and that the nine-month timeline underlying the estimate should be read as eleven to twelve months given the same staffing gap.

Three options were evaluated:

**Option A — Fund the full rewrite.** Commit $4.2–5.6 million and eleven to twelve months to rebuild Everlight on a supported framework, retaining the current customer base and product roadmap. This preserves $16 million of ARR and the cross-sell relationships Everlight has with a handful of regional funeral service consolidators, but it is the largest single capital commitment in the portfolio relative to the size of the business generating it — a rewrite costing roughly 30 percent of Everlight's annual revenue.

**Option B — Sunset and divest.** Given the shrinking revenue base, the technical cliff, and the loss of key personnel, sell Everlight now to a strategic or financial buyer willing to take on the rewrite themselves, or wind the business down over eighteen months while migrating customers to a competitor with a referral arrangement. This avoids the capital commitment entirely and allows management attention to concentrate on the five growing businesses, but Everlight has not been formally shopped and management has no current indication of achievable sale value; a distressed, time-pressured sale process beginning this late is unlikely to realize full value.

**Option C — Migrate customers to a third-party platform.** Partner with an existing funeral home software vendor to migrate Everlight's customer base onto their platform under a revenue-share or referral arrangement, retaining a portion of the ARR without bearing rewrite risk. This has not been explored with any specific counterparty and would likely take six to nine months to negotiate and execute even before migration begins, running close to the March 2027 deadline.

**Recommendation:** Management recommends Option A, funding the rewrite, with two conditions attached. First, the board should approve a budget ceiling of $5.6 million rather than $4.2 million, reflecting the realistic range given the staffing gap, so that the project is not reported as over budget the moment contract engineering costs are incurred. Second, the company should immediately begin recruiting two senior engineers with direct experience in the target framework, treating this as the critical path item — the rewrite cannot begin in earnest until the team is rebuilt, and the current two-person engineering team should not be asked to both maintain the legacy product and lead an unfamiliar rewrite simultaneously. Rationale: Everlight, despite its decline, still generates roughly $16 million of ARR at reasonable retention outside the recent founder-driven disruption, and 3 percent revenue decline is a materially better outcome than the loss of the entire business that would follow either a failed emergency migration in early 2027 or a fire-sale divestiture. The board should treat the $5.6 million as effectively committed capital for planning purposes and should expect a project update at the September board meeting once the engineering team has been rebuilt and a firmer estimate is available.

## 5. VetLedger Integration Status

VetLedger's headline growth of 27 percent obscures an integration workstream that is materially behind plan and carries operational risk that has not yet, but could, affect customer retention. At acquisition close in November 2025, the integration plan called for VetLedger's approximately 750 practices to be migrated from VetLedger's legacy, largely manual billing process onto Fairmount's shared billing and collections platform within six months — by May 2026. As of this review, migration is four months behind that schedule, and 190 practices, roughly a quarter of the customer base, remain on manual invoicing performed by a small team that was originally slated to be reduced to a skeleton support function by the second quarter.

The delay traces to two causes. First, VetLedger's legacy billing data was less standardized than Fairmount's integration team assumed during diligence — a meaningful share of historical invoicing had been handled through practice-specific spreadsheet workarounds rather than within VetLedger's own system, complicating clean data migration. Second, the integration team was staffed assuming support from two VetLedger engineers who were expected to remain through the transition; both left within the first four months of the deal closing, for reasons related to compensation restructuring rather than the departures affecting Everlight, and their departure removed institutional knowledge of the legacy billing logic at the same time the data quality issue was discovered.

The immediate operational risk is twofold. Manual invoicing for 190 practices is labor-intensive, error-prone relative to the automated system, and not scalable as VetLedger continues to add new customers at its current growth rate — every month of delay adds new practices to the eventual migration workload rather than shrinking it. There is also latent retention risk: manual invoicing has produced a higher rate of billing errors and disputes than the automated system, and while VetLedger's gross retention has not yet shown deterioration, the customer success team has flagged an increase in billing-related support tickets among the manually invoiced cohort.

Management's revised plan targets full migration completion by the end of the third quarter of fiscal 2026, four months later than original plan but with a firmer basis than the original estimate: the data standardization work is now substantially complete, and a dedicated integration project manager was added in June with the specific mandate of closing this workstream. The $61 million acquisition price implied a return case built on VetLedger's growth trajectory and eventual integration into Fairmount's shared services infrastructure to improve unit economics; the growth case is tracking ahead of underwriting, and the integration delay, while a genuine execution miss, is not currently viewed as threatening the underlying investment thesis provided the revised third-quarter timeline holds.

## 6. Second-Half Outlook

Management's base case for the second half of fiscal 2026 assumes the following by business unit:

| Business Unit | 2H FY2026 Expectation |
|---|---|
| DentalPath | Growth stabilizes near 5–6%; no near-term retention improvement expected absent DSO product launch traction |
| RxCounter | Flat to slightly negative; customer count decline continues, pricing offset diminishes |
| CivicDesk | Bookings growth moderates to ~14–16% as sales hiring slows; services margin improves toward breakeven by Q4 as new consultants ramp |
| StoreVault | Growth holds near 11%; payments attach continues rising toward 38–40% by year-end, contingent on processor renewal outcome |
| Everlight | Revenue decline continues in the 2–4% range; rewrite project begins but does not affect FY2026 financials materially |
| VetLedger | Growth moderates modestly to ~22–24% as the business laps its strongest post-acquisition quarters; billing migration completes by Q3 |

On a consolidated basis, management expects portfolio ARR to reach approximately $151–153 million by fiscal year-end, with Adjusted EBITDA growing from $65.0 million to a range of $67–69 million, driven primarily by CivicDesk's services margin recovery and continued StoreVault payments mix shift, partially offset by RxCounter's continued decline and the initial costs of the Everlight rewrite. Under this base case, net leverage is expected to end the fiscal year in the range of 4.35x to 4.50x, restoring roughly 0.25 to 0.40 turns of covenant cushion relative to the current 4.61x, assuming no further debt paydown beyond scheduled amortization and no change in the debt balance to fund the Everlight rewrite.

This outlook carries three specific risks the board and lender group should track. First, if RxCounter's customer losses accelerate beyond the current 8.3 percent annualized rate — plausible if the competing bundled offering expands its wholesaler partnerships — the resulting revenue decline would directly compress EBITDA and could erode covenant cushion faster than CivicDesk's margin recovery restores it. Second, the CivicDesk services margin recovery depends on successfully hiring and ramping twelve implementation consultants in a specialized, in-demand skill set within a single quarter; a hiring shortfall would delay margin recovery into fiscal 2027. Third, the Everlight rewrite's realistic cost range of $4.2–5.6 million, combined with the ongoing VetLedger integration spend, will draw down available liquidity; while this does not mechanically affect the leverage covenant as defined, it does reduce the company's flexibility to absorb any additional adverse surprise, whether at RxCounter, CivicDesk, or elsewhere in the portfolio, without approaching the lender group for additional accommodation.

Management's recommendation to the board is to approve the Everlight rewrite budget as outlined in Section 4, to continue supporting the CivicDesk implementation hiring plan even though it will pressure near-term profitability further before it improves it, and to schedule a dedicated leverage and liquidity update at the September board meeting rather than waiting for the standard third-quarter review, given how little covenant headroom currently exists.

## Appendix A: Definitions and Methodology

**Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)** is calculated as the annualized value of all active subscription contracts as of the last day of the reporting period, excluding one-time implementation fees, professional services revenue, and usage-based revenue that does not recur on a predictable basis. Embedded payments revenue at StoreVault is included in ARR at the net revenue Fairmount retains after processor costs, not at gross transaction volume.

**Gross Retention** is measured as the percentage of ARR retained from the customer base existing at the start of the trailing twelve-month period, excluding any expansion revenue from upsells, seat growth, or module attach within surviving accounts. It is a downgrade-and-churn measure only. Where gross retention is shown as "implied" in Section 2.1, the figure has been estimated from customer count and revenue movement data rather than pulled directly from the billing system, because CivicDesk, StoreVault, Everlight, and VetLedger do not yet report on Fairmount's consolidated retention dashboard; DentalPath and RxCounter, the two longest-held businesses, report directly.

**Net Leverage** is defined per the credit agreement as total funded debt less unrestricted cash and cash equivalents, divided by trailing twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA as defined in the credit agreement, which permits customary add-backs for transaction costs, non-cash stock compensation, and certain one-time integration expenses. The $65.0 million Adjusted EBITDA figure used throughout this review includes approximately $2.1 million of add-backs, consisting primarily of VetLedger transaction and integration costs and severance associated with the Everlight leadership departures. Without add-backs, trailing twelve-month EBITDA on a GAAP-adjacent basis would be approximately $62.9 million, implying leverage of 4.77x — technically outside the covenant on an unadjusted basis. The board and lender group should note this distinction explicitly: compliance currently depends on permitted add-backs rather than underlying cash profitability, and the add-back pool is not unlimited under the credit agreement's stepdown schedule, which reduces permitted integration-related add-backs by 25 percent at the start of fiscal 2027.

**Services Gross Margin**, referenced in the CivicDesk section, is calculated as implementation and professional services revenue less the fully loaded cost of the implementation organization, including salaries, benefits, travel, and an allocation of shared support costs. It excludes subscription revenue and subscription cost of goods sold, which are reported separately and remain profitable across all six businesses.

Management ran three sensitivity scenarios against the second-half outlook described in Section 6 to stress-test the covenant position:

**Scenario 1 — RxCounter attrition accelerates.** If RxCounter's customer loss rate rises from the current 8.3 percent annualized pace to 12 percent, driven by further expansion of the competing bundled offering, segment ARR would decline to approximately $24.5 million by fiscal year-end rather than holding flat, reducing consolidated EBITDA by an estimated $1.3 million relative to base case. **Scenario 2 — CivicDesk hiring shortfall.** If the twelve planned implementation consultant hires are only 60 percent complete by fiscal year-end, a plausible outcome given current market scarcity for municipal software implementation talent, services margin would remain negative through year-end rather than approaching breakeven, reducing consolidated EBITDA by an estimated $1.8 million relative to base case and pushing year-end leverage toward 4.60x.

**Scenario 3 — Combined downside.** If both Scenario 1 and Scenario 2 occur simultaneously, without any offsetting upside from StoreVault or VetLedger outperformance, consolidated EBITDA would be approximately $3.1 million below base case, and year-end net leverage would land at approximately 4.72x — inside the 4.75x ceiling but with only 0.03x of headroom, effectively eliminating any margin for a further surprise in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

No scenario modeled produces a covenant breach by fiscal year-end under current assumptions. However, Scenario 3 illustrates that the combination of two individually plausible, moderate setbacks — neither of which would be surprising in isolation given each business's current trajectory — is sufficient to consume nearly all of the leverage improvement the base case otherwise projects. Management's recommendation, beyond the specific actions itemized in Section 7, is that the CFO's office prepare a contingency financing discussion with the lender group proactively rather than reactively, so that if second-half results track toward Scenario 2 or 3, the company is not initiating that conversation for the first time at or near a covenant test date.

## Appendix D: Organizational Notes

Aaron Kleinfeld's product organization has been realigned during the first half to reflect the portfolio's shifting priorities: two product managers previously supporting DentalPath's mature roadmap have been reassigned to CivicDesk's self-service configuration toolkit and to Everlight's rewrite planning, respectively. This reallocation reflects a broader principle management intends to apply through the second half — engineering and product resources will follow the businesses with the most acute near-term problems (CivicDesk's implementation burden, Everlight's technical debt, VetLedger's integration) even where that means slower roadmap progress at the more stable businesses, DentalPath and StoreVault, for the remainder of the fiscal year.

Simone Baptiste's finance organization added a dedicated FP&A analyst in the second quarter with sole responsibility for tracking the covenant calculation and the sensitivity scenarios described in Appendix C on a monthly rather than quarterly basis, reporting directly to the CFO. This role was created specifically in response to the narrowing cushion described in Section 2.2 and is expected to give management earlier visibility into any deviation from the second-half base case than the standard quarterly reporting cadence would otherwise provide.
