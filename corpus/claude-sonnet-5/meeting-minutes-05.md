## Board Committee on Quality, Safety, and Compliance
### Minutes of Meetings — February 5, March 5, April 2, and May 7, 2026

## Meeting of February 5, 2026

**Date:** February 5, 2026
**Time:** 3:00 p.m. – 4:52 p.m., Central Time
**Place:** Prairie Gate Regional Health, Administrative Building, Conference Room A, Watertown, South Dakota

**Members Present:** Dr. Ingrid Solheim, Chair; Rochelle Amankwah, Chief Nursing Officer; Dr. Vikram Sethi, Chief Medical Officer; Bethany Ojeda, Quality Director; Marcus Threadgill, Compliance Officer; Danae Lindstrom, Chief Executive Officer

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Sandra Vollmer, Risk Manager; Dr. Owen Tisdale, Medical Director, Emergency Department; Corinne Baptiste, Director of Human Resources; Faith Kowalski, Executive Assistant, recording

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Dr. Solheim called the meeting to order at 3:00 p.m. and noted that a quorum was present.

### 2. Approval of Prior Minutes
The minutes of the January 8, 2026 meeting were presented. Amankwah moved approval; Sethi seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Standing Quality Dashboard
Ojeda presented the January dashboard. Hand hygiene compliance stood at 88 percent, essentially flat month over month. Two falls with injury were reported in January, both on the medical-surgical unit. One central line-associated bloodstream infection was recorded; no catheter-associated urinary tract infections were reported. The thirty-day readmission rate was 12.4 percent. HCAHPS overall hospital rating remained at the 67th percentile nationally. Sepsis bundle compliance was reported at 51 percent, which Ojeda flagged as the dashboard's principal concern, well below the 90 percent internal target. Solheim directed that sepsis compliance be carried as a standing discussion item until it reached target.

### 4. Serious Safety Event Review
Vollmer presented the serious safety event log for January, which contained one event: the January 14 transfer of a patient in active labor from the emergency department to a receiving facility in Sioux Falls without stabilizing treatment, the subject of the federal validation survey described below. Vollmer walked the committee through the root-cause timeline: the patient presented at 1:40 a.m. with contractions, the on-call obstetrician was not contacted by the emergency physician, and the transfer was initiated at 2:55 a.m. without a documented physician certification of the risks and benefits required for an unstabilized transfer. Tisdale confirmed that the emergency physician involved has since completed remedial EMTALA training and that the department's on-call escalation protocol has been rewritten to require direct physician-to-physician contact with obstetrics before any transfer of a laboring patient. Sethi asked that the case be presented in de-identified form at the March medical staff meeting. Amankwah stated that nursing documentation gaps identified in the event — specifically the absence of a completed transfer checklist — had already been addressed through a mandatory competency module completed by all ED nursing staff as of January 30.

### 5. Survey and Correction Plan Status
Threadgill reported that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services validation survey, conducted January 12 through 16, 2026, resulted in a finding of immediate jeopardy in the emergency department tied to the transfer described above, and a condition-level finding on medication reconciliation across two nursing units. An abatement plan was submitted to the state survey agency on January 20, and a follow-up visit on January 27 confirmed that the immediate jeopardy had been removed. Threadgill presented the full plan of correction for committee review, including: revised EMTALA policy and central logging requirements; mandatory EMTALA education for all emergency department and obstetric physicians and nursing staff, to be completed by February 28; and a medication reconciliation pilot on the medical-surgical and telemetry units, with pharmacy technician support at admission and discharge, beginning February 10. Lindstrom moved to accept the plan of correction as presented and to authorize its submission to the state survey agency; Solheim seconded. The motion carried unanimously. Threadgill stated he would file the accepted plan with the state agency no later than February 12, 2026.

### 6. Sepsis Bundle Compliance Discussion
The committee returned to the 51 percent sepsis bundle compliance figure. Sethi attributed the shortfall principally to delayed lactate redraws and inconsistent antibiotic administration timing in the emergency department. Amankwah proposed assigning a dedicated nurse educator to conduct bedside coaching on the day and night shifts. Solheim asked that a physician champion be identified to pair with the educator.

### 7. New Business
None raised.

### 8. Assignments
- Sethi to identify a physician champion for the sepsis bundle initiative and report back at the March 5 meeting.
- Amankwah to assign a nurse educator to bedside sepsis coaching, effective February 9, 2026, and to report early compliance data at the March 5 meeting.
- Threadgill to file the accepted plan of correction with the state survey agency by February 12, 2026.
- Ojeda to add an EMTALA transfer log metric to the standing dashboard beginning with the March report.
- Tisdale to confirm completion of EMTALA remedial training for all ED physicians by February 28, 2026, and to report completion rates to Threadgill.

### 9. Adjournment
There being no further business, Amankwah moved to adjourn; Threadgill seconded. The motion carried unanimously, and the meeting adjourned at 4:52 p.m.

## Meeting of March 5, 2026

**Date:** March 5, 2026
**Time:** 3:00 p.m. – 5:18 p.m., Central Time
**Place:** Prairie Gate Regional Health, Administrative Building, Conference Room A, Watertown, South Dakota

**Members Present:** Dr. Ingrid Solheim, Chair; Rochelle Amankwah, Chief Nursing Officer; Bethany Ojeda, Quality Director; Marcus Threadgill, Compliance Officer

**Members Absent:** Dr. Vikram Sethi, Chief Medical Officer (attended by telephone from 3:40 p.m. to 4:10 p.m. only, due to a scheduling conflict); Danae Lindstrom, Chief Executive Officer (arrived late, at 4:05 p.m., from an off-site meeting with departing obstetric staff)

**Others Attending:** Sandra Vollmer, Risk Manager; Dr. Priya Chandrasekaran, Interim Chief, Obstetrics and Gynecology; Corinne Baptiste, Director of Human Resources; Wilhelm Radke, outside counsel, Radke & Sturm, P.C.; Faith Kowalski, Executive Assistant, recording

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Solheim called the meeting to order at 3:00 p.m. She noted that Sethi would join by telephone and that Lindstrom was expected later in the meeting, and confirmed a quorum was present among voting members in attendance.

### 2. Approval of Prior Minutes
Amankwah moved approval of the February 5, 2026 minutes; Ojeda seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Standing Quality Dashboard
Ojeda reported February results. Hand hygiene compliance rose to 90 percent. No falls with injury were reported. There were no central line-associated bloodstream infections and no catheter-associated urinary tract infections in February. The readmission rate held at 12.1 percent. Sepsis bundle compliance rose to 61 percent following the assignment of a dedicated nurse educator, which Amankwah reported had begun rounding on February 9 as directed. Sethi, by telephone, reported that Dr. Owen Tisdale had agreed to serve as physician champion for the sepsis initiative and had begun weekly chart audits. The new EMTALA transfer log metric showed zero unstabilized transfers in February.

### 4. Serious Safety Event Review
Vollmer reported no new serious safety events in February. She noted that the January obstetric transfer event remained open in the risk management system pending resolution of the associated claim, discussed later in executive session.

### 5. Survey and Correction Plan Status
Threadgill reported that the plan of correction remained under state agency review, with a revisit survey anticipated within sixty to ninety days. He then reported the results of an outside mock survey conducted February 24 through 26 by a consulting firm engaged to test readiness ahead of the anticipated revisit. The mock survey returned thirty-four findings across the hospital, including gaps in restraint documentation, expired supplies in two crash carts, incomplete infection control rounding logs, and inconsistent registered nurse competency verification for moderate sedation. Threadgill characterized none of the thirty-four findings as rising to condition-level severity but stated that the volume reflected weak survey readiness broadly, not only in the areas previously cited. Ojeda proposed a corrective action tracker with named owners and due dates for each finding, to be reviewed monthly by the committee until closed. Solheim directed that the tracker be a standing dashboard attachment beginning in April.

### 6. Obstetric Service Line Developments
Chandrasekaran reported that two of the hospital's four obstetricians had submitted resignations during the preceding week, with departure dates of April 30 and May 15, respectively. Both physicians cited, among other reasons, concerns about liability exposure following the January event and dissatisfaction with on-call burden given the department's staffing level. Baptiste reported that recruitment for obstetric coverage was underway but that replacement physicians were unlikely to be onboarded before late summer at the earliest, and that locum tenens coverage was being priced. Lindstrom, arriving at 4:05 p.m., reported that she had met that afternoon with the two departing physicians in an effort to address their concerns and that the meeting had not altered either physician's decision. The committee discussed convening a special session in advance of the April board meeting to consider options for the obstetric service line, given the near-term staffing exposure. Solheim directed that a formal recommendation be prepared for the April 2 committee meeting for referral to the full board.

### 7. Travel Nurse Spending
Amankwah reported that travel nurse spending for the year to date stood at $4.7 million against a budgeted allocation of $1.9 million, an overage attributable principally to sustained vacancy rates in the emergency department and obstetric nursing pools following the January survey and subsequent staff attrition. Lindstrom stated that finance would prepare a variance report for the board's finance committee, and that the quality committee's interest was specifically whether travel nurse reliance was affecting quality outcomes. Amankwah stated that no adverse trend had yet been observed in nursing-sensitive indicators but that she considered the current reliance level unsustainable past the second quarter.

### 8. New Business
None raised.

### 9. Assignments
- Ojeda to build a corrective action tracker for the thirty-four mock survey findings, with named owners and due dates, for review at the April 2 meeting.
- Threadgill to obtain and report an estimated date for the state agency revisit survey by the April 2 meeting.
- Baptiste to report locum tenens obstetric coverage costs and availability at the April 2 meeting.
- Chandrasekaran to prepare a summary of obstetric service line volumes and staffing scenarios for the April 2 meeting.
- Amankwah to bring a nursing vacancy and travel labor report to the April 2 meeting.

### 10. Adjournment
Threadgill moved to adjourn; Amankwah seconded. The motion carried unanimously, and the meeting adjourned at 5:18 p.m.

## Meeting of April 2, 2026

**Date:** April 2, 2026
**Time:** 3:00 p.m. – 6:12 p.m., Central Time
**Place:** Prairie Gate Regional Health, Administrative Building, Conference Room A, Watertown, South Dakota

**Members Present:** Dr. Ingrid Solheim, Chair; Rochelle Amankwah, Chief Nursing Officer; Dr. Vikram Sethi, Chief Medical Officer; Bethany Ojeda, Quality Director; Marcus Threadgill, Compliance Officer; Danae Lindstrom, Chief Executive Officer

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Sandra Vollmer, Risk Manager; Dr. Priya Chandrasekaran, Interim Chief, Obstetrics and Gynecology; Corinne Baptiste, Director of Human Resources; Wilhelm Radke, outside counsel, Radke & Sturm, P.C.; three members of the public during the public comment portion only; Faith Kowalski, Executive Assistant, recording

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Solheim called the meeting to order at 3:00 p.m. and confirmed a full quorum.

### 2. Approval of Prior Minutes
Sethi moved approval of the March 5, 2026 minutes; Ojeda seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Standing Quality Dashboard
Ojeda reported March results. Hand hygiene compliance held at 90 percent. One fall without injury was recorded on the obstetric unit; no falls with injury were reported hospital-wide. No central line-associated bloodstream infections or catheter-associated urinary tract infections were reported. Sepsis bundle compliance rose to 74 percent, which Sethi attributed to Tisdale's weekly audits and continued nurse educator coaching. Ojeda presented the corrective action tracker for the thirty-four mock survey findings: twenty-one were closed as of April 1, eleven were in progress with due dates in April, and two remained open pending vendor replacement of expired crash cart supply kits, expected by April 15.

### 4. Serious Safety Event Review
Vollmer reported no new serious safety events in March. She reported that the January transfer event's associated malpractice claim, filed by the patient's family in late March, would be addressed in executive session later in the meeting.

### 5. Survey and Correction Plan Status
Threadgill reported that the state survey agency had scheduled a revisit survey for early May, and that the medication reconciliation pilot on the two nursing units had shown improved documentation completeness, from 71 percent to 96 percent, over its first six weeks. He stated that he expected the revisit to close out the January citations if current performance held.

### 6. Public Comment
Solheim opened the floor for public comment on the obstetric service line item on the agenda. A representative of a citizen group presented a petition bearing 1,900 signatures urging the hospital to retain full labor and delivery services and expressing concern about the distance to the nearest alternative maternity unit. Two additional community members spoke in support of retaining the service. Solheim thanked the speakers and stated that their comments would be entered into the record and considered by the committee.

### 7. Obstetric Service Line Discussion
Chandrasekaran presented service line data: the department delivered 188 babies in the preceding twelve months, with two of four obstetricians departing by mid-May as previously reported. Baptiste reported that locum tenens obstetric coverage had been quoted at approximately $180,000 per month per full-time equivalent, and that two locum physicians had tentatively agreed to begin coverage in June, contingent on credentialing. Amankwah reported that nursing vacancy in the obstetric unit stood at three of eleven budgeted positions, with travel nurse usage covering the gap, and that year-to-date travel nurse spending across the hospital had risen to $4.7 million against the $1.9 million budget referenced at the prior meeting, with no material change since March. Lindstrom presented three options for the committee's consideration in its recommendation to the full board: (a) suspend labor and delivery services temporarily pending recruitment of permanent obstetric staff; (b) maintain the service line using locum tenens coverage and an accelerated permanent recruitment effort, with a ninety-day contingency review; or (c) close the labor and delivery unit permanently and establish a transfer agreement with a receiving hospital in Sioux Falls.

Extended discussion followed. Sethi stated that a temporary suspension carried its own clinical risk, since patients would still present in labor at the emergency department regardless of unit status, and that maintaining coverage through locums, however costly, preserved a functioning on-call structure. Amankwah agreed and noted the community impact reflected in the petition. Threadgill stated that closure would eliminate the immediate liability exposure associated with the service line but acknowledged the access concerns raised in public comment. Solheim called the question on a motion, made by Amankwah and seconded by Sethi, to recommend to the full board that Prairie Gate Regional Health maintain the labor and delivery service line through locum tenens coverage and accelerated permanent physician recruitment, with a mandatory ninety-day contingency review to be presented to this committee, and with immediate implementation of the revised on-call escalation and EMTALA protocols adopted in February. The vote was taken by roll call: Solheim, aye; Amankwah, aye; Sethi, aye; Ojeda, aye; Lindstrom, aye; Threadgill, nay. The motion carried five to one. Threadgill stated for the record that his dissent reflected his view that the ninety-day review period was too long given ongoing liability exposure, not disagreement with the goal of retaining the service. Solheim directed that the recommendation, including the roll call vote and Threadgill's stated basis for dissent, be forwarded to the full board for its April meeting.

### 8. Executive Session
At 5:20 p.m., on motion by Lindstrom, seconded by Solheim, and carried unanimously, the committee resolved to enter executive session pursuant to SDCL 1-25-2 for the purpose of consultation with legal counsel regarding pending litigation, specifically the malpractice claim arising from the January 14, 2026 obstetric transfer. Present in executive session were Solheim, Amankwah, Sethi, Ojeda, Lindstrom, Threadgill, Vollmer, and Radke. Kowalski left the room and did not record substantive discussion. Radke briefed the committee on the claim, filed March 26, 2026, in circuit court, and on preliminary case evaluation and reserve recommendations. The committee discussed litigation strategy and settlement posture. No votes were taken in executive session. The committee returned to open session at 6:02 p.m. Solheim reported for the record only that the committee had received a litigation update from counsel, had given direction to counsel and to risk management regarding next steps, and had taken no action requiring a public vote.

### 9. New Business
None raised.

### 10. Assignments
- Threadgill to schedule the state agency revisit survey confirmation and circulate the date to the committee within one week.
- Ojeda to close out the remaining two mock survey findings by April 15, 2026, and confirm closure to the committee in writing.
- Baptiste to finalize locum tenens obstetric contracts for June coverage and report credentialing status at the May 7 meeting.
- Chandrasekaran to prepare the first of the ninety-day contingency reviews for presentation no later than the July 2026 meeting.
- Vollmer, with Radke, to continue management of the malpractice claim and to update the committee at each regular meeting until resolution.

### 11. Adjournment
Sethi moved to adjourn; Amankwah seconded. The motion carried unanimously, and the meeting adjourned at 6:12 p.m.
