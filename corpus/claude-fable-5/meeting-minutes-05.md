**Body:** Board Committee on Quality, Safety, and Compliance, Prairie Gate Regional Health
**Date:** Thursday, February 5, 2026
**Time:** 7:00 a.m. – 9:20 a.m.
**Place:** Boardroom, Administrative Wing, Prairie Gate Regional Hospital, Watertown, South Dakota
**Members Present:** Dr. Ingrid Solheim (Chair), Gerald Voss, Anita Frerichs, Rev. Calvin Odell
**Members Absent:** Lucinda Barrows-Teel (excused)
**Others Attending:** Danae Lindstrom, Chief Executive Officer; Dr. Vikram Sethi, Chief Medical Officer; Rochelle Amankwah, Chief Nursing Officer; Bethany Ojeda, Quality Director; Marcus Threadgill, Compliance Officer; Sharon Kepley, Recording Secretary

---

**1. Call to Order and Quorum.** Dr. Solheim called the meeting to order at 7:00 a.m. and confirmed that a quorum was present. She noted that Ms. Barrows-Teel had given advance notice of her absence and had been excused.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes.** The minutes of the January 8, 2026 regular meeting were presented. Mr. Voss moved to approve the minutes as written; Rev. Odell seconded. The motion carried 4–0.

**3. Conflict of Interest Declarations.** Dr. Solheim asked whether any member had a conflict with any item on the agenda. No conflicts were declared.

**4. Standing Quality Dashboard.** Ms. Ojeda presented the monthly quality dashboard covering December 2025 performance:

- **Sepsis bundle compliance:** 51 percent, against a target of 85 percent and a prior-quarter average of 58 percent. Ms. Ojeda attributed the decline to emergency department staffing turnover and inconsistent lactate reordering within the three-hour window.
- **Hospital-acquired infections:** one catheter-associated urinary tract infection; zero central line infections; one *C. difficile* case under review.
- **Falls with injury:** two, both on the medical-surgical unit, both on night shift.
- **Thirty-day all-cause readmissions:** 14.9 percent, within target.
- **Medication reconciliation completion at discharge:** 72 percent, flagged red in light of the federal citation discussed under Item 6.
- **Patient experience (HCAHPS overall rating):** 68th percentile.

Dr. Solheim stated that the sepsis figure was unacceptable and asked for a corrective trajectory. Ms. Ojeda committed to presenting a sepsis bundle improvement plan with monthly milestones at the March 5 meeting. Dr. Sethi agreed to co-sponsor the plan with the emergency department medical director.

**5. Serious Safety Event Review.** Ms. Amankwah reported that one event met the serious safety event definition since the prior meeting: the January 14, 2026 transfer of a patient in active labor from the emergency department to a facility in Sioux Falls without documented stabilization or an accepting physician confirmed in the record. The root cause analysis, completed January 28, identified three causal factors: absence of an on-call obstetric coverage grid posted in the emergency department, an outdated transfer policy that did not reflect current federal requirements, and inadequate triage training for two recently hired emergency nurses. The patient delivered en route; mother and infant were reported stable at the receiving facility. Mr. Threadgill confirmed the event had been reported to the state as required. The committee discussed the case in detail. Rev. Odell asked whether the family had been contacted through the disclosure process; Ms. Amankwah confirmed that a disclosure conversation had occurred on January 16 with Dr. Sethi present.

**6. Federal Validation Survey — Immediate Jeopardy and Plan of Correction.** Mr. Threadgill reviewed the January 2026 federal validation survey. Surveyors had cited immediate jeopardy in the emergency department arising from the January 14 transfer, together with a condition-level deficiency on medication reconciliation. He reported that the immediate jeopardy was abated on-site on January 22 following implementation of a removal plan that included: immediate revision and staff attestation of the transfer and stabilization policy; posting of a 24/7 obstetric on-call schedule in the emergency department; mandatory education for all emergency department physicians, advanced practice providers, and nurses, completed January 21 at 100 percent; and institution of a transfer log audited daily by the nursing supervisor. The abatement was verified by surveyors before their departure.

Mr. Threadgill then presented the full plan of correction submitted to the federal agency on February 2, addressing both the emergency department findings and the medication reconciliation condition. Key elements included a pharmacist-led reconciliation redesign, an electronic hard-stop at discharge effective March 1, and weekly audits of 30 charts reported to Ms. Ojeda.

Ms. Frerichs moved that the committee accept the plan of correction as submitted and direct management to report implementation status at each meeting until the revisit survey closed the citations; Mr. Voss seconded. The motion carried 4–0.

**Action items assigned:**
- Ms. Ojeda: sepsis bundle improvement plan due at the March 5, 2026 meeting.
- Mr. Threadgill: weekly written status memoranda on the plan of correction to committee members, beginning February 13, 2026.
- Ms. Amankwah: report on transfer log audit results and any deviations at the March 5, 2026 meeting.
- Dr. Sethi: confirm obstetric on-call coverage adequacy in writing to the Chair by February 20, 2026.

**7. Compliance Report.** Mr. Threadgill reported that the compliance hotline received three contacts in January, none substantiated as compliance violations; one was referred to Human Resources. Annual compliance training completion stood at 91 percent, with completion required by February 28.

**8. Executive Session.** At 8:55 a.m., Mr. Voss moved to enter executive session to discuss matters of potential litigation and peer review protected under state law, with Ms. Lindstrom, Dr. Sethi, and Mr. Threadgill remaining; Rev. Odell seconded. The motion carried 4–0. The committee entered executive session at 8:57 a.m. and returned to open session at 9:15 a.m. Dr. Solheim reported that no action was taken in executive session.

**9. Adjournment.** There being no further business, Ms. Frerichs moved to adjourn; Mr. Voss seconded. The motion carried 4–0, and the meeting adjourned at 9:20 a.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Sharon Kepley, Recording Secretary

**1. Call to Order and Quorum.** Dr. Solheim called the meeting to order at 7:00 a.m. and confirmed a quorum with all five members present.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes.** The minutes of February 5, 2026 were presented. Rev. Odell moved approval; Ms. Barrows-Teel seconded. The motion carried 5–0.

**3. Conflict of Interest Declarations.** No conflicts were declared.

**4. Standing Quality Dashboard.** Ms. Ojeda presented January 2026 performance:

- **Sepsis bundle compliance:** 56 percent, a five-point improvement, still well below the 85 percent target. She then presented the sepsis bundle improvement plan requested on February 5, including an automated sepsis screening alert (go-live March 16), a dedicated sepsis coordinator role (0.5 FTE, posted), physician-specific compliance feedback beginning in April, and monthly milestones targeting 65 percent by April, 72 percent by June, and 85 percent by September.
- **Medication reconciliation completion:** 81 percent, up from 72 percent, ahead of the electronic hard-stop go-live on March 1.
- **Hospital-acquired infections:** zero for January.
- **Falls with injury:** one, ambulatory surgery unit.
- **Readmissions:** 15.4 percent, at threshold.
- **HCAHPS overall:** 66th percentile.

Ms. Barrows-Teel moved to accept the sepsis improvement plan and direct that any month falling short of milestone by more than five points be escalated to the Chair within five business days; Mr. Voss seconded. The motion carried 5–0.

**5. Serious Safety Event Review.** Ms. Amankwah reported no new serious safety events since February 5. She reported on the transfer log audit as assigned: 41 transfers occurred between January 22 and February 28; all 41 had documented stabilization assessments and confirmed accepting physicians. Two documentation timeliness deviations were identified and coached individually. Dr. Sethi confirmed he had delivered his written obstetric on-call coverage memorandum to the Chair on February 19, one day ahead of deadline, noting coverage was adequate at that date. The committee received both reports without objection.

**6. Plan of Correction Status.** Mr. Threadgill reported that all February corrective actions were completed on schedule. The medication reconciliation electronic hard-stop went live March 1; first-week reconciliation completion measured 94 percent. Weekly 30-chart audits showed sustained compliance in the emergency department transfer elements. He stated management anticipated requesting a revisit survey in April, once 60 days of sustained audit data were in hand.

**7. External Mock Survey Results.** Mr. Threadgill presented the report of Meridian Compliance Partners, engaged to conduct a mock survey February 23–26. The mock survey returned 34 findings: 3 rated high risk (emergency preparedness annual exercise documentation, ligature risk assessment gaps in two patient bathrooms, and expired supplies in a clinic crash cart), 12 moderate, and 19 low. He distributed a finding-by-finding remediation matrix with owners and dates. Dr. Solheim asked that the three high-risk findings be closed within 30 days. Ms. Frerichs moved that the committee accept the mock survey report, direct closure of all high-risk findings by April 6, 2026, moderate findings by May 29, 2026, and low-risk findings by June 30, 2026, with status reported at each meeting; Rev. Odell seconded. The motion carried 5–0.

**8. Workforce and Obstetrics Service Line Report.** Ms. Lindstrom reported that two of the hospital's four obstetricians, Dr. Emily Rasgorshek and Dr. Thomas Okafor, had given notice effective May 31 and June 30, 2026, respectively. Recruitment had been open since February 10 with no candidates advanced to interview. Mr. Braaten reported that travel nurse spending stood at $4.7 million fiscal year to date against a $1.9 million annual budget, driven principally by emergency department and obstetric unit vacancies; the vacancy rate for registered nurses was 17 percent. Ms. Lindstrom stated that management would bring a formal analysis of obstetrics service line options — sustain, restructure with certified nurse midwives and telehealth backup, or planned closure with transfer agreements — to the April 2 meeting, together with volume, financial, workforce, and community access data. She noted the service delivered 188 babies in the prior calendar year.

Committee members discussed the report at length. Rev. Odell emphasized the access implications for a rural service area with the nearest alternative delivery hospital more than 90 miles away. Ms. Barrows-Teel asked that the analysis include patient safety data comparing outcomes at low-volume obstetric programs. Dr. Sethi agreed to include that literature.

**Action items assigned:**
- Ms. Lindstrom: obstetrics service line options analysis due at the April 2, 2026 meeting.
- Mr. Braaten: travel nurse spending mitigation plan due at the April 2, 2026 meeting.
- Dr. Sethi: low-volume obstetrics safety literature summary due at the April 2, 2026 meeting.

**9. Compliance Report.** Mr. Threadgill reported annual compliance training closed at 97 percent completion, with the remaining employees on leave and scheduled upon return. Two hotline contacts in February were reviewed and closed without substantiated findings.

**10. Executive Session.** At 9:20 a.m., Ms. Frerichs moved to enter executive session to discuss potential litigation arising from the January 14, 2026 event, with legal counsel Deborah Skoglund attending by telephone and Ms. Lindstrom, Dr. Sethi, and Mr. Threadgill remaining; Mr. Voss seconded. **11. Adjournment.** Mr. Voss moved to adjourn; Ms. Barrows-Teel seconded. The motion carried 5–0, and the meeting adjourned at 9:45 a.m.

**4. Standing Quality Dashboard.** Ms. Ojeda presented February 2026 performance:

- **Sepsis bundle compliance:** 63 percent, two points below the April milestone of 65 percent but within the five-point escalation band adopted March 5. The screening alert went live March 16 as scheduled; the sepsis coordinator position had been filled with a start date of April 13.
- **Medication reconciliation completion:** 95 percent, sustained above 90 percent for four consecutive weeks.
- **Hospital-acquired infections:** one central line-associated bloodstream infection in the intensive care unit; a focused review was underway with results due at the May meeting.
- **Falls with injury:** zero.

The committee received the dashboard. Dr. Solheim assigned Ms. Ojeda to present the central line infection review findings at the May 7, 2026 meeting.

**5. Serious Safety Event Review.** Ms. Amankwah reported one new event under evaluation: a March 21 wrong-site regional anesthetic block in the surgical suite, halted before incision when the timeout process identified the error; the block resolved without lasting harm. The event was classified as a precursor safety event with serious potential. A root cause analysis was scheduled for April 9. The committee directed that the completed analysis and corrective actions be presented at the May 7 meeting. Ms. Amankwah also confirmed she had delivered the obstetric nurse staffing contingency plan to the Chair on March 19, ahead of the March 20 deadline.

**6. Plan of Correction and Mock Survey Status.** Mr. Threadgill reported that management submitted its readiness attestation to the federal agency on March 27 and requested the revisit survey; the agency indicated the revisit would occur unannounced within 45 days. All emergency department and medication reconciliation audit metrics remained at or above compliance thresholds for eight consecutive weeks. On the mock survey, he reported all three high-risk findings closed as of March 31 — ahead of the April 6 deadline — including completed ligature risk remediation, a documented emergency preparedness exercise conducted March 24, and full crash cart inventory replacement with a monthly check protocol. Eight of twelve moderate findings were closed; the remainder were on track for May 29.

**7. Obstetrics Service Line — Options Analysis and Committee Recommendation.** Ms. Lindstrom presented the analysis requested on March 5. She reviewed: (a) volume of 188 deliveries in calendar 2025, trending flat over five years; (b) a projected physician complement of two obstetricians after June 30, below the minimum of three required for sustainable call coverage; (c) a service line operating loss of $2.1 million annually before allocation of travel nurse premium costs; (d) recruitment pipeline status, with one locum tenens candidate available beginning June 1 and two permanent candidates in early screening; and (e) community access data showing the nearest alternative delivery hospital 92 miles away in Sioux Falls, with winter transport risk documented by the ambulance service. Dr. Sethi presented the requested literature summary on low-volume obstetric program safety, noting evidence of elevated risk below approximately 200 annual deliveries when staffing is unstable, but also documented harms associated with rural obstetric unit closures, including increased out-of-hospital births and preterm outcomes.

Mr. Braaten presented three financial scenarios and the travel nurse mitigation plan assigned March 5, which included conversion bonuses that had yielded four travel-to-permanent conversions in March, a projected reduction of travel spending to a $2.8 million annualized run rate by the fourth quarter, and a new float pool.

Ms. Lindstrom presented management's recommendation: **sustain the obstetrics service line for a defined 18-month stabilization period**, supported by (1) locum tenens coverage beginning June 1, 2026; (2) an accelerated recruitment package for two permanent obstetricians and two certified nurse midwives, funded at up to $1.4 million; (3) a formal telehealth maternal-fetal medicine consultation agreement with a Sioux Falls health system; and (4) quarterly viability reviews by this committee against defined volume, staffing, and safety thresholds, with a mandatory reassessment at the January 2027 meeting.

The committee entered extended discussion. Dr. Solheim read into the record a community petition bearing 1,900 signatures, delivered to the board on March 26, 2026, urging continuation of obstetric services in Watertown; the petition was appended to the meeting file. Rev. Odell spoke in support of the stabilization approach. Ms. Barrows-Teel asked that the quarterly viability thresholds be quantified before the full board vote; Ms. Lindstrom agreed to circulate proposed thresholds by April 10, 2026.

Ms. Frerichs moved that the committee recommend to the full Board of Directors adoption of the 18-month obstetrics stabilization plan as presented, inclusive of the four supporting elements and quarterled viability reviews, with quantified thresholds to be ratified by this committee at its May 7, 2026 meeting; Rev. Odell seconded. The motion carried 4–0, with Mr. Voss abstaining consistent with his disclosure. Dr. Solheim stated she would present the recommendation at the full board meeting of April 16, 2026.

**8. **1. Call to Order and Quorum.** Dr. Solheim called the meeting to order at 7:00 a.m. and confirmed a quorum with four members present. Rev. Odell had been excused in advance.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes.** The minutes of April 2, 2026 were presented. Ms. Frerichs moved approval; Mr. Voss seconded. The motion carried 4–0.

**3. Conflict of Interest Declarations.** No conflicts were declared. Mr. Voss noted his prior disclosure regarding the obstetrics service line remained on file; because Item 7 involved threshold ratification for that service line, he stated he would again abstain from the vote.

**4. Standing Quality Dashboard.** Ms. Ojeda presented March 2026 performance:

- **Sepsis bundle compliance:** 68 percent, exceeding the April milestone of 65 percent. The sepsis coordinator began April 13; early lactate reorder compliance rose to 84 percent.
- **Medication reconciliation completion:** 96 percent, ten consecutive weeks above 90 percent.
- **Hospital-acquired infections:** zero new infections in March. Ms. Ojeda presented the central line infection review assigned April 2: the review identified a dressing change protocol deviation; corrective actions included skills revalidation for all intensive care nurses, completed April 24, and addition of a two-nurse dressing change standard.
- **Falls with injury:** one, emergency department, under review.
- **Readmissions:** 13.2 percent.
- **HCAHPS overall:** 73rd percentile.

The committee received the dashboard and the infection review report. Dr. Solheim commended the sepsis trajectory and directed continued monthly milestone reporting through September.

**5. Serious Safety Event Review.** Ms. Amankwah presented the completed root cause analysis of the March 21 wrong-site regional block, as assigned. Causal factors included block administration occurring before the surgical timeout and inconsistent site-marking visibility after draping. Corrective actions implemented: a mandatory separate pre-block timeout effective April 15, anesthesia-specific site verification checklist, and observational audits of 20 consecutive cases, all 20 compliant as of May 1. Ms. Barrows-Teel moved to accept the root cause analysis and corrective action plan, with a sustainment audit report due at the August 6, 2026 meeting; Ms. Frerichs seconded. The motion carried 4–0. No new serious safety events were reported for the period.

**6. Revisit Survey — Closure of Citations.** Mr. Threadgill reported that the unannounced federal revisit survey occurred April 21–22, 2026. Surveyors verified full implementation of the plan of correction and, by written notice dated April 29, 2026, found the facility in substantial compliance, closing both the emergency department deficiency and the medication reconciliation condition-level finding. He reported that ten of twelve moderate mock survey findings and eleven of nineteen low-risk findings were now closed, with the remainder on track for the May 29 and June 30 deadlines respectively. Mr. Voss moved that the committee formally acknowledge closure of the federal citations and direct that the audit cadence for transfers and medication reconciliation continue at reduced frequency — monthly rather than weekly — through December 2026, with any metric falling below threshold reported to the Chair within five business days; Ms. Barrows-Teel seconded. The motion carried 4–0. Dr. Solheim, on behalf of the committee, recorded thanks to the staff of the emergency department, pharmacy, and quality office.

**7. Obstetrics Service Line — Threshold Ratification and Update.** Ms. Lindstrom reported that the full Board of Directors, at its April 16, 2026 meeting, adopted the committee's recommended 18-month stabilization plan by a vote of 9–1. As assigned, she had circulated quantified viability thresholds to members on April 9, 2026. The thresholds presented for ratification were: (a) minimum 40 deliveries per rolling quarter; (b) a physician-plus-midwife complement sufficient for continuous call coverage without single-provider stretches exceeding 72 hours; (c) no more than one preventable serious safety event in the service line per rolling 12 months; and (d) travel nurse premium costs in the obstetric unit not exceeding $450,000 per quarter after October 1, 2026. Breach of any threshold for two consecutive quarters would trigger a mandatory service line reassessment by this committee. She additionally reported that the locum tenens obstetrician was confirmed to begin June 1, one permanent obstetrician candidate had completed a site visit on April 28 with an offer extended May 4, and the telehealth maternal-fetal medicine agreement was executed April 30.

Ms. Frerichs moved to ratify the four quarterly viability thresholds as presented and to place the first quarterly viability review on the September 3, 2026 agenda; Ms. Barrows-Teel seconded. The motion carried 3–0, with Mr. Voss abstaining.

**8. Financial and Workforce Report.** Mr. Braaten delivered the first monthly travel nurse run-rate report assigned April 2: April spending annualized to $3.6 million, down from the $4.7 million pace, with six additional travel-to-permanent conversions signed and the float pool launching June 8. Registered nurse vacancy stood at 14 percent, down from 17 percent. The committee received the report and directed continuation of monthly reporting.

**9. Committee Charter Revision and Reporting Calendar.** Dr. Solheim presented a proposed revised committee charter, drafted with counsel following the survey cycle. She also presented a 12-month reporting calendar fixing the months in which credentialing reports, infection control plans, compliance work plan reviews, patient experience deep-dives, and the obstetrics quarterly viability reviews would recur. Ms. Barrows-Teel moved to adopt the revised charter and the annual reporting calendar and to forward both to the full board for ratification at its May 21, 2026 meeting; Ms. Frerichs seconded.
