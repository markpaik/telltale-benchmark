## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the requirements and steps for controlling hazardous energy at Teton Fabrication Works so that machines and equipment are isolated from all energy sources and rendered inoperative before employees perform servicing, maintenance, die changes, jam clearing, or repair work where the unexpected energization, start-up, or release of stored energy could cause injury. This procedure implements corrective actions required under the informal settlement agreement following the March 2026 incident, including machine-specific written energy control procedures for all 148 pieces of equipment, documented periodic inspections, and closure of gaps identified in the subsequent internal audit (61 missing or outdated procedures, three lock boxes with unaccounted keys, and absent periodic inspection records for both shifts over the prior two years).

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all Teton Fabrication Works employees, supervisors, maintenance technicians, and contractors who work on, service, or are affected by equipment on the production floor, including press brakes, fiber lasers, robotic weld cells, the powder coat line, overhead conveyor systems, and associated ancillary equipment. It applies to all energy sources present on site, including 480 volt electrical circuits, pneumatic systems operating at approximately 110 psi, hydraulic accumulators capable of retaining pressure after shutdown, and gravity energy present in overhead conveyor sections and suspended loads. It applies across both production shifts and to all 148 machines listed in the master equipment inventory maintained in the maintenance system. This procedure does not apply to hot tap operations, cord-and-plug equipment under exclusive employee control (Section 12.2), or normal production operating controls that do not expose an employee to contact with hazardous energy, except as those situations are addressed in the exceptions section below.

## 3. Definitions

**Affected Employee** — An employee whose job requires operating a machine on which servicing is performed, or whose job requires working in an area where servicing is performed, but who does not perform lockout/tagout.

**Authorized Employee** — An employee who has completed training under this procedure and is designated to perform lockout/tagout on specific equipment.

**Energy-Isolating Device** — A mechanical device that physically prevents transmission or release of energy, including a manually operated electrical disconnect, a line valve, a block, or a blocking pin.

**Group Lockout** — Use of a group lockout box, hasp, or comparable device allowing multiple authorized employees to place individual locks so that no single employee's removal of a lock re-energizes equipment while others are still exposed.

**Lockout Device** — A device that uses a positive means, such as a lock, to hold an energy-isolating device in a safe position.

**Minor Servicing Exception** — Routine, repetitive, and integral activity to production, such as clearing a jam on a fiber laser, that may be performed without full lockout provided alternative measures under Section 12.3 are used.

**Normal Production Operations** — The utilization of a machine to perform its intended production function.

**Servicing and/or Maintenance** — Activities such as die changes, adjusting, lubricating, cleaning, repairing, and unjamming machines, including activities where an employee could be exposed to unexpected energization or release of stored energy.

**Tagout Device** — A prominent warning tag securely attached to an energy-isolating device to indicate it may not be operated until the tag is removed by the employee who applied it.

**Zero Energy State** — Confirmation, by direct test or verified indicator, that all forms of energy at a machine have been isolated and dissipated to a level that cannot cause injury.

## 5. Machine-Specific Procedure Development, Content, and Revision

5.1 Every piece of equipment in the 148-item master equipment inventory shall have a written, machine-specific energy control procedure before it may be serviced under lockout. No machine may be added to production use without a completed and approved procedure.

5.2 A maintenance technician, in consultation with the shift supervisor for that area, drafts the machine-specific procedure using the standard template stored in the maintenance system. The template requires, at minimum: machine ID and location, a diagram or photo of each energy-isolating device, the specific lockout device and hasp required for each point, the sequence of shutdown steps, the method to verify zero energy for each energy type present (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, gravity), and restart steps.

5.3 Ruthann Begay reviews each draft procedure for completeness against the template and confirms all energy sources for that machine are identified, including energy sources that are not obvious from the main disconnect, such as hydraulic accumulators or gravity-loaded conveyor sections.

5.4 Darius Whitfield or his designee gives final sign-off before the procedure is uploaded to the maintenance system and a laminated copy is posted at the machine.

5.5 Every machine-specific procedure is reviewed at minimum annually, coinciding with the annual inspection in Section 10, and immediately upon any of the following: a modification to the machine's energy sources or isolation points, an incident or near miss involving that machine, or discovery during an audit that the procedure is missing, outdated, or inconsistent with the machine as installed.

5.6 As of the effective date of this revision, the 61 procedures identified in the internal audit as missing or outdated are designated priority items. Maintenance shall complete and post a compliant procedure for each within 60 days, tracked weekly by Ruthann Begay, with status reported to Darius Whitfield until closure.

## 6. Notification and Preparation for Shutdown

6.1 Before lockout begins, the authorized employee notifies the machine operator and the shift supervisor that the machine will be taken out of service, the reason (die change, repair, jam clearance, scheduled maintenance), and the expected duration.

6.2 The authorized employee reviews the posted machine-specific procedure for that piece of equipment, including all listed energy sources, isolation points, and required lockout devices.

6.3 If the machine is running, the operator brings it to normal operating stop using the standard shutdown controls before de-energization begins. Materials in process, dies, or workpieces creating a pinch or crush hazard on shutdown are cleared or supported before further steps proceed.

## 7. Isolation of Energy Sources

7.1 The authorized employee isolates every energy source identified in the machine-specific procedure, in the sequence stated in that procedure. At minimum, this includes:

- **Electrical (480V):** Open the machine's main disconnect and verify it is in the off position.
- **Pneumatic (110 psi):** Close the machine's pneumatic supply isolation valve and bleed the line at the designated bleed port until the line pressure gauge reads zero.
- **Hydraulic (accumulator-equipped systems):** Close the hydraulic isolation valve and cycle the manual accumulator bleed-down valve per the machine-specific procedure; do not rely on the absence of pump operation as an indicator, since accumulators retain pressure after shutdown.
- **Gravity (overhead conveyor sections and suspended loads):** Engage the mechanical blocking pin, prop, or chain specified in the machine-specific procedure to prevent movement of any suspended or elevated component; do not rely on brakes alone.

7.2 If a machine-specific procedure identifies an energy source not listed above, the authorized employee follows the isolation steps as written in that procedure.

## 8. Lockout and Tagout Device Application

8.1 After isolating each energy source, the authorized employee applies their own personal lock and a tag to each energy-isolating device. Each lock is uniquely keyed to that employee; no employee applies another employee's lock except as provided in Section 11 (shift change) and Section 12.1 (removal of an absent employee's lock).

8.2 The tag includes the employee's name, date, and reason for lockout, and states "Do Not Operate."

8.3 Where an isolating device cannot directly accept a lock, the authorized employee installs the appropriate hasp, chain, or blocking device specified in the machine-specific procedure before applying the lock.

8.4 Group lockout boxes are used per Section 9 when more than one authorized employee will work on the same machine.

## 9. Verification of Zero Energy State

9.1 After all locks and tags are applied, the authorized employee verifies isolation is effective before beginning work. This step is mandatory and is not satisfied by visual inspection of the disconnect alone.

9.2 Verification steps, at minimum:

- **Electrical:** Attempt to start the machine using the normal operator controls; the machine must not start. Return controls to the off/neutral position afterward. Where accessible and required by the machine-specific procedure, use a voltage tester at the load side of the disconnect to confirm absence of voltage.
- **Pneumatic:** Confirm the line pressure gauge at the point of work reads zero and manually attempt to actuate the component (e.g., cylinder) using normal controls to confirm no residual pressure moves it.
- **Hydraulic:** Confirm the accumulator pressure gauge reads zero after the bleed-down step; do not proceed if any residual pressure is indicated.
- **Gravity:** Visually confirm the mechanical block, pin, or prop is fully engaged and load-bearing before positioning any body part beneath or within range of the suspended component.

9.3 If verification indicates any energy is still present, the authorized employee stops, re-isolates the source, and repeats verification before proceeding. Work does not begin until zero energy state is confirmed for every energy source present.

## 10. Servicing, Release from Lockout, and Restart

10.1 Only authorized employees whose locks are on the equipment perform servicing, die changes, or repair work on that equipment.

10.2 Before release from lockout, the authorized employee: removes tools and materials from the machine, confirms all guards are reinstalled, confirms all employees are clear of the machine and hazardous areas, and notifies affected employees that lockout is about to be removed.

10.3 Each authorized employee removes their own lock and tag only, in reverse order of application, and only after confirming with any other authorized employees on the same equipment that all locks may be safely removed.

10.4 The authorized employee reactivates energy-isolating devices per the machine-specific restart sequence and confirms normal, controlled operation before returning the machine to the operator.

10.5 The operator confirms the machine is functioning correctly before resuming normal production.

## 11. Shift Change and Continuity of Lockout

11.1 When work under lockout is not complete at the end of a shift, the outgoing authorized employee does not remove their lock without first coordinating a lock transfer with the incoming authorized employee and shift supervisor.

11.2 Lock transfer sequence: the incoming authorized employee applies their own lock and tag to each isolating device already locked out. Only after the incoming lock is confirmed in place does the outgoing employee remove their lock. At no point are all locks removed from an isolating device simultaneously while work remains incomplete.

11.3 The shift supervisor on both the outgoing and incoming shift verifies and initials the shift-change log in the maintenance system confirming lock transfer was completed and that the machine-specific procedure remains posted and unchanged.

11.4 This lock transfer requirement directly addresses the audit finding of no documented periodic inspection or shift-to-shift continuity record for either shift over the prior two years; the shift-change log is retained as a compliance record under Section 14.

## 12. Group Lockout

12.1 When two or more authorized employees are required to service the same machine, or when multiple crafts (for example, electrical and mechanical maintenance) work on the same equipment, a group lockout box is used.

12.2 The first authorized employee isolates all energy sources and applies a group lockout hasp to each isolating device, then places the keys for those locks inside the designated group lockout box and applies a personal lock to the box.

12.3 Each additional authorized employee joining the job applies their own personal lock to the group lockout box before beginning work. No employee may open the box or access a device key without their own lock in place on the box.

12.4 As work is completed, each employee removes their lock from the box in turn. Energy-isolating devices are not reactivated, and box keys are not removed, until every employee's lock has been removed from the box.

12.5 Given the audit finding of three lock boxes with unaccounted keys, Ruthann Begay conducts a physical key audit of every group lockout box quarterly, reconciling the number of keys inside each box against the master key log in the maintenance system. Any discrepancy is investigated and closed within 5 business days, with the box removed from service until resolved.

## 13. Annual Inspection

13.1 Ruthann Begay, or a maintenance technician she designates who is not the primary user of the machine-specific procedure being reviewed, conducts a documented periodic inspection of the energy control procedure for every one of the 148 machines at least once per calendar year.

13.2 The inspection includes observing an authorized employee perform an actual lockout on that machine, reviewing the observed sequence against the written machine-specific procedure, interviewing the authorized employee on their responsibilities under this procedure, and confirming with the authorized employee that they can identify all energy sources present on the machine.

13.3 The inspection is conducted separately for first shift and second shift operations for every machine that is used on both shifts, so that shift-specific practices are independently verified rather than assumed.

13.4 Findings are documented on the standard inspection form in the maintenance system, including any deviation observed, corrective action assigned, owner, and due date. Deviations are closed and verified before the next scheduled inspection.

13.5 A rolling schedule is maintained in the maintenance system showing due dates for all 148 inspections across both shifts; Ruthann Begay reviews the schedule monthly and reports completion status to Darius Whitfield until the program reaches steady-state compliance.

## 14. Training and Authorization Records

14.1 The training coordinator maintains records for three employee categories: authorized employees, affected employees, and shift supervisors, with distinct training content for each.

14.2 Authorized employee training covers recognition of hazardous energy sources, the specific isolation and verification steps in this procedure, hands-on demonstration on at least one representative machine per equipment type (press brake, laser, robotic weld cell, powder coat line, conveyor), and the exceptions in Section 15. Authorized employee status is not granted in the maintenance system until this training is documented as complete and a supervisor has observed a successful practical demonstration.

14.3 Affected employee training covers recognition of lockout devices, the prohibition on operating locked-out equipment, and reporting requirements if a lockout appears bypassed or improperly applied.

14.4 Retraining is conducted whenever: a new energy source or machine is introduced, a periodic inspection identifies a deviation involving that employee, a job assignment changes, or this procedure is revised in a way that changes an employee's duties.

14.5 The training coordinator issues a current authorized employee list to Ruthann Begay quarterly, cross-checked against maintenance system access permissions so that only currently authorized employees can create or close a lockout work order.

## 15. Exceptions

**15.1 Removal of an Absent Employee's Lock.** If an authorized employee leaves the facility without removing their lock, only the shift supervisor, together with Ruthann Begay or her designee, may authorize removal. Before removal: verify by phone or other direct contact, where feasible, that the employee is not exposed to the equipment; if contact cannot be made, physically inspect the machine and surrounding area to confirm no one is present in a hazardous position; document the removal on the incident/exception log, including names, time, and justification; and ensure the employee is informed before their next shift that their lock was removed and why.

**15.2 Cord-and-Plug Equipment.** Equipment connected by a cord and plug, where the plug is under the exclusive control of the employee performing the servicing, may be de-energized by unplugging rather than full lockout, provided the plug remains in the employee's direct control or view at all times during the work.

**15.3 Minor Servicing.** Routine, repetitive tasks integral to production, such as clearing a jam on a fiber laser cutting head, may be performed without full lockout only when all of the following apply: the task is listed as a qualifying minor servicing task in the machine-specific procedure; alternative protective measures specified in that procedure are used (for example, a laser interlock gate, a two-hand anti-tie-down control, or a designated safe-clearing mode that prevents the beam or motion source from activating); and the employee has been trained specifically on that alternative measure. If the qualifying conditions are not met, full lockout under Sections 7 through 9 is required, with no exception.

**15.4 Contractor Coordination.** Before an outside contractor, including the conveyor vendor working on the powder coat line, begins any work involving hazardous energy, the Teton maintenance lead and the contractor's supervisor exchange information about each party's energy control procedures. The contractor either uses their own lockout devices, distinguishable from Teton's by color or tag, or is issued Teton devices and trained on this procedure before work begins. The Teton maintenance lead verifies isolation and zero energy state jointly with the contractor before work starts and confirms restart readiness jointly before re-energizing.

**15.5 Testing Requiring Temporary Re-Energization.** When troubleshooting or repair requires the machine to be temporarily re-energized for testing (for example, to verify a repaired sensor or drive), the authorized employee follows this sequence: clear tools, materials, and personnel from the point of operation; remove locks and tags only long enough to perform the specific test; re-isolate and re-apply locks and tags for each energy source immediately after the test is complete; and repeat zero energy verification under Section 9 before resuming servicing. A machine is never left running unattended or handed back to production status during a testing sequence; it returns to full lockout for continued service work.

## Appendix A: Machine-Specific Procedure Template Fields

Each of the 148 machine-specific procedures required under Section 5 is built from the following standardized template fields, maintained as a fillable form in the maintenance system so that new procedures remain consistent in format and completeness:

- Machine ID / Asset Number (matches maintenance system tag)
- Machine Description and Location (building zone, line, bay)
- Equipment Type (press brake, fiber laser, robotic weld cell, powder coat line component, conveyor section, ancillary equipment)
- Energy Types Present (checklist: electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, gravity, other — with a free-text field for any energy source not covered by the standard checklist)
- Primary Disconnect/Isolation Point Location and Photo
- Secondary or Downstream Isolation Points (for example, a local disconnect in addition to the main breaker, or a branch pneumatic valve in addition to the main air supply valve)
- Required Lockout Hardware per Isolation Point (lock type, hasp, chain, blocking pin, specific part number where applicable)
- Shutdown Sequence (numbered steps in the order energy sources must be isolated)
- Verification Method per Energy Type (the specific test or observation required, referencing Section 9 of this procedure)
- Stored Energy Notes (any accumulator, capacitor, spring, or suspended load specific to this machine, with the applicable bleed-down or blocking method)
- Restart Sequence (numbered steps, including any required test run before returning the machine to the operator)
- Qualifying Minor Servicing Tasks, if any, and the specific alternative protective measure approved for each
- Author, Date Drafted, EHS Review Date, Final Approval Date, Next Scheduled Review Date

A procedure is not considered complete, and may not be posted at the machine, until every field above is populated; a field may not be marked "not applicable" without EHS sign-off confirming that judgment.

## Appendix E: Equipment-Type Isolation Notes

The following notes supplement, but do not replace, the individual machine-specific procedure required for each of the 148 assets. They are provided so that a maintenance technician drafting or revising a procedure under Section 5 has a consistent starting point for each equipment category present at the facility.

**Press Brakes (200-ton and other tonnage classes).** In addition to the main 480 volt disconnect, press brakes in this fleet use hydraulic ram systems with an accumulator that can retain pressure after the main disconnect is opened and after the hydraulic pump has audibly stopped. The machine-specific procedure must specify the manual accumulator bleed-down valve location and require the pressure gauge at that valve to read zero before verification is considered complete. Ram blocking pins or a mechanical prop bar, where installed, must be engaged any time work is performed between the upper and lower die, in addition to electrical and hydraulic isolation, regardless of ram position at shutdown. This requirement directly addresses the failure mode in the March 2026 incident, where restoration of electrical power alone was sufficient to cause ram movement during a die change.

**Fiber Lasers.** Isolation points include the main 480 volt disconnect, the pneumatic assist-gas supply valve, and, on models so equipped, a capacitor bank that can retain electrical charge briefly after shutdown; the machine-specific procedure must state the manufacturer's specified discharge time or method before electrical work begins. The laser resonator and beam path present a distinct hazard from mechanical energy sources and must be addressed separately in the procedure, including confirmation that the beam shutter or enable key is in the locked-off position. Clearing a material jam is addressed as a qualifying minor servicing task under Section 15.3 only where the machine-specific procedure specifies the safe-clearing mode or interlocked access gate approved for that model; jams requiring access to the cutting head, drive components, or beam path itself require full lockout.

**Robotic Weld Cells.** Isolation includes the cell's main 480 volt disconnect and the pneumatic supply to end-of-arm tooling and fixture clamps. Robotic arms retain the ability to move under stored servo energy or gravity if a joint is not mechanically at rest; the machine-specific procedure must identify whether the installed robot requires a brake-engagement check or physical arm support before an employee enters the cell envelope. Entry into the cell for any purpose other than normal production loading/unloading through the designated safeguarded opening is treated as servicing and requires full lockout of the cell controller in addition to the disconnect.

**Powder Coat Line.** This line combines electrical drive energy for the conveyor, pneumatic energy for spray gun actuation and material valves, and gravity energy in the overhead conveyor sections used to transport parts through the booth, cure oven, and load/unload stations. The machine-specific procedure for each segment of this line must separately address conveyor drive lockout and the mechanical blocking method for any elevated or inclined conveyor section, since the drive motor being locked out does not by itself prevent gravity-driven movement of parts or the chain on a downhill section. Work coordinated with the outside conveyor vendor on this line follows Section 15.4 in addition to the applicable machine-specific procedure.

**Overhead Conveyor Sections (General).** Where a conveyor section is elevated, inclined, or carries suspended loads independent of the powder coat line, the machine-specific procedure must identify the specific mechanical blocking device, pin, or chain used to prevent gravity-driven movement, and must require visual and physical confirmation that the blocking device is load-bearing before an employee positions any part of their body beneath or within the potential path of the suspended component, consistent with Section 7.1.
