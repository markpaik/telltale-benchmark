# Inventory of a House That Was Never Built

**Site.** A quarter-acre on the north side, sloping badly, with a stand of black walnuts that would have to come down or else the whole thing gets built around them. The people who considered buying it walked the lot twice, once in April when the ground was soft and once in October when they could see the shape of the land through the bare stems. Between those two visits, everything about the house changed. In April it had a screened porch. By October the porch was gone and there was a second chimney.

**Foundation.** Poured concrete, though there was an argument for a crawlspace on account of the slope. The argument was had in a car outside a hardware store and it was not really about the crawlspace. One of them wanted the house to be a thing that would outlast them and the other wanted a house they could afford in the next eighteen months. Both of these are ways of loving a house. Neither one wins.

**Framing.** Standard stick, sixteen on center, except along the south wall where the windows were going to be tall enough that the header needed doubling. This was the first detail that felt real. When you know how a wall has to be reinforced to hold a window, the window has begun to exist. You can stand at it. You can be standing at it in February with a cup of something, watching the neighbors' dog trespass across what would have been the vegetable beds, and feel the particular cold that comes off a big pane at seven in the morning.

**Roof.** Metal, standing seam, dark. Chosen for the rain sound. There is no engineering justification for choosing a roof by the sound it makes but this is the part of the house that got decided first and never wavered, not once, not through the crawlspace fight or the resurrection and second death of the screened porch. Whatever else the house was going to be, it was going to be loud in a storm and the loudness was going to be a comfort. Some people build a house around a hearth. Some build it around a kitchen. This one was built around a specific acoustic experience of weather, which is to say it was built around the desire to be safely inside something.

**Kitchen.** Two feet longer than any kitchen either of them had ever cooked in. The island was contentious — an island implies people standing around while someone cooks, which implies people, which implies a version of their lives that had guests in it regularly, which was aspirational in a way that made one of them slightly embarrassed and the other slightly defensive. The island stayed in the plans. It was going to be walnut, possibly from the trees, which is the kind of idea that is very beautiful and requires knowing someone with a mill.

**The room with no name.** Twelve by fourteen, northeast corner, one window. At various points it was an office, a guest room, a nursery, a library, and once, briefly and with great enthusiasm and no follow-through, a darkroom. This room did more work than any other room in the house. Every future they were considering had to be fit into it, and it held them all, one at a time, patiently, the way an empty room will. It's the room I think about. Not the kitchen. The unbuilt house's whole capacity to be more than one life was concentrated in that twelve by fourteen and it never had to choose.

**Stairs.** Never resolved. There was a slope problem and a landing problem and at some point the stairs became the place where all the unresolved things went to live. Every house that doesn't get built has one element like this — a joint that won't close, an angle that keeps coming out wrong on paper. It isn't really the stairs. But you can spend a lot of evenings on the stairs and feel productive.

**Landscaping.** Nothing, but the walnuts stayed. Somewhere between April and October the house had stopped requiring their removal. It had learned to sit differently on the ground. This is the strange thing about houses you don't build: they improve. A real house gets built once, wrong in ten small ways, and then you live in the wrongness until it stops being visible. The unbuilt one revises. It gets better every year. It has the best qualities of a house and none of the qualities of a place.

**Disposition of the property.** Sold in the spring to a couple who put up something reasonable with vinyl siding and an attached garage, and who by all accounts are happy, and who cut down the walnuts.

**Disposition of the house.** Retained. There is no mechanism for surrendering it. It comes back in the specific weather it was designed for — the first hard rain of autumn, when you're under a roof that makes the wrong sound — and it is fully furnished, and the light is coming in through the south windows at the angle those doubled headers made possible, and no one is standing at them.
