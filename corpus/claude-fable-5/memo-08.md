# SAUGATUCK HILLS REGIONAL SCHOOL DISTRICT
## Internal Memorandum Packet — Elementary Consolidation Study and FY28 Budget Planning

---

**MEMORANDUM 1 OF 5**

**TO:** All Building Principals; Central Office Administrative Staff
**FROM:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools
**DATE:** March 9, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Opening of the Elementary Consolidation Study; Timeline and Expectations Through the June Board Vote

I am writing to formally open the district's Elementary Consolidation Study and to set out what I expect from each of you between now and the Board of Education's anticipated vote in late June. Most of you have heard some version of what is coming through building conversations or through coverage of the February 10 referendum result. I want you to hear the full picture from me, in one place, so that we are all working from the same facts and the same calendar.

The context is not complicated, but it is serious. Our enrollment has fallen from 4,610 students in 2020 to 4,190 today, a decline of just over nine percent, and our demographer projects continued softening at the elementary level through at least 2031. Our operating budget stands at $103 million, and Mr. Sanfilippo's preliminary FY28 projection shows a $3.2 million shortfall driven by contractual salary obligations, health insurance trend, and out-of-district tuition growth. The community's rejection of the $46 million renovation bond, by a margin of 54 to 46 with 31 percent turnout, removed the capital pathway we had planned to rely on to modernize our aging elementary buildings and forces us to confront the operating question directly: we are maintaining six school buildings for an enrollment that fits comfortably in five.

The study will therefore evaluate a specific proposal, which I want to name plainly rather than leave to rumor. The administration is examining the closure of Ashford Lane Elementary at the end of the 2026–27 school year. Ashford Lane enrolls 218 students in a building operating at 54 percent of capacity, the lowest utilization in the district by a wide margin. Under the proposal, 118 of those students would be redistricted to Bridgemill Elementary and the remainder, along with students already attending on space-available placements, would be assigned to Hollow Road Elementary. The two self-contained special education programs currently housed at Ashford Lane would be consolidated into program hubs at Bridgemill and Hollow Road, a move Ms. Weissman will address separately with special education staff because it intersects with the state's corrective action requirements. Mr. Sanfilippo estimates recurring annual savings of $1.9 million. The closure would displace 31 staff members, of whom 14 are non-tenured; our goal is to place every tenured staff member and as many non-tenured staff members as attrition and vacancies permit.

I want to be equally plain about what this study is not. It is not a decision. The Board of Education has made no commitment, and two board members have already stated publicly that they oppose closure. The board is entitled to a study that is honest about costs, honest about tradeoffs, and honest about what we do not know. If your data complicates the administration's case, I want that data anyway. A study that only confirms what the superintendent's office already believes is not worth the paper.

The timeline is as follows. Between now and April 3, principals will submit verified enrollment, capacity, and section-size data for their buildings using the template Assistant Superintendent's office will distribute this week; do not rely on October 1 counts, as we need current figures reflecting mid-year moves. During the same window, the Transportation Office will model revised bus routes for the redistricted attendance zones, and Facilities will complete a deferred-maintenance assessment of Ashford Lane so the board can weigh avoided capital costs. On April 14, Mr. Sanfilippo will present the revised FY28 budget scenarios — with and without closure — to the board's Finance Committee, and his memo to that effect will circulate to this group as well. Community forums at Ashford Lane will run from late April through May; Principal Marte will host and I will attend each one. Ms. Weissman's special education transition plan, aligned with the corrective action plan due to the state on June 30, will go to her staff in May. I will bring a final administrative recommendation to the board at its June 15 workshop, with a vote anticipated June 29.

Three expectations for the months ahead. First, accuracy before advocacy. Each of you has attachments to your buildings and your colleagues, and I do not ask you to pretend otherwise, but every number you submit must survive scrutiny, because every number will receive it. Second, protect your staff from speculation without misleading them. Ashford Lane staff in particular deserve straight answers about the placement process, and Principal Marte and Human Resources will jointly manage individual conversations; please route staff questions there rather than improvising answers. Third, keep instruction at the center. Our students, including the 218 at Ashford Lane, are owed a full and normal spring, and I will hold every building leader responsible for making sure adult anxiety does not become classroom disruption.

Questions about the study process should come to my office. Questions about budget figures go to Mr. Sanfilippo, special education matters to Ms. Weissman, and Ashford Lane community engagement to Principal Marte. I will send this group a status update after each major milestone.

Thank you for your professionalism in what will be a difficult season. We have hard choices in front of us; we do not have the option of making them carelessly.

**MEMORANDUM 2 OF 5**

**TO:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent; Board of Education Finance Committee
**FROM:** Marco Sanfilippo, Business Manager
**DATE:** April 14, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Revised FY28 Budget Projections — Scenarios With and Without Ashford Lane Closure

This memo presents the revised FY28 budget projections requested as part of the Elementary Consolidation Study, modeling the district's financial position both with and without the proposed closure of Ashford Lane Elementary effective July 1, 2027. All figures have been reviewed with our auditors' advisory team and reflect assumptions current as of April 10, 2026.

**Baseline Position.** The FY27 adopted budget of $103.0 million carries forward into FY28 with the following major cost drivers: contractual salary increases under the current teachers' agreement averaging 3.4 percent ($2.1 million); certified and non-certified step movement ($640,000); health insurance renewal, currently projected by our consultant at 8.5 percent trend ($1.4 million); special education out-of-district tuition growth ($710,000, discussed below); and transportation contract escalation ($290,000). Against these increases we project essentially flat revenue: the Education Cost Sharing grant is level-funded in the Governor's proposed biennium, and our declining enrollment provides no offsetting formula gain. Modest reductions from already-planned attrition ($480,000) and utility renegotiation ($260,000) leave a structural gap of $3.2 million for FY28 absent further action. I want the committee to understand clearly that this is a structural gap, not a one-year problem; without a recurring solution it compounds into FY29 and FY30.

**Scenario A — No Closure.** Maintaining all six buildings requires closing the $3.2 million gap through operating reductions and revenue requests. To be concrete about what that means, I have modeled a reduction package: elimination of 22 to 26 certified positions district-wide through a combination of attrition and layoff ($1.9 to $2.2 million), reduction of the elementary library media and world language programs to itinerant staffing ($340,000), a 15 percent cut to athletic and co-curricular budgets ($185,000), deferral of the technology refresh cycle by one year ($410,000), and increased pay-to-participate fees ($90,000 in projected revenue). Even this package assumes the towns accept a levy increase at the top of recent tolerance, which the February referendum result does not encourage. I note for the record that a district-wide staffing reduction of this size would raise average class sizes in every building, not only at the elementary level, and would fall disproportionately on programs rather than facilities.

**Scenario B — Closure of Ashford Lane.** Closure generates estimated recurring savings of $1.9 million annually, composed as follows: staffing consolidation net of positions retained for redistricted sections, $1.24 million (the 31 displaced positions reduce to a net of approximately 15.5 FTE eliminated after re-staffing Bridgemill and Hollow Road for larger enrollments); plant operations including custodial, utilities, and routine maintenance, $410,000; administrative and clerical consolidation, $175,000; and food service and miscellaneous operating costs, $75,000. Against these savings, closure carries costs: transportation route additions of $140,000 annually for the redistricted zones; one-time transition costs in FY28 of approximately $520,000 for moving, minor renovations at the receiving schools to accommodate the special education program hubs, and unemployment obligations for non-tenured staff not placed; and mothballing costs for the Ashford Lane building of $85,000 annually pending disposition. Net recurring savings therefore approximate $1.68 million in FY28, rising to $1.86 million in FY29 once transition costs roll off. Closure narrows the FY28 gap to roughly $1.5 million, which can be closed through attrition-based reductions already identified without a district-wide layoff or program elimination.

Two additional considerations belong in the committee's deliberations. First, the deferred capital question. The Facilities assessment completed April 8 identifies $7.3 million in capital needs at Ashford Lane over the next ten years — roof replacement, HVAC end-of-life, ADA compliance, and window systems — needs that the failed bond would have addressed and that now have no funding source. Closure avoids this entire liability. Retention requires the board to identify a capital plan for a building at 54 percent utilization, which I cannot responsibly recommend. Second, disposition value. The Ashford Lane property has not been appraised for sale or lease, and I have deliberately excluded any disposition proceeds from these projections; any eventual value is upside, not a planning assumption.

**Sensitivity and Risks.** The savings estimate is sensitive to three variables. If health insurance trend comes in at 10 percent rather than 8.5 percent, the gap widens by $250,000 under either scenario. If special education out-of-district placements exceed projection — a real risk given the pending corrective action plan and the possibility that families dissatisfied with the hub transition seek outside placement — each additional placement costs $85,000 to $140,000. Ms. Weissman and I are coordinating closely on this exposure, and I defer to her forthcoming memo on the programmatic side. Finally, the staffing savings assume the retirement incentive currently under discussion with the SHEA leadership yields at least nine elections; fewer retirements shift savings from attrition to layoff, which changes the human cost but not materially the dollars.

My office will provide the Finance Committee with the full line-item workbooks and will model any alternative scenarios the board wishes to see, including partial-closure and grade-reconfiguration options, though I will state my professional view that neither alternative examined to date approaches the recurring savings of Scenario B. I am available at the April 21 committee meeting for detailed questions.

**MEMORANDUM 3 OF 5**

**TO:** All Special Education Certified and Support Staff; Building Principals
**FROM:** Hannah Weissman, Director of Special Services
**DATE:** May 11, 2026
**SUBJECT:** State Corrective Action Plan Requirements and the Proposed Special Education Program Hubs at Bridgemill and Hollow Road

I am writing to all special education staff about two matters that are related but must not be confused with one another: the corrective action plan we owe the Connecticut State Department of Education by June 30, and the proposed consolidation of our two self-contained programs at Ashford Lane into program hubs at Bridgemill and Hollow Road. Both will shape our work over the coming year, and I want every member of this department to understand exactly what is required, what is proposed, and what is protected regardless of any building decision.

**The Corrective Action Plan.** As you know, the State Department of Education completed its special education monitoring review in January. The findings were sobering and I will not soften them: 19 percent of sampled IEPs were out of compliance, concentrated in two areas — secondary transition planning that did not meet the requirements for measurable postsecondary goals and age-appropriate transition assessment, and discrepancies between service minutes written in IEPs and service minutes actually delivered and documented. These are not paperwork technicalities. Transition planning determines whether our older students leave us prepared for what comes next, and service delivery is the core promise of every IEP. When we write 120 minutes of specialized instruction per week, families are entitled to 120 minutes, and our documentation must prove it.

The corrective action plan due June 30 will commit the district to the following, and I am telling you now so that nothing in it surprises you. First, every IEP for a student aged 14 or older will be reviewed and, where deficient, amended through reconvened PPT meetings before October 15; case managers will receive a list of affected students by June 1, and the department will fund substitute coverage and summer hours for this work. Second, we are implementing a uniform service-delivery log, completed weekly by every provider — special education teachers, related service providers, and paraprofessionals delivering IEP-directed services — with monthly reconciliation against IEP minutes by department chairs. I recognize this is an added documentation burden and I have kept the instrument as lean as the state will accept. Third, all case managers and secondary special education teachers will complete transition-planning training in August, before the school year begins; this is mandatory and compensated. The state will conduct a follow-up visit in the fall, likely October or November, and will pull files again. I intend for us to pass that review decisively, not narrowly, and I will be candid: the district's credibility with the state, and with our families, depends on it.

**The Program Hubs.** Separately, the consolidation study now before the board proposes closing Ashford Lane Elementary, which houses two of our self-contained programs — the elementary intensive-needs classroom and the primary communication and behavior program. Under the proposal, the intensive-needs program would relocate to Bridgemill and the communication and behavior program to Hollow Road, each becoming a district "hub" with dedicated space, an adjacent related-services suite, and a sensory room, funded from the transition budget Mr. Sanfilippo has presented to the Finance Committee. I have walked both receiving buildings with Facilities and with the current program teachers, and the identified spaces are, frankly, better than what our students occupy now: larger, on the ground floor, closer to accessible entrances, and adjacent to age-appropriate general education classrooms for inclusion.

I want to state several commitments plainly, because families and staff have asked and deserve unambiguous answers. Every student's IEP travels with the student; a building change alters no service, no minute, and no goal, and no program change may occur except through a properly convened PPT. Program staffing teams will move intact wherever possible — my strong intention is that students in these programs see familiar teachers and paraprofessionals on the first day in the new building, and I have asked Human Resources to treat program continuity as a placement priority within contractual constraints. Transportation for students in these programs will be individually planned, with ride-time limits honored, and families will receive routes before the end of the 2026–27 school year, not in August. Finally, each affected family will be offered an individual transition meeting and building visits in spring 2027, with additional visits for students who benefit from repeated exposure to new environments.

Let me also address the concern I have heard in several buildings: that the hub consolidation is being driven by the corrective action plan, or alternatively that the corrective action plan makes the move reckless. Neither is accurate. The compliance findings concern transition documentation and service delivery, and they must be fixed whether we operate five buildings or six. But I will not pretend the two are unrelated in practice, because both depend on the same people doing careful work in the same year. That is why I have front-loaded the compliance work into this summer and fall — so that if the board approves closure, the 2026–27 school year can be devoted to planning an orderly program transition rather than fighting two fires at once. If the board does not approve closure, everything in the first half of this memo still happens, on the same dates.

Department meetings to review the corrective action plan in detail are scheduled for May 19 at the high school and May 21 at Bridgemill; attendance at one session is required. Questions about the hub proposal may come to me directly at any time, and I would rather answer the same question forty times than have staff or families operating on rumor. Thank you for the seriousness with which this department has met a difficult finding. The measure of a program is not whether it ever falls short but what it does next.

**MEMORANDUM 4 OF 5**

**TO:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent
**FROM:** Odalys Marte, Principal, Ashford Lane Elementary School
**DATE:** June 5, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Status Report — Community Forums and Staff Placement Process at Ashford Lane

This memo summarizes the four community forums held at Ashford Lane between April 28 and May 27, reports on the staff placement process to date, and flags several issues that I believe should inform your June 15 recommendation to the board. I have tried to report what I heard accurately, including the parts that are hard to hear, because I think you are better served by a faithful account than a diplomatic one.

**The Forums.** We held forums on April 28, May 7, May 19, and May 27, alternating evening and morning sessions to accommodate working families, with Spanish and Portuguese interpretation at all sessions and childcare at the evening sessions. Combined attendance was approximately 340, with the May 7 session the largest at roughly 130. You attended all four; Mr. Sanfilippo presented the budget scenarios at the first two; Ms. Weissman joined the May 19 session, which focused on the special education program moves.

The tenor evolved over the four sessions. The first forum was raw. Families were angry, some at the proposal and many at what they perceived as a decision already made — a perception fed, I must note, by the fact that the study opened barely six weeks after the referendum failed, which some read as the administration having a closure plan in reserve. The strongest recurring themes across all sessions were these. First, walkability: Ashford Lane is the district's only true neighborhood school, with 61 percent of students walking, and families in the Sasco Mill neighborhood in particular see the school as the center of a community with few other institutions. Redistricting converts nearly all of those walkers into bus riders. Second, equity: several parents observed, correctly, that Ashford Lane serves the district's highest concentration of multilingual learners and its highest free and reduced lunch percentage, and asked pointedly why the building carrying the least political weight was the one proposed for closure. I do not think the demographics drove the recommendation — the utilization numbers are what they are — but the question deserves a direct answer in your board memo, because it will be asked again in June. Third, receiving-school capacity: parents pressed hard on projected class sizes at Bridgemill and Hollow Road, and the answers improved over the four sessions as Mr. Sanfilippo's section modeling firmed up, but skepticism remains, and the two dissenting board members' public comments have amplified it.

I also want to report what shifted. By the fourth forum, a meaningful portion of the room had moved from "don't close the school" to "if you close the school, here is what we need": guaranteed sibling co-assignment in redistricting, ride times under 25 minutes for elementary students, preservation of the Ashford Lane dual-language strand intact at a single receiving school rather than split, and a community process on the future of the building, which several attendees hope could house the town's early childhood and family resource programs. I would characterize these as the terms of a grudging peace, and I recommend the district commit to all four explicitly. The dual-language strand in particular should move intact to Bridgemill; splitting it would be educationally indefensible and would reignite the equity argument with justification.

**Staff Placement.** Of the 31 staff members displaced by a closure, 17 are tenured certified staff, 14 are non-tenured certified or non-certified staff. Human Resources and I have now completed individual placement conferences with all 31, and I want to acknowledge HR's care in this process — every conversation happened in person, with union representation offered and accepted in most cases. As of this week, the picture is as follows. All 17 tenured staff members have identified placements at Bridgemill, Hollow Road, or the middle schools, contingent on the board's vote; twelve received their first-choice building. Of the 14 non-tenured staff, six have been matched to projected vacancies arising from the retirement incentive, which yielded eleven elections district-wide, exceeding Mr. Sanfilippo's threshold of nine. Five more are competitive candidates for postings expected over the summer. Three — two instructional paraprofessionals and one part-time clerical position — currently have no likely placement, and I ask that the district commit to priority recall rights for them through the 2027–28 school year. Morale in the building is fragile but professional. I will say for the record that this staff has run a full and rigorous spring for 218 children while their own futures were undecided, and whatever the board decides, they have earned the district's public gratitude.

**Issues for Your Recommendation.** Three items. First, if the vote proceeds June 29 as planned, families need final redistricting assignments no later than December 2026 — a full six months before the transition — and I would put that date in writing now. Second, the May 19 forum surfaced real anxiety among special education families despite Ms. Weissman's strong presentation; the individual transition meetings she has promised should be scheduled, not merely offered, with outreach in home languages. Third, I ask that whatever happens to the building, the district commit publicly that it will not sit dark and fenced in the middle of Sasco Mill; nothing at the forums generated more emotion than the image of a shuttered school at the heart of the neighborhood.

I remain available for whatever the next weeks require. It has been the privilege of my career to lead this school, and I intend to lead it well through whichever ending or continuation the board chooses.

**MEMORANDUM 5 OF 5**

**TO:** Leticia Ramos-Byrd, Chair, and Members of the Board of Education
**FROM:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools
**DATE:** June 22, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Administrative Response to the Written Objection of June 12 Concerning Redistricting and Class Size

Chair Ramos-Byrd, at your request I am responding in writing to the June 12 letter submitted by two members of the board objecting to the administration's recommendation to close Ashford Lane Elementary. The letter raises two substantive concerns — that redistricting 118 students will destabilize elementary attendance patterns and disproportionately burden the Sasco Mill neighborhood, and that consolidation will push class sizes at Bridgemill and Hollow Road beyond educationally sound levels. Both concerns are legitimate subjects for board deliberation, and I take them seriously. I also believe both are answerable with the data now in hand, and this memo answers them so that the full board can vote on June 29 with a complete record.

**On Redistricting.** The objection states that redistricting "uproots an entire neighborhood from its school." The factual core is accurate: 118 students will move to Bridgemill and the remaining Ashford Lane students to Hollow Road, and a majority of current walkers will become bus riders. I will not minimize that cost, and Principal Marte's forum report, which the board has received, documents it honestly. But the board should weigh three points alongside it. First, the alternative is not stability; it is a different instability. Scenario A in Mr. Sanfilippo's April 14 memo closes the $3.2 million FY28 gap without closure only by eliminating 22 to 26 certified positions district-wide and cutting elementary library media and world language to itinerant staffing. Those cuts land in every classroom in every building, including Ashford Lane's, and they recur and deepen in FY29. The objecting members' letter proposes no alternative reduction package, and the board should not vote as though one exists. Second, the redistricting plan now incorporates the four commitments that emerged from the community forums: guaranteed sibling co-assignment, elementary ride times capped at 25 minutes, the dual-language strand moving intact to Bridgemill, and a public process on the building's future use, with the town's expressed interest in an early childhood and family resource center as the leading option. These are not gestures; they will appear as conditions in the closure resolution itself if the board adopts my recommended language. Third, on the equity question raised at the forums and echoed in the objection: Ashford Lane was identified by building utilization — 54 percent, against a district elementary average of 81 percent — and by the $7.3 million in unfunded capital liability documented in the April facilities assessment. No other building presents comparable figures. I have asked our consultant to complete an equity impact review of the redistricting lines before implementation, and I will bring its findings to the board in the fall. If the lines burden any group of students disproportionately in ways the plan can remedy, we will remedy them.

**On Class Size.** The objection asserts that consolidation will produce elementary classes "approaching or exceeding 27 students." The section-by-section modeling completed in May does not support that figure, and I want the record to show precisely what the modeling does show. With 118 students redistricted to Bridgemill and approximately 95 to Hollow Road, and with 15.5 net FTE eliminated but the remaining Ashford Lane sections re-established at the receiving schools, projected 2027–28 class sizes are as follows: at Bridgemill, kindergarten through grade 2 sections average 19.8 students with a maximum of 21; grades 3 through 5 average 22.4 with a maximum of 24. At Hollow Road, the corresponding figures are 20.1 with a maximum of 22 in the primary grades and 23.1 with a maximum of 25 in the intermediate grades. Every projected section falls within board Policy 6151's guidelines of 22 in primary and 25 in intermediate grades. The 27-student figure in the objection appears to derive from an early March back-of-envelope calculation that assumed no sections would be added at receiving schools; that assumption was never the administration's plan, and the staffing to add those sections is fully costed in Mr. Sanfilippo's Scenario B. I will further recommend that the closure resolution include a standing commitment: if any receiving-school section exceeds policy guidelines at the October 1, 2027 count, the administration will add a section or co-teacher within 30 days, funded from the closure savings. I would rather bank $1.6 million than $1.9 million and remove this issue from doubt permanently. I would also invite the board to compare this commitment with Scenario A, under which a district-wide reduction of 22 to 26 positions makes class-size growth in every building not a risk to be mitigated but an arithmetic certainty.

**Two Further Points.** The objection suggests the board delay the vote for a year of further study. I recommend against delay, for reasons the board should weigh openly. The FY28 budget must be built beginning this fall; a delayed decision forfeits the closure savings for FY28 and forces the district into Scenario A's cuts for at least one year, cuts that would then have to be partially reversed if closure followed — the most expensive possible sequence. Delay also extends the uncertainty that Ashford Lane's staff and families have carried since March, and Principal Marte's report makes clear what that uncertainty costs; 28 of 31 displaced staff now have identified or likely placements, an outcome that depends on this summer's vacancy cycle and will not repeat as favorably next year. Finally, the objection expresses concern that the special education hub moves add risk while the district is under a state corrective action plan. Ms. Weissman's May 11 memo addresses this directly, and I endorse her sequencing: the compliance work concludes this summer and fall, the state's follow-up visit occurs before any program moves, and the hub transition, if approved, occupies the full 2026–27 planning year with intact staffing teams, individual family transition meetings, and improved facilities at both receiving schools. The corrective action plan is an argument for careful sequencing, which we have, not for indefinite deferral.

I respect the two members' dissent and I expect they will press these questions again on June 29; they should. The board's decision will be better for having been contested. But I ask every member to vote on the record as it now stands — the April budget scenarios, the facilities assessment, the May section modeling, the forum commitments, and the placement outcomes — rather than on figures and fears that the record has since answered. My recommendation stands: close Ashford Lane Elementary effective July 1, 2027, subject to the conditions described above, and direct the administration to return in September with the implementation calendar, the equity impact review scope, and the community process on the building's future.

I am available to any member individually before the vote, and Mr. Sanfilippo, Ms. Weissman, and Principal Marte will attend on June 29 to answer questions in their areas.
