# PINE HOLLOW AREA SCHOOL DISTRICT NO. 274

## Board of Education — Minutes of Four Regular Meetings, September–December 2025

# REGULAR MEETING — MONDAY, SEPTEMBER 15, 2025

**Body:** Board of Education, Pine Hollow Area School District No. 274
**Date:** Monday, September 15, 2025
**Time:** Called to order at 7:02 p.m.; adjourned at 10:03 p.m.
**Place:** Media Center, Pine Hollow Area High School, 1400 Cavalier Drive, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members present:** Rosalind Achterberg, Chair; Hector Villalpando, Vice Chair; Grant Halvorsen, Treasurer; Beverly Tenneson, Clerk; Curtis Ellingboe, Director; Paulette Simard, Director; Mai Xiong, Director (7 of 7)

**Members absent:** None

**Others attending:** Terrence Baldacci, Superintendent; Nadia Kalinowski, Business Manager; Odalys Marchetti, Director of Teaching and Learning; Devin Osterhaus, High School Principal; Katherine Aune, Principal, Birchdale Elementary; Dr. Priya Ramanathan, Upper Midwest Demographics; Rhoda Kittelson, AIA, Northstar Facility Advisors; Colleen Draxten, President, Pine Hollow Education Association; Janine Krupke, Board Recording Secretary; approximately 60 members of the public and two representatives of the *Pine Hollow Register*

---

### 1. Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 7:02 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance. Recording Secretary Krupke called the roll and reported all seven members present.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda

Director Villalpando moved, and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded, to approve the agenda as published. Chair Achterberg noted the addition of Item 9(b), seating of the boundary committee, which had been posted with the agenda packet on September 11. **Voice vote: all in favor. Motion carried 7–0.**

### 3. Consent Agenda

Clerk Tenneson moved, and Director Ellingboe seconded, to approve the consent agenda as presented, consisting of:

- Minutes of the regular meeting of August 18, 2025, and the special meeting of August 27, 2025;
- Verified accounts payable in the amount of $1,847,203.41 and wire transfers of $612,884.00;
- Personnel report, including the resignations of Angela Sturdevant (Grade 3, Sundberg Elementary) and Ryan Kotchevar (Industrial Technology, High School); the hiring of Miriam Osei-Bonsu (Grade 5, Cottonwood Ridge), Blake Ferrante (Special Education, Middle School), and Dolores Tapio (paraprofessional, Birchdale Elementary); and one unpaid leave request;
- Donations of $2,500 from the Birchdale Elementary PTO for playground equipment, $1,000 from Pine Hollow American Legion Post 442 for the marching band, and $750 from Kandiyohi Farmers Cooperative for FFA travel;
- Overnight field trip requests for the High School cross-country team (Sept. 26–27, Duluth) and the FFA chapter (Oct. 9–11, Indianapolis).

**Roll call vote:** Achterberg, aye; Villalpando, aye; Halvorsen, aye; Tenneson, aye; Ellingboe, aye; Simard, aye; Xiong, aye. **Motion carried 7–0.**

### 4. Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 7:14 p.m., stating the three-minute limit per speaker and the board's practice of not responding during the comment period. Six persons spoke, and comment closed at 7:36 p.m.

- **Denise Ruttger**, Birchdale Township, asked whether the facilities study had been written before or after the enrollment numbers were known.
- **Aaron Kleist**, Pine Hollow, urged the board to publish building-by-building cost data before any vote.
- **Colleen Draxten**, PHEA President, said the association's members had gone two years without a settled contract and asked the board to bring an improved offer to the September 30 session.
- **Marcy Uphoff**, Sundberg, spoke in support of the November operating levy.
- **Tomás Regalado**, Pine Hollow, asked about bus ride times for elementary students if any building closed.
- **Lucille Bergerson**, Birchdale Township, presented a petition she said carried 411 signatures opposing closure of Birchdale Elementary. The petition was received and filed by the Clerk.

### 5. Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported that school opened September 2 without incident; that the September 5 head count was 1,338 students against a projection of 1,340; that four licensed vacancies remained (two special education, one high school mathematics, one elementary music) and were being covered by long-call substitutes and a Tier 1 permission variance; and that 2025 MCA results showed reading proficiency at 47.1 percent and mathematics at 41.8 percent, both within one point of the prior year. He reported that the district's five-year deferred maintenance list now totaled $11.9 million across six buildings and that the long-term facilities maintenance revenue stream would fund approximately $4.1 million of that amount.

### 6. Enrollment Projection

Dr. Priya Ramanathan of Upper Midwest Demographics presented the 2025 enrollment study. She reported enrollment of 1,340 students in September 2025, down from 1,910 in September 2014, a decline of 570 students, or 29.8 percent, over eleven years. She attributed roughly 62 percent of the decline to resident births, which fell from 168 in 2010 to 104 in 2023, and the remainder to net open-enrollment loss, which stood at 91 students out and 34 students in. Her middle-series projection showed 1,246 students in 2028 and 1,181 students in 2030.

Treasurer Halvorsen asked what the projection assumed about the referendum. Dr. Ramanathan said the model assumed no change in open-enrollment behavior and did not attempt to price the effect of program reductions. Director Xiong asked for the projection by building. Dr. Ramanathan agreed to furnish building-level tables to Ms. Kalinowski by September 26. No action was taken.

### 7. First Reading — Facilities Study

Ms. Kittelson of Northstar Facility Advisors presented the first reading of the 2025 Facilities and Utilization Study. She reported district-wide elementary utilization at 58 percent; Birchdale Elementary at 41 percent, with 187 students in a building rated for 450; a Facility Condition Index at Birchdale of 0.47, with $6.4 million of identified need over ten years, of which $2.2 million was roof, boiler, and electrical service; and four options ranging from status quo to consolidation of all elementary grades into two buildings.

The study's recommendation was Option C: close Birchdale Elementary and reassign its attendance area between Cottonwood Ridge and Sundberg Elementary Schools, with projected annual operating savings of $1.1 to $1.3 million and avoided capital cost of $4.9 million.

Vice Chair Villalpando asked what the recommendation assumed about transportation. Ms. Kittelson said routing analysis showed 34 students whose ride time would exceed 55 minutes, up from 11 today. Director Simard asked whether the study had considered closing the middle school and grade-reconfiguring instead; Ms. Kittelson said Option D examined that and produced smaller savings with higher one-time cost.

This was a first reading. No motion was made and no action was taken. Chair Achterberg stated that a second reading would be scheduled following the November 4 election.

### 8. Finance Report

Business Manager Kalinowski reported the general fund at $23.6 million in FY2026 expenditures against $22.4 million in projected revenue. Unaudited June 30, 2025 general fund balance was $2.71 million, or 11.5 percent of expenditures. Under current assumptions, she projected a fund balance of $0.94 million at June 30, 2026, and negative $1.86 million at June 30, 2027, producing a cumulative operating shortfall of $2.9 million. She stated that a negative unassigned fund balance exceeding 2.5 percent of expenditures would place the district in statutory operating debt under Minnesota Statutes section 123B.83, requiring a special operating plan filed with and approved by the Commissioner of Education.

Treasurer Halvorsen asked Ms. Kalinowski to confirm the shortfall net of the proposed levy. She said passage of the November referendum would reduce the FY2027 gap by approximately $1.05 million but would not by itself avoid statutory operating debt.

### 9. New Business

**(a) Proposed FY2026 Property Tax Levy — Timeline.** Ms. Kalinowski reviewed the September 30 maximum levy certification deadline, the November 25 mailing of proposed tax notices by the county auditors, and the requirement to hold the truth-in-taxation presentation and certify the final levy before December 30. No action was required.

**(b) Seating of the Boundary Committee.** Superintendent Baldacci presented a slate of eleven committee members solicited by application in August. Treasurer Halvorsen moved, and Director Xiong seconded, to seat the boundary committee as follows: Katherine Aune and Jorge Peñaloza (administration); Heather Lindgren-Otieno and Samuel Wrightsman (teachers); Dorothy Kvamme, Elias Nooney, Jenna Berkstrom, Paul Two Bulls, and Ana-Lucía Serrato (parents, one from each attendance area); Wayne Hjelmstad (Birchdale Township supervisor); and Director Simard as board liaison.

The committee's charge, as adopted, was to model attendance boundary alternatives under both a four-building and a three-building elementary configuration, to report transportation and class-size consequences, and to deliver a written report to the board no later than the regular meeting of February 16, 2026. The committee was directed to hold no fewer than two public listening sessions.

**Roll call vote:** Achterberg, aye; Villalpando, aye; Halvorsen, aye; Tenneson, aye; Ellingboe, aye; Simard, aye; Xiong, aye. **Motion carried 7–0.**

**(c) Referendum Information.** Superintendent Baldacci reviewed the board's June 16 resolution calling the November 4 election on an operating referendum of $780 per adjusted pupil unit for ten years. He reminded the board of the statutory line between district information and advocacy, and reported that the independent Vote Yes for Pine Hollow Kids committee had registered with the Campaign Finance Board on August 22. No action was taken.

### 10. Closed Session — Labor Negotiations Strategy

At 9:14 p.m., Clerk Tenneson moved, and Director Ellingboe seconded, to close the meeting under Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03 to consider strategy for labor negotiations with the Pine Hollow Education Association, including negotiation positions, proposals, and counterproposals. **Roll call vote: 7–0.** Chair Achterberg stated the statutory basis on the record and noted that the closed session would be electronically recorded and preserved for two years.

Present in closed session: all seven board members, Superintendent Baldacci, Business Manager Kalinowski, and Director of Teaching and Learning Marchetti.

The board returned to open session at 9:58 p.m. Chair Achterberg reported that the board had given direction to its negotiating team and that no action had been taken in closed session.

### 11. Announcements and Adjournment

Chair Achterberg announced the next regular meeting for Monday, October 20, 2025, at 7:00 p.m. in the High School Media Center.

Director Simard moved, and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded, to adjourn. **Voice vote: all in favor.** The meeting adjourned at 10:03 p.m.

Superintendent Baldacci reported the official October 1 enrollment count at 1,343 students, three above projection; the completion of all state-required safety drills; the hiring of a permanent high school mathematics teacher effective January 5; and the scheduling of the community facilities hearing. He reported that 61 students were open-enrolled out to Willmar, Benson, and Montevideo, a net increase of nine from the prior year.

### 6. Audit Report — Fiscal Year Ended June 30, 2025

Ms. Brommer of Wenzel & Brommer LLP presented the audited financial statements. She reported an unmodified opinion on the financial statements; no material weaknesses and one significant deficiency in internal control relating to segregation of duties in activity accounts at the High School; and full compliance with the Minnesota Legal Compliance Audit Guide. Audited general fund balance at June 30, 2025 was $2,713,942, of which $2,190,377 was unassigned, equal to 9.3 percent of expenditures. She noted that the FY2025 general fund operating deficit was $1,142,000, the third consecutive deficit year.

Treasurer Halvorsen asked what fund balance percentage the firm considered prudent. Ms. Brommer said the board's own Policy 704 set a floor of 12 percent, and the district was below its own policy.

Clerk Tenneson moved, and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded, to accept the audited financial statements for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025, as presented, and to direct administration to prepare a written response to the significant deficiency. **Roll call vote:** Achterberg, aye; Villalpando, aye; Halvorsen, aye; Tenneson, aye; Ellingboe, aye; Simard, aye; Xiong, aye. **Motion carried 7–0.**

### 7. Finance Report and Referendum Campaign Report

Ms. Kalinowski presented the referendum information report required by the board's June resolution. She reported that the district had mailed a neutral information piece to 4,880 households on October 3; that seven community information sessions had been held with a combined attendance of 386; that the question, if approved, would raise $1,046,000 in the first year; and that the tax impact would be $186 per year on a residential homestead valued at $250,000 and $8.94 per acre on tillable agricultural land, before the school building bond agricultural credit.

She reviewed the polling places, the absentee schedule at the Kandiyohi and Swift County auditors' offices, and the canvass timeline. She reported that as of October 17, 431 absentee ballots had been requested, compared with 288 in November 2023.

Superintendent Baldacci said the administration had prepared two budget frameworks, one assuming passage and one assuming failure, and would bring the applicable framework to the November 17 meeting.

Director Xiong asked that the failure framework be published before the election rather than after. Superintendent Baldacci said the framework was in the packet as Attachment 7-C and was posted on the district website.

### 8. Teacher Contract Negotiations — Status and Mediation

Superintendent Baldacci reported that the district and the Pine Hollow Education Association had met on July 22, August 19, September 9, and September 30, and that the parties had filed a joint request for mediation with the Bureau of Mediation Services on October 2. The association's position was 4.5 percent on the salary schedule in each year of a two-year agreement; the district's position was 1.75 percent in the first year and 0.5 percent in the second, with the district absorbing the increase in health insurance premium.

Vice Chair Villalpando moved, and Director Simard seconded, to confirm the mediation date of Thursday, November 6, 2025, at 4:00 p.m. at the district office with Mediator Roland Pfeiffer of the Bureau of Mediation Services, and to authorize the board's negotiating team of Villalpando, Halvorsen, Superintendent Baldacci, and Business Manager Kalinowski to attend with authority to make and receive proposals within parameters set in closed session.

Clerk Tenneson moved, and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded, to receive the second reading of the 2025 Facilities and Utilization Study; to schedule a public hearing on the recommendation for Thursday, November 6, 2025, at 6:30 p.m. in the Birchdale Elementary gymnasium; and to place the recommendation on the November 17, 2025 regular meeting agenda for possible action.

Director Xiong stated that scheduling the hearing on the same evening as mediation was poor practice and asked that the hearing be moved. Chair Achterberg said the gymnasium and a recording crew were reserved and the date would stand, and noted for the record that Directors Villalpando and Halvorsen would be at mediation and would receive the hearing recording.

# REGULAR MEETING — MONDAY, NOVEMBER 17, 2025

**Body:** Board of Education, Pine Hollow Area School District No. 274
**Date:** Monday, November 17, 2025
**Time:** Called to order at 7:00 p.m.; adjourned at 11:52 p.m.
**Place:** Auditorium, Pine Hollow Area High School (relocated from the Media Center by the Chair on November 13 to accommodate anticipated attendance; notice of the location change was posted November 13 at the district office, all school buildings, and the district website)

**Members present:** Rosalind Achterberg, Chair; Hector Villalpando, Vice Chair; Grant Halvorsen, Treasurer; Beverly Tenneson, Clerk; Curtis Ellingboe, Director; Paulette Simard, Director; Mai Xiong, Director (7 of 7)

**Members absent:** None

**Others attending:** Terrence Baldacci, Superintendent; Nadia Kalinowski, Business Manager; Anders Wilhelmsen, School Attorney, Rustad, Boyle & Ness, P.A.; Odalys Marchetti, Director of Teaching and Learning; Katherine Aune, Principal, Birchdale Elementary; Rhoda Kittelson, Northstar Facility Advisors; Colleen Draxten, PHEA President; Janine Krupke, Recording Secretary; approximately 430 members of the public

---

### 1. Call to Order

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. All seven members answered roll. The Chair announced fire exits, stated that the meeting was being video-recorded and livestreamed, and asked the audience to refrain from applause or demonstration.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda

Clerk Tenneson moved, and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded, to approve the agenda. Director Xiong moved to amend by moving Item 9, the consolidation recommendation, to follow public comment. Director Ellingboe seconded. **Roll call on the amendment:** Achterberg, nay; Villalpando, nay; Halvorsen, nay; Tenneson, nay; Ellingboe, aye; Simard, aye; Xiong, aye. **Amendment failed 3–4.** **Roll call on the main motion: 7–0. Motion carried.**

### 3. Consent Agenda

Director Simard moved, and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded, to approve the consent agenda: minutes of October 20, 2025; accounts payable of $1,933,077.84 and wire transfers of $604,220.00; the personnel report, including four resignations and two hires; donations of $2,180; the administration's written response to the FY2025 audit finding; and the 2026 board meeting calendar (third Monday of each month, with the January meeting on January 19 and the February meeting on February 16). **Roll call vote: 7–0. Motion carried.**

### 4. Canvass and Acceptance of the November 4, 2025 Election Results

Business Manager Kalinowski, as school district election official, presented the abstracts of the Kandiyohi and Swift County canvassing boards for the special election held November 4, 2025.

**Question 1 — Approval of School District Referendum Revenue Authorization ($780 per adjusted pupil unit, ten years):**
- Yes: 2,914 (46.2 percent)
- No: 3,393 (53.8 percent)
- Total ballots cast: 6,307
- Registered voters: 9,842; turnout 64.1 percent

Ms. Kalinowski certified that the question failed and that no referendum revenue authorization was created.

Treasurer Halvorsen moved, and Clerk Tenneson seconded, to accept and enter into the record the canvass of the November 4, 2025 special election, to declare Question 1 not approved, and to direct the district election official to file the required certifications with the Minnesota Department of Education and the county auditors. **Roll call vote:** Achterberg, aye; Villalpando, aye; Halvorsen, aye; Tenneson, aye; Ellingboe, aye; Simard, aye; Xiong, aye. **Motion carried 7–0.**

### 5. Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 7:26 p.m. She announced that 44 persons had signed the register, that each would have three minutes, and that the board would not respond during the period. Comment closed at 10:30 p.m. Forty-two persons spoke; two registered speakers did not appear when called. The period consumed three hours and four minutes.

Of the forty-two speakers, thirty-nine spoke in opposition to closing Birchdale Elementary, two spoke in favor of proceeding with consolidation, and one spoke on an unrelated transportation matter. Speakers are recorded in the order called: L. Bergerson, W. Hjelmstad, L. Nooney, T. Regalado, D. Ruttger, K. Fenske, J. Berkstrom, P. Two Bulls, M. Stromsodt, C. Draxten, A. Kleist, R. Sowinski, H. Lindgren-Otieno, B. Halloran, E. Nooney, D. Kvamme, S. Wrightsman, G. Stoltenow, M. Uphoff, A. Serrato, T. Skarpness, V. Ojibway, R. Chanthavong, D. Merkovich, F. Aandahl, J. Pfundheller, L. Tomczak, N. Bly, C. Ostenson, M. Torrence, K. Rausch, P. Delacroix, B. Sandvik, H. Yang, O. Baumgartner, T. Kittelsen, R. Espeland, J. Mokdad, C. Vue, D. Lindquist, S. Ferrell, and A. Wrightsman.

The following themes were raised repeatedly and are recorded in summary as required by board practice:

**(a) Process.** Twenty-two speakers said the board had scheduled its vote before the boundary committee reported and before the district had published a full transportation plan. Several asked that no vote occur until the February 16 boundary report.

**(b) Transportation.** Fourteen speakers addressed ride times, citing the study's finding that 34 students would exceed 55 minutes. Mr. Hjelmstad said township road conditions in March and November were not reflected in the routing model.

**(c) Community effect.** Eleven speakers, including Ms. Bergerson and Ms. Kvamme, said Birchdale Elementary was the last public institution in Birchdale Township and that closure would accelerate the loss of families and property value.

**(d) Alternatives.** Nine speakers proposed alternatives: a four-day week, closing the district office and relocating administration into the middle school, reducing administrative positions, leasing unused high school space, and a second referendum in 2026 at a lower amount.

**(e) Finances.** Six speakers questioned the $1.1 to $1.3 million savings estimate. Mr. Kleist submitted a four-page written analysis, which was received and filed.

**(f) In favor.** Ms. Uphoff and Mr. Ostenson said the arithmetic of 1,340 students in six buildings was not survivable and that delay would deepen the cuts falling on students in every building.

Chair Achterberg recessed the meeting from 10:30 p.m. to 10:42 p.m.

### 6. Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported that following the referendum's failure the administration had activated the second budget framework; that the district would be required to file with the Commissioner of Education if the projected fund balance materialized; and that reductions would need board direction no later than the January meeting to permit compliance with the notice requirements of Minnesota Statutes section 122A.40, subdivision 10, for any unrequested leave of absence.

### 7. Finance Report

Ms. Kalinowski presented revised projections incorporating the election result. She reported a projected June 30, 2026 general fund balance of $912,000, or 3.9 percent, and a projected June 30, 2027 balance of negative $1,863,000, or negative 7.7 percent of expenditures, a cumulative two-year operating shortfall of $2.9 million. She stated that the negative projection exceeded the 2.5 percent threshold of Minnesota Statutes section 123B.83 and that the district would enter statutory operating debt at June 30, 2027, absent action, requiring a special operating plan approved by the Commissioner and limiting the district's spending authority.

She presented a reduction menu totaling $2.94 million, of which the Birchdale consolidation represented $1.18 million and staffing reductions of 14.5 full-time equivalent positions represented $1.24 million, with the balance in activities, curriculum, technology replacement, and deferred maintenance.

### 8. Negotiations Report

Vice Chair Villalpando reported that mediation was held November 6 with Mediator Pfeiffer; that the parties met in joint and separate session for five hours; that no agreement was reached; and that a second mediation session had been scheduled for Tuesday, December 9, 2025. He reported that the association had moved to 3.9 percent and 3.5 percent, and the district to 2.0 percent and 1.0 percent.

### 9. Recommendation on Elementary Consolidation

Ms. Kittelson summarized the November 6 public hearing, at which 28 persons spoke and 340 attended, and presented no change to the recommendation.

Treasurer Halvorsen moved, and Clerk Tenneson seconded:

> *That the Board of Education close Birchdale Elementary School effective at the end of the 2025–2026 school year, reassign the Birchdale attendance area between Cottonwood Ridge Elementary and Sundberg Elementary in accordance with boundaries to be adopted, and direct the Superintendent to prepare a transition plan.*

Discussion followed. Director Xiong said the board could not responsibly vote to close a building before its own boundary committee reported, and that the committee had been seated by unanimous vote nine weeks earlier for exactly that purpose. Director Ellingboe said he did not dispute the arithmetic but objected to the sequence. Treasurer Halvorsen said each month of delay cost the district approximately $98,000 in unmade decisions and narrowed the options for staff. Clerk Tenneson said the notice requirements for licensed staff made January the practical outside limit. Vice Chair Villalpando disclosed on the record that his sister-in-law was employed as a paraprofessional at Birchdale Elementary, stated that he had consulted School Attorney Wilhelmsen, and said he intended to abstain from any vote on closure. Attorney Wilhelmsen stated for the record that in his opinion no statutory conflict of interest existed but that abstention was within the member's discretion.

**Motion to table.** Director Xiong moved, and Director Ellingboe seconded, to table the motion to the regular meeting of December 15, 2025, and to direct the boundary committee to deliver an interim report by December 8, 2025.

### 11. Process for Filling the Board Vacancy

School Attorney Wilhelmsen advised that under Minnesota Statutes section 123B.09, subdivision 5b, the board must fill the vacancy by appointment within 90 days of the vacancy occurring, that is, on or before March 15, 2026, and that the appointee would serve until an election is held and the successor qualifies, with the seat to appear on the ballot at the general election of November 3, 2026.

Clerk Tenneson moved, and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded, to adopt the following process for filling the vacancy created by the resignation of Director Mai Xiong:

1. That the board formally declare the vacancy at its regular meeting of January 19, 2026;
2. That notice of the vacancy be posted at the district office and all school buildings, published in the *Pine Hollow Register* on January 7 and January 14, 2026, posted on the district website, and distributed by school messenger to all district households;
3. That applications, consisting of a completed district form and a letter of interest of not more than two pages, be filed with the Superintendent's office no later than 4:30 p.m. on Friday, February 6, 2026;
4. That applications be public data upon receipt and be posted with the February 16 board packet;
5. That the board interview all eligible applicants at a special meeting on Monday, February 9, 2026, at 6:00 p.m., using a common set of questions approved by the Chair and Vice Chair, with each interview open to the public;
6. That the board make the appointment by resolution at its regular meeting of Monday, February 16, 2026, and that the appointee take the oath of office at that meeting;
7. That eligibility be limited to persons eligible to vote in the district who have resided in the district for at least 30 days, consistent with Minnesota Statutes section 123B.09;
8. That no seated board member participate in soliciting or endorsing an applicant before the interviews.

**Roll call vote:** Achterberg, aye; Villalpando, aye; Halvorsen, aye; Tenneson, aye; Ellingboe, aye. **Motion carried 5–0.**

Chair Achterberg further directed that until the appointment is made, the board continue to operate with six members, that four members constitute a quorum, and that Director Simard assume Ms. Xiong's seat on the policy committee and Vice Chair Villalpando serve as interim liaison to the boundary committee.
