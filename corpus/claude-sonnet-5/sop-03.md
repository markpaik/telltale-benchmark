## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the mandatory steps for accepting, documenting, handling, loading, and reporting dangerous goods shipments carried by Chugach Air Cargo Services (CACS) on scheduled and charter freight operations between Anchorage and its sixteen served rural Alaska communities. It is issued to close the gaps identified in the 2025 internal audit — declaration errors, lapsed recurrent training among agents signing acceptance checklists, and the absence of a written path for goods discovered undeclared or hidden after acceptance — and to prevent recurrence of the event that led to the FAA's $178,000 civil penalty. Compliance with this procedure is mandatory for every employee who qualifies, accepts, checks, plans, loads, or reports dangerous goods shipments at Anchorage or any village station.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all CACS employees and contract station agents who handle cargo tendered for carriage on CACS aircraft, including the Anchorage headquarters cargo counter, all eight staffed village station counters, dangerous goods checkers, load planners, ramp handlers, pilots in command, dispatch, and the duty officer. It covers all classes of dangerous goods regularly or occasionally tendered to CACS, including but not limited to Section II lithium batteries, dry ice carried with perishable seafood, small arms ammunition, aviation fuel in drums, and diagnostic or biological specimens. It applies to scheduled freight, charter freight, and combination passenger/cargo flights where dangerous goods are carried in the cargo compartment. It does not apply to dangerous goods carried by passengers in accordance with the passenger exceptions of 49 CFR 175.10, which are governed by a separate procedure.

## 3. Definitions

**Acceptance Agent** — The CACS or contract station employee who receives a shipment at the counter, performs the first-level acceptance check, and either accepts or rejects the shipment.

**Certified Scale** — A weighing device that has been calibrated and certified within the preceding twelve months by a recognized weights-and-measures authority or manufacturer-authorized service provider.

**DG-201** — The standard CACS Dangerous Goods Acceptance Checklist, completed in Hermes or on the paper backup form at stations without terminal access, for every dangerous goods shipment tendered.

**DG Checker** — A CACS employee, distinct from the acceptance agent, who holds current dangerous goods recurrent training and performs the second, independent verification of a shipment before it is released to load planning.

**Duty Officer** — The CACS operations employee reachable twenty-four hours a day by phone or radio who is the required point of contact for irregular dangerous goods events, rejections requiring escalation, and post-acceptance discoveries.

**Hermes** — The CACS cargo management system used to record waybills, shipper's declarations, checklist data, load plans, and the notification to captain.

**Notification to Captain (NOTOC)** — The document, generated in Hermes before departure, that lists all dangerous goods loaded on a given flight by class, quantity, ULD or compartment location, and any special loading or handling instruction, and that is presented to and acknowledged by the pilot in command before departure.

**Section II Lithium Battery Shipment** — A shipment of lithium metal or lithium ion cells or batteries packed under the applicable Section II packing instruction of the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, exempt from the shipper's declaration but subject to marking, quantity, and handling requirements.

**Shipper's Declaration** — The shipper-completed and signed document required under 49 CFR 172.204 and IATA DGR Section 8 that identifies the proper shipping name, UN number, class or division, packing group, quantity, and packing details of a dangerous goods shipment, and certifies that it has been prepared in accordance with applicable regulations.

**Undeclared Dangerous Goods** — A dangerous good tendered without required documentation, markings, or shipper notification, whether through omission, concealment, or the shipper's failure to recognize the article as regulated.

**Village Station** — Any of the eight CACS-staffed outstations other than Anchorage at which acceptance agents perform first-level cargo acceptance, including but not limited to Bethel, Nome, Kotzebue, and Dillingham.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager (Yelena Sorokin)** owns this procedure, maintains its currency against 49 CFR Parts 171–180 and the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, conducts or delegates the quarterly self-audit described in Section 12, tracks and closes corrective actions, maintains the training program and its records, and serves as the primary point of contact with the FAA for reportable events.

**Vice President of Operations (Cheryl Aningayou)** approves this procedure and all revisions, ensures adequate staffing and equipment — including certified scales — at each station, allocates resources for training and corrective action, and is the executive notified of any civil penalty exposure or reportable incident within two hours of discovery.

**Acceptance Agents** perform the first-level check at the counter, complete DG-201, verify shipper qualification and documentation, inspect marking and labeling, weigh and measure the shipment, and accept or reject it in accordance with Section 5 through Section 9 of this procedure.

**Dangerous Goods Checkers** perform the mandatory second, independent check of every dangerous goods shipment before it is released to load planning, verify the acceptance agent's DG-201 entries, and countersign the checklist. No shipment may proceed to load planning without a DG checker's countersignature.

**Load Planners** apply quantity limits, segregation requirements, and aircraft-specific loading restrictions under Section 8, build the load plan in Hermes, and generate the notification to captain.

**Ramp Handlers** load and secure dangerous goods shipments in accordance with the load plan and any special handling instructions on the NOTOC, and report any observed package damage before, during, or after loading.

**Station Managers** at each village station are responsible for local compliance with this procedure, for maintaining current training records for their agents, for equipment including scales and placarding materials, and for immediate escalation to the duty officer of any event described in Section 11.

**Duty Officer** is reachable at all times, receives escalation calls for rejections, damaged packages, post-acceptance discoveries, and other irregular events, and initiates the incident reporting sequence in Section 11 when required.

**Pilot in Command** reviews and acknowledges the notification to captain before departure and has final authority to refuse carriage of any dangerous goods shipment.

## 5. Shipper Qualification and Pre-Alert

5.1. Before accepting a dangerous goods shipment from any shipper for the first time, the acceptance agent shall verify the shipper's identity using a government-issued photo identification for individual shippers, or a business account record in Hermes for commercial shippers, and record the identification type and number on DG-201.

5.2. Commercial shippers who tender dangerous goods on a recurring basis (seafood processors, medical facilities, ammunition dealers, fuel distributors) shall be established as qualified accounts in Hermes by the station manager, with a record of the shipper's dangerous goods training status where the shipper's own personnel certify shipments. A shipper account shall not be marked qualified unless the station manager has obtained and filed a copy of the shipper's current dangerous goods training certificate or a signed statement of reliance on a third-party preparer.

5.3. Charter customers and irregular shippers of dangerous goods (including hunting or fishing lodges tendering ammunition, or clinics tendering diagnostic specimens) shall pre-alert the Anchorage cargo desk or the receiving station no less than two hours before intended tender, describing the article, quantity, and intended packaging. The receiving agent shall confirm in Hermes that the article, in the quantity described, is permitted on the intended aircraft type and flight before confirming acceptance of the pre-alert.

5.4. Pre-alerts for Section II lithium battery shipments, dry ice with seafood, and diagnostic specimens do not require a shipper's declaration but do require the pre-alert described in 5.3 so that load planning can reserve segregation space and quantity allowance in advance.

5.5. If a pre-alert is not received for a shipment that on arrival at the counter is identified as dangerous goods, the acceptance agent shall not refuse the shipment solely for lack of pre-alert but shall complete the full acceptance check under Sections 6 through 9 before any commitment to carry is made, and shall note the absence of pre-alert on DG-201.

## 6. Document Verification

6.1. For every shipment requiring a shipper's declaration, the acceptance agent shall verify, item by item, against the DG-201 checklist:

a. Proper shipping name matches the entry in the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations List of Dangerous Goods, with no unauthorized abbreviation.

b. UN or ID number is present, correctly formatted, and consistent with the proper shipping name.

c. Class or division and, where applicable, subsidiary risk are shown and consistent with the UN number.

d. Packing group is shown where required and consistent with the proper shipping name.

e. Quantity and, where applicable, net quantity per package are shown in the correct unit and do not exceed the limit for the packing instruction cited.

f. Packing instruction number is shown and is a valid instruction for the item and quantity declared.

g. Total quantity and number of packages are shown and match the physical shipment.

h. Emergency contact telephone number is shown and is a number staffed twenty-four hours a day, verified where practicable by the agent calling the number on file at account setup.

i. The shipper's certification statement is present in full, unaltered, and signed by hand or authorized electronic signature with a printed name and date, by an individual whose training under Section 10 is current.

j. Aircraft limitation marking (passenger and cargo aircraft, or cargo aircraft only) matches the flight the shipment is tendered for.

6.2. The acceptance agent shall enter each verification point as pass or fail on DG-201 in Hermes. A single failed item is sufficient grounds to hold the shipment pending correction; it shall not be accepted with an item marked failed.

6.3. Where the audit finding of the 2025 review is most directly addressed: agents shall cross-check the declared quantity against the physical count or weight of packages before marking item (g) as passed. Discrepancy between declared and physical quantity, however small, is an automatic fail requiring shipper correction or rejection under Section 9.

6.4. For Section II lithium battery shipments, ammunition, and other classes exempt from the shipper's declaration, the acceptance agent shall instead complete the exempt-shipment block of DG-201, verifying package marking under Section 7, quantity limits under Section 8, and, for ammunition, a completed CACS ammunition acceptance addendum confirming the shipment is sporting ammunition in Division 1.4S quantities within retail-pack limits.

6.5. Every completed DG-201, whether for a declared or exempt shipment, shall be routed in Hermes to a dangerous goods checker before the shipment is released toward load planning. The DG checker shall independently re-verify items 6.1(a) through 6.1(j) or the applicable exempt-shipment items, and shall not merely countersign the acceptance agent's entries. A DG checker who identifies a discrepancy shall return the shipment to the acceptance agent and shall not resolve it unilaterally.

## 7. Package Marking and Labeling Inspection

7.1. The acceptance agent shall physically inspect every package, not only the accompanying paperwork, and confirm:

a. Proper shipping name and UN number are marked on the outer package in durable, legible form.

b. Hazard class label(s) and any subsidiary risk label are affixed, undamaged, and of the correct size and orientation for the package dimensions.

c. Package orientation arrows are present where the package contains liquid in an inner packaging capable of leaking.

d. Overpack markings, if an overpack is used, duplicate all markings and labels of the packages inside unless those markings are visible through the overpack.

e. Shipper's name and address, and consignee's name and address, are marked on the package.

f. UN packaging specification marking is present and undamaged where a UN specification package is required, and the marking is consistent with the packing group and gross mass of the package.

g. Dry ice packages additionally show the net weight of dry ice in kilograms and are marked "Carbon Dioxide, Solid" or "Dry Ice" with UN 1845 and the Class 9 label.

h. Lithium battery packages show the lithium battery handling label with the applicable telephone number, and, for Section II shipments, the package is marked with the Section II lithium battery mark.

7.2. Any package with a missing, illegible, damaged, or incorrect marking or label shall be held. The acceptance agent shall attempt to have the shipper correct the marking or label at the counter; if the shipper cannot correct it, the shipment shall be rejected under Section 9.

7.3. The acceptance agent shall visually inspect every package for evidence of damage, leakage, staining, odor, bulging, or unusual sound or movement before proceeding to weighing. A package showing any of these signs shall not be accepted and shall be handled under Section 13.3.

## 8. Quantity and Segregation Limits by Aircraft Type

8.1. CACS operates the Cessna Grand Caravan, the Beechcraft 1900C, and the Saab 340, in addition to charter-only use of larger equipment. Load planners shall apply the quantity, segregation, and location limits for the specific aircraft assigned to the flight, as published in the CACS Weight and Balance and Dangerous Goods Loading Tables, referenced in Hermes by tail number and aircraft type. This procedure sets the baseline limits below; the loading tables govern where they are more restrictive.

8.2. Dry ice carried with seafood or other perishables shall not exceed 200 kilograms net weight of dry ice on any single Caravan or 1900C flight, and shall not exceed the Saab 340 cargo compartment ventilation-based limit shown in the loading tables. Dry ice packages shall be positioned nearest the cargo door or ventilation source specified in the loading tables and shall never be stacked so as to obstruct compartment ventilation.

8.3. Lithium battery shipments, whether Section II or fully regulated, shall be segregated from Class 1 (ammunition and other explosives) by the minimum distance specified in the IATA DGR segregation table, and shall not be loaded in direct contact with dry ice packages due to the risk of low-temperature battery venting behavior; a minimum one-package buffer or physical barrier is required.

8.4. Ammunition (Division 1.4S) shall be loaded away from any Class 3 flammable liquid, including aviation fuel in drums, by the full segregation distance in the IATA DGR Table 9.3.A, and shall never be loaded in the same net or pallet position as fuel drums.

8.5. Aviation fuel in drums is limited to the quantity per flight specified in the loading tables for the assigned aircraft type and shall be loaded upright, secured against shifting, with drum bungs oriented per the loading tables, and positioned to allow the fastest possible access for removal in an emergency.

8.6. Diagnostic specimens classified as Category B (UN 3373) are not subject to quantity segregation limits from other dangerous goods but shall be positioned to avoid crushing by heavier freight and shall not be loaded adjacent to dry ice unless the specimen packaging itself uses dry ice as a refrigerant, in which case Section 8.2 governs.

8.7. Where a flight's combined dangerous goods tender would exceed any limit in this section or the loading tables, the load planner shall hold the excess for the next available flight and shall notify the shipper through the acceptance agent of record. Load planners shall not split a single package to fit within a limit.

8.8. Load planners shall record the final segregation and location decisions in Hermes as part of the load plan, which becomes the source data for the notification to captain under Section 10.

## 9. Acceptance or Rejection and Documentation

9.1. A shipment shall be accepted only when every item under Sections 6 and 7 has passed both the acceptance agent's check and the DG checker's independent verification, and the shipment fits within the quantity and segregation limits of Section 8 for the assigned flight.

9.2. Where a shipment fails any single item, the acceptance agent shall mark the shipment "Held" in Hermes, not "Rejected," and shall give the shipper the specific reason and, where correctable at the counter, the opportunity to correct it (for example, an omitted emergency phone number or a missing label available at the station).

9.3. Where a shipment cannot be corrected at the counter, the acceptance agent shall mark it "Rejected" in Hermes, generate the Dangerous Goods Rejection Notice, and provide a copy to the shipper. The rejection notice shall state the specific regulatory basis for rejection, reference the failed DG-201 item number(s), and be signed by the acceptance agent and countersigned by the DG checker or, if the DG checker is unavailable, by the station manager.

9.4. Every rejection shall be logged in the Hermes Dangerous Goods Rejection Log, which is reviewed weekly by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager for patterns by shipper, commodity, or station.

9.5. A rejected shipment shall not be re-presented for carriage until the deficiency is corrected and shall then go through the full acceptance process again from Section 6, with a new DG-201; a corrected shipment shall not be waved through on the strength of the earlier partial check.

9.6. Acceptance agents and DG checkers have independent authority to reject a shipment and are not required to obtain station manager or duty officer approval to do so. A station manager may not overrule a rejection without first consulting the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager or, outside business hours, the duty officer.

## 10. Load Planning and Notification to Captain

10.1. On release from the DG checker, the shipment moves in Hermes to the load planner's queue. The load planner shall confirm the quantity and segregation determinations of Section 8 for the specific aircraft and flight, and shall record ULD, net, or compartment position for each dangerous goods package.

10.2. The load planner shall build the notification to captain in Hermes not later than thirty minutes before scheduled departure, listing for each dangerous goods item: proper shipping name, UN number, class and subsidiary risk, packing group, quantity, number of packages, loading location, and any special handling or emergency information relevant to that class (for example, dry ice net weight for ventilation planning, or fuel drum orientation).

10.3. The load planner shall route the notification to captain to the assigned pilot in command through Hermes and, at stations without a printer, by radio or phone read-back, with the pilot's acknowledgment recorded in Hermes before the load is released to ramp handlers.

10.4. Ramp handlers shall load and secure each dangerous goods package in the position shown on the load plan and shall report to the load planner immediately, before departure, any discrepancy between the load plan and the physical shipment, including a package that appears at the ramp but was not on the load plan.

10.5. The pilot in command shall review the notification to captain before departure and has authority to refuse carriage of any item; a refusal shall be recorded in Hermes with the pilot's stated reason and reported to the duty officer.

10.6. No flight carrying dangerous goods shall depart without a completed, acknowledged notification to captain on file in Hermes. The duty officer is the point of escalation if the notification cannot be completed within the thirty-minute window in 10.2, and may authorize a short delay but shall not authorize departure without the notification.

## 11. Discrepancy and Incident Reporting to the FAA

11.1. This procedure defines a reportable event as any of the following: an undeclared or misdeclared dangerous goods shipment discovered at any point in the shipment's handling; a package that leaks, vents, smokes, or shows other evidence of an uncontrolled hazardous reaction at any station, in flight, or on the ramp; loading of a shipment in violation of the segregation or quantity limits of Section 8 that is discovered after loading; or any other event meeting the reporting criteria of 49 CFR 171.15 and 171.16.

11.2. The employee who discovers a reportable event shall immediately notify the duty officer by phone or radio, regardless of time of day, and shall not wait for the next business day or shift change.

11.3. The duty officer shall notify the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager and the Vice President of Operations within two hours of discovery, and shall begin the FAA Form 8020 telephonic notification process required under 49 CFR 171.15 within twelve hours of discovery for events meeting that section's criteria, and no later than the immediate notification deadline in 171.15 for the more severe event categories it defines (fire, violent rupture, serious injury, or death associated with dangerous goods).

11.4. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager shall complete and submit FAA Form 8020-9, the written hazardous materials incident report, within thirty days of discovery, as required by 49 CFR 171.16, and shall retain a copy in the incident file described in Section 14.

11.5. Every reportable event, whether or not it meets the federal reporting threshold, shall also be logged in the internal Hermes Dangerous Goods Incident Log with a description, root cause where determined, and corrective action, and shall be reviewed at the next scheduled dangerous goods compliance meeting chaired by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager.

11.6. A near-miss — an event that did not meet the federal reporting threshold but revealed a procedural gap, such as a package accepted with a marking error later caught by the DG checker — shall be logged in the same manner and included in the quarterly trend review under Section 12, even though no FAA report is filed.

## 12. Training and Qualification Records

12.1. No employee may perform acceptance, checking, load planning, or duty officer functions involving dangerous goods without holding a current dangerous goods training certificate for that function, issued after CACS initial or recurrent training.

12.2. Recurrent training is required no later than twenty-four calendar months from the date of the previous training certificate. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager shall maintain the master training roster in Hermes and shall generate an automated alert to the employee and their station manager sixty days, thirty days, and seven days before an individual's training expiration.

12.3. An employee whose recurrent training has lapsed shall not perform any function covered by Section 5 through Section 10 of this procedure until retrained, without exception for staffing shortages. A station manager who permits a lapsed employee to perform these functions is subject to disciplinary action and the acceptance is subject to the corrective action process in Section 12.5.

12.4. Every DG-201 and every rejection notice shall carry the acceptance agent's and the DG checker's employee identification numbers, which Hermes shall automatically cross-check against the training roster at the moment of entry; Hermes shall block submission of a DG-201 by an employee whose training status is lapsed, and shall require a documented duty officer override, logged separately, for any exception.

12.5. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager shall run a monthly report of all DG-201 and rejection notices signed during the preceding month, cross-referenced against the training roster, to confirm no lapsed-training signature occurred outside a logged override. Any such occurrence shall be treated as a near-miss under Section 11.6.

12.6. Training records, including certificates, test scores, and the master roster, shall be retained for the duration of the employee's employment plus three years, per Section 14.

## 13. Exception Paths

**13.1 Village Stations Without a Certified Scale.** At any village station where the certified scale is out of service or a certified scale has never been installed, the acceptance agent shall use the aircraft's calibrated cargo scale, where fitted, to weigh the shipment; if no calibrated scale of any kind is available, the agent shall record the shipper-declared weight, mark the DG-201 "Weight Unverified — No Certified Scale," and notify the duty officer before acceptance. The duty officer shall authorize acceptance only for shipments where the declared weight is well within the aircraft's dangerous goods quantity limit with a conservative margin, and shall log the authorization. The station manager shall report scale outages to the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager within twenty-four hours, and a scale out of service for more than seven days shall be escalated to the Vice President of Operations for equipment replacement priority.

**13.2 Medevac Charters.** Dangerous goods carried in direct support of a medevac charter (for example, patient-carried oxygen, medical device batteries, or specimens being transported for the same patient) shall be accepted under an expedited path: the acceptance agent shall complete an abbreviated DG-201 covering package marking, quantity limit, and segregation from other cargo on the charter, but the two-hour pre-alert of Section 5.3 and the full documentary review of Section 6 are waived given the time-critical nature of the flight. The DG checker's independent verification is not waived and shall be completed either before departure or, if the checker is not physically present, by phone with the acceptance agent reading each DG-201 item aloud for confirmation, logged as a verbal DG check. The duty officer shall be notified of every medevac dangerous goods acceptance in real time.

**13.3 Damaged Packages Found on the Ramp.** A ramp handler who discovers a package showing damage, leakage, staining, odor, or other abnormal condition after acceptance but before or during loading shall stop handling that package immediately, not move it further than necessary to isolate it from other cargo and from personnel, and notify the load planner and duty officer at once. The duty officer shall direct isolation of the package in the station's designated dangerous goods holding area, away from aircraft and fuel, and shall determine, in consultation with the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager, whether the package can be safely repackaged for continued carriage, must be returned to the shipper, or requires emergency response. The flight shall not depart until the duty officer confirms the package has been removed from the load plan or safely resolved. The event shall be evaluated against the reportable-event criteria in Section 11.1.

**13.4 Undeclared Goods Discovered in a Package Already Accepted.** An employee at any point in the chain — acceptance, checking, load planning, ramp, or destination station — who discovers or reasonably suspects that an accepted package contains an undeclared dangerous good (for example, a battery pack, aerosol, or flammable liquid found during damage inspection or by odor, weight, or shipper admission after the fact) shall immediately stop movement of that package, notify the duty officer, and, if the package is already loaded, notify the pilot in command through the duty officer before departure or, if already in flight, through dispatch to the pilot immediately. The duty officer shall isolate the package on the ground following the same holding-area protocol as Section 13.3. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager shall determine within twenty-four hours whether the item meets the criteria for FAA reporting under Section 11 and shall document the determination in the incident file regardless of outcome. The shipper account shall be flagged in Hermes for a documentation and packaging review before any future shipment from that account is accepted, and repeated undeclared tenders from the same shipper shall be referred to the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager for a determination on whether to suspend the account.

## 14. Records

The following records shall be maintained in Hermes, with a redundant offline backup retained by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager, for a minimum of two years unless a longer period is specified:

- Completed DG-201 checklists and shipper's declarations: two years from the date of shipment, per 49 CFR 175.30.
- Dangerous Goods Rejection Notices and Rejection Log entries: two years.
- Notification to captain records, including pilot acknowledgment: two years.
- FAA Form 8020 telephonic notification records and Form 8020-9 written incident reports: five years.
- Internal Dangerous Goods Incident Log entries, including near-misses: five years.
- Training certificates and the master training roster: duration of employment plus three years.
- Scale calibration certificates and out-of-service logs: three years.
- Quarterly self-audit reports and corrective action records: five years.

The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager shall conduct a quarterly self-audit sampling not fewer than fifty acceptance transactions across Anchorage and the village stations, following the same sampling method used in the 2025 internal audit, and shall report findings and corrective actions to the Vice President of Operations within thirty days of each quarter's close.
