## Board of Directors — Minutes of Regular Meetings
### January 20, February 17, March 17, and April 21, 2026

## Meeting of January 20, 2026

**Body:** Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Board of Directors, Regular Meeting
**Date:** Tuesday, January 20, 2026
**Time:** 6:31 p.m. to 9:12 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, 394 East First Street, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Colleen Rasmussen, Sanjay Mehta, Louis Bergeron

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen, General Manager; Deb Sorenson, Controller; approximately 14 member-owners in the audience

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:31 p.m. Suh called the roll. All nine directors were present, and a quorum was declared.

### 2. Approval of Minutes
Okonkwo moved to approve the minutes of the December 16, 2025 regular meeting as circulated. Suh seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 3. Financial Report
Kozlowski presented the fiscal year 2025 year-end financial statements, prepared with Controller Sorenson. She reported that the Cooperative closed the year with net sales of $18.4 million, essentially flat against fiscal 2024, and an operating loss of $340,000, compared with operating income of $61,000 the prior year. Gross margin had fallen to 34.1 percent from 36.8 percent, which Kozlowski attributed to competitive price reductions taken in the produce and grocery departments beginning in April 2025 in response to the opening of a discount grocer on Central Entrance, together with higher inbound freight and shrink. She noted that labor as a percentage of sales had risen from 24.9 percent to 26.3 percent. Unrestricted cash stood at $460,000 at year-end, and the Cooperative had drawn $180,000 against its $750,000 line of credit with North Shore Federal Credit Union. Kozlowski stated that the annual audit engagement letter with Hendrickson & Vue, CPAs, had been signed and that fieldwork would begin in February. Tallbear asked whether the pricing reductions would continue into 2026; Haugen responded that department managers were reviewing category-by-category margin recovery opportunities. The report was accepted as information; no vote was required.

### 4. General Manager's Report
Haugen reported that comparable-store sales for the fourth quarter of 2025 were down 2.1 percent and that transaction counts had declined 3.4 percent while average basket size had risen slightly. He reported current staffing of 112 employees, with two open positions in the deli. He reported that the store's produce cooler compressor had failed in December and been replaced at a cost of $14,200, covered by the equipment reserve. Haugen then introduced, as new business to follow later in the agenda, an opportunity to acquire a vacant 22,000-square-foot former supermarket building at 1140 Belknap Street in Superior, Wisconsin, listed at $4.2 million, which he and Ramanathan had toured in December at the invitation of the listing broker.

### 5. Committee Reports
Kozlowski, for the Finance Committee, reported that the committee had met January 8 to review the draft FY2025 statements prior to their presentation to the full board and recommended the board proceed to the audit. Suh, for the Governance Committee, reported that the committee had begun preparing a slate-development timeline for the June 2026 election, with three board seats up for renewal, and that a call for candidates would go out in the March newsletter. Rasmussen, for the Member Relations Committee, reported plans for the spring annual meeting, tentatively scheduled for late June, and noted ongoing work on a member survey to be fielded in February.

### 6. Old Business
None.

### 7. New Business

**a. Superior, Wisconsin Expansion Proposal.** Haugen presented an overview of the Belknap Street property, including site plan, estimated buildout costs of $650,000, and a preliminary pro forma prepared in-house projecting the second location would require approximately $4.85 million in total capital. Fitzgerald spoke in support of pursuing the opportunity, noting his own experience with commercial property in the Superior Street corridor and his view that a Superior, Wisconsin location would capture cross-border shoppers currently traveling to Duluth. Okonkwo and Kozlowski expressed concern about undertaking a major capital commitment in the same year the Cooperative posted an operating loss. Ramanathan proposed that the board obtain an independent feasibility study before further discussion.

Okonkwo moved that the board table further consideration of the acquisition of the Belknap Street property in Superior, Wisconsin, pending completion of an independent market feasibility study. Tallbear seconded. Discussion followed regarding the scope and cost of such a study. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

**b. Engagement of Feasibility Consultant.** Kozlowski moved that the board authorize the General Manager to engage Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors, and specifically consultant Priya Venkataraman, to conduct a market feasibility study of the proposed Superior, Wisconsin location, at a cost not to exceed $18,500, funded from the FY2026 professional services budget, with a report to the board no later than the February meeting. Mehta seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 8. Public Comment
Three member-owners addressed the board. Harold Pietrzak asked whether a second store would draw staff and inventory away from the Duluth store. Genevieve Okafor spoke in favor of expansion, citing long drives she made to shop in Superior. Ruth Sandvik asked the board to hold a members' forum before any decision was made. Ramanathan thanked the speakers and stated that a forum would be considered once the feasibility study was complete.

### 9. Action Items
- Haugen to execute engagement letter with Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors by January 27, 2026.
- Kozlowski and Finance Committee to review FY2026 budget impact of any expansion scenario, for report at the February meeting.
- Suh to post notice of the feasibility study to the membership by January 30, 2026.
- Rasmussen to finalize member survey questions by February 1, 2026.

### 10. Adjournment
Bergeron moved to adjourn. Fitzgerald seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0. The meeting adjourned at 9:12 p.m.

## Meeting of February 17, 2026

**Body:** Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Board of Directors, Regular Meeting
**Date:** Tuesday, February 17, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m. to 9:47 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, 394 East First Street, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Colleen Rasmussen, Sanjay Mehta, Louis Bergeron

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen, General Manager; Deb Sorenson, Controller; Priya Venkataraman, Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors; approximately 41 member-owners in the audience

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Suh called the roll. All nine directors were present, and a quorum was declared. Ramanathan noted the unusually large audience and reminded attendees of the public comment procedure.

### 2. Approval of Minutes
Tallbear moved to approve the minutes of the January 20, 2026 meeting as circulated. Rasmussen seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 3. Financial Report
Kozlowski presented the January 2026 monthly financial statements. She reported a net loss for the month of $28,000 against a budgeted loss of $9,000, driven by comparable-store sales down 3.2 percent and continued gross margin pressure of 34.4 percent. Unrestricted cash had declined to $410,000, and the line of credit draw had increased to $250,000. Kozlowski stated that Hendrickson & Vue's audit fieldwork was underway and no material issues had been identified to date. Mehta asked whether the January decline reflected typical seasonality; Kozlowski said January was traditionally soft but that the year-over-year decline exceeded historical patterns.

### 4. General Manager's Report
Haugen reported that both open deli positions had been filled. He reported that the produce department had begun a modest margin-recovery initiative, restoring approximately half of the prior year's price reductions on select high-velocity items, with early results showing no measurable sales decline. He reported ongoing conversations with the Duluth Grocers' Cooperative Alliance regarding shared purchasing that could improve margin in dry grocery by an estimated 40 basis points beginning in the second quarter.

### 5. Committee Reports
Suh, for the Governance Committee, reported that four member-owners had expressed interest in the three open board seats and that candidate statements were due March 1. Rasmussen reported the member survey had gone out February 3 with 380 responses received to date, and that a preliminary theme was member concern about pricing and, separately, interest in a second location. Kozlowski, for the Finance Committee, reported it had reviewed a sensitivity analysis of expansion scenarios and would await the consultant's report before further recommendation.

### 6. Old Business

**Feasibility Study Presentation.** Venkataraman presented the findings of the market feasibility study commissioned January 20. She reported that the Superior, Wisconsin trade area supported an estimated $6.1 million in annual natural foods grocery demand, of which she projected the Cooperative could capture $2.8 million to $3.4 million in year one, growing to $4.5 million by year four. She projected the new store would operate at a loss in years one through three, reaching operating break-even in year four, with cumulative losses over that period estimated at $610,000 to $780,000 before turning positive. She stated the $4.2 million purchase price was consistent with comparable commercial sales in the corridor and that total project cost, including buildout, fixtures, and opening inventory, would run approximately $5.1 million, requiring new debt of at least $3.5 million beyond the Cooperative's existing obligations. She identified financing capacity, given the Cooperative's current operating loss, as the principal risk, along with the possibility that a member capital campaign would be needed to close a funding gap.

Extensive board discussion followed. Fitzgerald and Tallbear spoke in favor of proceeding, citing long-term growth and the corridor's underserved demand. Kozlowski and Okonkwo expressed concern that the Cooperative's current financial condition could not absorb several years of losses at a second location while the core store was itself operating at a deficit. Ramanathan stated her view that the risk to the existing store's balance sheet was too great at this time. Suh asked what member capital campaign amount would be required; Venkataraman estimated $900,000 to $1.2 million. Rasmussen noted the member survey results suggested support was not unanimous among the membership. Mehta and Bergeron asked clarifying questions about financing terms.

### 7. New Business

Fitzgerald moved that the Cooperative proceed with the acquisition of the property at 1140 Belknap Street, Superior, Wisconsin, substantially on the terms described in the Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors feasibility study, and that the General Manager be authorized to negotiate a purchase agreement, not to exceed $4.2 million, for subsequent board approval. Tallbear seconded.

Following further discussion, a roll call vote was taken:

- **In favor:** Fitzgerald, Tallbear, Mehta, Bergeron (4)
- **Opposed:** Ramanathan, Okonkwo, Kozlowski, Suh, Rasmussen (5)

The motion failed, 5–4.

Following the vote, Okonkwo moved that the Finance Committee be directed to explore alternative, smaller-scale expansion formats that would require less initial capital, for report back to the board within 60 days. Kozlowski seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 8. Public Comment
Eleven member-owners spoke during the public comment period. Genevieve Okafor and three others urged the board to reconsider and pursue the Superior location, citing convenience and community need. Harold Pietrzak and five others urged caution, citing the Cooperative's current losses. Ruth Sandvik asked that any future expansion proposal be brought to a membership vote rather than decided solely by the board. Ramanathan noted the comment and stated the board would take the request under advisement.

### 9. Action Items
- Finance Committee (Kozlowski, chair) to report on smaller-format expansion alternatives no later than the April meeting.
- Haugen to communicate the outcome of the vote to the membership via newsletter by February 24, 2026.
- Suh to compile candidate statements for the June election by March 1, 2026.
- Rasmussen to complete the member survey summary by March 10, 2026.

### 10. Adjournment
Mehta moved to adjourn. Bergeron seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0. The meeting adjourned at 9:47 p.m.

## Meeting of March 17, 2026

**Body:** Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Board of Directors, Regular Meeting
**Date:** Tuesday, March 17, 2026
**Time:** 6:32 p.m. to 9:58 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, 394 East First Street, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Colleen Rasmussen, Sanjay Mehta

**Members Absent:** Louis Bergeron (excused, out of state)

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen, General Manager; Deb Sorenson, Controller; approximately 60 member-owners in the audience

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:32 p.m. Suh called the roll. Eight of nine directors were present, and a quorum was declared. Ramanathan noted the exceptionally large audience and stated that the public comment period would be conducted under the Cooperative's standard rules, with each speaker allotted two minutes.

### 2. Approval of Minutes
Kozlowski moved to approve the minutes of the February 17, 2026 meeting as circulated. Tallbear seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

### 3. Financial Report
Kozlowski presented the February 2026 monthly financial statements. She reported a net loss for the month of $31,000, comparable-store sales down 2.8 percent, and gross margin of 34.6 percent, a modest improvement attributable to the produce margin-recovery initiative reported in February. Unrestricted cash had declined to $375,000, and the line of credit draw had increased to $340,000 of the $750,000 facility. Kozlowski reported that the FY2025 audit had been completed with no material findings and an unmodified opinion, and that the audited statements would be posted for the membership by April 1. Okonkwo asked about the trend in the line of credit draw; Kozlowski responded that she expected it to continue rising through the second quarter absent a change in sales trend.

### 4. General Manager's Report
Haugen reported on operations, including a successful late-February promotional event that had modestly improved transaction counts. He then stated that, after considerable reflection, he had submitted his resignation as General Manager, effective May 1, 2026, to pursue an opportunity outside the region, and that he had provided the board a written resignation letter dated March 12, 2026, which he read into the record. He stated his intention to assist with an orderly transition through his departure date. The board thanked Haugen for his service. Ramanathan asked that the letter be entered into the permanent record as Exhibit A to these minutes.

### 5. Committee Reports
Suh, for the Governance Committee, reported that three candidates had filed complete statements for the June election and that a fourth interested member-owner had inquired about the vacancy process generally. Rasmussen presented the completed member survey summary: of 612 responses, 54 percent expressed concern about current pricing, 61 percent supported "some form" of a second location if it did not jeopardize the Duluth store's finances, and 340 respondents specifically opposed the Belknap Street proposal as presented. Kozlowski, for the Finance Committee, reported preliminary work on smaller-format alternatives, including a satellite or express-format store of 6,000 to 9,000 square feet, and stated a fuller report would come in April as previously directed.

### 6. Old Business
None. Ramanathan noted that, given the change in management leadership, further old business regarding the Superior, Wisconsin proposal would be addressed under new business.

### 7. New Business

**a. Acceptance of Resignation.** Ramanathan moved that the board accept, with regret, the resignation of General Manager Nils Haugen effective May 1, 2026. Suh seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

**b. General Manager Search Committee.** Okonkwo moved that the board establish a General Manager Search Committee, chaired by Okonkwo, with Rasmussen and Mehta as members, and Sorenson serving in a non-voting staff support role, charged with recommending both an interim staffing arrangement effective May 1 and a process for a permanent search, for report at the April meeting. Tallbear seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

**c. Expansion Proposal Status.** Tallbear moved that the board suspend further consideration of the Belknap Street, Superior, Wisconsin acquisition as previously proposed, and direct the Finance Committee to bring forward, at the April meeting, a revised proposal reflecting a smaller-format alternative consistent with the direction given February 17. Mehta seconded. Discussion touched on the petition described below. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

### 8. Public Comment
The public comment period, extended by unanimous consent from its customary thirty minutes to a full hour given the size of the audience, was occupied in its entirety. Member-owner Solveig Andersen opened by presenting the board with a petition bearing 512 signatures of member-owners opposing "any expansion of the Cooperative to a second location at this time," which she stated had been circulated over the preceding three weeks at the store and at community events. She asked that the petition be entered into the record; Suh confirmed it would be filed as Exhibit B to these minutes. Twenty-two additional member-owners spoke in the time remaining, the substantial majority echoing the petition's concerns about the Cooperative's current financial condition, several specifically citing the reported operating loss and declining margin, and a smaller number urging the board not to abandon the idea of growth altogether but to consider it only once the core store's finances had stabilized. Two speakers, including Geneviey Okafor, reiterated support for a Superior location. Ramanathan thanked the speakers and stated that the petition would be considered in connection with the smaller-format proposal to come before the board in April.

### 9. Action Items
- Suh to file Haugen's resignation letter and the member petition as exhibits to the March 17, 2026 minutes.
- General Manager Search Committee (Okonkwo, chair; Rasmussen; Mehta) to recommend an interim staffing arrangement and permanent search process by the April meeting.
- Finance Committee (Kozlowski, chair) to bring a revised, smaller-format expansion proposal to the April meeting.
- Kozlowski to post the audited FY2025 financial statements to the membership by April 1, 2026.
- Haugen to prepare a transition memorandum for the incoming interim manager by April 15, 2026.

### 10. Adjournment
Fitzgerald moved to adjourn. Okonkwo seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0. The meeting adjourned at 9:58 p.m.

## Meeting of April 21, 2026

**Body:** Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Board of Directors, Regular Meeting
**Date:** Tuesday, April 21, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m. to 10:04 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, 394 East First Street, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald (present for Items 1 through 7; see Item 7c), Colleen Rasmussen, Sanjay Mehta, Louis Bergeron

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen, General Manager; Deb Sorenson, Controller; Denise Aho, Store Operations Manager; approximately 35 member-owners in the audience

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Suh called the roll. All nine directors were present, and a quorum was declared.

### 2. Approval of Minutes
Okonkwo moved to approve the minutes of the March 17, 2026 meeting as circulated, including Exhibits A and B. Rasmussen seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 3. Financial Report
Kozlowski presented the March 2026 monthly financial statements. She reported a net loss for the month of $19,000, an improvement from February attributable to reduced discretionary spending and the continuing margin-recovery initiative; gross margin for the month was 35.0 percent. Unrestricted cash stood at $348,000, and the line of credit draw had risen to $380,000. Kozlowski then addressed the FY2024 patronage dividend, noting that $85,000 remained scheduled for cash distribution to member-owners in May 2026 under the Cooperative's patronage policy. Given the current cash position and the line of credit trend, she recommended the board suspend the cash portion of that distribution and instead retain the amount as member equity, to be revisited at year-end. Okonkwo asked whether this required advance member notice; Kozlowski confirmed notice provisions in the bylaws would be satisfied by posting to the membership within ten days of board action.

### 4. General Manager's Report
Haugen delivered his final report to the board before his May 1 departure. He reported continuing operational stability, thanked the staff for their work through a difficult period, and introduced Denise Aho, the store's Operations Manager since 2019, whom the General Manager Search Committee had identified as the recommended interim manager. The board thanked Haugen for his six years of service.

### 5. Committee Reports

**General Manager Search Committee.** Okonkwo reported that the committee had interviewed two internal candidates and recommended appointing Denise Aho as Interim General Manager effective May 1, 2026, at an interim salary of $88,000 annualized, with the appointment to run through the completion of a permanent search expected to conclude by early autumn. He further recommended the committee's charge be expanded to include the permanent search, with an outside recruiter to be engaged if a qualified pool did not emerge internally within 90 days.

**Finance Committee.** Kozlowski reported that the committee, pursuant to the direction given February 17 and March 17, had developed a revised smaller-format proposal: an approximately 7,500-square-foot store to be leased, rather than the Belknap Street property purchased outright, at a different site on Tower Avenue in Superior, Wisconsin, with estimated buildout and opening costs of $1.1 million financed primarily through a combination of a $700,000 term loan and $400,000 in existing reserves, with no member capital campaign required. She stated the committee's preliminary analysis suggested a substantially shorter path to break-even, estimated at 18 to 24 months, given the lower fixed-cost base, but that the committee recommended further due diligence, including a lease negotiation and updated sales projections from Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors, before any commitment.

**Governance Committee.** Suh reported that four candidates remained on the June ballot and that the vacancy created by Fitzgerald's resignation, addressed later in the meeting, would be added to the ballot as a fourth open seat, subject to board action.

### 6. Old Business

Tallbear moved that the board accept the Finance Committee's revised smaller-format proposal for a leased location on Tower Avenue in Superior, Wisconsin, in concept, and refer the matter to a newly constituted ad hoc Expansion Committee, chaired by Kozlowski and including Tallbear and Mehta, to complete due diligence, including an updated feasibility assessment from Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors, and to report back to the full board no later than the July meeting, with no purchase, lease, or other binding commitment to be made without further board approval. Mehta seconded. Discussion touched on the March 17 petition; Ramanathan noted the smaller-format approach was intended, in part, to respond to member concern about financial risk while preserving the possibility of growth. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

### 7. New Business

**a. Interim General Manager Appointment.** Okonkwo moved that the board appoint Denise Aho as Interim General Manager of the Cooperative, effective May 1, 2026, at an annualized salary of $88,000, reporting directly to the board through the President, with the appointment to continue until a permanent General Manager is hired. Bergeron seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

**b. Suspension of Patronage Dividend.** Kozlowski moved that the board suspend the scheduled cash distribution of the $85,000 FY2024 patronage dividend, retaining the amount as member equity pending review of the Cooperative's financial position at the close of FY2026, and direct staff to post notice of this action to the membership within ten days as required by the bylaws. Suh seconded. Okonkwo asked whether affected member-owners could inquire individually about their allocated amounts; Kozlowski confirmed the Controller's office would respond to such inquiries. The motion carried unanimously, 9–0.

**c. Resignation of Director Owen Fitzgerald.** Fitzgerald stated that, owing to the demands of his commercial property business and family obligations, he was resigning from the board effective immediately, and he read a brief letter of resignation into the record, which Suh confirmed would be filed as Exhibit C to these minutes. The board thanked Fitzgerald for his service. Fitzgerald then left the meeting at 8:47 p.m.

Ramanathan moved that the board accept, with regret, the resignation of Owen Fitzgerald effective April 21, 2026, and declare the resulting vacancy on the board. Tallbear seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0, with eight directors present for the vote.

Suh then moved that the vacancy be added as a fourth open seat on the June 2026 ballot, to be filled by member election for the remainder of the unexpired term, rather than filled by board appointment in the interim, and that the Governance Committee amend the candidate call accordingly. Okonkwo seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

### 8. Public Comment
Nine member-owners spoke. Several asked about the status of the petition delivered in March; Ramanathan explained the smaller-format proposal and its referral to the Expansion Committee. Two speakers asked about the patronage dividend suspension and its effect on individual member equity accounts; Kozlowski responded that individual allocations were unaffected in amount, only in timing of cash payout. One speaker asked about the process for filling Fitzgerald's board seat; Suh described the June ballot process just adopted.

### 9. Action Items
- Sorenson and Aho to prepare a transition plan for the Interim General Manager's first 90 days, for report at the May meeting.
- Suh to post notice of the patronage dividend suspension to the membership by May 1, 2026.
- Suh to amend the June election candidate call to include the fourth vacant seat by April 28, 2026.
- Ad hoc Expansion Committee (Kozlowski, chair; Tallbear; Mehta) to engage Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors for an updated feasibility assessment of the Tower Avenue location and report to the board no later than the July meeting.
- Suh to file Fitzgerald's resignation letter as Exhibit C to these minutes.

### 10. Adjournment
Rasmussen moved to adjourn. Mehta seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0. The meeting adjourned at 10:04 p.m.

## Meeting of May 19, 2026

**Body:** Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Board of Directors, Regular Meeting
**Date:** Tuesday, May 19, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m. to 9:21 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, 394 East First Street, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Colleen Rasmussen, Sanjay Mehta, Louis Bergeron

**Members Absent:** None. (Board seat vacant following resignation of Owen Fitzgerald, April 21, 2026.)

**Others Attending:** Denise Aho, Interim General Manager; Deb Sorenson, Controller; approximately 19 member-owners in the audience

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call
Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Suh called the roll. Eight of the board's eight current seats were filled and present, constituting a quorum. Ramanathan noted for the record that the ninth seat remained vacant pending the June election, as directed at the April 21 meeting.

### 2. Approval of Minutes
Kozlowski moved to approve the minutes of the April 21, 2026 meeting as circulated, including Exhibit C. Tallbear seconded. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

### 3. Financial Report
Kozlowski presented the April 2026 monthly financial statements. She reported a net loss for the month of $11,000, the smallest monthly loss since the previous August, on comparable-store sales down 1.4 percent, an improved trend from the 2.8 to 3.2 percent declines reported earlier in the year. Gross margin for the month was 35.3 percent. Unrestricted cash stood at $362,000, and the line of credit draw had held steady at $380,000, which Kozlowski characterized as a stabilizing sign after four months of steady increases. She reported that notice of the suspended patronage dividend had been posted to the membership on April 29, as directed, and that the Controller's office had fielded 23 individual member inquiries, all of which had been resolved by explaining that allocated equity amounts were unchanged. Okonkwo asked whether the improved trend was attributable to the produce and grocery margin-recovery initiatives or to broader seasonal factors; Kozlowski responded that staff believed it reflected both, with the initiatives contributing an estimated 60 basis points and seasonal spring produce sales contributing the remainder.

### 4. General Manager's Report
Aho, delivering her first report as Interim General Manager, thanked the board for its confidence and reported that the transition from Haugen had proceeded without disruption to store operations. She reported current staffing of 110 employees, with two vacancies in grocery stocking currently posted. She reported that she had met individually with each department manager during her first two weeks and identified labor scheduling efficiency as an early opportunity, estimating a potential 20-basis-point improvement in labor cost as a percentage of sales by the third quarter through revised scheduling software already licensed but underutilized. She stated she intended to bring a fuller 90-day operational assessment to the board in June, consistent with the transition plan directed at the April meeting.

### 5. Committee Reports

**General Manager Search Committee.** Okonkwo reported that the committee, now charged with the permanent search as directed April 21, had drafted a position description and compensation range, benchmarked against three comparably sized Upper Midwest cooperatives, and recommended posting the position in early June with an outside recruiter engaged only if an insufficient pool emerged by August 1. Rasmussen asked whether Aho would be eligible to apply for the permanent position; Okonkwo confirmed she would be, at her discretion, and that the committee would manage any resulting conflict of interest by having Aho recuse herself from search committee deliberations, which she had already done.

**Ad Hoc Expansion Committee.** Kozlowski reported that the committee had engaged Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors, and Priya Venkataraman specifically, to update the feasibility work for the smaller-format Tower Avenue location in Superior, Wisconsin, as directed April 21, at a cost not to exceed $9,500. She reported that a site visit and initial lease discussion with the property owner had occurred on May 6, and that updated projections were expected in time for the committee's report at the July meeting, as previously scheduled. Tallbear added that the property owner had indicated willingness to negotiate tenant-improvement allowances that could reduce the Cooperative's buildout costs below the $1.1 million estimate presented in April.

**Governance Committee.** Suh reported that four candidates remained on the ballot for the four open seats — three regular seats and the vacancy created by Fitzgerald's resignation — and that ballots would be mailed to the membership by June 1 in advance of the annual meeting, at which results would be announced. Suh reported no additional candidates had come forward since the April filing deadline extension.

### 6. Old Business
None.

### 7. New Business

**a. Annual Meeting Logistics.** Rasmussen moved that the board set the date of the 2026 annual membership meeting for Tuesday, June 23, 2026, at 6:00 p.m., at the Duluth Depot Great Hall, to accommodate anticipated attendance larger than the store's community room could hold. Mehta seconded. Discussion touched on cost of the venue, approximately $1,400, to be funded from the member relations budget. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

**b. Interim Financial Policy on Discretionary Spending.** Kozlowski moved that, pending further improvement in the Cooperative's cash position, all discretionary capital expenditures in excess of $10,000 require prior board approval rather than General Manager authorization alone, a threshold reduced from the existing $25,000 delegated authority. Okonkwo seconded. Aho stated she supported the tightened threshold as a prudent interim measure. The motion carried unanimously, 8–0.

### 8. Public Comment
Six member-owners spoke. Two asked about the timing of the July report on the Tower Avenue proposal; Ramanathan confirmed the Expansion Committee's report would be presented at the regular July meeting and would be posted to the membership in advance.
