# ROCK RIVER LOGISTICS GROUP
## Cutover Steering Committee — Minutes of Meeting

## Meeting 1

**Date:** September 4, 2025
**Time:** 9:00 a.m. – 10:47 a.m., Central Time
**Place:** Rock River Logistics Group Headquarters, Rockford, Illinois, Board Room A, with video connection to the Janesville Distribution Center

**Members Present:** Yolanda Brissette, Chief Operating Officer (Chair); Sanjay Mirchandani, Vice President of Technology; Tomas Escalante, Site Director, Janesville; Freya Lindqvist, Director of Transportation; Anders Wahlgren, Program Manager

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Rebecca Dziedzic, Engagement Lead, Meridian Yard Systems (non-voting); Marisol Duarte, Recording Secretary

---

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Ms. Brissette called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. and Ms. Duarte confirmed a quorum of all five voting members present.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes**

The minutes of the August 21, 2025 meeting were approved without objection on a motion by Mr. Mirchandani, seconded by Ms. Lindqvist.

**3. Standing Status Report**

Mr. Wahlgren presented the standing program status report. He rated the overall program status Red, downgraded from Amber at the prior meeting. He reported that of the $6.8 million contract value, $5.1 million had been invoiced to date against completed milestones, with $1.7 million remaining, including a $680,000 retention held pending final acceptance. He stated that the September 15, 2025 go-live date, itself the second revision from an original March 2025 target and a subsequent July 2025 target, was at risk based on the Janesville pilot results to be presented under Item 4.

**4. Janesville Pilot Readiness and Defect Metrics**

Mr. Escalante presented pilot results from the Janesville distribution center, which had been operating the Meridian Yard Systems warehouse management system in parallel with legacy processes since August 11, 2025. He reported pick accuracy of 62 percent against the contractual standard of 99.2 percent. He reported 218 open defect tickets, broken down as 22 Severity 1 (critical, no workaround), 89 Severity 2 (major, workaround available), and 107 Severity 3 (minor). He reported that the site had hired 14 temporary workers, at an average loaded cost of $22 per hour, to perform manual cycle counts and re-picks required to compensate for system errors, and that this staffing had not been budgeted in the original business case. Ms. Lindqvist added that outbound trailer loading had been delayed an average of 94 minutes per shift for the preceding two weeks as a direct consequence of pick errors.

**5. Vendor Response**

Ms. Dziedzic, on behalf of Meridian Yard Systems, attributed the pick accuracy shortfall principally to slotting logic misconfiguration and to RF scanner session timeouts that she said Meridian's development team had reproduced and was actively patching. She stated that Meridian expected to close 60 percent of open Severity 1 and Severity 2 tickets within two weeks and committed to providing a written root-cause report. Mr. Mirchandani asked whether the scanner timeout issue was a licensed third-party component defect or custom code; Ms. Dziedzic stated she would confirm in writing.

**6. Discussion**

Members discussed whether to proceed with the September 15 go-live date. Ms. Brissette noted that proceeding at 62 percent pick accuracy risked customer service failures at a site serving several key accounts. Mr. Escalante stated that Janesville could not sustain the temporary staffing model into peak season. Mr. Wahlgren recommended holding the go-live pending a defect burn-down to at least 90 percent pick accuracy.

**7. Motion and Vote**

Ms. Lindqvist moved to hold the September 15, 2025 go-live date and to require a defect burn-down plan and updated readiness metrics at the next meeting. Mr. Mirchandani seconded. The motion carried 5–0. Ms. Brissette, Mr. Mirchandani, Mr. Escalante, Ms. Lindqvist, and Mr. Wahlgren voted in favor. None opposed.

**8. Action Items**

- Anders Wahlgren to prepare an updated defect burn-down plan with weekly milestones, due September 11, 2025.
- Rebecca Dziedzic to deliver a written root-cause report on the pick accuracy shortfall and RF scanner timeout defect, due September 10, 2025.
- Tomas Escalante to report daily pick accuracy trend data and temporary staffing cost to date, due September 8, 2025.
- Sanjay Mirchandani to obtain written confirmation from Meridian on the ownership (licensed component versus custom code) of the scanner timeout defect, due September 9, 2025.

**9. Next Meeting**

The next meeting was set for September 18, 2025.

**10. Adjournment**

Ms. Brissette adjourned the meeting at 10:47 a.m.

## Meeting 2

**Date:** September 18, 2025
**Time:** 9:00 a.m. – 11:32 a.m., Central Time
**Place:** Rock River Logistics Group Headquarters, Rockford, Illinois, Board Room A

**Members Present:** Yolanda Brissette (Chair); Sanjay Mirchandani; Tomas Escalante; Freya Lindqvist; Anders Wahlgren; and, invited pursuant to Section 4.3 of the Committee charter for matters concerning contract remedies, Marguerite Okonkwo-Voss, Chief Executive Officer, and Desmond Achterberg, General Counsel

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Rebecca Dziedzic, Engagement Lead, Meridian Yard Systems (non-voting, excused during closed session); Marisol Duarte, Recording Secretary

---

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Ms. Brissette called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. She noted for the record that Ms. Okonkwo-Voss and Mr. Achterberg had been invited as voting members for this meeting under the Committee's charter provision permitting expansion of voting membership for agenda items concerning contract remedies, given the change order dispute and cure and termination discussion scheduled under Items 5 and 6.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes**

The minutes of the September 4, 2025 meeting were approved without objection on a motion by Mr. Escalante, seconded by Ms. Lindqvist.

**3. Standing Status Report**

Mr. Wahlgren reported the program status remained Red. Pick accuracy at Janesville had improved to 68 percent, still well below the 99.2 percent contractual standard. Open defect tickets had declined to 196, comprising 15 Severity 1, 81 Severity 2, and 100 Severity 3. Temporary staffing at Janesville had increased from 14 to 21 workers in anticipation of peak-season volume, at an incremental monthly cost of approximately $61,000. Mr. Wahlgren stated the September 15 go-live had not occurred, consistent with the prior meeting's decision, and no new go-live date had been set.

**4. Vendor Response on Root-Cause Report**

Ms. Dziedzic presented Meridian's written root-cause report, delivered September 10 as directed. She stated the RF scanner timeout defect was in custom middleware written by Meridian, not a licensed component, and that a patch had been deployed to a test environment on September 15 pending validation. She reported 61 percent of Severity 1 and 2 tickets from the September 4 meeting had been closed, short of the 60 percent target only in absolute terms because new tickets had been opened in the interim.

**5. Disputed Change Order**

Mr. Mirchandani presented a $410,000 change order submitted by Meridian on September 11 for what Meridian characterized as "additional configuration services" related to slotting logic customization at Janesville. He stated Rock River's position was that this work fell within the original statement of work and that, in any event, work performed to remediate defects should not be separately billable under the contract's warranty provisions. Ms. Dziedzic stated Meridian's position that the slotting logic customization requested by Janesville exceeded the baseline configuration scope defined in Exhibit C of the agreement and that Meridian considered the amount properly due.

**6. Discussion of Cure and Termination Provisions**

Mr. Achterberg reviewed Section 12.3 of the master services agreement, which defines a cure period of 30 days following written notice specifying material defects, after which the Company may terminate for cause if the defects remain uncured. He noted that issuing a cure notice would start a contractual clock and could affect ongoing negotiation of the disputed change order.

**7. Closed Session**

On a motion by Ms. Brissette, seconded by Mr. Mirchandani, the Committee voted 5–0 (the two invited members not voting on procedural motions) to enter closed session under the Committee's charter to discuss contract remedies with counsel. Ms. Dziedzic and Ms. Duarte were excused at 10:14 a.m. The Committee returned to open session at 10:51 a.m. Ms. Duarte recorded that the closed session addressed potential reservation-of-rights language, the relative merits of issuing an immediate cure notice versus continuing informal remediation, and litigation risk associated with the disputed change order; the substance of counsel's advice was not recorded in these minutes.

**8. Motions and Votes**

Mr. Achterberg moved that the Company withhold payment on the disputed $410,000 change order pending further technical and contractual review, and that General Counsel prepare a reservation-of-rights letter to Meridian. Ms. Lindqvist seconded. The motion carried 7–0, with Ms. Brissette, Mr. Mirchandani, Mr. Escalante, Ms. Lindqvist, Mr. Wahlgren, Ms. Okonkwo-Voss, and Mr. Achterberg all voting in favor.

Ms. Brissette moved that the Committee defer issuance of a formal cure notice under Section 12.3 and instead permit Meridian a 30-day informal remediation period beginning September 18, 2025, with progress reviewed at the October 2 meeting. Mr. Escalante seconded. The motion carried 6–1, with Mr. Achterberg dissenting on the ground that the informal period unnecessarily delayed the Company's contractual remedies.

**9. Action Items**

- Desmond Achterberg to prepare a reservation-of-rights letter regarding the disputed change order, due September 25, 2025.
- Anders Wahlgren to track and report defect burn-down against the informal remediation period, weekly beginning September 22, 2025.
- Rebecca Dziedzic to provide Meridian's documentation supporting the change order scope claim, due September 26, 2025.
- Sanjay Mirchandani to validate the RF scanner middleware patch in the test environment and report results, due September 24, 2025.

**10. Next Meeting**

The next meeting was set for October 2, 2025.

**11. Adjournment**

Ms. Brissette adjourned the meeting at 11:32 a.m.

## Meeting 3

**Date:** October 2, 2025
**Time:** 9:00 a.m. – 11:15 a.m., Central Time
**Place:** Rock River Logistics Group Headquarters, Rockford, Illinois, Board Room A, with video connection to the Eau Claire Distribution Center

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Ms. Brissette called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. Ms. Duarte noted Ms. Okonkwo-Voss's absence and confirmed a quorum of the remaining voting members.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes**

The minutes of the September 18, 2025 meeting were approved without objection on a motion by Mr. Mirchandani, seconded by Mr. Escalante.

**3. Standing Status Report**

Mr. Wahlgren reported the program status remained Red. Janesville pick accuracy had risen to 71 percent, against the 99.2 percent standard. Open defect tickets stood at 178, comprising 11 Severity 1, 74 Severity 2, and 93 Severity 3. He reported that the informal 30-day remediation period authorized September 18 was ongoing, with 16 business days elapsed of 30.

**4. Eau Claire Deployment**

Mr. Wahlgren reported that the planned October 6, 2025 go-live at the Eau Claire distribution center had been deferred indefinitely. He stated that Meridian's implementation team remained fully committed to Janesville remediation and could not adequately support a second concurrent site, and that Eau Claire's own readiness assessment showed pick accuracy of 58 percent in limited parallel testing. Mr. Escalante stated that deferring Eau Claire was necessary to avoid replicating Janesville's failure mode at a second site.

**5. Customer Impact and Chargeback**

Ms. Lindqvist reported that the Company's largest customer, representing approximately 31 percent of consolidated revenue, had issued a $290,000 chargeback on October 1, 2025, citing repeated late outbound shipments and inventory discrepancies traced to the Janesville pilot. She stated the customer had placed its master services agreement with the Company on notice, with a 60-day cure period of its own running from October 1. Mr. Achterberg stated that the customer's notice referenced specific service-level failures for the weeks of September 8 and September 15.

**6. Vendor Response**

Ms. Dziedzic acknowledged the customer impact and stated Meridian would deploy two additional senior configuration engineers to Janesville at no additional charge for a period of four weeks. She proposed that Meridian withdraw the disputed $410,000 change order in exchange for the Company agreeing not to escalate a formal cure notice during the informal remediation period. Mr. Achterberg stated the Company would consider the proposal but would not commit to forgoing contractual remedies in exchange for withdrawal of a change order the Company disputed on its merits.

**7. Discussion**

Members discussed whether the informal remediation period should be shortened given the customer chargeback and contract-on-notice status. Ms. Brissette stated that the November 1 peak-season change freeze, after which no further system changes could be made until January, made the timing of any go-live or cure decision urgent. Mr. Wahlgren recommended the Committee reserve a decision on formal cure notice for the October 16 meeting, by which time the 30-day informal period would have concluded.

**8. Motion and Vote**

Mr. Escalante moved to formally ratify the deferral of the Eau Claire deployment with no new date set pending resolution of Janesville, and to direct that Eau Claire remain on the legacy system until further Committee action. Ms. Lindqvist seconded. The motion carried 6–0.

**9. Action Items**

- Freya Lindqvist to provide a written summary of the customer chargeback notice and service-level failure documentation, due October 6, 2025.
- Desmond Achterberg to respond in writing to Meridian's proposal to withdraw the change order, due October 8, 2025.
- Anders Wahlgren to prepare a decision memo on formal cure notice for the October 16 meeting, including a re-baseline schedule option, due October 14, 2025.
- Tomas Escalante to prepare a plan for sustaining Eau Claire on the legacy system through year-end, due October 9, 2025.

**10. Next Meeting**

The next meeting was set for October 16, 2025.

**11. Adjournment**

Ms. Brissette adjourned the meeting at 11:15 a.m.

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

He reported that the Meridian system remained suspended from production use but that defect remediation work continued in a non-production environment for purposes of the cure period. He cautioned that because the system was not in live production, pick accuracy could not be independently verified against operational data and that any figures reported by Meridian should be treated as vendor-supplied pending the Company's own validation testing.

Mr. Achterberg reported that the formal cure notice under Section 12.3 had been delivered to Meridian Yard Systems on October 17, 2025, as directed, and that the 30-day cure period would expire November 16, 2025. ## Meeting 6

**Date:** November 13, 2025
**Time:** 9:00 a.m. – 12:22 p.m., Central Time
**Place:** Rock River Logistics Group Headquarters, Rockford, Illinois, Board Room A

---

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Ms. Brissette called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. Ms. Duarte confirmed all seven voting members present.

**2. Approval of Prior Minutes**

The minutes of the October 30, 2025 meeting were approved without objection on a motion by Ms. Lindqvist, seconded by Mr. Mirchandani.

**3. Standing Status Report**

Mr. Wahlgren reported the program status remained Red, though he noted improving trend lines across all tracked metrics. He reported that joint validation testing with Meridian had run from November 5 through November 12 in a live parallel environment at Janesville, with the final consolidated report due November 14. Based on data through November 12, he reported pick accuracy of 91 percent against the 99.2 percent contractual standard, and 74 open defect tickets, comprising 3 Severity 1, 28 Severity 2, and 43 Severity 3. He stated the legacy system remained the system of record for all six sites and that Meridian's system had operated in parallel, non-production mode only.

**4. Validation Testing Results**

Mr. Mirchandani presented the Company's independent review of the testing conducted under the October 30 motion. He stated that Company personnel, rather than Meridian staff, had performed 60 percent of the test picks and had independently verified accuracy calculations. He stated the 91 percent figure was the Company's own measurement, not solely Meridian's reported figure. He noted that the three remaining Severity 1 tickets involved intermittent failures in the interface between the warehouse management system and Ms. Lindqvist's transportation management system, affecting load-planning data for outbound trailers, and that Meridian had committed to resolving those three tickets by November 15.

**5. Cure Notice Determination Criteria**

Mr. Achterberg presented the criteria prepared pursuant to the October 30 action item for determining whether validation results satisfied the October 17 cure notice. He stated the criteria required, at minimum, pick accuracy of 90 percent or greater, sustained over no fewer than five consecutive operating days, with no unresolved Severity 1 defects, all as independently verified by Company personnel. He stated that the November 12 data satisfied the pick accuracy and duration thresholds but that the outstanding Severity 1 interface defects meant the criteria were not yet fully met as of the meeting date. He recommended the Committee defer a final cure determination until the November 14 final report and the resolution of the remaining Severity 1 tickets, and proposed a special meeting on November 16, 2025, the final day of the cure period, to make that determination.

**6. Vendor Response**

Ms. Dziedzic stated Meridian was confident the three remaining Severity 1 tickets would close by November 15 and requested that, in the event the cure criteria were met, the Committee also take up withdrawal or reduction of the disputed $410,000 change order at the same meeting, given the resource commitment Meridian had made since October 2. Mr. Achterberg stated the change order would be addressed on its own timeline and was not linked to the cure determination.

**7. Customer Relationship Update**

Ms. Lindqvist reported that weekly written updates had been provided to the customer that issued the October 1 chargeback on November 3 and November 10, consistent with the October 30 motion. She stated the customer's own cure period ran through December 1, 2025, and that the customer had informally indicated it would await the outcome of the Company's cure determination with Meridian before deciding whether to withdraw the chargeback or reinstate full account status.

**8. Re-Baseline Plan**

Mr. Wahlgren presented the finalized re-baseline plan directed at the October 16 and October 30 meetings. The plan proposed a target go-live date of February 9, 2026 at Janesville, contingent on successful resolution of the cure notice, followed by sequential go-lives at the remaining five sites at three-week intervals through April 2026. He estimated total incremental cost of the re-baseline, inclusive of extended vendor support, additional internal program staffing, and continued contingency for temporary labor, at $1.24 million, consistent with his October 30 preliminary estimate. Ms. Okonkwo-Voss asked whether the plan assumed favorable resolution of the change order dispute; Mr. Wahlgren confirmed it did not, and that the $1.24 million estimate was exclusive of any change order outcome.
