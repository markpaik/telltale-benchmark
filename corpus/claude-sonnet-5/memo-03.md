# RIVERBEND COMMUNITY SERVICES
## Internal Memo Packet — Sycamore Street Family Center Absorption
### April–May 2027

---

**TO:** All Riverbend Staff
**FROM:** Ayesha Nkemelu, Executive Director
**DATE:** April 20, 2027
**RE:** Board Vote to Absorb Sycamore Street Family Center

I am writing to tell you directly, before you hear it secondhand, that on April 15 the Board of Directors voted 9 to 4 to bring Sycamore Street Family Center into Riverbend, effective July 1. I want to explain why the board acted, what it means for Sycamore's staff and clients, and what it does not yet mean for the rest of you.

Sycamore has served Manchester families for eighteen years. Two weeks ago its executive director told our board, in a meeting I attended at Miriam Rothstein's invitation, that Sycamore could not make its next payroll. The agency carries $480,000 in deferred payables to vendors and its own retirement plan, two facility leases that run through 2030, and 620 open client cases in housing stabilization, family support, and after-school programming. Sycamore's board asked ours to consider a merger rather than let the agency close outright and scatter those cases across a county with no one positioned to receive them on short notice.

The board did not take this vote lightly, and I want to be honest that it was not unanimous. Four members voted no, largely over the financial exposure at a moment when we are already absorbing a $3.1 million cut to our resettlement reimbursements and working through the corrective action plan from our FY26 audit. I take those concerns seriously, and I will say more about them in a separate memo to the board that I expect will circulate more broadly once it's finalized.

Here is what the vote means concretely. Sycamore's 40 employees will become Riverbend employees on July 1. Every one of Sycamore's direct service staff — case managers, the two family support specialists, the after-school coordinators — will be offered a position here at their current salary, with no gap in benefits. We are not using this transition to eliminate front-line service capacity in Manchester. Sycamore's direct service staff are represented by SEIU Local 32BJ; ours are not. That difference does not resolve itself with a memo, and I don't want to pretend otherwise. Hector Bonilla and I have retained outside labor counsel to determine our obligations as a potential successor employer, and we have committed to Sycamore's union that we will not make unilateral changes to wages or working conditions for those employees while that question is being worked out. I will have more to say about this once we've heard back from counsel.

Sycamore's two program directors have resigned in the past two weeks, which is not unusual in a transition like this and does not reflect on the staff who remain. Sunny Trinh is developing a plan for how those vacancies are covered and how the 620 active cases move over without a lapse in service; you will hear from her directly by the end of next week.

I know this raises the obvious question of what it costs us and whether it touches your own position. It does. Hector is finalizing a revised FY28 budget that accounts for both the federal cut to resettlement and the cost of this absorption, and it will include the elimination of 14 positions across the agency. That is a hard sentence to write to 180 people, and I am not going to soften it. I do not yet know which 14 positions, and I will not know until Hector's analysis is complete and I have reviewed it with program directors. No one should assume anything about their own role based on this memo. I will tell you as soon as I know something real to tell you, and I will not let rumor fill that space if I can help it.

I recognize that a lot is landing at once — a federal funding cut, an audit finding, a merger, and layoffs, all within a few months of each other. I don't think minimizing that helps anyone. What I can tell you is that the board's decision was made in the belief that closing Sycamore's doors and leaving 620 families with nowhere to go was a worse outcome than the difficulty of integrating it, and that Riverbend is financially positioned to make this work if we manage it carefully. I will hold an all-staff call on May 4 to take questions directly. In the meantime, please direct anything urgent to your program director rather than to Sycamore staff, who have enough uncertainty of their own right now.

**TO:** Sunny Trinh, Director of Programs
**FROM:** Hector Bonilla, Chief Financial Officer
**DATE:** May 3, 2027
**RE:** Revised FY28 Budget — Resettlement Reimbursement Loss, Sycamore Absorption, and Position Eliminations

Ayesha asked me to circulate the revised FY28 budget to program directors ahead of the May 12 board finance committee meeting, with enough detail that you can plan around it rather than be surprised by it. This memo walks through the three changes driving the revision: the federal reimbursement cut, the Sycamore absorption, and the resulting position eliminations.

**Resettlement Reimbursement.** In March, our federal resettlement funder notified us of a change to the per-case reimbursement formula, effective with 90 days' notice, that reduces our resettlement revenue line by $3.1 million on an annualized basis against a program that previously ran just under $6.8 million. This is not a one-time cut we can bridge with reserves; it is a structural change to how the program is funded going forward, and the FY28 budget treats it as permanent unless and until we hear otherwise. I want to be direct that this single change accounts for the great majority of the budget gap this memo addresses, and that the Sycamore absorption, while significant, is the smaller of the two pressures.

**Sycamore Absorption — Balance Sheet Items.** Effective July 1, Riverbend assumes Sycamore's $480,000 in deferred payables. Of that, roughly $310,000 is owed to vendors and contractors and can be worked into a payment plan over the first two quarters of FY28 without disrupting cash flow; the remaining $170,000 is an underfunded obligation to Sycamore's staff retirement plan that we are required to true up within the plan year. We are also assuming two facility leases — the Manchester main site and a satellite location — running through 2030, at a combined annual cost of $214,000, which is now built into the FY28 facilities line and continues at that level for three years absent renegotiation. Sycamore's revenue — state DCF contracts and a Manchester-based foundation grant totaling approximately $1.9 million — transfers with the program and largely offsets the addition of 40 staff to payroll, though not fully; there is a net absorption cost to Riverbend of approximately $340,000 in FY28 once the payables amortization and lease costs are included.

**Position Eliminations.** Between the resettlement shortfall and the absorption cost, the FY28 budget as revised requires $3.44 million in expense reduction beyond what program growth or new revenue can reasonably be expected to cover in the time available. After review with Ayesha and each program director, the budget eliminates 14 positions agency-wide, effective with the start of FY28:

- Resettlement program: 6 positions (case management and employment services), reflecting the direct scale-down required by the reimbursement change
- Administration and finance: 3 positions, including one vacant grants-accounting role we do not intend to fill
- Development: 1 position (a database/gift-processing role; see Charlene's separate memo on the campaign implications)
- Adult literacy: 2 positions, both part-time instructor roles being consolidated into fewer, full-time positions
- Eviction prevention: 2 positions

No Sycamore direct service positions are included in this list; those roles transfer intact per Ayesha's April 20 memo. I recognize that does not make this list easier for anyone, and I know program directors will each need to work through the individual conversations involved. HR is preparing severance and transition packages consistent with our personnel policy, and Ayesha has asked that no notifications go out to affected staff before May 20, once the board has formally adopted the budget.

**Net Position.** With these reductions, the revised FY28 budget projects total expenses of $21.6 million against $20.9 million in projected revenue, a $700,000 operating deficit to be covered from unrestricted reserves, which stood at $2.3 million as of the FY26 audit. That leaves us with roughly 15 weeks of unrestricted reserve coverage after the draw, below our board-adopted minimum of 90 days. I am recommending the finance committee treat FY28 as a bridge year and revisit reserve policy at the FY29 budget cycle, contingent on the development committee's bridge fundraising closing the current-year gap Charlene describes separately. I am available to walk through any of these figures with your teams before the May 12 meeting.

**TO:** Program Managers — Resettlement, Adult Literacy, Eviction Prevention
**FROM:** Sunny Trinh, Director of Programs
**DATE:** April 28, 2027
**RE:** Sycamore Case Transfer Plan and New Subrecipient Monitoring Procedure

This memo covers two things that need to be in place before July 1: how we assign Sycamore's 620 active cases, and the new subrecipient monitoring procedure required by our FY26 audit finding. They are related in one important way — both require documentation discipline we have not consistently practiced, and both are being watched closely by the board and, in the case of monitoring, by our federal cognizant agency.

**Case Transfer.** Sycamore currently carries 620 open cases across three service lines: 210 in housing stabilization, 260 in family support/case management, and 150 in after-school and youth programming. With two of Sycamore's three program directors having resigned, there is no one on the Sycamore side positioned to manage a clean handoff, so we are building the transfer plan from our side.

Each case will be reviewed individually rather than transferred in bulk. Beginning May 10, a team of four Riverbend case managers — two from eviction prevention and two from our existing family services unit — will spend three weeks in Manchester alongside remaining Sycamore staff, reviewing case files, confirming current status, and re-screening for eligibility under our own program guidelines, which differ from Sycamore's in a few respects, particularly around income documentation for housing stabilization. I expect a portion of the 620 — I'd estimate 8 to 12 percent — will not meet our eligibility criteria as currently written; those cases will be referred out rather than closed abruptly, and I want every program manager to identify at least one appropriate referral partner in Manchester before the review begins.

Ongoing case assignment after the review period will follow geography, not program line: Manchester and greater Tolland County cases stay with the retained Sycamore staff and the two family support specialists who accepted transfer, reporting through a Riverbend program manager rather than a Sycamore director, since those positions are not being backfilled. Hartford-adjacent cases already close to our existing footprint may be reassigned to Hartford-based staff where caseload allows. I will hold caseload review sessions with each program manager individually the week of May 17 to finalize assignments; please come with your current caseload numbers so we can see where there is real capacity and where there is not.

**Subrecipient Monitoring — New Procedure.** Separately, and on a related timeline, our FY26 single audit identified a material weakness in how we monitor subrecipients, meaning organizations to which we pass through federal funds rather than spend it directly ourselves. The finding centers on two things: we did not consistently obtain or review subrecipients' annual audits or equivalent financial statements, and we did not document site visits or corrective follow-up when we did conduct them. This is not a new procedure invented for Sycamore — it governs every subrecipient relationship we hold, including the three community organizations that receive pass-through resettlement funds — but Sycamore's absorption raises the profile of the finding, since some of what was previously a subrecipient relationship becomes direct service delivery, which itself needs to be documented as such in our next audit cycle.

Effective immediately, every program manager with subrecipient relationships must maintain the following, which I will confirm in file reviews beginning in June: a current copy of each subrecipient's most recent audit or, for smaller organizations not subject to single audit, reviewed financial statements, on file before any new subaward is issued; a signed site visit report, using the template Hector's office is finalizing this week, at minimum twice per contract year, with any corrective action items tracked to closure and dated; and quarterly financial reports from each subrecipient reconciled against draw requests before payment is released, rather than after, which was our previous practice. I know this adds administrative burden at a moment when we are already stretched, and I don't have a way around that. Hector is bringing on a part-time contract accountant through the fall specifically to support this documentation; details on how to request that support will follow from his office. I'd rather over-communicate this now than have us carry a repeat finding into the FY28 audit.

**TO:** Development Committee
**FROM:** Charlene Whitcomb, Director of Development
**DATE:** May 5, 2027
**RE:** Campaign Shortfall and Bridge Request to Three Family Foundations

I want to give the committee a clear-eyed picture of where the annual campaign stands, because the honest answer is that we are behind in a way that requires a change in strategy, not just more effort along the current path.

As of April 30, we have raised $3.3 million against the $4.2 million goal for the fiscal year ending August 31, which leaves $900,000 to close in the four months remaining. Our historical pattern is that roughly 30 percent of annual campaign dollars come in during the final quarter, driven by a handful of major gifts and the year-end appeal; on that pattern alone we would expect to close somewhere near $3.9 million, not $4.2 million, even without the additional pressure Sycamore and the resettlement cut have put on this year specifically. Two things have changed since we set this goal in September: the $3.1 million resettlement cut became public in March, which has already prompted two individual major donors to ask direct questions about the agency's financial stability rather than simply renewing, and the Sycamore absorption, announced in April, has not yet been positioned to donors as anything other than a risk, when I believe it can be told as a story about capacity and mission, if we tell it deliberately.

I am not going to recommend we revise the goal downward. I think that sends the wrong signal to the board and to major donors at exactly the moment we need confidence in this organization's management, not a lowered bar. Instead, I am asking the committee to approve a bridge request to three family foundations with which we have existing relationships but no current active grant, timed specifically to the operating gap Hector's revised budget describes.

The three foundations are the Beaumont Family Foundation, which funded our eviction prevention program's launch in 2019 and has expressed interest in "systems-level" grants to organizations managing growth; the Kestrel Family Foundation, whose program officer sits on the board of a Manchester civic association and has already asked me informally about Sycamore; and the Willowbrook Family Foundation, a smaller funder with a history of one-time bridge grants to agencies through mergers, having made a similar grant to a food bank consolidation in 2023. I am proposing requests of $250,000 to Beaumont, $150,000 to Kestrel, and $100,000 to Willowbrook, totaling $500,000 against the $900,000 gap, framed explicitly as bridge support tied to the Sycamore integration rather than general operating request, since all three foundations have shown more willingness to fund a defined transition than an ongoing deficit.

That leaves $400,000 to close through the existing campaign — major gifts, the year-end appeal, and a small number of remaining corporate asks. I believe that portion is achievable if we move quickly on donor communication about Sycamore before it becomes something donors hear about secondhand and read as instability rather than growth. I am asking the committee's approval to submit letters of inquiry to all three foundations by May 20, with full proposals to follow within three weeks of any positive response, and I am asking two committee members to make direct introductory calls to Beaumont and Kestrel given existing personal relationships, since a cold LOI to either is less likely to move quickly enough to matter this fiscal year.

I want to flag one complication honestly: Hector's revised budget eliminates one development position, a role currently focused on gift processing and database management. I support that decision given the overall budget picture, but I want the committee to understand it means the remaining development team is executing this bridge campaign, the year-end appeal, and the annual gala planning with three staff instead of four, at the same moment we are asking those three staff to take on a more complex donor narrative than usual. I will need the committee's direct engagement on foundation calls and donor conversations in a way that has not always been necessary in past years.

**TO:** Board of Directors
**FROM:** Ayesha Nkemelu, Executive Director
**DATE:** May 10, 2027
**RE:** Response to Board Dissent and SEIU Local 32BJ Correspondence on Successorship

I am writing to respond directly to the concerns raised by the four board members who voted against the Sycamore absorption, and separately to the letter we received on April 24 from SEIU Local 32BJ regarding successorship obligations for Sycamore's represented staff. Miriam asked that I address both in writing before the May 12 meeting so the full board has the same information going into that discussion.

**On the Dissenting Vote.** As I understand it from the April 15 meeting and follow-up conversations, the four no votes rested on three concerns, and I want to take each seriously rather than treat the vote as settled and behind us.

First, the concern that the board acted without adequate financial due diligence given how little time we had between Sycamore's request and the vote. That is fair criticism of the timeline, even if I believe the underlying decision was right. We had eleven days between Sycamore's initial disclosure and the board meeting, driven by their payroll deadline, not by our preference. Hector's revised budget, now complete, gives the board the fuller financial picture that was not available on April 15 — the $480,000 in payables, the lease obligations through 2030, and the net absorption cost of roughly $340,000 annually. I think it is fair for dissenting members to ask why we didn't insist on more time; my answer is that more time meant Sycamore closing before we could finish evaluating it, and 620 clients losing services with no transition plan at all. I don't think that was a better alternative, but I understand why moving that fast made some board members uncomfortable with the process itself, independent of the outcome.

Second, the concern about financial exposure at a moment when we are already absorbing the resettlement cut. This is the more serious of the two concerns, in my view, because it is not about process but about capacity. Hector's budget shows we can absorb this without breaching covenants on our line of credit, but it does require a reserve draw below our 90-day policy minimum, and it depends on Charlene's committee closing a bridge gap that is not yet closed. I do not want to understate that risk to the board. What I can say is that the alternative — declining the absorption and letting Sycamore close — would not have improved our financial position; it would have left the resettlement cut as our only problem, but it would not have generated any offsetting benefit, and 620 families and 40 staff would have been left with nothing. I believe the board weighed that correctly, but I want dissenting members to know I don't consider their concern about exposure unreasonable — I consider it the central risk we now have to manage well.

Third, one member's stated concern about mission drift — that family support and after-school programming in Manchester sits at some distance from our current three service lines and that absorbing it risks diluting focus rather than adding capacity. I take this seriously as a governance question independent of the Sycamore situation specifically, and I'd like the board's strategic planning committee to take up service-line scope as a standing agenda item this fall, rather than resolve it in this memo.

**On the Union Letter.** SEIU Local 32BJ's April 24 letter asserts that Riverbend, as a successor employer, is obligated to recognize the union and bargain over terms and conditions of employment for the approximately 24 Sycamore employees in the existing bargaining unit, and asks that we commit in writing not to alter wages, hours, or working conditions unilaterally pending that process.

We have retained outside labor counsel, Reyes & Ochoa, to advise us on our obligations under the successorship doctrine established in NLRB v. Burns International and its progeny. Counsel's preliminary view, which I want the board to understand is preliminary, is that Riverbend is likely to be found a successor employer for purposes of a bargaining obligation, given that we are continuing the same operations, in the same location, with substantially the same workforce and no hiatus in service. Successorship in this context generally does not bind us to Sycamore's existing collective bargaining agreement's specific terms, but it does very likely obligate us to recognize and bargain with the union over a new agreement covering that unit, and to refrain from unilateral changes to existing terms in the interim. I have communicated to the union, consistent with counsel's advice, that we do not intend to alter wages or working conditions for transferring bargaining-unit employees before that bargaining process concludes, and I have proposed an initial meeting for the week of May 24.

I recognize this raises a fairness question some board members have already asked me about informally: whether it is sustainable to have one represented workforce and one non-represented workforce doing comparable direct service work under the same roof. I don't have a resolution to offer the board today beyond acknowledging that this is a live question, one that the bargaining process itself may or may not settle, and one I expect to bring back to this board with more specifics once we have completed at least an initial bargaining session. I would rather tell the board honestly that this is unresolved than suggest a false sense of closure ahead of the May 12 meeting.

**Next Steps and What I Am Asking of the Board on May 12.**

I want to be specific about what action, if any, I need from the board at the upcoming meeting, because I don't want this memo to read as background information with no decision attached to it. There are three items.

First, I am asking the board to formally ratify the FY28 budget as revised by Hector, including the reserve draw below policy minimum, with the explicit understanding that this ratification is conditioned on a finance committee check-in at the end of the first quarter to confirm the bridge fundraising Charlene describes is tracking toward closing the gap. If it is not tracking by October, I want the board to have reserved the authority to revisit position eliminations beyond the 14 already identified, rather than discover in January that we are short with no plan in place. I would rather build that checkpoint in now than have the board feel, six months from now, that it approved an open-ended risk.

Second, I am asking the board to authorize me and Hector to continue engaging Reyes & Ochoa through the initial bargaining sessions with SEIU Local 32BJ, and to set a budget authorization for outside counsel not to exceed $40,000 through the end of calendar year 2027. I recognize legal spend is not where anyone wants to see scarce dollars go in a year like this one, but I don't think we can respond credibly to the union's letter, or manage the fairness question raised internally, without competent counsel guiding that process from the start rather than after a dispute has already hardened.

Third, and this is more a request than a formal action item, I am asking the four members who voted against the absorption to stay engaged rather than treat the vote as a closed matter they were outvoted on. Miriam and I have talked about this, and we agree that the board is stronger if the members most skeptical of this decision are the ones asking the hardest questions at each finance committee and program committee meeting between now and the FY28 audit, not because I expect to be proven wrong, but because that scrutiny is exactly what a $22 million agency taking on this much change at once needs from its governing board. I would rather have that disagreement inside the room, in writing, on the record, than have it surface later as a surprise.

I know this is a lot to absorb in one memo, on top of everything the staff memo, Hector's budget, Sunny's case transfer plan, and Charlene's foundation request already put in front of the board this month. I am available to any board member individually before Tuesday if it would help to talk through any of this ahead of the full meeting, and I would rather take those calls now than have questions go unasked in the room on the 12th.
