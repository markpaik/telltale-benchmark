## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the standard method by which Northline Public Schools receives, dates, tracks, evaluates, and acts upon referrals for special education evaluation, from initial referral through eligibility determination and IEP development. It is issued to correct deficiencies identified in the April 2026 state citizen complaint decision, which found that 38 of 112 initial evaluations completed during the 2025-26 school year exceeded the 35-school-day evaluation timeline (average 71 school days), that two middle schools maintained no referral log, and that prior written notice was missing from 22 student files. This procedure implements the corrective action order's requirement for a written, board-level-approved procedure, establishes the monthly timeline reporting process required for twelve months following approval, and defines how compensatory services determinations will be made for students affected by the identified delays.

This procedure applies district-wide and is binding on all staff who receive, process, or act on referrals for special education evaluation, regardless of building, program, or student residency status.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers every referral for an initial special education evaluation received by Northline Public Schools, in any of the three permitted intake forms (SE-100 paper referral, online counseling request, or written parent letter), at any of the district's 19 schools. It covers the point of receipt through eligibility determination and, where the student is found eligible, through development of the initial IEP. It applies to district-resident students enrolled in Northline schools, district-resident students enrolled in approved private schools, out-of-district students whose parents request evaluation while residing within district boundaries, and students for whom a suspected disability is raised for the first time during a disciplinary proceeding.

This procedure does not cover annual IEP reviews, three-year reevaluations, Section 504 eligibility determinations, or the delivery of ongoing special education services once an IEP is in effect; those processes are governed by separate procedures referenced in Section 9.

## 3. Definitions

**Referral** — Any written or electronic communication from a parent, guardian, teacher, building administrator, or other district staff member expressing concern that a student may have a disability and may need special education, submitted through Form SE-100, the online counseling request system, or a parent letter addressed to any Northline staff member.

**School day** — Any day, including a partial day, that students are in attendance at school for instructional purposes, as defined by the district instructional calendar. School days exclude weekends, board-approved holidays, and days when school is closed districtwide for weather or emergency.

**Calendar day** — Every day of the week, including weekends and holidays, unless otherwise specified.

**Date of receipt** — The school day on which a referral, in any of its three permitted forms, is physically or electronically received by any Northline staff member, regardless of which building or department subsequently handles it. The date of receipt starts the 25-school-day timeline described in Section 5.2, irrespective of internal routing delays.

**Prior Written Notice (PWN)** — The written notice, documented on Form SE-101, that the district must provide to a parent within a reasonable time before proposing or refusing to initiate or change the identification, evaluation, or educational placement of a student, or the provision of a free appropriate public education (FAPE).

**Consent** — Written parental agreement, documented on Form SE-102, authorizing the district to conduct an initial evaluation. Consent must be voluntary and may be revoked at any time.

**Evaluation window** — The 35-school-day period, beginning on the date the district receives signed parental consent to evaluate, within which all assessments must be completed and an eligibility meeting held, as tracked on the district timeline dashboard.

**Eligibility meeting** — The meeting of the IEP team, including required participants under WAC 392-172A-03020, at which evaluation results are reviewed and a determination is made as to whether the student meets criteria for one or more of Washington's disability categories and needs specially designed instruction.

**Timeline dashboard** — The district's centralized, records-clerk-maintained tracking system that logs the date of receipt, the 25-school-day decision deadline, the consent date, the 35-school-day evaluation deadline, and the eligibility meeting date for every open referral districtwide.

**Compensatory services** — Additional special education or related services, determined on a student-by-student basis using Form SE-105, intended to remedy delay-related loss of FAPE for students whose evaluations exceeded applicable timelines.

**Building referral log** — The building-level record, maintained by the registrar at each school, of every referral received at that building, cross-referenced to the districtwide timeline dashboard.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Director of Special Education (Yvonne Castellanos)** owns this procedure, approves all exceptions and extensions that fall outside the standard paths described in Section 5.8, reviews the timeline dashboard weekly, prepares the monthly compliance report required under the corrective action order, and convenes the compensatory services determination team for each affected student.

**Deputy Superintendent (Dr. Malia Fonoti)** approves this procedure and any subsequent revision, receives the monthly compliance report, and authorizes staffing and budget decisions necessary to maintain timeline compliance, including contracted evaluation support made necessary by the current school psychologist vacancies.

**Building administrators** are the first point of accountability at each school. They ensure every referral received at their building — regardless of form — is forwarded to the registrar for date-stamping on the same school day it is received, notify the school psychologist or designated evaluation coordinator within one school day of a new referral, and ensure the building referral log is updated weekly and reconciled against the districtwide timeline dashboard.

**School psychologists and speech-language pathologists** conduct assessments, complete evaluation planning worksheets, coordinate multidisciplinary assessment schedules, draft evaluation reports, and participate in eligibility meetings. Each is responsible for entering assessment start and completion dates into the district's IEP system within one school day of the event.

**General education teachers** supply intervention data, classroom observation data, and academic performance documentation within five school days of a written request from the evaluation coordinator, using the standardized intervention data template stored in the IEP system.

**Registrars** date-stamp every referral on the day of receipt regardless of form, enter the referral into Skyward and the building referral log the same day, and route the referral to the building administrator and the records clerk within one school day.

**Records clerk (district office)** maintains the districtwide timeline dashboard, enters every referral within one school day of notification from a registrar, flags referrals approaching the 25-school-day and 35-school-day deadlines at the 15-school-day and 25-school-day marks respectively, and generates the monthly timeline report for the Director of Special Education.

**Interpreters and translation services (contracted and in-house)** are engaged by the evaluation coordinator whenever a family's home language is not English, per Section 5.9.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Receiving and Date-Stamping a Referral, Regardless of Form

5.1.1 A referral may arrive at any Northline school or department in one of three forms: a completed Form SE-100 submitted to the front office, an online counseling request submitted through the district's family portal, or a written parent letter addressed to any staff member expressing concern about a possible disability.

5.1.2 Any staff member who receives a parent letter or verbal request that appears to constitute a referral must hand-deliver or scan it to the building registrar by the end of that same school day. Staff may not hold a referral to "review it first" or route it informally to a teacher or counselor before the registrar has date-stamped it.

5.1.3 The building registrar date-stamps the referral with the actual date of receipt — not the date it reaches the registrar's desk, if different — and enters it into Skyward under the student's record and into the building referral log the same school day.

5.1.4 Every school, without exception, must maintain a building referral log. The log records, at minimum: student name and Skyward ID, date of referral receipt, form of referral (SE-100, online request, or letter), referring party, and date forwarded to the records clerk. Middle schools that previously kept no log — consistent with the finding in the April 2026 complaint decision — must establish this log using the standard district template (Section 9) no later than the effective date of this procedure.

5.1.5 The registrar forwards the date-stamped referral to the building administrator and to the district records clerk within one school day of receipt. The records clerk enters the referral into the timeline dashboard within one additional school day, which starts the 25-school-day clock described in Section 5.2, calculated from the original date of receipt recorded by the registrar — not the date the records clerk makes the dashboard entry.

5.1.6 If a referral arrives through the online counseling request system, the system automatically time-stamps the date of receipt; the records clerk confirms this entry against the timeline dashboard within one school day and notifies the relevant building administrator.

### 5.2 The 25-School-Day Decision to Evaluate

5.2.1 Within 25 school days of the date of receipt, the district must decide either to (a) proceed with an initial evaluation or (b) decline to evaluate.

5.2.2 The building administrator convenes a brief referral review, including the school psychologist or SLP and, where relevant, the referring general education teacher, within 10 school days of receipt to review existing data (attendance, grades, prior interventions, behavior data, health information) and determine whether an evaluation is warranted.

5.2.3 If the team determines evaluation is warranted, the evaluation coordinator prepares Form SE-102 (Consent for Evaluation) and Form SE-101 (Prior Written Notice) for a decision to evaluate, and sends both to the parent no later than the 20th school day following receipt, to allow time for parent response before the 25-school-day decision deadline.

5.2.4 If the team determines evaluation is not warranted, the evaluation coordinator prepares Form SE-101 documenting the decision to decline, including the specific data relied upon, and sends it to the parent no later than the 25th school day following receipt.

5.2.5 The records clerk marks the 25-school-day decision as met or missed in the timeline dashboard on the date the decision is communicated to the parent, not on the date it is made internally.

### 5.3 Consent and Prior Written Notice

5.3.1 Prior written notice is required at two points, at minimum: when the district proposes to evaluate and when the district proposes an eligibility determination and, if applicable, an initial placement. PWN must be documented on Form SE-101 and must include the action proposed or refused, the reasons for it, a description of each evaluation procedure or data source used, and a statement of parent procedural safeguards.

5.3.2 PWN must be provided in the parent's native language or other mode of communication unless it is clearly not feasible to do so, consistent with Section 5.9 of this procedure.

5.3.3 Every PWN issued must be scanned into the student's file in the district's IEP system by the evaluation coordinator within one school day of the date it is sent to the parent. A missing or undated PWN in a student file constitutes noncompliance with this procedure and must be immediately reported to the Director of Special Education.

5.3.4 Consent for evaluation must be documented on Form SE-102 and signed by the parent or eligible adult student. The evaluation coordinator logs the date signed consent is received in Skyward and the timeline dashboard the same school day it is received. This date is the start of the 35-school-day evaluation window described in Section 5.5.

5.3.5 If a parent has not responded to a request for consent within 10 school days of the district's proposal, the evaluation coordinator makes at least two documented attempts to contact the parent (phone and written) before the 25-school-day decision deadline, and documents each attempt in Skyward.

### 5.4 Evaluation Planning and Assignment of Assessors

5.4.1 Upon receipt of signed consent, the school psychologist or SLP assigned to the case completes Form SE-103 (Evaluation Planning Worksheet) within three school days, identifying: suspected disability categories to be assessed, specific assessment instruments and procedures to be used, which assessors will conduct each component (school psychologist, SLP, occupational or physical therapist, general education teacher, or other specialist), and a proposed internal schedule of assessment dates that ends no later than the 30th school day of the 35-school-day window, preserving five school days for report-writing and eligibility meeting scheduling.

5.4.2 The building administrator confirms assessor assignments within two school days of the evaluation planning worksheet's completion, accounting for current staffing. Given the district's current vacancy of 4 of 11 school psychologist positions, the Director of Special Education maintains a standing list of contracted, licensed school psychologists available for overflow assignments; building administrators must request contracted support through the Director's office if no district-employed psychologist can meet the internal schedule set in Section 5.4.1.

5.4.3 The evaluation coordinator requests intervention and observational data from the student's general education teacher(s) using the standard data request template in the IEP system; teachers must return this data within five school days of the request, per Section 4.

5.4.4 All evaluation planning worksheets, consent forms, and assessment instruments used are stored in the district's IEP system, cross-referenced to the student's Skyward record, and are never stored solely in a building-level or individual staff file.

### 5.5 The 35-School-Day Evaluation Window and How It Is Tracked

5.5.1 The evaluation window is 35 school days from the date signed parental consent is received, as recorded in Section 5.3.4. All assessments, the evaluation report, and the eligibility meeting must be completed within this window unless an exception path under Section 5.8 applies.

5.5.2 The records clerk enters the 35-school-day deadline into the timeline dashboard on the same day consent is logged, and the dashboard automatically flags the case at the 15-school-day mark (informational, sent to the evaluation coordinator and building administrator) and at the 25-school-day mark (escalation, sent to the evaluation coordinator, building administrator, and Director of Special Education) if assessments are not yet complete.

5.5.3 Each assessor updates the IEP system with the completion date of their individual assessment component within one school day of completing it. The evaluation coordinator monitors the IEP system weekly to confirm all components are on pace to meet the internal schedule set in Section 5.4.1.

5.5.4 If, at the 25-school-day flag, any assessment component remains incomplete, the building administrator and the Director of Special Education review staffing and determine within two school days whether contracted assessment support is needed to complete the evaluation within the 35-school-day window.

5.5.5 The evaluation coordinator compiles all individual assessment reports into a single evaluation report and distributes it to the IEP team members no later than three school days before the scheduled eligibility meeting, and never later than the 33rd school day of the evaluation window, preserving at least two school days to schedule and hold the eligibility meeting within the 35-school-day window.

5.5.6 The records clerk marks the 35-school-day deadline as met or missed in the timeline dashboard on the actual date of the eligibility meeting, not the date the evaluation report is completed.

### 5.6 The Eligibility Meeting

5.6.1 The eligibility meeting must occur within the 35-school-day evaluation window and must include, at minimum: the parent, a district administrator or designee, the student's general education teacher, the school psychologist or other qualified evaluator, and, when speech-language services were assessed, the SLP.

5.6.2 The evaluation coordinator schedules the meeting to accommodate parent availability, offering at least two proposed dates and times, and documents scheduling attempts in Skyward. Meetings may be held remotely by video conference at the parent's request.

5.6.3 At the meeting, the team reviews all evaluation data, determines whether the student meets eligibility criteria under one or more of the 14 disability categories recognized in WAC 392-172A-01035, and determines whether the student needs specially designed instruction as a result.

5.6.4 The team documents the eligibility determination on the standard eligibility determination form in the IEP system, and the evaluation coordinator issues Form SE-101 (PWN) documenting the eligibility decision and, if the student is found eligible, the district's proposal to develop an IEP, within one school day of the meeting.

5.6.5 If the student is found not eligible, the PWN documents the basis for that determination and the parent's right to challenge it, and the case is closed in the timeline dashboard on the date of the meeting.

### 5.7 Developing the Initial IEP Within 30 Calendar Days

5.7.1 If the student is found eligible, the IEP team must develop and finalize an initial IEP within 30 calendar days of the eligibility determination.

5.7.2 The building administrator schedules the IEP meeting within 10 calendar days of the eligibility meeting and confirms attendance of required IEP team members, including at least one special education teacher or service provider who will implement the IEP.

5.7.3 The evaluation coordinator prepares a draft present levels of performance statement, drawing on the evaluation report, no later than five calendar days before the scheduled IEP meeting, and shares it with the case manager assigned to write the IEP.

5.7.4 The IEP team finalizes goals, services, accommodations, and placement at the meeting. The case manager enters the completed IEP into the IEP system and issues Form SE-101 documenting the district's placement proposal within one school day of the meeting.

5.7.5 The records clerk logs the IEP finalization date in the timeline dashboard and confirms the 30-calendar-day deadline was met, calculated from the date of the eligibility meeting recorded in Section 5.6.4.

5.7.6 Special education services begin no later than the date specified in the IEP, which may not exceed the 30-calendar-day deadline absent a documented parent-requested delay.

### 5.8 Exception Paths

The following exception paths apply when standard timelines under Sections 5.2 through 5.7 cannot be met due to defined circumstances. Every exception must be documented in the timeline dashboard with the specific exception category selected from the list below; "other" or undocumented delays are not permitted and must be escalated to the Director of Special Education as a compliance concern.

**5.8.1 Student Transfers Mid-Evaluation**

If a student transfers into Northline from another Washington district while an evaluation is already in progress, the receiving building administrator contacts the sending district's special education office within two school days to obtain existing evaluation data and determine what assessments remain incomplete. The 35-school-day clock continues from the original consent date recorded by the sending district; it does not restart. If the sending district cannot be reached or existing data is insufficient to complete a timely evaluation, the evaluation coordinator documents the reason in the timeline dashboard under "transfer — data pending" and the Director of Special Education authorizes an extension not to exceed 15 additional school days, communicated to the parent via Form SE-101.

If a student transfers out of Northline mid-evaluation, the evaluation coordinator forwards all completed assessment data and the evaluation planning worksheet to the receiving district within three school days of notification of the transfer and closes the case in the timeline dashboard as "transferred out," noting the receiving district's name and date of transfer.

**5.8.2 Parent Does Not Produce the Student or Revokes Consent**

If a parent fails to make the student available for a scheduled assessment, the evaluation coordinator documents the missed appointment in Skyward and attempts to reschedule within five school days, with at least two documented contact attempts. Days during which the district is actively attempting, in good faith, to reschedule are logged in the timeline dashboard as "parent unavailability" and are excluded from the 35-school-day count, up to a maximum of 15 school days; delays beyond 15 school days require the Director of Special Education to determine next steps, which may include proceeding with available data or closing the referral.

If a parent revokes consent in writing at any point after initially providing it, the evaluation coordinator ceases all further assessment activity within one school day, issues Form SE-101 acknowledging the revocation, and closes the case in the timeline dashboard as "consent revoked," with the date of revocation recorded. The district retains any assessment data already collected in the IEP system per the records retention schedule in Section 6.

**5.8.3 Agreed Written Extensions**

The district and parent may mutually agree in writing to extend the 35-school-day evaluation window, using Form SE-104 (Extension Agreement), which specifies the reason for the extension and the new target completion date. Extensions may not exceed 15 additional school days without renewed written agreement. The evaluation coordinator obtains the parent's signature before the original 35-school-day deadline passes; an extension agreement executed after the deadline has already lapsed does not cure a timeline violation and must instead be logged as a missed timeline, triggering the compensatory services review in Section 5.10. All extension agreements are scanned into the IEP system and logged in the timeline dashboard with the original and revised deadlines both visible.

**5.8.4 Referrals Arriving in June**

Referrals received in the final 15 school days of the academic year, or after the last day of school and before the first day of the following school year, follow a modified timeline. The 25-school-day decision period and the 35-school-day evaluation window are calculated using school days only, meaning summer break days do not count toward either deadline. However, the evaluation coordinator must, within five school days of receiving a June referral, notify the parent in writing (Form SE-101) of the applicable timeline and confirm whether the family wishes to begin assessment activity before the end of the current school year or defer to the start of the following school year. If a family requests deferral, this is logged in the timeline dashboard as "summer deferral — parent requested," and the 25-school-day and 35-school-day clocks resume on the first school day of the following school year. If the district has capacity and the family does not request deferral, assessment activity may begin before summer break and continue, using extended school year staff where available, provided the district notifies the parent of which assessors will be available over summer.

**5.8.5 Private School and Out-of-District Referrals**

For district-resident students enrolled in a private school within Northline's boundaries, the district conducts a timely, meaningful consultation with the private school and the parent before evaluation, coordinated by the Director of Special Education's office rather than a single building administrator, since no single Northline building has enrollment authority over the student. The 25-school-day and 35-school-day timelines apply identically to private school referrals; the evaluation coordinator assigned is a district-employed or contracted evaluator, and assessments are typically conducted at the student's private school or at the nearest Northline building, as agreed with the parent.

For students who reside outside Northline's boundaries but whose parents request evaluation because the student attends a Northline school under an interdistrict agreement or open enrollment, the Director of Special Education's office confirms residency and enrollment status within three school days of referral and determines, in consultation with the student's district of residence, which district holds evaluation responsibility. If Northline holds responsibility, standard timelines in Sections 5.2 through 5.7 apply in full.

**5.8.6 Suspected Disability Raised During a Disciplinary Proceeding**

If, during a disciplinary proceeding (including a manifestation determination review), school staff or a parent raises a suspected disability for a student not previously referred, the building administrator treats this as a new referral effective the date it is raised, regardless of whether it is raised verbally in a meeting. The registrar date-stamps the referral that same day per Section 5.1, and the standard 25-school-day and 35-school-day timelines begin immediately. The Director of Special Education is notified within one school day whenever a referral arises from a disciplinary proceeding, given the heightened procedural and legal sensitivity, and personally reviews the evaluation plan under Section 5.4 before assessments begin. Disciplinary consequences may proceed under separate district discipline procedures while the evaluation is pending, subject to the protections and limitations described in WAC 392-172A-05146 through -05156, which the Director of Special Education's office applies directly.

### 5.9 Interpretation and Translation for Families with Home Languages Other Than English

5.9.1 At the point of referral intake (Section 5.1), the registrar checks Skyward for the family's designated home language. Northline families report 33 distinct home languages, and approximately 9 percent of referrals require interpretation.

5.9.2 If the family's home language is not English, the evaluation coordinator requests interpreter support through the district's language services office within two school days of the referral date, for use at every subsequent meeting involving the family, including the referral review, consent discussion, and eligibility and IEP meetings.

5.9.3 All PWN and consent documents (Forms SE-101, SE-102) are translated into the family's home language before being sent, using the district's approved translation vendor; if a written translation cannot be completed within the applicable timeline, the evaluation coordinator arranges a live interpreted phone or video review of the document with the family and documents this accommodation in Skyward, followed by delivery of the written translation as soon as available.

5.9.4 Interpreter and translation costs and scheduling do not extend the 25-school-day or 35-school-day timelines; the evaluation coordinator must build interpreter availability into scheduling from the point of referral.

### 5.10 Monthly Timeline Reporting and Compensatory Services Determinations

5.10.1 Beginning September 2026 and continuing for twelve consecutive months, the records clerk generates a monthly report from the timeline dashboard showing, for every referral active during that month: date of receipt, 25-school-day decision date (met/missed, with days over if missed), consent date, 35-school-day evaluation deadline (met/missed, with days over if missed), eligibility meeting date, and IEP finalization date if applicable.

5.10.2 The Director of Special Education reviews the report by the 5th of each following month, and submits it to Dr. Fonoti and to the state agency identified in the corrective action order by the 10th of each month.

5.10.3 For every student whose initial evaluation exceeded the 35-school-day timeline — including the 38 students identified in the April 2026 complaint decision and any subsequent student identified through this monitoring process — the Director of Special Education convenes a compensatory services determination team, including the parent, the case manager, and a school psychologist, within 20 school days of identifying the delay. The team completes Form SE-105 (Compensatory Services Determination Worksheet), documenting the length of delay, the services the student would likely have received during that period, and the specific compensatory services offered.

5.10.4 Compensatory services determinations for the 38 students identified in the April 2026 complaint decision must be completed no later than 60 calendar days after this procedure's effective date.
