## MEETING NO. 1

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services (Waterloo, Iowa) and Hawkeye Youth Bridge (Cedar Falls, Iowa)
**Date:** Wednesday, January 14, 2026
**Time:** 4:00 p.m. – 6:22 p.m.
**Place:** Boardroom, Cedar Valley Family Services, Waterloo, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair; Board Chair, Cedar Valley Family Services); Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair; Board Chair, Hawkeye Youth Bridge); Sun-Hee Park (Treasurer, Cedar Valley Family Services); Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz (Chief Executive, Cedar Valley Family Services); Roland Buchbinder (Founder and Executive Director, Hawkeye Youth Bridge); Tariq Al-Rashid (Board Member, Hawkeye Youth Bridge)

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priscilla Ostergaard, Esq. (Ostergaard & Whitcomb PLC, joint transaction counsel); Everett Nakagawa (Principal, Alloy Strategy Group, proposed integration consultant); Marcus Ohlendorf (Chief Financial Officer, Cedar Valley Family Services); Yolanda Greer (Executive Assistant, Cedar Valley Family Services; Recording Secretary)

### 1. Call to Order and Establishment of Quorum

Co-Chair Marchetti called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. Ms. Greer called the roll. All six members were present and a quorum was established. Co-Chair Marchetti stated that the committee had been seated by resolutions of the two parent boards adopted in November and December 2025, and that its work would be advisory to those boards, which retained sole authority to approve any transaction.

### 2. Opening Remarks of the Co-Chairs

Co-Chair Marchetti reviewed the external timeline: the Black Hawk Community Foundation had offered a $500,000 challenge grant contingent on a signed agreement between the two organizations by June 30, 2026, and the shared $1.4 million state contract was scheduled for reprocurement in July 2026. She stated that the committee's calendar had been built backward from those dates. Co-Chair Kimani stated that the Hawkeye Youth Bridge board had entered the process in good faith but that its members held a range of views on the appropriate depth of combination, and he asked that the record reflect that no outcome was predetermined.

### 3. Adoption of the Committee Charter

The draft charter, circulated by Ms. Greer on January 6, 2026, was taken up. The draft defined the committee's purpose as evaluating "a merger or other combination" of the two organizations, set a quorum of four members, required a majority of the full committee for any motion to carry, and directed that all recommendations be referred to both parent boards.

Mr. Buchbinder moved to amend the charter throughout to substitute the word "affiliation" for the word "merger," stating that the substituted language would keep all structural options open, would be less alarming to staff and donors, and would better reflect what the Hawkeye Youth Bridge board had authorized. Mr. Al-Rashid seconded the motion. In discussion, Ms. Park opposed the amendment, stating that the Foundation's grant letter of December 18, 2025 used the word "merger" and that the committee should not signal retreat from the funder's terms. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz concurred. Mr. Kimani stated that while he was sympathetic to the concern, the charter as drafted already contemplated "other combination" structures and the amendment was unnecessary.

The motion to amend **failed on a vote of four to two** (Ayes: Buchbinder, Al-Rashid; Nays: Marchetti, Kimani, Park, Feldstein-Ruiz).

Ms. Park then moved adoption of the charter as circulated. Mr. Kimani seconded. The motion **carried five to one** (Ayes: Marchetti, Kimani, Park, Feldstein-Ruiz, Al-Rashid; Nay: Buchbinder). Mr. Buchbinder asked that the record reflect that his vote against adoption was based on the language question and not on opposition to the exploration itself, and the record so reflects.

### 4. Presentation of Transaction Counsel: Structural Options

Ms. Ostergaard presented a memorandum, dated January 9, 2026, describing four structures available under Iowa law: (a) statutory merger under Iowa Code Chapter 504, in which one corporation survives; (b) member substitution, in which one organization becomes the sole corporate member of the other, preserving both corporate identities and licenses during transition; (c) asset transfer and dissolution; and (d) contractual affiliation with shared services. She reviewed the regulatory consequences of each, including successor liability, assignment or non-assignment of the state contract, treatment of restricted funds, and notice obligations to the Iowa Attorney General. She noted that member substitution was frequently used where one party held contracts or accreditations that would not survive a statutory merger, and that it could serve as a first phase toward full merger. The committee asked questions; no action was taken.

### 5. Engagement of Integration Consultant

Mr. Nakagawa presented Alloy Strategy Group's proposal, dated January 8, 2026, for due diligence coordination, cultural assessment, and integration planning at a fixed fee of $48,000, to be split between the organizations sixty-forty in proportion to budget size. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz moved to recommend that both parent boards approve the engagement on the terms presented. Mr. Al-Rashid seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.**

### 6. Adoption of the Due Diligence Scope

Mr. Ohlendorf presented a due diligence scope document covering financial statements and audits for three fiscal years, tax filings, benefit plans and pension obligations, real property and leases, insurance, litigation, licensure and accreditation, restricted gifts, HR files and compensation, and information systems. Ms. Park moved adoption of the scope, with document exchange to be completed by February 4, 2026 and a report to the committee at its February meeting. Mr. Kimani seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.**

### 7. Funder Communications

Ms. Marchetti read into the record the letter of December 18, 2025 from the Black Hawk Community Foundation, signed by its president, Corinne Vasquez-Thorpe, confirming the $500,000 challenge grant conditioned on "a signed definitive merger or combination agreement executed by both boards no later than June 30, 2026." Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz reported that the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services had confirmed in writing on January 5, 2026 that the reprocurement of the shared $1.4 million contract would open in July 2026 and that joint or consolidated bids would be accepted.

### 8. Action Items

- Ms. Ostergaard to circulate a mutual confidentiality and non-disclosure agreement for signature by all members by **January 21, 2026**.
- Mr. Ohlendorf and Mr. Buchbinder to complete the document exchange under the due diligence scope by **February 4, 2026**.
- Mr. Nakagawa to present a due diligence findings report at the **February 11, 2026** meeting.
- Ms. Greer to distribute draft minutes within seven days.

### 9. Adjournment

There being no further business, Co-Chair Kimani moved adjournment, seconded by Ms. Park; carried unanimously. The meeting adjourned at 6:22 p.m. The next meeting was set for **Wednesday, February 11, 2026, at 4:00 p.m.** at Hawkeye Youth Bridge, Cedar Falls.

Respectfully submitted, Yolanda Greer, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING NO. 2

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge
**Date:** Wednesday, February 11, 2026
**Time:** 4:00 p.m. – 7:05 p.m.
**Place:** Conference Room, Hawkeye Youth Bridge, Cedar Falls, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair); Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair); Sun-Hee Park; Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz; Roland Buchbinder; Tariq Al-Rashid

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priscilla Ostergaard, Esq. (transaction counsel); Everett Nakagawa (Alloy Strategy Group); Marcus Ohlendorf (CFO, Cedar Valley Family Services); Hyacinth Okafor, CPA (Partner, Denniston & Cray CPAs, financial due diligence); Yolanda Greer (Recording Secretary)

### 1. Call to Order and Quorum

Co-Chair Kimani called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. All six members were present; a quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Minutes

The minutes of the January 14, 2026 meeting were presented. Mr. Al-Rashid moved approval; Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. The minutes were **approved unanimously, six to zero.**

### 3. Financial Due Diligence Report

Ms. Okafor presented the report of Denniston & Cray CPAs, dated February 9, 2026, on the financial due diligence of both organizations. She reported that Cedar Valley Family Services' records were substantially in order, with two minor items relating to grant receivable aging that management had already addressed. With respect to Hawkeye Youth Bridge, she reported three significant findings:

**(a) Unrecorded pension withdrawal liability.** Hawkeye Youth Bridge had participated until 2023 in a multiemployer defined-benefit pension plan and, upon withdrawal, had incurred a withdrawal liability assessed at **$310,000**, payable in installments. The liability had not been recorded on the organization's statement of financial position and had not been disclosed in its fiscal year 2024 or 2025 audited financial statements. Ms. Okafor stated that recording the liability would materially change Hawkeye's reported net asset position.

**(b) Lapsed building lease.** The lease on Hawkeye's program facility at 1140 Ridgeline Avenue, Cedar Falls, had expired on August 31, 2025, and the organization had continued in occupancy as a holdover tenant on a month-to-month basis without a written renewal. The landlord could terminate on thirty days' notice, placing program continuity and the state contract's site requirements at risk.

**(c) Unfiled unrelated business income tax returns.** Hawkeye had generated unrelated business income from facility rentals and a fee-for-service catering activity in fiscal years 2023, 2024, and 2025, but had not filed IRS Form 990-T for any of those years. Ms. Okafor estimated the combined tax, penalties, and interest exposure at $22,000 to $40,000, subject to abatement requests.

Mr. Buchbinder responded that the pension withdrawal assessment had been under informal dispute with the plan and that he had understood, on advice received at the time, that it need not be booked until the dispute was resolved. He acknowledged the lease and the 990-T filings as administrative oversights during a period of staff turnover in the finance office. Ms. Park stated that, as treasurer of Cedar Valley, she considered the findings serious and that they altered the risk profile of any combination. Ms. Ostergaard advised that none of the findings was necessarily fatal to a transaction, that a member substitution structure could ring-fence historical liabilities more effectively than a statutory merger, and that immediate remediation was advisable regardless of the transaction's outcome.

### 4. Motion to Pause the Exploration Process

Mr. Al-Rashid moved that the committee suspend the exploration process for ninety days to permit Hawkeye Youth Bridge to remediate the findings before further transaction work proceeded. Mr. Buchbinder seconded the motion. In discussion, Mr. Al-Rashid stated that Hawkeye should not negotiate "from a position of embarrassment." Ms. Park spoke in support of the motion, stating that Cedar Valley's board could not responsibly evaluate a combination against unquantified liabilities. Ms. Marchetti, Mr. Kimani, and Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz opposed the motion, each citing the June 30 deadline attached to the Foundation's challenge grant and the July reprocurement, and stating that remediation could proceed in parallel with, rather than instead of, the committee's work.

The motion **failed on a tie vote of three to three** (Ayes: Al-Rashid, Buchbinder, Park; Nays: Marchetti, Kimani, Feldstein-Ruiz). Co-Chair Kimani ruled that under Section 6 of the charter, a motion required the affirmative vote of a majority of the full committee, and the motion therefore **did not carry**. The exploration process continued.

### 5. Remediation Directives

Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz moved that the committee recommend to the Hawkeye Youth Bridge board that it: (a) record the $310,000 pension withdrawal liability and restate or disclose as its auditors advise; (b) direct counsel to negotiate a written lease renewal of not less than twenty-four months for the Ridgeline Avenue facility by March 31, 2026; and (c) engage Denniston & Cray to prepare and file the delinquent Forms 990-T with a penalty abatement request by April 15, 2026. Ms. Park seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.** Mr. Buchbinder accepted responsibility for reporting progress at each subsequent meeting.

### 6. Counsel's Presentation: Disclosure Obligations

Ms. Ostergaard presented a memorandum on disclosure obligations arising from the findings, advising that the Foundation and the state contracting agency should receive accurate information if asked, that no affirmative disclosure to the state was yet required, and that the committee's communications should be routed through the co-chairs. The committee accepted the advice without objection.

### 7. Staff Communications Plan

Mr. Nakagawa reported rising staff anxiety at both organizations, referencing questions received during site visits. He recommended a joint staff town hall in early March, ahead of any structural recommendation. Mr. Kimani moved that a joint town hall be held on **March 5, 2026**, hosted at the Cedar Valley Waterloo campus with video connection to Cedar Falls, facilitated by Mr. Nakagawa, with both executives and both co-chairs present. Ms. Marchetti seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.**

### 8. Funder Communications

Ms. Marchetti reported that she and Mr. Kimani had met with Ms. Vasquez-Thorpe of the Black Hawk Community Foundation on February 3, 2026; the Foundation had reaffirmed the June 30 deadline and asked for a written status update by April 15, 2026. The committee directed the co-chairs to provide that update.

### 9. Action Items

- Mr. Buchbinder to bring the remediation directives to the Hawkeye board at its **February 24, 2026** meeting and report at the March committee meeting.
- Ms. Ostergaard to draft lease renewal terms for the Ridgeline Avenue facility by **February 27, 2026**.
- Mr. Nakagawa and Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz to finalize town hall logistics and a joint staff FAQ by **February 26, 2026**.
- Ms. Okafor to deliver a restated pro forma combined balance sheet reflecting the recorded liability by **March 6, 2026**.

### 10. Adjournment

Ms. Park moved adjournment; Mr. Al-Rashid seconded; carried unanimously. The meeting adjourned at 7:05 p.m. The next meeting was set for **Wednesday, March 11, 2026, at 4:00 p.m.** in Waterloo.

Respectfully submitted, Yolanda Greer, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING NO. 3

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge
**Date:** Wednesday, March 11, 2026

**Members Absent:** None

### 1. Call to Order and Quorum

Co-Chair Marchetti called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. All six members were present; a quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Minutes

The minutes of the February 11, 2026 meeting were presented. Mr. Kimani moved approval with one correction requested by Mr. Buchbinder, clarifying that the pension dispute advice he described had been received orally from a prior benefits broker. Ms. Park seconded. The minutes were **approved as corrected, six to zero.**

### 3. Report on the Joint Staff Town Hall of March 5, 2026

Mr. Nakagawa reported on the joint town hall held March 5, 2026, attended by approximately 96 staff in person and by video across both organizations. He summarized the questions raised, which concentrated on job security, seniority and benefits alignment, program continuity, and the future of the Hawkeye Youth Bridge name and identity.

Ms. Marchetti then read into the record a letter dated March 9, 2026, addressed to both boards and **signed by 22 employees of Hawkeye Youth Bridge**, representing a majority of that organization's 41 staff. The letter expressed support for continued collaboration but opposed "absorption of Hawkeye Youth Bridge into a larger bureaucracy," requested written commitments that no direct-service positions be eliminated for eighteen months following any transaction, asked that the Hawkeye name survive in some form, and requested staff representation in integration planning.

Mr. Buchbinder stated that he had not solicited the letter but shared its concerns. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz stated that Cedar Valley had no plans to reduce direct-service staffing and that projected savings were located in back-office consolidation and attrition. After discussion, Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz moved that the co-chairs send a joint written response to the signatories by **March 20, 2026**, committing that (a) any structural recommendation would include a staffing transition plan; (b) no direct-service layoffs were contemplated in the first year of any combination; and (c) a staff advisory group of six members, three from each organization, would be seated if the boards approved a transaction. Mr. Al-Rashid seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.** The letter was ordered appended to these minutes as Exhibit A.

### 4. Hawkeye Youth Bridge Financial Condition Report

Ms. Okafor presented the restated pro forma statements, dated March 6, 2026, reflecting the recorded $310,000 pension withdrawal liability. She reported that Hawkeye Youth Bridge's **unrestricted operating reserves had fallen to 1.2 months of expenses** as of February 28, 2026, down from 2.4 months at fiscal year-end, driven by the first pension installment payment of $62,000, delayed state reimbursements, and a fall appeal that had underperformed budget by $85,000. She stated that at the current burn rate, reserves would fall below one month by June without corrective action.

Mr. Ohlendorf presented the combined pro forma, showing that the merged entity would carry approximately 3.4 months of unrestricted reserves. Ms. Park stated that the figures confirmed both the urgency and the risk of the transaction, and asked that cash position be a standing agenda item. The co-chairs so directed.

Mr. Buchbinder reported on remediation: the Hawkeye board had voted on February 24, 2026 to record the pension liability; lease renewal negotiations were underway, with the landlord offering a three-year term at a 9 percent rent increase; and Denniston & Cray had been engaged to prepare the delinquent Forms 990-T, on schedule for filing by April 15, 2026.

Ms. Park moved that the committee recommend to the Cedar Valley board a bridge accommodation, in the form of a shared-services agreement under which Cedar Valley would provide payroll and finance-office support to Hawkeye at cost beginning April 1, 2026, subject to counsel's review, in order to stabilize operations pending a decision by the parent boards. Mr. Kimani seconded. The motion **carried unanimously, six to zero.**

### 5. Discussion of Surviving Name and Identity

Mr. Nakagawa presented brand-assessment findings, reporting strong recognition of the Cedar Valley Family Services name among funders and referral sources and strong attachment to the Hawkeye Youth Bridge name among youth participants, alumni, and Cedar Falls donors. Extended and at times sharp discussion followed. Mr. Buchbinder stated that the disappearance of the Hawkeye name would be experienced by staff and alumni as the erasure of twenty-two years of work, and proposed "Hawkeye Bridge, a division of Cedar Valley Family Services." Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz objected to a divisional structure appearing in the legal name. Ms. Marchetti proposed that the corporate name question be separated from the program name question. Mr. Al-Rashid stated that the naming discussion was premature before a structure was chosen and characterized portions of the exchange as unproductive; the co-chairs concurred.

Mr. Kimani moved that the question of the surviving corporate name be **tabled** to the April meeting and taken up only after the committee acted on structure, with the consultant to bring naming options tied to each structural scenario. Ms. Marchetti seconded. The motion **carried five to one** (Nay: Buchbinder).

### 6. Counsel's Report

Ms. Ostergaard reported that the Attorney General notification requirements had been mapped for each structure, that the confidentiality agreements were fully executed, and that the state agency had confirmed informally on March 4, 2026 that a member substitution would not require novation of the existing contract, whereas a statutory merger would. She stated that this distinction was material given the July reprocurement timeline.

### 7. Funder Communications

Mr. Kimani reported a telephone conference with Ms. Vasquez-Thorpe on March 6, 2026, in which the Foundation acknowledged the due diligence findings at a summary level and confirmed that the challenge grant remained available, provided the June 30 signing deadline was met. The written status update remained due April 15, 2026.

- Co-chairs to submit the written status update to the Black Hawk Community Foundation by **April 15, 2026**.

### 9. Adjournment

Mr. Al-Rashid moved adjournment; Ms. Park seconded; carried unanimously. The meeting adjourned at 7:18 p.m. The next meeting was set for **Wednesday, April 8, 2026, at 4:00 p.m.** in Cedar Falls.

## MEETING NO. 4

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge
**Date:** Wednesday, April 8, 2026
**Time:** 4:00 p.m. – 7:40 p.m.
**Place:** Conference Room, Hawkeye Youth Bridge, Cedar Falls, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair); Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair); Sun-Hee Park; Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz; Roland Buchbinder; Tariq Al-Rashid

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priscilla Ostergaard, Esq. (transaction counsel); Everett Nakagawa (Alloy Strategy Group); Marcus Ohlendorf (CFO, Cedar Valley Family Services); Hyacinth Okafor, CPA (Denniston & Cray CPAs); Yolanda Greer (Recording Secretary)

### 1. Call to Order and Quorum

Co-Chair Kimani called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. All six members were present; a quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Minutes

The minutes of the March 11, 2026 meeting were presented. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz moved approval; Mr. Kimani seconded. The minutes were **approved unanimously, six to zero.**

### 3. Remediation and Interim Measures Update

Mr. Buchbinder reported that the Ridgeline Avenue lease renewal had been executed on March 27, 2026 for a three-year term; that the delinquent Forms 990-T for fiscal years 2023 through 2025 had been filed on April 3, 2026 with a reasonable-cause abatement request; and that the recorded pension liability installment schedule was current. Ms. Okafor confirmed the filings. Mr. Ohlendorf reported that the shared-services agreement had been approved by both parent boards during the week of March 23, 2026 and had taken effect April 1, 2026, and that Hawkeye's April cash position had stabilized at 1.3 months of reserves.

### 4. Consultant's Final Report and Recommended Structure

Mr. Nakagawa presented Alloy Strategy Group's final report, dated April 3, 2026, jointly reviewed by Ms. Ostergaard for legal accuracy. The report recommended a **member substitution structure** in two phases: in Phase One, effective upon signing, Cedar Valley Family Services would become the sole corporate member of Hawkeye Youth Bridge, with both corporations continuing in existence, preserving Hawkeye's licenses, accreditations, and the existing state contract without novation; in Phase Two, targeted within twenty-four months, the boards would evaluate full statutory merger. The report cited the non-novation confirmation from the state, the ring-fencing of Hawkeye's historical liabilities within the subsidiary during Phase One, and the ability to meet the Foundation's June 30 signing deadline as the decisive factors.

The report further recommended:

**(a) Governance.** A reconstituted eleven-member board for the combined enterprise with a **seven to four split**, seven seats designated by Cedar Valley Family Services and four by Hawkeye Youth Bridge, the split reflecting the approximate three-to-one budget proportion while guaranteeing Hawkeye a substantial minority voice; Hawkeye designees to include at least one seat reserved for a Cedar Falls community representative.

**(b) Transition budget.** A **$265,000 transition budget**, comprising $88,000 for systems and data migration, $64,000 for legal and accounting fees, $47,000 for retention stipends for key Hawkeye staff, $36,000 for rebranding and communications, and $30,000 contingency; to be funded from the Foundation challenge grant if secured, with the balance of the grant applied to combined-entity reserves.

**(c) Naming.** The corporate survivor to operate as Cedar Valley Family Services, with the Hawkeye youth programs preserved under the program name "Hawkeye Youth Bridge, a program of Cedar Valley Family Services," and the Cedar Falls facility designated the Buchbinder Center, subject to board approval.

**(d) Staffing.** No direct-service layoffs in the first twelve months, consistent with the March 20, 2026 commitment letter to staff; back-office consolidation through attrition.

### 5. Discussion

Extended discussion followed. Mr. Buchbinder stated that he could not support the recommendation, objecting principally to the seven to four board split, which he characterized as ending Hawkeye's independent voice, and to the demotion of the Hawkeye name to program status. He stated that a five-to-four or six-to-five split would have been acceptable to him. Mr. Al-Rashid stated that he shared reservations about the governance split but acknowledged the strength of the financial case. Ms. Park stated that the ring-fencing of historical liabilities in Phase One had resolved her February concerns and that she would support the recommendation. Ms. Marchetti and Mr. Kimani each spoke in support, citing the reserve position, the June 30 deadline, and the July reprocurement, at which Mr. Nakagawa projected a consolidated bid would be materially stronger than parallel bids.

### 6. Motion on the Structural Recommendation

Ms. Park moved that the committee recommend to both parent boards approval of the member substitution structure, the eleven-member board with a seven to four designation split, the $265,000 transition budget, and the naming and staffing provisions, all as set out in the Alloy Strategy Group report of April 3, 2026, with definitive agreements to be prepared by Ms. Ostergaard for execution no later than **June 30, 2026**. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded.

The motion **carried four to two** (Ayes: Marchetti, Kimani, Park, Feldstein-Ruiz; Nays: Buchbinder, Al-Rashid). Mr. Buchbinder and Mr. Al-Rashid each asked that their dissents be recorded, and the record so reflects. Mr. Al-Rashid stated for the record that, the committee having decided, he would present the recommendation fairly to the Hawkeye board.

### 7. Referral to the Parent Boards

Mr. Kimani moved that the recommendation, together with the consultant's report, counsel's structural memoranda, the due diligence reports of February 9 and March 6, 2026, and the staff letter and response, be **referred to each parent board** for action at special meetings to be held no later than **May 6, 2026**, with the co-chairs to present jointly at both meetings. Ms. Marchetti seconded. The motion **carried five to one** (Nay: Buchbinder).

### 8. Resignation of Mr. Buchbinder from the Committee

Mr. Buchbinder then read a letter of resignation, dated April 8, 2026, resigning from the committee effective at the close of the meeting. He stated that he could not in good conscience advocate for a recommendation he had voted against, that his continued participation would place him in conflict with his duties as executive director during the transition, and that he intended to remain in his staff role and cooperate fully with whatever the boards decided. Co-Chair Kimani accepted the resignation on behalf of the committee with thanks for Mr. Buchbinder's twenty-two years of service to Hawkeye Youth Bridge, and stated that the Hawkeye board would determine whether to name a replacement. The letter was ordered appended as Exhibit B.

### 9. Funder and Staff Communications
