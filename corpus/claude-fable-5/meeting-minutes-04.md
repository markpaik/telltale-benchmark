# Pine Hollow Area School District
## Independent School District — Board of Education
### Official Minutes: Regular Meetings of September 15, October 20, November 17, and December 15, 2025

---

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF EDUCATION

**Body:** Board of Education, Pine Hollow Area School District
**Date:** Monday, September 15, 2025
**Time:** 6:30 p.m.
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Deborah Sorensen; Director Mai Xiong; Director Paul Twetten; Director Lila Beckstrom

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Kelli Ostrander, Executive Assistant to the Superintendent (recording); Dr. Miriam Chenoweth, ATS Demographics LLC; James Feldt, Feldt & Ryerson Facilities Consulting; approximately 60 members of the public; one representative of the Pine Hollow Courier

### 1. Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:31 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance. The Chair noted that all seven members were present and a quorum existed.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda

Director Twetten moved and Director Beckstrom seconded approval of the agenda as printed. The motion carried on a voice vote, 7–0.

### 3. Consent Agenda

The consent agenda included: (a) minutes of the regular meeting of August 18, 2025; (b) bills payable in the amount of $1,412,608.44; (c) personnel report, including the resignation of paraprofessional Janice Kubly effective September 26, 2025, and the hire of long-term substitute teacher Alan Perreault at Meadow Ridge Elementary; (d) acceptance of a donation of $2,500 from the Pine Hollow Lions Club for the high school robotics program; and (e) approval of the out-of-state FFA trip to Indianapolis, October 28–November 1, 2025.

Treasurer Halvorsen moved and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded approval of the consent agenda as presented. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Twetten, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 7–0.

### 4. Public Comment

Chair Achterberg reviewed the public comment guidelines: three minutes per speaker, comments addressed to the board as a whole, and no board response during the comment period. Six persons addressed the board:

- **Sandra Lofquist**, parent, Birchdale Elementary, expressed concern about rumors that the facilities study would recommend closing a building and asked the board to release the study to the public before any vote.
- **Rev. Daniel Okafor**, Birchdale community, stated that Birchdale Elementary anchored the town and urged the board to consider ripple effects on local businesses.
- **Tom Sivertsen**, farmer and district resident, said the district could not afford six buildings for 1,340 students and asked the board to "make the hard call now instead of a harder one later."
- **Emily Vang**, teacher and building representative at Pine Hollow Middle School, asked the board to remember that staff had gone two years without a settled contract raise above inflation and to bargain in good faith this fall.
- **Craig Determan**, resident, asked whether the November referendum would still be needed if a school were closed.
- **Priya Raghunathan**, parent, asked how attendance boundaries would be redrawn and how families could participate.

Chair Achterberg thanked the speakers and noted that the boundary committee item later in the agenda addressed Ms. Raghunathan's question.

### 5. Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported that the school year had opened smoothly on September 2 with all bus routes staffed, though the district continued to carry two driver vacancies covered by mechanics with CDL endorsements. He reported that fall MCA preliminary data would come to the board in October, that the new elementary literacy curriculum was in its first full year of implementation, and that homecoming week was scheduled for September 22–26. He thanked the Lions Club for the robotics donation acknowledged on the consent agenda.

The Superintendent then previewed the two major presentations on the agenda, stating: "Tonight the board will see, in one evening, both the demographic reality we face and one professional recommendation for how to respond to it. No decision is being asked of the board tonight beyond receiving these reports and seating the committee."

### 6. Finance Report

Business Manager Kalinowski presented the finance report for the period ending August 31, 2025. She reported a general fund budget of $23.6 million for fiscal year 2026, with revenues tracking at 14.2 percent of budget and expenditures at 15.1 percent, consistent with seasonal patterns. The unassigned general fund balance stood at $1.94 million, or 8.2 percent of expenditures, against a board policy target of 12 percent.

Ms. Kalinowski presented an updated multi-year projection showing a cumulative shortfall of $2.9 million by June 30, 2027, absent new revenue or expenditure reductions. She stated that at that level the district would fall into statutory operating debt as defined by Minnesota law, which would require a special operating plan subject to approval by the Commissioner of Education. Treasurer Halvorsen asked what combination of measures would avoid statutory operating debt. Ms. Kalinowski answered that passage of the November operating levy at $460 per pupil would close approximately $1.7 million of the gap, and that the balance would require expenditure reductions in the range of $1.2 million. Director Xiong asked that future finance reports show the projection both with and without the referendum. Ms. Kalinowski agreed to do so. The report was received without formal action.

### 7. Enrollment Projection Report — Dr. Miriam Chenoweth, ATS Demographics LLC

Dr. Chenoweth presented the district's five-year enrollment projection. She reported current enrollment of 1,340 students, down from 1,910 in 2014, a decline of 29.8 percent over eleven years. She attributed the decline to falling birth rates in the three-county area, net out-migration of young families, and open enrollment losses of approximately 55 students per year, principally to the Lakewood Consolidated district. Her medium projection showed enrollment of 1,215 by 2030; her low projection showed 1,140.

Vice Chair Villalpando asked whether any scenario showed growth. Dr. Chenoweth answered that only the high scenario, which assumed a reversal of open enrollment losses and completion of the proposed Kettle Creek housing development, showed enrollment stabilizing near 1,320, and that she considered it unlikely. Director Beckstrom asked about kindergarten cohorts; Dr. Chenoweth reported that the incoming kindergarten class of 148 compared to a graduating class of 171. The report was received without formal action.

### 8. First Reading — Facilities Utilization Study, Feldt & Ryerson Facilities Consulting

Mr. Feldt presented the first reading of the facilities utilization study commissioned by the board in April 2025. He reported that the district's four elementary buildings operated at a combined 58 percent of functional capacity, with Birchdale Elementary, enrolling 187 students, at 46 percent. He reported deferred maintenance across the district of $11.3 million, of which $3.1 million was attributable to Birchdale, including roof replacement and a failing boiler plant.

The study presented three options: (a) status quo with across-the-board reductions; (b) consolidation of Birchdale Elementary into Meadow Ridge and Pine Hollow Elementary, with estimated annual operating savings of $840,000; and (c) consolidation to two elementary buildings by 2028, with estimated annual savings of $1.6 million but requiring capital investment in additions. The consultants' recommendation was Option B, with Option C to be studied further.

Director Xiong asked what transportation cost increases were assumed under Option B. Mr. Feldt answered approximately $110,000 annually, netted within the $840,000 savings figure. Director Twetten asked whether the Birchdale building could be repurposed; Mr. Feldt said disposition options would be addressed in the final report. Chair Achterberg stated that this constituted a first reading only, that the final report would return in November, and that no closure decision was before the board. The report was received without formal action.

### 9. Action Item — Establishment of Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee

Superintendent Baldacci recommended the board establish a fifteen-member Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee comprising two parents from each elementary attendance area, two teachers, one principal, the transportation coordinator, two community members at large, the Business Manager, and one board member as liaison, charged with recommending revised elementary attendance boundaries to the board by December 1, 2025, under any consolidation scenario the board might adopt.

Director Xiong moved and Clerk Sorensen seconded adoption of the resolution establishing the Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee as presented, with Director Xiong appointed as board liaison. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Twetten, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 7–0.

**Action items assigned:** Superintendent Baldacci to publish the committee application by September 19, 2025, and bring appointments to the October 20 meeting. Ms. Kalinowski to post the enrollment projection and facilities study to the district website by September 17, 2025.

### 10. Board Member Reports

Treasurer Halvorsen reported on the September 9 meeting of the finance committee. Director Beckstrom reported on the regional school boards association fall workshop. Chair Achterberg reminded members of the October 20 regular meeting.

### 11. Adjournment

Vice Chair Villalpando moved and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded adjournment. The motion carried on a voice vote, 7–0. The meeting adjourned at 9:04 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Deborah Sorensen, Clerk
Approved: October 20, 2025

Superintendent Baldacci requested the addition of item 9(b), setting a mediation date in the teacher contract negotiations, and the addition of a closed session under Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03 for labor negotiations strategy. ### 4. Public Comment

Eight persons addressed the board:

- **Sandra Lofquist**, Birchdale parent, presented a petition bearing 411 signatures asking the board to reject the consolidation recommendation. The petition was received by the Clerk and entered into the record.
- **Gary Holmstad**, Birchdale business owner, stated the school's closure would reduce daytime traffic to Birchdale's remaining businesses by an estimated 30 percent.
- **Emily Vang**, teacher representative, said the union remained "far apart" from the district and urged the board to authorize its negotiators to improve the 1.75 percent offer, stating that neighboring districts had settled between 3 and 4 percent.
- **Marcus Bly**, resident, spoke in favor of the operating levy and urged residents to vote yes on November 4.
- **Colleen Reierson**, resident, spoke against the levy, stating the district should "right-size before it asks for more."
- **Dr. Anita Falk**, Birchdale parent and physician, cited research on transportation time and young children's outcomes and asked the board to obtain route-time estimates before any vote.
- **Jerry Tackaberry**, resident, asked why the district had not pursued shared services with Lakewood Consolidated.
- **Priya Raghunathan**, parent, thanked the board for seating the boundary committee and asked that its meetings be open to the public. Chair Achterberg stated they would be.

### 5. Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported preliminary October 1 enrollment of 1,338, two below projection. He reported that the boundary committee had received 31 applications and that his recommended appointments appeared under item 8. He reported on the successful FFA trip authorization, homecoming activities, and a bus safety inspection with no out-of-service findings. He stated that the final facilities report from Feldt & Ryerson would be delivered to the board on November 10 and presented publicly on November 17, and that any administrative recommendation on Birchdale Elementary would accompany that report.

### 6. Finance Report

Business Manager Kalinowski presented the finance report for the period ending September 30, 2025. Revenues stood at 21.6 percent of budget and expenditures at 23.4 percent. As requested by Director Xiong at the September meeting, the projection was presented in two scenarios: with the referendum passing, a projected June 2027 deficit of $1.2 million, avoidable through identified reductions; without the referendum, the full $2.9 million shortfall and entry into statutory operating debt. Treasurer Halvorsen asked when the board would need to certify the payable 2026 levy; Ms. Kalinowski answered that final levy certification would occur at the December meeting following the Truth in Taxation hearing. The report was received without formal action.

### 7. Report — November 4, 2025 Operating Levy Referendum Campaign

Marta Delacroix, co-chair of Citizens for Pine Hollow Schools, an independent committee, presented an informational report on the referendum. She reported that the ballot question sought a new operating referendum authority of $460 per pupil for ten years, generating approximately $1.7 million annually, at an estimated cost of $14 per month on a $250,000 home. She reported that the committee had held four town halls, distributed 3,200 mailers, and organized door-knocking in all six precincts. She reported that opposition centered on the unresolved facilities question and stated candidly that "the closure conversation is hurting the yes vote."

Chair Achterberg reminded the board and audience that the district's role was limited to factual information and that advocacy rested with the citizen committee. Superintendent Baldacci confirmed that the district's informational mailer had been reviewed by legal counsel. The report was received without formal action.

### 8. Action Item — Appointments to the Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee

Superintendent Baldacci recommended fifteen appointees as listed in the enclosure, drawn from 31 applicants and balanced across the four elementary attendance areas. Director Xiong, as board liaison, endorsed the slate and reported the committee's first meeting was set for October 28, 2025.

Director Xiong moved and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded approval of the appointments as presented. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–0.

### 9. Labor Relations

**(a) Status Report.** Superintendent Baldacci reported that the district and the Pine Hollow Education Association had met five times since June without reaching agreement on the 2025–2027 collective bargaining agreement. He reported the association's position at 4.5 percent annual salary schedule increases and the district's offer at 1.75 percent, with health insurance contributions also unresolved. He reported that on October 9 the association had requested mediation through the Minnesota Bureau of Mediation Services.

**(b) Action Item — Setting of Mediation Date.** Treasurer Halvorsen moved and Clerk Sorensen seconded a motion to accept the Bureau of Mediation Services' proposed mediation date of November 21, 2025, and to authorize the district negotiating team of Superintendent Baldacci, Business Manager Kalinowski, and Attorney Greeley to represent the district in mediation. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–0.

### 10. Closed Session — Labor Negotiations Strategy

At 8:22 p.m., Vice Chair Villalpando moved and Director Beckstrom seconded a motion to close the meeting pursuant to Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03 to discuss labor negotiation strategy relating to the Pine Hollow Education Association contract. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–0. The board entered closed session at 8:26 p.m. with Superintendent Baldacci, Business Manager Kalinowski, and Attorney Greeley present. The closed session was recorded as required by law.

The board returned to open session at 9:18 p.m. Chair Achterberg reported that the board had discussed negotiations strategy and given direction to its negotiating team, and that no action was taken in closed session.

### 11. Board Member Reports

Director Xiong reported that the boundary committee's schedule would be posted publicly. Treasurer Halvorsen reported on the October 14 finance committee meeting. Chair Achterberg reminded the public that the referendum election was November 4 and that the next regular meeting was November 17.

**Action items assigned:** Superintendent Baldacci to distribute the final facilities report to board members by November 10, 2025. Ms. Kalinowski to prepare route-time estimates under the consolidation scenario, as raised in public comment, for the November 17 meeting.

### 12. Adjournment

Treasurer Halvorsen moved and Director Xiong seconded adjournment. The motion carried on a voice vote, 6–0. The meeting adjourned at 9:25 p.m.

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF EDUCATION

**Body:** Board of Education, Pine Hollow Area School District
**Date:** Monday, November 17, 2025
**Time:** 6:30 p.m.
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, Pine Hollow, Minnesota (overflow seating with audio in the adjacent cafeteria)

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Deborah Sorensen; Director Mai Xiong; Director Paul Twetten; Director Lila Beckstrom

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Kelli Ostrander (recording); James Feldt, Feldt & Ryerson Facilities Consulting; Principal Dana Whitcomb, Birchdale Elementary; approximately 240 members of the public; representatives of the Pine Hollow Courier and KWCM radio

### 1. Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:32 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance. All seven members were present. The Chair noted the size of the audience, announced the overflow arrangement, and stated that public comment would be extended beyond the customary thirty minutes given the significance of the agenda.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda

Director Beckstrom moved and Clerk Sorensen seconded approval of the agenda as printed. The motion carried on a voice vote, 7–0.

### 3. Consent Agenda

The consent agenda included: (a) minutes of the regular meeting of October 20, 2025; (b) bills payable in the amount of $1,466,281.90; (c) personnel report, including two paraprofessional hires and one family medical leave; (d) approval of the 2026–2027 school calendar first draft for public review; and (e) the quarterly gifts and donations report totaling $6,850.

Vice Chair Villalpando moved and Director Twetten seconded approval of the consent agenda as presented. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Twetten, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 7–0.

### 4. Canvass and Acknowledgment of November 4, 2025 Referendum Results

Business Manager Kalinowski reported the certified results of the November 4, 2025 special election on the operating levy referendum: 1,884 yes votes (46.2 percent) and 2,194 no votes (53.8 percent). The question failed. Clerk Sorensen moved and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded a resolution canvassing and certifying the returns as presented. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Twetten, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 7–0.

Chair Achterberg stated: "The voters have spoken, and the board accepts that verdict. It does not change our arithmetic; it narrows our options."

### 5. Final Report — Facilities Utilization Study, and Administrative Recommendation

Mr. Feldt presented the final facilities report, unchanged in substance from the September first reading. He presented the requested route-time analysis, showing that under consolidation, average one-way ride times for former Birchdale students would increase from 22 minutes to 34 minutes, with a maximum of 51 minutes.

Superintendent Baldacci then presented the administrative recommendation: that the board close Birchdale Elementary effective June 30, 2026; reassign its 187 students to Meadow Ridge Elementary and Pine Hollow Elementary per boundaries to be recommended by the Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee; and reduce 14.5 full-time equivalent positions district-wide through a combination of attrition, retirements, and layoff, for combined annual savings of approximately $1.55 million. He stated that with the referendum's failure, the recommendation was, in his judgment, "no longer one option among several, but the minimum necessary step to keep this district out of statutory operating debt and in control of its own decisions."

### 6. Public Comment

Chair Achterberg announced that 42 persons had signed up to speak and that the three-minute limit would be enforced uniformly. Public comment began at 7:24 p.m. and concluded at 10:26 p.m., a period of three hours, with two brief recesses. All 42 registered speakers were heard. All spoke in opposition to the closure of Birchdale Elementary. The Clerk recorded the following summary of themes, with representative speakers noted; a full speaker list was appended to the official minutes:

- **Sandra Lofquist, Dr. Anita Falk, and 11 other parents** addressed the effect of increased bus ride times on young children, citing the district's own analysis showing rides up to 51 minutes one way.
- **Rev. Daniel Okafor, Gary Holmstad, and 8 other Birchdale residents and business owners** addressed the economic and civic consequences of closure for the town of Birchdale, including projected loss of the community's largest daytime employer.
- **Principal Dana Whitcomb**, speaking as a private citizen, described Birchdale's academic results, noting its reading proficiency was the highest of the four elementary buildings.
- **Emily Vang and 5 other staff members** questioned the sequencing of a closure vote before mediation of the teacher contract and asked whether the 14.5 position cuts would fall disproportionately on Birchdale staff.
- **Jerry Tackaberry, Colleen Reierson, and 6 other residents** proposed alternatives, including a four-day school week, administrative reductions, sale of the district office building, and a renewed levy attempt in 2026 tied to a facilities plan.
- **Seven Birchdale students and alumni**, including senior Noah Determan and fifth grader Willa Falk, spoke about their experiences at the school. Chair Achterberg thanked the students specifically for their participation.
- **Three speakers**, including former board member Arlen Gustafson, asked the board to table the decision until January to allow independent verification of the savings estimates and a community task force review.

At the conclusion of public comment, Chair Achterberg thanked all speakers and stated that the board had heard the community "clearly and at length."

### 7. Board Discussion and Motion to Table

Director Xiong stated that she could not support a closure vote the same night the community had spent three hours testifying, and that the boundary committee's report, due December 1, should be in hand before any decision. Director Beckstrom concurred. Treasurer Halvorsen stated that delay carried real cost, noting that layoff and closure timelines required decisions by early winter to take effect for the 2026–2027 school year. Vice Chair Villalpando stated he was prepared to support the recommendation but would not oppose a thirty-day tabling. Director Twetten asked the administration to return in December with responses to the alternatives raised in public comment, including the four-day week.

Director Xiong moved and Director Beckstrom seconded a motion to table the administrative recommendation to close Birchdale Elementary until the regular meeting of December 15, 2025, and to direct the administration to return with (a) the Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee report, (b) a written analysis of the alternatives proposed in public comment, and (c) a building-by-building breakdown of the proposed 14.5 FTE reduction. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, no; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Twetten, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–1. The recommendation was tabled.

### 8. Superintendent's Report

Given the hour, Superintendent Baldacci abbreviated his report. He reported that mediation with the Pine Hollow Education Association remained scheduled for November 21, 2025, and that American Education Week activities had proceeded in all buildings. His full written report was entered into the record.

### 9. Finance Report

Business Manager Kalinowski presented the report for the period ending October 31, 2025, in abbreviated form. Revenues stood at 30.1 percent of budget and expenditures at 31.8 percent. She confirmed that, with the referendum's failure, the operative projection was the $2.9 million shortfall by June 2027 and statutory operating debt absent board action. She announced the Truth in Taxation hearing set for 6:00 p.m. on December 15, 2025, preceding the regular meeting. The report was received without formal action.

**Action items assigned:** Superintendent Baldacci to deliver the alternatives analysis and FTE breakdown to the board by December 8, 2025. Director Xiong to present the boundary committee report on December 15. Ms. Ostrander to append the full public comment speaker list to the minutes.

### 10. Adjournment

Director Twetten moved and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded adjournment. The motion carried on a voice vote, 7–0. The meeting adjourned at 11:19 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Deborah Sorensen, Clerk
Approved: December 15, 2025

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF EDUCATION

**Body:** Board of Education, Pine Hollow Area School District
**Date:** Monday, December 15, 2025
**Time:** 6:30 p.m. (following the Truth in Taxation hearing held 6:00–6:25 p.m.)
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, Pine Hollow, Minnesota (overflow seating with audio in the adjacent cafeteria)

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Deborah Sorensen; Director Mai Xiong; Director Lila Beckstrom

**Members Absent:** Director Paul Twetten (absent due to hospitalization; excused)

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Kelli Ostrander (recording); Attorney Susan Greeley, district labor counsel (closed session only); Principal Dana Whitcomb; approximately 190 members of the public; representatives of the Pine Hollow Courier and KWCM radio

### 1. Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:31 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance. Six members were present; Director Twetten was absent, excused, following a medical emergency. The Chair asked that the board's well wishes to Director Twetten be entered into the record. A quorum existed.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda

Superintendent Baldacci requested a closed session under Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03 for labor negotiations strategy following the November 21 mediation. Treasurer Halvorsen moved and Vice Chair Villalpando seconded approval of the agenda as amended. The motion carried on a voice vote, 6–0.

### 3. Consent Agenda

The consent agenda included: (a) minutes of the regular meeting of November 17, 2025, with the appended public comment speaker list; (b) bills payable in the amount of $1,529,470.55; (c) personnel report, including the retirement of teacher Margaret Ohnstad effective June 2026; (d) second reading and adoption of the 2026–2027 school calendar; and (e) approval of the annual audit report presented by Carlson Beckley CPAs, which rendered an unmodified opinion.

Clerk Sorensen moved and Director Beckstrom seconded approval of the consent agenda as presented. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–0.

### 4. Public Comment

Chair Achterberg noted that 19 persons had registered and that the three-minute limit applied. Seventeen speakers opposed closure of Birchdale Elementary, largely restating the themes recorded in the November 17 minutes; among them, Sandra Lofquist presented an updated petition of 693 signatures, received by the Clerk, and Rev. Okafor asked the board, if it proceeded, to commit to a community process for the building's future use. Two speakers, Tom Sivertsen and Colleen Reierson, urged the board to approve the closure, citing the failed referendum and the finance projections. Public comment concluded at 7:41 p.m.

### 5. Superintendent's Report — Alternatives Analysis and Responses Directed on November 17

Superintendent Baldacci presented the written analysis directed by the board on November 17. He reported: (a) a four-day school week would save an estimated $280,000 annually, insufficient alone and with unresolved childcare and instructional-time concerns; (b) administrative reductions of one director position, already included in the 14.5 FTE plan, would save $128,000; (c) the district office building's sale would yield a one-time estimated $340,000 and was recommended for separate study; and (d) a renewed 2026 levy could not be relied upon to prevent statutory operating debt given certification timelines. He presented the FTE breakdown: of the 14.5 positions, 6.0 were expected through retirement and attrition, and the reductions were distributed district-wide rather than concentrated at Birchdale, with all Birchdale probationary and continuing-contract teachers offered placement rights in the receiving buildings under the master agreement. He restated the administrative recommendation to close Birchdale Elementary effective June 30, 2026.

### 6. Report — Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee

Director Xiong presented the committee's report, adopted by the committee on December 1, 2025, by a vote of 12–3. The report recommended, contingent on any closure decision, boundary revisions assigning 104 former Birchdale students to Meadow Ridge Elementary and 83 to Pine Hollow Elementary, with route adjustments limiting maximum one-way ride time to 45 minutes. Director Xiong stated that the committee had done its work in good faith but noted, speaking for herself, that "a majority of the committee wished to record that it was planning for an outcome it did not endorse." The report was received without formal action, to be taken up if closure were approved.

### 7. Finance Report and Levy Certification

Business Manager Kalinowski presented the finance report for the period ending November 30, 2025, showing revenues at 39.4 percent and expenditures at 40.2 percent of budget, and reconfirmed the $2.9 million projected shortfall by June 2027 absent action. Following the Truth in Taxation hearing held at 6:00 p.m., at which two residents spoke, she recommended certification of the final payable 2026 levy at $4,218,662, the maximum authorized, representing a 1.9 percent increase over payable 2025.

Treasurer Halvorsen moved and Clerk Sorensen seconded adoption of the resolution certifying the final payable 2026 property tax levy at $4,218,662. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, yes; Beckstrom, yes. Motion carried 6–0.

### 8. Action Item — Resolution to Close Birchdale Elementary School and Reduce Programs and Positions

Chair Achterberg stated that the item tabled on November 17 was now before the board, all directed materials having been delivered. Vice Chair Villalpando moved and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded a motion to remove the item from the table. Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, yes; Xiong, no; Beckstrom, no. Motion carried 4–2.

Vice Chair Villalpando then moved and Treasurer Halvorsen seconded adoption of the resolution: (a) closing Birchdale Elementary School effective June 30, 2026; (b) directing the administration to implement the Attendance Boundary Advisory Committee's boundary recommendations for the 2026–2027 school year; (c) discontinuing positions and reducing programs equivalent to 14.5 full-time equivalents effective June 30, 2026, per the schedule presented, subject to the master agreement and applicable statute; and (d) directing the Superintendent to convene a community task force by March 1, 2026, on the future use of the Birchdale building.

In discussion, Treasurer Halvorsen stated the resolution was "the least bad option left to us" after the referendum's failure. Director Beckstrom stated she would vote no, citing the ride-time analysis and Birchdale's academic performance. Director Xiong stated she would vote no, saying the district "chose its timeline over its community's trust." Clerk Sorensen stated that she believed the finances compelled action but could not reconcile voting in Director Twetten's absence on a matter of this magnitude, and announced she would abstain. Chair Achterberg stated she would vote yes "with a heavy heart and open eyes."

Roll call: Achterberg, yes; Villalpando, yes; Halvorsen, yes; Sorensen, abstain; Xiong, no; Beckstrom, no. The motion carried 3–2, with one abstention and one member absent. The resolution was adopted.
