Marisol,

Here is where the buildings actually sit as of the March 1 count. I am giving you headcount, design capacity, and utilization, plus the operating cost that is genuinely avoidable if a building goes dark.

Ponderosa Elementary — 317 students, design capacity 720, utilization 44 percent. Building opened 1968, roof replaced 2011, boiler original. Avoidable operating cost if closed: $1.42 million. Juniper Flat Elementary — 402 students, design capacity 660, utilization 61 percent. Opened 1991, best mechanical systems of the four. Avoidable operating cost: $1.61 million.

Sawyer Creek Elementary — 289 students, design capacity 570, utilization 51 percent. Opened 1974. This is our most expensive building per student to operate, $13,800 against a district elementary average of $10,900. Avoidable operating cost: $1.38 million.

Deschutes View Elementary — 511 students, design capacity 580, utilization 88 percent. Opened 2004. This building is not a closure candidate. It is in the study because it is the receiving building in two of three packages and its capacity headroom is 69 seats, which is the single tightest number in this entire exercise.

Three packages.

Package A closes Sawyer Creek only. Students distribute to Deschutes View and Juniper Flat. Gross savings $1.9 million, one-time costs $600,000 — moving, boundary notification, some classroom furniture, one modular relocation. Five-year net $8.9 million. This is the small, safe option.

Package B closes Sawyer Creek and Ponderosa. Gross savings $3.2 million, one-time costs $850,000. Five-year net $15.1 million. Requires Deschutes View to absorb roughly 190 students, which it cannot do without two modulars, which is in the $850,000.

Package C closes Sawyer Creek, Ponderosa, and Juniper Flat, and reconfigures the remaining elementary footprint into a grade-band structure — two K-2 buildings and two 3-5 buildings on the west side. Gross savings $4.6 million, one-time costs $1.1 million. Five-year net $21.9 million. This is the option that actually closes the gap in combination with the reductions already in the draft.

Two things I want on the record before anyone reacts.

First, none of these packages closes $8.4 million by itself. Package C gets us $4.6 million in year one against $1.1 million in one-time costs, so first-year net is $3.5 million. The remaining $4.9 million comes from reductions that are already drafted and that are going to hurt in their own right. Consolidation is necessary and it is not sufficient. I would rather everyone understand that on March 3 than in June.

Second, my savings numbers assume we do not backfill. Every consolidation I have worked on has a phase where people discover that the receiving buildings need a counselor and a half and another custodian and an extra bus route, and 20 percent of the savings evaporates in the first eighteen months. I have haircut the numbers by 12 percent already. I would not haircut them further and I would not let anyone tell you they are conservative.

Grant, Marisol,

Transportation and boundaries. I have run the routing model on all three packages and the news ranges from manageable to genuinely bad.

Package A, Sawyer Creek closes. Sawyer Creek's attendance area is geographically large and already almost fully bused — 261 of 289 students ride. Splitting them between Deschutes View and Juniper Flat adds two routes and extends average ride time from 24 minutes to 31. Annual added transportation cost $148,000. I would call this absorbable. Grant, note that this comes off your $1.9 million.

Package B adds Ponderosa. This is where it gets hard. Terrance is right and Grant is right to flag it. Ponderosa's 96 walkers become riders, and they are not conveniently clustered — they are spread across a dense grid with eleven crossings that our safety protocol will not let elementary students traverse unaccompanied at the distances involved. I need four additional routes, not three, because two of the pickup clusters are on the wrong side of Wilson Road and the turnaround costs me a full run. Annual added transportation cost for Package B is $340,000 all in, and I need one additional bus purchase at $142,000, which belongs in your one-time column and is not currently there.

Average ride time for former Ponderosa students goes from 11 minutes to 33 minutes. For the 41 students in McKinney-Vento status, whose pickup points change during the year by definition, it is worse and less predictable.

Package C is the one I want to talk about carefully because the grade-band structure changes the problem qualitatively rather than quantitatively. Under a K-2 / 3-5 split, every family with children in both bands is running two schools. In our current elementary population, 618 families have siblings spanning that line. Those families need two dropoffs and two pickups, or they need bus service to two buildings on overlapping bell schedules, which means either staggered start times across the district or a substantially larger fleet.

I priced both. Staggered starts are cheaper — $210,000 added annual transportation — but they push the earliest elementary start to 7:35 and the latest to 9:15, and the 9:15 building dismisses at 3:55, which collides with our secondary athletics transportation and with a lot of working parents. Non-staggered requires six additional routes and two bus purchases and comes in at $495,000 annual plus $284,000 one-time.

So my honest read: Package C's $4.6 million is really $4.1 million, and that is before Grant's other backfill risk.

One more thing on boundaries. Whatever we do, Deschutes View's 69 seats of headroom is doing an enormous amount of work in these models. Under Package B, Deschutes View takes about 190 students into 69 seats plus two modulars. Modulars are 26 seats each. That is 121 seats against 190 students. The other 69 go to Juniper Flat, which means Juniper Flat's boundary moves east, which means about 40 current Juniper Flat families get reassigned to make room. Reassigning families who were not in a closing school is politically the most expensive thing in this entire exercise and it is invisible in every version of the savings table.
