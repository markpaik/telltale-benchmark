## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the mandatory process by which Chugach Air Cargo Services (CACS) accepts, verifies, documents, plans, loads, and carries dangerous goods, and by which CACS reports discrepancies and incidents to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). It exists to ensure full compliance with 49 CFR Parts 171 through 180 and the current edition of the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (IATA DGR), to protect employees, aircraft, and the communities we serve, and to prevent recurrence of the deficiencies identified in the 2025 FAA enforcement action and the subsequent internal audit, specifically: declaration errors, acceptance by personnel with lapsed recurrent training, and the absence of a written procedure for hidden or undeclared dangerous goods.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- All dangerous goods shipments tendered for carriage on any CACS scheduled or charter flight, on all nine aircraft, from the Anchorage hub and all eight village stations.
- All CACS employees and contract agents who accept, check, document, plan, load, or handle cargo, and all duty officers.
- All commodities regulated as dangerous goods, including but not limited to Section II lithium batteries, dry ice (UN 1845), cartridges for weapons/ammunition (UN 0012, UN 0014), aviation fuel in drums (UN 1863), and diagnostic specimens (UN 3373).
- Exception situations defined in Section 6.10: village stations without a certified scale, medevac charters, damaged packages discovered on the ramp, and undeclared dangerous goods discovered in a package already accepted.

This procedure does **not** apply to company materials (COMAT) carried under a separate approval, or to items carried by passengers or crew under 49 CFR 175.10; those are addressed in SOP-DG-110.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Acceptance** | The point at which CACS takes custody of a shipment and confirms it complies with all applicable regulations, evidenced by a completed and signed form DG-201. |
| **Acceptance agent** | A trained and currently qualified employee who performs the first check of a tendered shipment at the counter. |
| **Certified scale** | A scale bearing a current calibration certificate issued within the preceding 12 months by an approved calibration vendor. |
| **DG checker** | A trained and currently qualified employee who performs the independent second check of documentation, marking, labeling, and packaging before a shipment is released to load planning. |
| **DG-201** | The CACS dangerous goods acceptance checklist, completed for every dangerous goods shipment and retained as a record. |
| **Duty officer** | The CACS manager on call at all hours, reachable at the number posted in every station, with authority to make operational decisions on dangerous goods matters when the DG Compliance Manager is unavailable. |
| **Hermes** | The CACS cargo management system of record for booking, acceptance, tracking, weight, load planning, and manifesting. |
| **Hidden dangerous goods** | Items tendered as general cargo that are, or may contain, dangerous goods, whether by shipper ignorance or concealment. |
| **NOTOC** | Notification to Captain: the document generated in Hermes before departure listing all dangerous goods loaded, their locations, and emergency response drill codes. |
| **Pre-alert** | Advance notice of an intended dangerous goods shipment entered in Hermes at least 24 hours before tender for scheduled service. |
| **Recurrent training interval** | 24 months from the last completed dangerous goods training, per 49 CFR 172.704. Training is expired on the day after the 24-month anniversary. |
| **Rejection** | A documented refusal to accept a tendered shipment, recorded on form DG-205. |
| **Section II lithium batteries** | Lithium cells or batteries meeting the limited quantities and conditions of IATA Packing Instructions 965–970 Section II (as applicable and permitted in the current edition). |
| **Shipper's Declaration (DGD)** | The Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods required by 49 CFR 172.204 and IATA DGR Section 8, completed and signed by the shipper. |
| **UN specification packaging** | Packaging marked and tested in accordance with 49 CFR Part 178 and IATA DGR Section 6. |

## 4. Responsibilities

**Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager (Yelena Sorokin).** Owns this procedure. Maintains the currency of regulatory references, approves shipper qualification, maintains the training matrix, conducts quarterly audits of completed DG-201 files, files all FAA incident and discrepancy reports, and serves as final authority on acceptance questions.

**Vice President of Operations (Cheryl Aningayou).** Approves this procedure and any revisions. Ensures resourcing for training, scales, placards, labels, and Hermes system functions. Reviews the quarterly audit summary and the annual FAA reporting log.

**Acceptance agents (Anchorage counter and eight village stations).** Perform the first check: verify shipper qualification, documentation, marking, labeling, package condition, and weight. Complete Part A of form DG-201. May accept only if their recurrent training is current; Hermes enforces this lockout (Section 6.8.3). Reject non-compliant shipments and complete form DG-205.

**Dangerous goods checkers.** Perform the independent second check of every dangerous goods shipment before release to load planning. Complete Part B of form DG-201. A DG checker may not second-check a shipment they accepted.

**Load planners.** Apply segregation, quantity, and aircraft-type limits (Section 6.4); assign positions; and generate the NOTOC in Hermes before departure.

**Ramp handlers.** Load in accordance with the load plan, verify labels are visible and packages undamaged at loading, secure per the aircraft loading schedule, and immediately report any damage, leak, or discrepancy to the duty officer.

**Duty officer.** Reachable at all hours. Makes real-time decisions on exception situations, initiates incident response, and notifies the DG Compliance Manager of any reportable event within the timelines in Section 6.7.

**Station managers (village stations).** Ensure scale certification, label and form stock, current regulation access, and that no untrained personnel accept cargo.

**Pilots in command.** Review and sign the NOTOC before departure; retain a copy on board; may refuse any shipment.

## 5. Prerequisites

Before any station may accept dangerous goods, the station must have:

1. At least one acceptance agent with current (within 24 months) dangerous goods training, verified in the Hermes training module.
2. A certified scale with a calibration certificate dated within the preceding 12 months, posted at the scale. (Exception path: Section 6.10.1.)
3. Current-edition IATA DGR (paper or verified electronic access) and access to 49 CFR via the posted electronic link.
4. Stock of forms DG-201 (acceptance checklist), DG-205 (rejection record), DG-210 (discrepancy/incident report), hazard labels, handling labels, and overpack markings.
5. The posted duty officer contact card and emergency response guidance (current ERG).

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Shipper Qualification and Pre-Alert

**6.1.1** No dangerous goods shipment may be booked from a shipper who is not registered in Hermes as a **Qualified DG Shipper**, except as provided in 6.1.5.

**6.1.2** To qualify a shipper, the acceptance agent or station manager submits a Shipper Qualification Request in Hermes containing: legal business or individual name, physical address, 24-hour emergency contact, the identity and training basis of the person who will sign declarations (49 CFR 172.704 requires shipper personnel to be trained), and the commodities intended for shipment. The DG Compliance Manager reviews and approves or denies within two business days. Approval is valid for 24 months.

**6.1.3** For every dangerous goods shipment on scheduled service, the shipper (or the accepting station on the shipper's behalf) enters a **pre-alert** in Hermes at least **24 hours before tender**, listing: UN number, proper shipping name, class/division, packing group (if applicable), number and type of packages, net quantity per package, and intended flight. Hermes flags the booking "DG-PENDING."

**6.1.4** Load planning will not confirm space for dangerous goods without a pre-alert. This protects segregation and quantity planning, particularly for dry ice loads up to the 200 kg per-flight company limit.

**6.1.5** **Walk-up exception.** A non-registered shipper tendering only the following may be accepted same-day without prior qualification, at the accepting station's discretion, provided all other requirements of this SOP are met: Section II lithium batteries contained in or packed with equipment, dry ice under 25 kg net accompanying perishables, UN 3373 diagnostic specimens, and small-arms ammunition (UN 0012/0014, Division 1.4S) up to 25 kg gross per shipment. The agent completes a Walk-Up Shipper Record in Hermes capturing name, ID type, address, and phone. All other commodities — including fuel drums and any fully regulated lithium batteries — require prior qualification and pre-alert, no exceptions.

**6.1.6** A shipper with two rejections for cause within 12 months is placed on **Enhanced Review**: every shipment from that shipper requires DG Compliance Manager sign-off in Hermes before acceptance. Three rejections for cause within 12 months results in suspension of DG shipping privileges pending retraining evidence.

### 6.2 Document Verification (First Check — Acceptance Agent)

**6.2.1** Open a DG-201 checklist in Hermes (or on paper at stations during a Hermes outage, entered into Hermes within 24 hours). Every line item must be marked YES, NO, or N/A. Any NO stops acceptance and routes to rejection (Section 6.5).

**6.2.2 Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods.** Verify, against the current IATA DGR List of Dangerous Goods (Section 4.2) and 49 CFR 172.101:

1. Two signed copies presented (one for the carrier file, one to travel with the shipment).
2. Shipper and consignee full names and addresses; consignee is a deliverable address in the destination community.
3. "Aircraft type" limitation box: **Cargo Aircraft Only** marked where required; verify the commodity/quantity is permitted on the intended aircraft (Section 6.4).
4. UN number, proper shipping name (with technical name in parentheses where required by an asterisk in the DGR), class/division, subsidiary hazard, and packing group all match the DGR entry exactly.
5. Quantity and packaging: number and type of packages, net quantity per package with correct unit, and packing instruction number correct for the entry and aircraft type.
6. Authorization column: any special provisions, overpack statement ("Overpack used" and total quantity where required), or approvals cited and attached.
7. 24-hour emergency response telephone number (49 CFR 172.604) that is monitored — for fully regulated goods, if in doubt, call it and verify it answers.
8. Shipper's certification statement present, signed, dated, with printed name and title.
9. No alterations without shipper initials; no correction fluid.

**6.2.3 Documentation not requiring a DGD.** For Section II lithium batteries: verify the air waybill or Hermes record carries the required statement per the applicable packing instruction; no DGD is required. For UN 3373: verify the shipment is described as "Biological Substance, Category B, UN 3373" on the airbill; no DGD required. For dry ice not accompanying other dangerous goods: airbill must show UN 1845, Carbon dioxide, solid (or Dry ice), number of packages, and net weight of dry ice per package; a DGD is not required but is acceptable if provided.

**6.2.4** If **any** entry on the DGD is incorrect, incomplete, or illegible, the agent may **not** correct it. Only the shipper may correct and initial changes, or provide a new declaration. This is a direct control against the audit finding of errors on 14 of 50 sampled declarations: agents verify; shippers correct.

**6.2.5** Enter the verified data into Hermes exactly as it appears on the DGD. Hermes performs an automatic cross-check against its embedded DGR table and flags mismatches; a flagged mismatch stops acceptance until resolved.

### 6.3 Package Marking, Labeling, and Condition Inspection

**6.3.1 Marking.** Verify on each package:

1. UN number and proper shipping name, durable and legible, matching the DGD.
2. Shipper and consignee names and addresses.
3. UN specification marking present and appropriate for the packing instruction and packing group (not required for limited quantity, Section II lithium, UN 3373, or dry ice, where the applicable alternative markings are verified instead).
4. Net quantity marking where required (e.g., dry ice net weight on each package).
5. Lithium battery mark (UN 3481/3091/3480/3090 as applicable) with a telephone number, correct size and format.
6. Limited quantity mark ("Y" mark for air) where applicable.
7. Orientation arrows on two opposite sides for packages containing liquids in combination packagings (including fuel drums in overpacks).
8. Overpacks: marked "OVERPACK" where inner markings are not visible, with all required marks/labels reproduced.

**6.3.2 Labeling.** Verify primary hazard label, subsidiary hazard label(s), Cargo Aircraft Only label where required, and handling labels (e.g., Class 9 for dry ice, "Keep Away From Heat" where applicable) — correct design, correct location (same surface near the shipping name where package size allows), not obscured, not contradicted by irrelevant old labels. **Remove or obliterate any old labels/markings from reused packagings before acceptance; if this cannot be done cleanly, reject.**

**6.3.3 Condition.** Inspect every package on all visible surfaces:

1. No leaks, stains, odors, dents compromising integrity, punctures, crushing, or wetness.
2. Closures secure; drum bungs tight and gasketed; for UN 1863 fuel drums, verify UN 1A1 or 1A2 specification marking, closure per closure instructions, and no bulging or corrosion.
3. No sound of loose contents where inners should be immobilized.
4. Sift-proof and shift-proof as appropriate for the contents.
5. For lithium batteries: package appears capable of withstanding handling; terminals-protection statement confirmed by shipper on the Hermes acceptance question; no evidence of heat damage.

**6.3.4 Weighing.** Weigh every package on the certified scale. Recorded weight must be within 5% of declared gross weight or the declared weight must be corrected by the shipper. Enter actual weight in Hermes; Hermes uses **actual** weight for load planning.

**6.3.5** The acceptance agent signs Part A of DG-201 with name, employee number, station, date, and time. Hermes will not allow Part A signature by any employee whose DG training expiry date has passed (see 6.8.3).

### 6.4 Quantity and Segregation Limits by Aircraft Type

**6.4.1** The load planner (and acceptance agent at pre-alert review) applies the limits below. Where CACS limits are stricter than the DGR, the CACS limit governs. The DGR per-package limits always govern in addition.

**Table 1 — Aircraft-type limits**

| Item | 737-400F (all-cargo) | Saab 340 freighter | Cessna 208 Caravan | CASA 212 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cargo Aircraft Only shipments | Permitted | Permitted | Permitted | Permitted |
| Dry ice, total per flight | 200 kg | 200 kg | 90 kg | 140 kg |
| UN 1863 fuel drums (PG III), per flight | 12 drums (max 2,640 L) | 4 drums | **Prohibited** | 2 drums |
| Class 1 (1.4S only), gross per flight | 150 kg | 75 kg | 25 kg | 50 kg |
| Fully regulated lithium batteries (UN 3480/3090) | Per DGR, CAO, DG Compliance Manager approval required | Same, max 35 kg net | **Prohibited** | Same, max 35 kg net |
| Section II lithium (in/with equipment) | Per DGR | Per DGR | Per DGR | Per DGR |
| UN 3373 specimens | Unlimited per DGR | Unlimited per DGR | Unlimited per DGR | Unlimited per DGR |

*Only Division 1.4S ammunition is authorized for carriage; all other Class 1 requires written DG Compliance Manager approval and applicable FAA authorization.*

**6.4.2 Dry ice ventilation and animals.** Dry ice sublimation limits per compartment are posted in each aircraft's loading schedule; the load planner verifies total dry ice against the compartment limit, not only the 200 kg company cap. Live animals are never loaded in the same compartment as dry ice on the Caravan; on other types, minimum 3 m separation and never downstream of airflow from dry ice.

**6.4.3 Segregation.** Apply IATA DGR Table 9.3.A. Key applications at CACS:

1. Class 3 (fuel drums) segregated from Class 1 (ammunition): do not load adjacent; minimum one pallet position or 2 m apart, and never in the same overpack or ULD.
2. Fully regulated lithium batteries (Class 9) separated from Class 3 flammable liquids by at least one position.
3. Dry ice away from live animals per 6.4.2 and away from hatching eggs.
4. Cargo Aircraft Only packages loaded where accessible in flight where required by the DGR, or per the alternative provisions applicable to the aircraft type; on the Caravan and CASA 212, CAO items are loaded per the aircraft-specific loading schedule appendix.
5. Damaged-package quarantine area (Section 6.10.3) is never within 10 m of the fuel drum staging area.

**6.4.4** Hermes enforces Table 1 automatically; a load exceeding a limit cannot be finalized. An override requires DG Compliance Manager or duty officer credentials and generates an audit log entry.

### 6.5 Second Check, Acceptance Decision, and Rejection

**6.5.1 Second check.** Before any DG shipment moves to load planning, a DG checker (not the accepting agent) independently re-verifies Sections 6.2 and 6.3 against the physical packages and documents, and completes Part B of DG-201 with signature, employee number, date, and time. At single-agent village stations where no second qualified person is on site, the second check is performed **remotely** by an Anchorage DG checker via the Hermes photo-verification workflow: the station agent uploads photographs of the DGD (both pages), all package faces, all markings and labels, and the scale readout; the Anchorage checker completes Part B remotely. No DG shipment departs a village station without a completed Part B.

**6.5.2 Acceptance.** When Parts A and B are complete with all YES/N/A responses, Hermes changes the shipment status to **DG-ACCEPTED**, prints the acceptance sticker (shipment number, UN number, destination, "DG"), and releases it to load planning. Apply the sticker to the package; stage the shipment in the marked DG staging area, segregated per 6.4.3.

**6.5.3 Rejection.** If any check fails:

1. Do not take custody. If the shipment is already in hand, place it in the DG hold area, physically separated and tagged "REJECTED — DO NOT LOAD."
2. Complete **form DG-205** in Hermes: shipper, description, tender date/time, the specific checklist item(s) failed, the regulatory citation (DGR paragraph or CFR section), the agent's name, and whether the shipment was returned to the shipper or held pending correction.
3. Give the shipper a printed DG-205 copy explaining exactly what must be corrected. The agent may explain the requirement but may not prepare, mark, label, or complete documents on the shipper's behalf.
4. Hermes links the DG-205 to the shipper's record for the Enhanced Review counter (6.1.6).
5. A rejected shipment held for correction may remain in the DG hold area up to 72 hours; after that it is returned to the shipper or, if unclaimed, the duty officer determines disposition.
6. If the reason for rejection reveals a package that is leaking, damaged, or hazardous as tendered, treat it under Section 6.10.3 and evaluate reportability under Section 6.7.

**6.5.4** A rejection is never "waived" verbally. Any deviation from a checklist requirement requires a documented determination by the DG Compliance Manager citing the regulatory basis (e.g., an applicable special provision), attached to the DG-201.

### 6.6 Load Planning, Loading, and Notification to Captain

**6.6.1** The load planner builds the load in Hermes, assigning each DG shipment a specific position, verifying Table 1 limits, compartment dry ice limits, segregation per 6.4.3, and CAO accessibility requirements.

**6.6.2** Ramp handlers load per the plan. At loading, the handler performs a final visual check: labels visible, no new damage, orientation arrows respected, packages secured against movement in all directions per the aircraft loading schedule. Fuel drums are loaded upright, closures up, strapped individually or palletized and netted. Any anomaly stops loading and goes to the duty officer.

**6.6.3 NOTOC.** Before departure, Hermes generates the **Notification to Captain** listing, for each DG shipment: air waybill number, proper shipping name, UN number, class/division and subsidiary hazard, packing group, number of packages, net quantity, loading position, CAO indicator, and the emergency response drill code. The load planner signs it; the loading supervisor confirms positions match the aircraft; the pilot in command reviews and signs before engine start. One signed copy goes in the trip envelope on board; one is retained in the station file; the Hermes record is permanent.

**6.6.4** No aircraft departs with dangerous goods aboard without a signed NOTOC. A last-minute load change involving DG requires a reissued NOTOC before departure — handwritten amendments are not permitted except a line-through deletion of an offloaded item, initialed by the load planner and PIC.

**6.6.5** The PIC may refuse any item. A refusal is recorded in Hermes with reason; the item returns to the DG staging area and re-enters planning for a later flight.

### 6.7 Discrepancy and Incident Reporting to the FAA

**6.7.1 Immediate internal notification.** Any employee who observes a leak, fire, smoke, venting, spill, suspected radioactive contamination, undeclared/misdeclared goods, a DG package damaged in our custody, or DG carried contrary to regulation notifies the **duty officer immediately** — before cleanup, before further movement of the package. The duty officer notifies the DG Compliance Manager within one hour.

**6.7.2 Immediate (telephonic) reports — 49 CFR 171.15.** The DG Compliance Manager (or duty officer if the Manager is unreachable) telephones the **National Response Center at 800-424-8802** **as soon as practical but within 12 hours** of any occurrence during transportation (including loading, unloading, or temporary storage) in which, as a direct result of dangerous goods: a person is killed or hospitalized; evacuation of the general public occurs for one hour or more; a flight pattern or routine is altered; fire, breakage, spillage, or suspected contamination involves radioactive material or an infectious substance; or a situation exists that, in the judgment of the person in possession, should be reported (e.g., continuing danger). A lithium battery fire, venting, or smoke event — such as the 2025 Anchorage warehouse venting — **is reportable under this section** and additionally requires immediate notification to the FAA per 49 CFR 171.15(a) for incidents aboard or intended for aircraft.

**6.7.3 Written incident reports — 49 CFR 171.16.** Within **30 days** of any incident requiring a 171.15 telephonic report, and of any unintentional release of dangerous goods or any occurrence of undeclared or improperly declared dangerous goods discovered in an air shipment, the DG Compliance Manager files **DOT Form F 5800.1** with PHMSA (electronically via the PHMSA portal) and provides a copy to the FAA and to the CACS incident file.

**6.7.4 Discrepancy reports — undeclared/hidden goods.** Discovery of undeclared or misdeclared dangerous goods in cargo offered for air transport is reported to the **FAA Regional Office (Alaskan Region) within 24 hours of discovery** by telephone or email, followed by the written 5800.1 within 30 days where required. The internal form DG-210 documents the discovery: who found it, when, where, package description, shipper, what was found, photographs, and disposition.

**6.7.5** The DG Compliance Manager maintains the **FAA Reporting Log** in Hermes recording every notification: date/time of event, date/time of each report, agency, person contacted, report reference number, and 5800.1 filing date. The VP of Operations reviews this log quarterly. Missing a reporting deadline is itself recorded and root-caused.

**6.7.6** No employee will be disciplined for making a good-faith report of an incident, discrepancy, or their own error. Concealment of a reportable event is grounds for termination.

### 6.8 Training and Qualification Records

**6.8.1 Training requirement.** Per 49 CFR 172.704 and IATA DGR 1.5, every employee performing a DG function (acceptance, checking, load planning, ramp handling, duty officer, flight crew interface) completes function-specific initial training **before performing the function unsupervised**, and recurrent training within **24 months** of the previous training. Training covers general awareness, function-specific duties, safety, security awareness, and emergency response, with a written test (pass mark 80%).

**6.8.2 Records.** For each employee, Hermes holds: name and employee number, function(s), training completion dates, curriculum version, test score, trainer name, and **training expiry date**. Records are retained for the duration of employment plus 90 days at minimum; CACS retains them for three years. Records are available on request to FAA inspectors.

**6.8.3 System lockout.** Hermes automatically blocks any employee whose training expiry date has passed from signing DG-201 Part A or B, finalizing a DG load plan, or issuing a NOTOC. Hermes emails the employee, their supervisor, and the DG Compliance Manager at 60, 30, and 7 days before expiry. **This control directly answers the audit finding of three acceptance checklists signed by agents with lapsed training: lapsed status now makes signature technically impossible, and any paper-workaround signature by a lapsed employee is a disciplinary matter.**

**6.8.4** During a Hermes outage, the station manager verifies currency against the printed monthly training roster posted at each station before permitting any DG signature.

### 6.9 Recognition of Hidden and Undeclared Dangerous Goods at the Counter

**6.9.1** Every acceptance agent screens **all** cargo — not only declared DG — for hidden dangerous goods indicators, using the posted Hidden DG Indicator Card (based on IATA DGR 2.2), including: descriptions such as "household goods," "machinery parts," "camping equipment," "auto parts," "electronics," "frozen food," "medical supplies," "tools," "chainsaw," "outboard motor," or "batteries"; sloshing sounds; chemical odors; old hazard labels; unusual weight; and shipper reluctance to describe contents.

**6.9.2** For any flagged shipment, the agent asks the shipper the standard questions in Hermes ("Does this contain batteries, fuel, aerosols, ammunition, chemicals, dry ice, or anything flammable, pressurized, or magnetic?") and records the answers. Common village-traffic examples: chainsaws and outboard motors (must be drained and purged, with shipper's written statement), snowmachine parts, camp stoves, bear spray (forbidden as general cargo — must be tendered as DG per the DGR entry or refused), and coolers with dry ice.

**6.9.3** If hidden DG is suspected and the shipper cannot resolve it, the agent refuses the shipment (DG-205) or, with the shipper's cooperation, converts it to a properly declared DG shipment. The agent may open a package **only** with the shipper present and consenting; otherwise refuse.

**6.9.4** Undeclared DG discovered **after** acceptance is handled under Section 6.10.4 and reported under 6.7.4.

### 6.10 Exception Paths

#### 6.10.1 Village station without a certified scale

1. If a station's scale certification has expired or the scale is unserviceable, the station manager immediately notifies the duty officer and the DG Compliance Manager and posts the station status in Hermes as **SCALE-DOWN**.
2. While SCALE-DOWN: the station may accept **only** the following DG, using the shipper's declared weight cross-checked against a documented reasonableness check (item count × unit weight from the manufacturer marking, recorded on DG-201): Section II lithium batteries, UN 3373 specimens, and dry ice shipments up to 25 kg declared net per package.
3. All other DG — including fuel drums, ammunition, and dry ice loads over 25 kg per package — must be **weighed on arrival in Anchorage on a certified scale before further carriage**, or refused at origin. Hermes tags such shipments **WEIGH-ON-ARRIVAL**; the Anchorage DG checker records actual weight and re-runs the Table 1 limit check before any onward flight. If Anchorage is the destination, weighing occurs before release to the consignee for record purposes.
4. On flights departing a SCALE-DOWN station, the load planner applies a **10% weight penalty** to all declared cargo weights for aircraft performance planning.
5. Scale recertification is arranged within 14 days; SCALE-DOWN status beyond 14 days requires VP of Operations acknowledgment.

#### 6.10.2 Medevac charters

1. Medevac charters carrying medical oxygen, patient-connected battery-powered equipment, and small quantities of dry ice or specimens operate under the applicable provisions of 49 CFR 175.8/175.10 and the DGR operator provisions; these items accompanying and required by the patient are handled under this exception path, not the standard acceptance flow.
2. The duty officer is notified of every medevac DG carriage before departure. Time permitting, a standard DG-201 is completed; when time does not permit, the PIC and duty officer complete the **Medevac DG Quick Sheet (form DG-215)**: item, quantity, location aboard, and securing method — before departure, with the full Hermes record completed within 24 hours after the flight.
3. Medical oxygen cylinders must be aviation-approved, secured upright or per the equipment mount, with valves protected; maximum cylinders per aircraft as stated in the aircraft loading schedule. Patient equipment lithium batteries are permitted installed in the equipment; spares are carried in carry-on-equivalent stowage with terminals protected, maximum two spares.
4. **No cargo dangerous goods** (fuel drums, ammunition, fully regulated lithium) are carried on a medevac flight.
5. A NOTOC is still required if any non-patient DG (e.g., specimens on the return leg) is carried; the DG-215 serves as the NOTOC for patient-required items.

#### 6.10.3 Damaged package discovered on the ramp

1. **Stop.** Do not move the package further; do not load it. If already loaded, do not fly; the PIC and duty officer decide on offload method.
2. The finder alerts the duty officer immediately and keeps people upwind and clear (minimum 10 m for leaks/odors; 25 m and fire response called for any lithium battery heat, smoke, or venting, and for any fuel leak).
3. The duty officer consults the ERG drill code from the NOTOC/DGD before approach. If the package can be safely handled, trained personnel move it to the **DG quarantine area** (marked, ventilated, ≥10 m from fuel staging) using appropriate PPE (nitrile gloves minimum; respirator and spill kit per the ERG for the class).
4. Photograph the package from all sides, the labels, and any leak. Complete form DG-210.
5. The DG Compliance Manager assesses reportability under 6.7.2/6.7.3. Any release of contents is at minimum a 5800.1 written report; lithium fire/venting and any hospitalization or evacuation triggers the 12-hour NRC call.
6. Disposition: repack in salvage packaging by trained personnel if contents are intact and the shipper authorizes; otherwise return to shipper or dispose through the contracted hazmat vendor. A damaged package never continues by air in its original condition.
7. Decontaminate the area and any equipment; the duty officer confirms in the DG-210 before the quarantine area is cleared.

#### 6.10.4 Undeclared dangerous goods discovered in a package already accepted

1. Applies when DG is found in a shipment accepted as general cargo — at the warehouse, on the ramp, at a downline station, or reported by a consignee.
2. **If discovered before departure:** pull the shipment from the load, amend the load plan, reissue the NOTOC if other DG remains aboard, and move the package to the DG quarantine area.
3. **If discovered in flight** (e.g., odor, smoke): PIC follows the emergency checklist and ICAO emergency response guidance; the duty officer alerts destination/diversion station to isolate the item on arrival.
4. **If discovered after arrival:** the station isolates the package, does not release it to the consignee, and notifies the duty officer.
5. In all cases: complete form DG-210 with photographs; the DG Compliance Manager reports the discovery to the **FAA within 24 hours** (Section 6.7.4) and evaluates 171.15/171.16 obligations. If the item vented, leaked, ignited, or was released, the 12-hour NRC call and 30-day 5800.1 also apply.
6. The shipper is contacted, informed of the violation, and given the DG-205/DG-210 findings in writing. The shipment is either properly re-tendered as declared DG (new acceptance from the start of Section 6.2) or returned by surface means. Hermes places the shipper on Enhanced Review (6.1.6) automatically upon a DG-210 undeclared finding.
7. The DG Compliance Manager conducts a root-cause review within 10 business days: how the item passed the hidden-DG screen (6.9), whether the Indicator Card or questions need revision, and whether retraining is required, documented in the quarterly audit summary.
