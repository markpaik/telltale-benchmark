# Obstetric Service Consolidation and Behavioral Health Capacity Development

**A Proposal to the Board of Directors, Cimarron Valley Health System**

**Submitted by:** Marisol Trevino, President and Chief Executive Officer
**On behalf of:** Anders Blomqvist, CFO; Danielle Kirkpatrick, CNO; Rajiv Mehrotra, CMO
**Finance Committee review:** September 18, 2026
**Full Board action requested:** October 2, 2026
**Total capital request:** $23.7 million

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Cimarron Valley Health System asks the Board to authorize a $23.7 million capital project that consolidates obstetric delivery services from Bison Ridge Critical Access Hospital onto the Enid campus, closes the Bison Ridge obstetric unit, and converts the vacated space and adjacent shell into a 14-bed inpatient behavioral health unit serving northwest Oklahoma. The behavioral health unit would open approximately 26 months after Board approval.

The system operates at a negative 2.8 percent margin. Bison Ridge delivered 96 babies last year against a break-even volume of 240. Two of the four obstetricians credentialed at Bison Ridge have given notice that they will not renew when their contracts end in June. Left unchanged, the Bison Ridge obstetric unit is projected to lose $3.1 million next year, and management does not believe a safe delivery service can be staffed there past June with the remaining physician complement. Separately, a March CMS validation survey cited nurse staffing on the Bison Ridge medical-surgical floor, a finding that must be corrected and sustained regardless of what the Board decides about obstetrics.

At the same time, the Enid emergency department absorbed 1,400 psychiatric boarding hours last year, an average of 31 hours per boarded patient. Those patients occupy emergency treatment space, consume sitter and security hours, and wait for beds that exist only in Oklahoma City. The system currently transfers behavioral health patients out of the region because it has no inpatient psychiatric capacity of its own.

This proposal treats these as one decision rather than two. The obstetric consolidation frees clinical space, nursing staff, and roughly $3.1 million a year of avoided loss. The behavioral health unit puts that space and a portion of those resources against the largest unmet inpatient need in the service area. Neither project stands alone as well as the two stand together.

Management does not present this as a costless decision. Dr. Mehrotra estimates that after consolidation, 41 percent of the service area population would sit more than 45 minutes from a delivery room. Board chair Loretta Pahsetopah has received petitions from the county commission and from an organized citizens group opposing the closure. Those objections are legitimate, and Section 8 of this proposal responds to them directly with a mitigation package that includes prenatal care retention at Bison Ridge, emergency delivery capability, transport support, and a community advisory structure. The Board should understand that no mitigation package fully replaces a local delivery room.

**The specific action requested on October 2** is set out in Section 11. In summary, management asks the Board to approve the project scope, authorize up to $14 million in tax-exempt bond financing, authorize regulatory filings for the obstetric service closure and the behavioral health licensure application, and approve a phased schedule with two named Board checkpoints at which the behavioral health construction phase may be deferred or stopped if funding conditions are not met.

## 2. Background and Current Conditions

### 2.1 System position

Cimarron Valley Health System operates a 186-bed acute care hospital in Enid, six ambulatory clinics across the region, and Bison Ridge Critical Access Hospital, a 25-bed facility 52 miles northwest of Enid. System operating margin for the most recent completed fiscal year was negative 2.8 percent. The system has funded operating losses from reserves and from delayed capital replacement for three consecutive years. Continued deferral of capital renewal is itself a risk; the Enid emergency department and several clinic sites have equipment approaching end of supported life.

### 2.2 Bison Ridge obstetric volumes

Bison Ridge delivered 96 babies in the last completed fiscal year. Volume has declined from a five-year peak and is now approximately 40 percent of the 240-delivery break-even volume calculated by Finance. The break-even figure assumes current staffing model, current payer mix, and current supply and malpractice costs; it is not a target management believes is achievable through marketing or physician recruitment in this service area.

Low-volume obstetric units carry a clinical dimension as well as a financial one. At 96 deliveries a year, an individual labor and delivery nurse may participate in fewer than a dozen births annually, and the unit may go multiple weeks between deliveries requiring emergency intervention. Skill maintenance in low-frequency, high-acuity events — postpartum hemorrhage, shoulder dystocia, emergency cesarean — depends heavily on simulation because live case exposure is thin. This is a recognized pattern in rural obstetrics and is a material factor in management's recommendation.

### 2.3 Physician staffing

Four obstetricians currently hold privileges supporting the Bison Ridge service. Two have confirmed they will not renew when their contracts end in June. Recruitment efforts over the preceding eighteen months have not produced a signed replacement. The remaining two physicians cannot sustain continuous call coverage for a 24/7 delivery service without locum tenens support, which management estimates at $900,000 to $1.2 million annually over and above the projected $3.1 million loss, with no assurance of continuity.

The practical consequence is that the Board's real choice in June is not between an open unit and a closed unit. It is between a planned consolidation with mitigations, transport arrangements, and community notice, and an unplanned interruption driven by coverage gaps.

### 2.4 CMS validation survey finding

A March CMS validation survey cited nurse staffing on the Bison Ridge medical-surgical floor. The citation concerns staffing levels and documentation of staffing assessment relative to patient acuity. A plan of correction has been submitted and accepted. Management wants the Board to be clear on two points. First, the citation is being remediated on its own track and is not contingent on this proposal. Second, the citation illustrates the staffing pressure at Bison Ridge that also affects obstetrics: the facility is competing for a thin regional nursing labor pool, and spreading that pool across a very low volume obstetric unit and a medical-surgical floor has made both harder to staff to standard.

The redeployment described in Section 5 is expected to strengthen medical-surgical coverage at Bison Ridge, which is directly responsive to the survey finding.

### 2.5 Psychiatric boarding in the Enid emergency department

The Enid emergency department recorded 1,400 psychiatric boarding hours last year, averaging 31 hours per patient. Boarding at this scale has several effects:

- Emergency treatment bays are occupied by patients who need inpatient psychiatric care, reducing effective ED capacity and increasing wait times for all presentations.
- Boarded patients require one-to-one observation, drawing nursing and sitter hours away from other assignments.
- Patients wait in an environment not designed for psychiatric care, which is poor care and carries safety and liability exposure.
- Patients are ultimately transferred to Oklahoma City or elsewhere, separating them from family and local outpatient follow-up, which worsens continuity after discharge.

There is no inpatient psychiatric program nearer than Oklahoma City. The nearest program is also the destination for our behavioral health staff recruitment competition, which Section 5 addresses.

## 3. What Is Proposed

Management proposes three linked actions.

**Action one: Consolidate obstetric delivery onto the Enid campus.** All scheduled and unscheduled deliveries for the service area would occur at the Enid hospital, which currently operates a labor and delivery unit with capacity to absorb the additional volume. The consolidated service would combine Bison Ridge's 96 annual deliveries with Enid's existing volume, producing a single unit with materially better case volume per nurse and per physician, sustainable call coverage, and consolidated cesarean, anesthesia, and neonatal resuscitation capability.

**Action two: Close the Bison Ridge obstetric unit.** Effective on a date to be set following regulatory notice and the transition steps in Section 9, Bison Ridge would cease providing scheduled delivery services. Bison Ridge would retain and expand prenatal and postpartum outpatient care, retain emergency stabilization and precipitous delivery capability in its emergency department, and retain its medical-surgical, emergency, swing bed, and outpatient services without change.

**Action three: Build a 14-bed inpatient behavioral health unit in the vacated space.** The vacated obstetric footprint at Bison Ridge, combined with adjacent underutilized space, would be renovated to house a 14-bed adult inpatient psychiatric unit meeting Oklahoma licensure requirements and CMS conditions of participation for psychiatric services, with an anticipated opening 26 months from Board approval.

### 3.1 Why the behavioral health unit at Bison Ridge rather than Enid

The Board will reasonably ask why the psychiatric beds are proposed at Bison Ridge when the boarding problem is in Enid. Management considered both sites. The reasons for Bison Ridge are:

- **Space cost.** The Enid campus has no comparable available footprint. Creating 14 psychiatric beds in Enid would require either new construction or displacing an existing revenue-generating service. Renovation cost at Enid was estimated at roughly $8 million above the Bison Ridge figure.
- **Ligature-resistant construction is easier in a discrete, self-contained unit** with its own entry and controlled circulation, which the Bison Ridge obstetric wing provides and the Enid options do not without extensive corridor reconfiguration.
- **Bison Ridge staff retention.** Placing the new unit at Bison Ridge allows redeployment of the existing nursing staff rather than layoffs, preserves Bison Ridge's employment base in its community, and gives the facility a growth service line at the same moment it loses one.
- **Transport is manageable.** A 52-mile transfer of a medically stabilized psychiatric patient from Enid to Bison Ridge is operationally straightforward compared to the current transfer to Oklahoma City, and it keeps the patient within the system and within reach of local family and outpatient follow-up.

Management acknowledges that the Bison Ridge location adds a transport step for Enid ED patients that an Enid-based unit would not. Section 7 addresses this in the alternatives analysis.

## 4. Why Now

Four conditions converge in the current fiscal year and make deferral more costly than action.

**Physician contract expiration in June.** The two departing obstetricians establish a hard date. After June, the service cannot be safely staffed at current cost. A decision made in October allows an orderly transition; a decision deferred to spring forces an abrupt one.

**Projected $3.1 million loss.** Each year of continued operation consumes $3.1 million that the system does not have at a negative 2.8 percent margin. Over the 26-month construction period alone, deferral would consume approximately $6.7 million — more than the entire foundation campaign target.

**Construction and financing conditions.** Bond market conditions and construction escalation both argue against delay. Finance estimates construction cost escalation of 4 to 6 percent annually for healthcare renovation in this market. A one-year deferral adds an estimated $700,000 to $1.0 million to project cost.

**Behavioral health capacity is a worsening problem.** Psychiatric boarding hours in the Enid ED have increased in each of the last three years. Every month without inpatient capacity is a month of patients held in an emergency department for an average of 31 hours.

## 5. Operations and Staffing

### 5.1 Consolidated obstetric service at Enid

The Enid labor and delivery unit would absorb approximately 96 additional annual deliveries, an increase of roughly 26 percent over current Enid volume. Management's operational assessment:

- **Physical capacity** is adequate within the existing licensed bed count. No additional LDR rooms are required at projected combined volume, though peak-day capacity will be monitored monthly during the first year.
- **Physician coverage** would be provided by the two continuing obstetricians plus the Enid obstetric group, producing a call rotation that Dr. Mehrotra assesses as sustainable and that improves on the current fragile arrangement at both sites.
- **Nursing** requires an incremental 4.5 FTE on the Enid labor and delivery unit. Ms. Kirkpatrick proposes filling these positions preferentially from the Bison Ridge obstetric nursing staff who elect to relocate or commute, with relocation assistance offered.
- **Anesthesia and neonatal** coverage at Enid is already in place at a higher level than Bison Ridge maintained, which represents a clinical quality gain for the transferred volume.

### 5.2 Redeployment of Bison Ridge nursing staff

Ms. Kirkpatrick's plan redeploys 31 Bison Ridge nurses. The system's commitment, which management asks the Board to affirm explicitly, is that no Bison Ridge nurse in good standing will be involuntarily separated as a result of this consolidation. Redeployment paths:

| Destination | Approximate FTE | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bison Ridge medical-surgical floor | 11 | Directly addresses March CMS staffing citation |
| Bison Ridge emergency department | 5 | Supports precipitous delivery and stabilization capability |
| Enid labor and delivery | 4.5 | Relocation assistance offered |
| Bison Ridge outpatient / prenatal clinic | 4 | Expanded prenatal service, Section 8 |
| Behavioral health unit (following training) | 5 | Contingent on unit opening; interim assignment in the gap |
| Bison Ridge swing bed / float | 1.5 | |

Between the obstetric closure and the behavioral health opening there is an interval of roughly 20 to 24 months. Nurses designated for the behavioral health unit would hold interim assignments during that period, with behavioral health orientation and certification training delivered in the final six months before opening. Management regards retention through this gap as a significant execution risk and addresses it in Section 10.

### 5.3 Behavioral health unit staffing

The unit requires 22 new positions, including:

| Role | FTE | Recruitment difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Psychiatrist (medical director) | 1.0 | **High** — critical path |
| Advanced practice psychiatric providers | 2.0 | High |
| Registered nurses (psychiatric) | 11.0 | Moderate — partly met by redeployment |
| Behavioral health technicians | 5.0 | Moderate |
| Licensed clinical social worker / case management | 2.0 | Moderate |
| Unit administrative support | 1.0 | Low |

The psychiatrist recruitment is the single greatest execution risk in this proposal, and management states that plainly. The nearest inpatient psychiatric program is in Oklahoma City, which is both the source of the recruiting competition and the current destination for our transferred patients. Management's approach:

1. **Begin recruitment immediately upon Board approval**, roughly 24 months before opening, rather than at the conventional 9 to 12 month mark.
2. **Engage a specialty psychiatric search firm** with rural placement experience; budgeted in Section 6.
3. **Offer a compensation package benchmarked at or above the 75th percentile** for the region, with loan repayment participation through federal and state rural programs.
4. **Structure a telepsychiatry partnership as both a bridge and a permanent supplement**, so that the unit is not dependent on a single physician's continued presence. Management has identified two potential telepsychiatry vendors and will bring a recommendation to the Finance Committee.
5. **Establish a named Board checkpoint** (Section 9) at which failure to secure psychiatrist commitment triggers a stop-or-defer decision before major construction spend.

### 5.4 Licensure and regulatory steps

**Obstetric closure:**
- Notification to the Oklahoma State Department of Health of service discontinuation, with required advance notice period.
- CMS notification and update of the Bison Ridge CAH service profile.
- Patient notification protocol for all currently enrolled prenatal patients, with individual care transition planning (Section 8).
- Medical staff bylaws action regarding obstetric privileges at Bison Ridge.
- Payer contract amendments reflecting service changes.

**Behavioral health licensure:**
- Application to the Oklahoma State Department of Health for inpatient psychiatric unit licensure.
- Oklahoma Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services coordination and, where applicable, certification for crisis and involuntary admission capability.
- CMS approval for the psychiatric unit under the applicable conditions of participation, including the distinct part unit requirements and the psychiatric-specific medical record, staffing, and treatment planning standards.
- Ligature risk assessment and environment of care design review, conducted at schematic design and again at substantial completion.
- Life safety and fire marshal review for the renovated occupancy.
- Accreditation survey coordination.
- Establishment of policies for involuntary holds, restraint and seclusion, elopement, and law enforcement interface, with the county sheriff and Enid police engaged during the design phase.

Management notes that psychiatric unit licensure is document-intensive and that the review timeline is the principal driver of the 26-month schedule, not construction duration.

### 6.3 The funding gap — stated plainly

The foundation campaign has secured $2.1 million against a $6.2 million target. **The unfunded gap is $4.1 million, or 17 percent of total project cost.** Management does not ask the Board to approve this project on the assumption that the gap will close on its own.

Mr. Blomqvist's assessment of the campaign: the $2.1 million represents early lead gifts from a small donor group. The campaign has not yet run a public phase, has not solicited the regional employer base, and has not approached state or federal rural health capital programs. Management believes the target is achievable but is not yet demonstrated.

Three protections are built into this proposal:

**First, sequencing.** The obstetric consolidation — which generates the $3.1 million annual savings — proceeds on its own schedule and does not depend on the behavioral health funding. If behavioral health financing fails entirely, the consolidation still occurs and still improves system margin.

**Second, a funding checkpoint.** Management proposes a binding Board checkpoint at month 10 (Section 9), at which the campaign must have reached **$4.5 million in pledges (73 percent)** for the construction phase to proceed to bid award. If that threshold is not met, the Board chooses among deferral, scope reduction to a 10-bed unit, or increased bond authorization.

**Third, a scope-reduction fallback.** Management has costed a 10-bed alternative at approximately $19.4 million. This is presented as a contingency, not a recommendation; a 10-bed unit has weaker operating economics because fixed staffing and licensure costs are spread over fewer beds.

### 6.4 Debt capacity and covenant considerations

The proposed $14 million issuance would be tested against existing covenants. Mr. Blomqvist will present the full covenant analysis, pro forma debt service coverage, and days cash on hand projections to the Finance Committee on September 18. Preliminary analysis indicates the issuance is supportable **contingent on realizing the obstetric consolidation savings**. The Board should understand the linkage: the consolidation is not merely a companion project to the bond issuance, it is part of the credit case for it.

## 7. Alternatives Considered

The Board should know that management examined five alternatives and can defend the rejection of each.

### 7.1 Maintain the status quo

**Rejected.** The $3.1 million projected loss is not sustainable at a negative 2.8 percent system margin, and the June physician departures make the clinical staffing model unworkable regardless of financial capacity. Locum coverage at $900,000 to $1.2 million annually would deepen the loss without solving the underlying volume problem. This alternative does not preserve local delivery; it defers an unplanned closure by perhaps 12 to 18 months at a cost of $4 million to $5 million.

### 7.2 Freestanding birth center at Bison Ridge

A midwife-led freestanding birth center for low-risk deliveries was examined in detail because it is the alternative most responsive to community concern.

**Findings:**
- Oklahoma licensure for freestanding birth centers requires a documented transfer agreement and defined transfer times. At 52 miles from the nearest cesarean capability, transfer time for an emergent complication would exceed the standard that management and Dr. Mehrotra consider defensible.
- Approximately 30 to 35 percent of Bison Ridge's 96 deliveries would not have met low-risk criteria, leaving a projected annual volume of 62 to 67 deliveries.
- Certified nurse-midwife recruitment faces the same rural market constraints as physician recruitment. Management could not identify a credible staffing path.
- Financial modeling projected an annual loss of $1.4 million to $1.8 million — better than $3.1 million, but still a persistent drain.
- Malpractice coverage for a freestanding center at this transfer distance was quoted at a premium that management judged prohibitive.

**Rejected** primarily on clinical safety grounds, secondarily on financial and staffing grounds. Dr. Mehrotra's position is that a birth center at this transfer distance creates a category of risk the system should not accept.

### 7.3 Telehealth-supported obstetrics at Bison Ridge

A model using remote physician supervision of on-site nursing and midwifery staff.

**Findings:**
- Telehealth can support prenatal care, consultation, and antenatal monitoring effectively, and **this proposal adopts those elements** (Section 8).
- Telehealth cannot substitute for on-site surgical capability during delivery. Emergency cesarean requires a surgeon, anesthesia, and an operating team physically present.
- The model does not resolve the on-site nursing skill-maintenance problem created by 96 annual deliveries.

**Rejected as a substitute for on-site delivery; adopted as a component of the retained prenatal service.**

### 7.4 Partner or sell the Bison Ridge obstetric service

Management approached two regional systems about assuming the obstetric service. Neither expressed interest at the current volume and payer mix. A third conversation regarding a broader Bison Ridge affiliation remains open but on a timeline that does not intersect the June physician departures.

**Not currently available.**

### 7.5 Behavioral health unit at Enid instead of Bison Ridge

**Findings:**
- Renovation or new construction at Enid was estimated at approximately $8 million above the Bison Ridge figure, bringing total project cost to roughly $31.7 million against a funding plan already short by $4.1 million.
- The Enid option would displace an existing service or require new construction on a constrained site.
- The Enid option would not retain Bison Ridge employment or provide Bison Ridge a replacement service line, materially worsening the community impact of the obstetric closure.

**Rejected on cost and community-impact grounds**, while acknowledging the transport disadvantage. Management estimates the Enid-to-Bison-Ridge transfer adds roughly 70 to 90 minutes to the admission process for boarded ED patients — substantially less than the current Oklahoma City transfer and, more importantly, keeping the patient in-system.

### 7.6 Obstetric consolidation without the behavioral health unit

This alternative — take the $3.1 million savings, leave the space vacant — is financially the strongest single option in the near term.

**Rejected** because it leaves the psychiatric boarding problem unaddressed, forfeits the Bison Ridge employment retention that makes the consolidation defensible to the community, and leaves the system with a shrinking rural facility and no growth strategy. It is, however, the **fallback position** embedded in the checkpoint structure of Section 9, and the Board retains the ability to arrive at it if funding conditions fail.

## 8. Access, Community Impact, and Mitigation

### 8.1 The access impact, stated directly

Dr. Mehrotra estimates that after consolidation, **41 percent of the service area population would sit more than 45 minutes from a delivery room.** This is the central cost of the proposal and management does not minimize it.

The published literature on rural obstetric unit closures is consistent: increased travel distance is associated with more out-of-hospital and unplanned-location births, increased preterm birth in some studies, and reduced prenatal visit adherence. Management expects some of these effects in our service area. The mitigation package below is designed to blunt them; it will not eliminate them.

### 8.2 Community opposition

Board chair Loretta Pahsetopah has received petitions from the county commission and from an organized citizens group. Management's position is that these petitions reflect a real and reasonable community interest and should be answered substantively rather than procedurally. The following commitments are proposed as binding elements of Board approval.

### 8.3 Mitigation commitments

**Retain and expand prenatal and postpartum care at Bison Ridge.** The Bison Ridge outpatient clinic would expand obstetric prenatal capacity, staffed by 4 redeployed nurses plus rotating physician and advanced practice provider coverage from the Enid obstetric group. Routine prenatal visits, ultrasound, antenatal testing, and postpartum follow-up would remain local. **Most obstetric care encounters are prenatal visits, not deliveries.** Under this plan, the large majority of a patient's obstetric visits remain at Bison Ridge.

**Maintain emergency delivery capability in the Bison Ridge emergency department.** Precipitous deliveries will occur. The Bison Ridge ED would maintain: obstetric emergency equipment and medications, annual competency training and simulation for ED nursing and physician staff, neonatal resuscitation certification for all ED clinical staff, and standing protocols developed jointly with the Enid obstetric service. Five redeployed nurses with obstetric experience would be assigned to the ED, strengthening this capability beyond its current state.

**Transport.** Management proposes: a dedicated obstetric transport protocol with the regional EMS provider including guaranteed response prioritization; a transport support fund providing mileage assistance for patients meeting income criteria for the Enid delivery and for prenatal visits requiring Enid travel; and lodging assistance for patients with high-risk pregnancies advised to be near the delivery site in the final weeks. Budgeted in the community transition program line.

**Individual care transition for currently enrolled patients.** Every patient currently receiving prenatal care at Bison Ridge would receive an individual transition plan developed with her provider, including delivery site planning, transport arrangement, and a named care coordinator. No patient will be notified of the change by general announcement alone.

**Telehealth prenatal augmentation.** Remote monitoring for hypertensive disorders and gestational diabetes, and telehealth maternal-fetal medicine consultation, would be available at the Bison Ridge clinic — capability the community does not have today.

**Community advisory committee.** Management proposes a standing committee including county commission representation, citizens group representation, and Bison Ridge medical staff, meeting quarterly through the transition and reporting to the Board twice annually. The committee would review access metrics, transport utilization, out-of-hospital birth incidence, and prenatal visit adherence.

**Public reporting of access metrics.** Management commits to reporting the following annually and publicly for five years: number of out-of-hospital or unplanned-location births in the service area; prenatal visit adherence rates for Bison Ridge clinic patients; EMS obstetric transport volumes and times; and Bison Ridge ED delivery incidents.

**Employment commitment.** No Bison Ridge nurse in good standing will be involuntarily separated as a result of this consolidation. Bison Ridge total employment is projected to be higher after the behavioral health unit opens than it is today.

### 8.4 What mitigation does not do

Management wants the Board to approve this with clear eyes. The mitigation package does not restore a local delivery room. Some families will travel 45 to 70 minutes in labor. Some will deliver on the way. The behavioral health unit does not compensate the specific families affected by the obstetric closure; it serves a different population. The Board is being asked to accept a real reduction in obstetric access in exchange for financial sustainability, improved obstetric clinical quality for the consolidated volume, and new behavioral health capacity. That is the trade, and it should be voted on as such.

### 10.1 The two risks the Board should focus on

Management believes the psychiatrist recruitment and the funding gap are qualitatively different from the others because either one can prevent the behavioral health unit from opening at all. Both are addressed by named checkpoints with explicit Board stop authority. The Board should approve this proposal only if it is prepared to exercise that authority — including, if necessary, to stop the behavioral health component after the obstetric consolidation has already occurred.

Management's view is that this is an acceptable posture. A consolidation-only outcome is financially positive and clinically defensible. It is not the preferred outcome, but it is not a failure state.
