# Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Dev Ramchandani
**Title:** Store Manager, Rio Rancho
**Review Period:** February 1, 2025 – January 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager

## Goals and Results

Dev's goals for the review period were to grow comparable sales ahead of the district, launch and scale the contractor sales desk pilot, hold shrink below target, and continue developing his assistant managers toward promotion readiness. He met or exceeded every one of these goals, and on the sales and pilot goals he delivered the strongest result in the district.

Rio Rancho posted comparable sales growth of 7.2 percent against a chain average that declined 1.8 percent, the best mark among all 22 stores. The contractor sales desk, which Dev proposed and piloted starting last spring, closed its first full year at 1.1 million dollars in sales, exceeding the case he built for it by a wide margin and giving the company a template that two other districts are now evaluating for their own stores. He did this while shrink at Rio Rancho finished at 1.3 percent, comfortably under the 1.6 percent company target and far under the 2.4 percent the chain posted overall. Staffing was stable all year; turnover among hourly associates was below district average, and time to fill open positions ran 18 days, ahead of the 21-day standard. Rio Rancho passed all three quarterly safety walks with no repeat findings.

## Key Metrics

| Metric | Rio Rancho | Target | Chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable sales | +7.2% | n/a | -1.8% |
| Shrink | 1.3% | 1.6% | 2.4% |
| Time to fill | 18 days | 21 days | — |
| Safety walks passed | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 | — |
| Contractor desk sales | $1.1M | n/a | n/a |

## Incidents and Feedback

There were no safety or loss incidents of note this year. Employee survey results place Rio Rancho among the top three stores in the district for engagement, and written comments consistently cite Dev's willingness to teach and to give people real responsibility on the contractor desk build-out. Customer survey results were solid at 71, above the district average though behind Santa Fe. Two of the four contractor-desk associates Dev trained this year have already been identified as bench strength for assistant manager openings elsewhere in the district.

## Self-Assessment (Employee)

> "I built the contractor desk from a spreadsheet to 1.1 million dollars with the staff I had, and I did it without letting the rest of the store slip. I've said clearly that I'm not planning to run a store for a fifth year — I want the regional operations role, and if that doesn't happen this cycle I need a real conversation about what does. I don't want this review to read like a formality while that decision is still open."

## Manager's Assessment

Dev is right that this review cannot be treated as routine. His performance this year was the best in the district on the two measures — sales growth and a new revenue channel — that the company says it cares most about, and he did it without compromising shrink, staffing, or safety to get there. I have no reservations recommending him for the regional operations manager opening; he is one of three internal finalists, and I have said so directly to the hiring committee.

I want to be equally direct about what happens if he is not selected. Dev told me in May that he does not intend to remain a store manager past this year, and I take that at face value. Separately from the promotion decision, Rio Rancho is one of the two stores converting to the smaller-format layout in March, and that conversion consolidates management of the combined footprint into a single store manager position rather than two. Those two facts arrive at the same time, and neither one is a performance issue — they are both business decisions that will determine whether Dev has a store manager job to be in past the spring regardless of how this review reads.

## Rating

**Overall: Exceeds Expectations**

This rating reflects results delivered this year and does not resolve the staffing and role questions described above; those will be addressed on their own timeline outside this review.

## Impact of Format Conversion and Missed District Bonus

The district bonus did not pay because chain-wide shrink finished at 2.4 percent against a 1.6 percent target; Rio Rancho's own shrink performance was well inside target and was not a contributor to the miss, and I want that on the record given how the bonus is calculated at the district rather than store level. On the format conversion, Rio Rancho combining with the neighboring location in March means the store as Dev has run it will not exist in its current form after that date. If Dev is selected for the regional role, this is moot for him personally but still requires a transition plan for whoever inherits the merged store. If he is not selected, we need a direct conversation before March about whether he wants to be considered for the manager seat at the combined store, take a lateral move elsewhere in the chain, or leave the company — all real possibilities given what he told me in May.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Resolve the regional operations manager decision and, in parallel, agree on Dev's path if the answer is no, with a firm date rather than an open-ended conversation.
2. If Dev remains in a store manager capacity through the March conversion, document the contractor sales desk model formally enough that it can be replicated without him, since two other districts are asking for it.
3. If a transition is needed, complete a structured handoff of Rio Rancho or the combined store, including the two associates identified as assistant manager bench strength, so the sales and shrink performance built this year does not erode with a change in leadership.
4. Continue mentoring toward promotion readiness for at least one direct report, independent of Dev's own next move.

# Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Pauline Villanueva-Estrada
**Title:** Store Manager, Santa Fe
**Review Period:** February 1, 2025 – January 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager

## Goals and Results

Pauline's goals for the year were to grow comparable sales, bring shrink back under the 1.6 percent target, reduce time to fill on open positions to the 21-day standard, and reduce turnover among assistant managers, an area flagged in last year's review. Results were mixed and, on the metrics the company weights most heavily, below expectation. Comparable sales at Santa Fe were flat for the year against a chain decline of 1.8 percent, which on its own would read as a reasonable outcome, but shrink rose to 2.9 percent, the highest in the district and nearly double the company target. Time to fill averaged 46 days against the 21-day standard, more than double, and two assistant managers left the store this year and named Pauline specifically in their exit interviews.

## Key Metrics

| Metric | Santa Fe | Target | Chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable sales | Flat | n/a | -1.8% |
| Shrink | 2.9% | 1.6% | 2.4% |
| Time to fill | 46 days | 21 days | — |
| Customer survey score | 78 | n/a | District-leading |

## Incidents and Feedback

Santa Fe leads the district on customer survey scores at 78, and that result is genuine and worth stating plainly: customers who shop the store report a consistently good experience, and that does not happen by accident. Set against that, this year's exit interviews from the two departing assistant managers describe a common pattern: Pauline retains every hiring approval herself rather than delegating any part of the process, which both departing managers said left them unable to make basic staffing decisions in their own departments and contributed directly to the 46-day time to fill. One exit interview stated that hourly positions sat open for weeks awaiting a sign-off that competed with Pauline's other responsibilities. The shrink increase to 2.9 percent has not been traced to a single incident; it appears distributed across the store and is consistent with a location that has run short-staffed in supervisory coverage for stretches of the year, which is itself connected to the hiring bottleneck.

## Self-Assessment (Employee)

> "Our customer scores are the best in the district and have been for three years running, and that doesn't happen without a manager who is close to every hire that comes through the door. I approve every hire myself because I have seen what happens at other stores when that gets delegated too fast — the wrong people get put in front of customers. I know time to fill is high. I would rather it be high than get it wrong."

## Manager's Assessment

I don't dispute that Santa Fe's customer experience is real and that Pauline is a significant reason for it after nine years in the store. But the connection she draws between personally approving every hire and protecting that customer score does not hold up against this year's numbers. Time to fill more than doubling the standard is not a quality control measure; it is a staffing gap that leaves the store short of supervisory coverage for weeks at a stretch, and the shrink result this year is consistent with exactly that kind of gap. Two assistant managers left this year citing the same bottleneck independently of each other, which tells me this is a pattern and not two isolated personality conflicts. Nine years of tenure and the strongest customer scores in the district are real credit, and I don't want this review read as dismissing them. But the shrink result, the hiring delay, and the departures are the same problem showing up three different ways, and it needs to be fixed directly rather than balanced against the customer number.

## Rating

**Overall: Partially Meets Expectations**

## Impact of Format Conversion and Missed District Bonus

Santa Fe's shrink result was a material contributor to the district finishing at 2.4 percent against a 1.6 percent target, and I want to say that plainly rather than let it sit only in the metrics table: this store's performance is a specific reason the district bonus did not pay for anyone this year, including managers whose own stores were on target. On the format conversion, Santa Fe is one of the two stores converting to the smaller footprint in March, and that store will be combined with Rio Rancho under a single manager. I have not yet made a recommendation on who leads that combined location, and this review is a factor in that decision but not the only one. I want to be direct with Pauline that the hiring and shrink issues identified here need to show real, documented improvement well before March if she wants to be considered for that seat, and that the customer service strength alone will not be sufficient grounds for the recommendation.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Delegate initial hiring approval for hourly and lead positions to assistant managers, with Pauline retaining final sign-off only, and bring time to fill under 30 days within the first quarter.
2. Reduce shrink to at or below 2.0 percent by mid-year through a documented cycle count and supervisory coverage plan, with monthly check-ins with Robles.
3. Retain both remaining assistant managers through the format conversion decision; conduct a documented one-on-one with each within 30 days addressing the concerns raised in the exit interviews.
4. Maintain the customer experience standard that has made Santa Fe the district leader while making the above changes — this is an addition to the job, not a trade against the strength already established.

# Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Keisha Amankwaa
**Title:** Store Manager, Farmington
**Review Period:** October 1, 2025 (date of promotion) – January 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager

## Goals and Results

Keisha was promoted from assistant manager to store manager at Farmington in October after the prior manager resigned mid-season, leaving the store with 40 percent of positions unfilled going into the holiday period. Her goal on taking the role, set jointly with Robles, was to return the store to full staffing without sacrificing the season's sales volume, with safety and shrink control to follow once staffing stabilized. She reached full staffing by February, faster than either of us expected given the depth of the hole she inherited, and did it without losing the holiday selling season. Set against that real accomplishment, the store failed two of the three quarterly safety walks conducted since her promotion, and the January inventory count came in 84,000 dollars short.

Because Keisha has been in the role only four months of the twelve covered by this cycle, this review weighs her results in that role rather than holding her to a full year's targets, but the safety and shrink results below need to be addressed with the same urgency as if they were.

## Key Metrics

| Metric | Farmington (since Oct.) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Staffing at promotion | 60% filled | 100% |
| Staffing by February | 100% filled | 100% |
| Safety walks passed | 1 of 3 | 3 of 3 |
| January inventory shortage | $84,000 | n/a |

## Incidents and Feedback

The two failed safety walks cited expired fire extinguisher inspection tags in the receiving area and unsecured pallet racking in seasonal overflow storage, both corrected within the timeframe required but both findings that should not have recurred on a second walk after being flagged once. The 84,000 dollar shortage identified in the January count has not yet been traced to a specific cause; a root-cause review comparing receiving records against the count is underway and due by the end of February. Employee feedback collected informally during the staffing rebuild has been positive about Keisha's visibility on the floor and her willingness to work open-to-close shifts herself during the worst of the staffing gap; several new hires specifically credited her for training them personally rather than leaving it to whoever was on shift.

## Self-Assessment (Employee)

> "I walked into a store at 60 percent staffing three weeks before the holidays with no notice and no handoff from the manager who left. Getting to full staffing by February, while still hitting our holiday numbers, is the thing I'm proudest of this year, and I don't think it would have happened if I hadn't been on the floor training people myself instead of sitting in the office. I know the safety walks and the inventory count are real problems and I'm not going to explain them away — I was focused on people because the store had no people, and I let some of the operational discipline slide while I did that. That's on me to fix now that staffing is stable."

## Manager's Assessment

Keisha's own account here is accurate, and I want to credit it directly: the staffing recovery she describes is a genuinely strong result under genuinely difficult circumstances, and the store would have been in far worse shape through the holidays without the personal hours she put in. That said, four months in, the safety and inventory results cannot be attributed only to the staffing crisis. A repeat finding on a safety walk after the first one flagged it is a follow-through problem, not a staffing problem, and an 84,000 dollar shortage is large enough that it needs a real answer, not just a plausible explanation tied to a chaotic fall. I am treating this review period as the beginning of Keisha's tenure rather than a full evaluation of a manager with a year in the chair, but the goals below are not soft ones, and I expect the same urgency on safety and shrink that she showed on staffing.

## Rating

**Overall: Partially Meets Expectations, in the context of a four-month tenure inheriting a store in crisis**

This rating reflects the safety and inventory results specifically. It is not a judgment on Keisha's capability, which the staffing recovery demonstrates clearly; it is a statement that the next two quarters need to close the gap between that capability and the operational discipline the store still needs.

## Impact of Format Conversion and Missed District Bonus

Farmington is not one of the two stores converting to the smaller format in March, so that change does not directly affect Keisha's store or position. The missed district bonus is worth naming directly with her: the 84,000 dollar shortage at Farmington was a contributor to the district's shrink result landing at 2.4 percent against the 1.6 percent target, alongside Santa Fe's result, and closing that gap next year is not only a store-level goal but connects to whether the district bonus pays for anyone in it.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Pass all four quarterly safety walks in the coming cycle with no repeat findings; any finding from a failed walk must be corrected and independently re-verified within two weeks, not carried to the next quarterly walk.
2. Complete the root-cause review of the January shortage by the stated deadline and implement whatever receiving or count control it identifies; target shrink at or below 1.8 percent for the coming fiscal year.
3. Formalize the training approach that produced strong new-hire feedback into a written onboarding checklist so it does not depend on Keisha personally working every shift.
4. Maintain full staffing through the coming holiday season with a documented contingency plan, so the store does not return to the position it was in last October if turnover occurs.

## Appendix: District Rating Scale Reference

For consistency across the three reviews above, ratings were assigned using the standard Zia Mercantile store manager scale:

**Exceeds Expectations** — Results meaningfully surpass the goals set for the role across the majority of measured categories, with no material deficiency in any single category serious enough to offset the overall result.

**Meets Expectations** — Results are consistent with the goals set for the role across measured categories, with any shortfalls minor and not indicative of a pattern.

**Partially Meets Expectations** — Results are mixed, with genuine strength in one or more categories offset by a shortfall in another category serious enough, or persistent enough, that it cannot be treated as incidental. A written improvement plan with defined metrics and a review date is required at this rating.

**Does Not Meet Expectations** — Results fall short of the goals set for the role across most measured categories, or a single category has failed seriously enough to place the store or its people at risk. A formal corrective action plan is required at this rating, with a defined timeline for reassessment.

None of the three reviews in this packet resulted in a Does Not Meet Expectations rating. Two resulted in Partially Meets Expectations, both carrying required improvement plans as documented in the individual reviews above, with defined metrics and reassessment dates rather than open-ended monitoring.
