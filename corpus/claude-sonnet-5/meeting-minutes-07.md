# CEDAR VALLEY FAMILY SERVICES / HAWKEYE YOUTH BRIDGE
## JOINT MERGER EXPLORATION COMMITTEE
### MINUTES

---

## MEETING ONE

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge

**Date:** January 14, 2026

**Time:** 4:00 p.m. to 6:20 p.m.

**Place:** Cedar Valley Family Services Administrative Offices, Board Conference Room, 815 Sycamore Street, Waterloo, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair, Cedar Valley Board Chair), Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair, Hawkeye Board Chair), Sun-Hee Park (Cedar Valley Treasurer), Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz (Cedar Valley Chief Executive), Roland Buchbinder (Hawkeye Founder and Executive Director), Tariq Al-Rashid (Hawkeye Board Member)

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priya Nakashima-Reyes, Esq. (outside counsel, Vantage Nonprofit Law Group, retained jointly by both boards); Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price (nonprofit merger consultant, Prairie Sector Partners); Wendell Ashworth (recording secretary, Cedar Valley development officer)

---

### Record of Business

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call.** Co-Chair Marchetti called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. Wendell Ashworth called the roll; all six members were present. Marchetti noted this was the inaugural meeting of the committee, seated by joint resolution of both boards in December 2025.

**2. Opening Remarks.** Marchetti and Kimani each offered brief remarks on the purpose of the committee, noting the shared service territory in Black Hawk County, the pending Black Hawk Community Foundation challenge grant of $500,000 contingent on a signed agreement by June 30, 2026, and the shared $1.4 million state contract scheduled for reprocurement in July 2026. Kimani stated that Hawkeye Youth Bridge entered the process "with open eyes and an open door, but not an open checkbook."

**3. Introduction of Counsel and Consultant.** Marchetti introduced Priya Nakashima-Reyes as jointly retained outside counsel and Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price as the engaged due diligence and integration consultant. Nakashima-Reyes summarized her engagement letter, confirming that she represented the committee jointly for purposes of process and structure but that each organization retained the right to engage separate counsel for final agreement review. Okonkwo-Price outlined his proposed due diligence workplan, covering financial, programmatic, legal, human resources, and reputational review, with a target completion date of April 2026.

**4. Adoption of Charter.** Nakashima-Reyes presented a draft charter for the committee, including scope of authority, meeting cadence (second Wednesday of each month), quorum requirements (four of six members), and reporting obligations to the two parent boards. Sun-Hee Park moved to adopt the charter as presented. Tariq Al-Rashid seconded. Feldstein-Ruiz asked that the charter be amended to clarify that the committee's recommendations were advisory only and required ratification by both boards. Nakashima-Reyes agreed to insert such language. The amended motion carried six to zero.

**5. Adoption of Due Diligence Scope.** Okonkwo-Price presented a detailed due diligence scope of work, including document requests for the prior three fiscal years' audited financial statements, IRS Forms 990, board minutes, employment agreements, real property leases, and litigation history from both organizations. Buchbinder asked whether the scope could be narrowed to avoid what he called "an audit of an audit." Feldstein-Ruiz responded that Cedar Valley's own audit committee had already reviewed the requested materials in the ordinary course and that production would not be burdensome. Park moved to adopt the due diligence scope as presented. Al-Rashid seconded. The motion carried five to one, with Buchbinder opposed.

**6. Discussion of Terminology.** Buchbinder raised concern that the committee's working documents, including the charter just adopted, repeatedly used the word "merger," which he stated was causing anxiety among Hawkeye staff and donors. He moved to substitute the word "affiliation" for "merger" throughout the charter and all future committee materials. Al-Rashid seconded the motion. Marchetti stated for the record that she believed precise language served the process better than euphemism, and that funders including the Black Hawk Community Foundation had used the term "merger" in their own written communications. Feldstein-Ruiz agreed, stating that the state contract reprocurement documents likewise referenced "merger or consolidation of service providers" as a qualifying condition. Park stated she would support whatever term the committee chose but urged consistency. Kimani stated that as Hawkeye's chair he felt the word "merger" was accurate to what was being explored and should not be avoided. Following discussion, the motion to substitute "affiliation" for "merger" failed four to two, with Buchbinder and Al-Rashid in favor and Marchetti, Kimani, Park, and Feldstein-Ruiz opposed.

**7. Funder Communication — Black Hawk Community Foundation.** Feldstein-Ruiz reported that she and Kimani had jointly received a letter dated December 18, 2025, from Community Foundation President Ingrid Vasquez-Thorne, confirming the $500,000 challenge grant offer contingent on execution of a definitive merger agreement no later than June 30, 2026. Feldstein-Ruiz distributed copies of the letter to the committee. Marchetti directed that all future funder correspondence be logged and copied to the full committee, and asked Feldstein-Ruiz and Kimani to prepare a joint acknowledgment letter to Vasquez-Thorne confirming receipt and the committee's intent to pursue the timeline. No objection was raised.

**8. State Contract Reprocurement.** Park briefed the committee on the shared $1.4 million state contract with the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services, Bureau of Family Support, noting that both organizations currently held separate but overlapping contracts and that the state's reprocurement notice, expected in July 2026, was likely to favor a single consolidated bidder in the service region. Okonkwo-Price stated this would be incorporated into his due diligence workplan as a discrete workstream.

**9. Scheduling.** The committee confirmed its next meeting for February 11, 2026, at Hawkeye Youth Bridge's offices in Cedar Falls, and agreed that Okonkwo-Price would present preliminary due diligence findings at that meeting.

**10. Adjournment.** There being no further business, Marchetti adjourned the meeting at 6:20 p.m. on motion of Al-Rashid, seconded by Park, carried unanimously.

Respectfully submitted, Wendell Ashworth, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING TWO

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge

**Date:** February 11, 2026

**Time:** 4:00 p.m. to 6:45 p.m.

**Place:** Hawkeye Youth Bridge Administrative Offices, Conference Room A, 220 Main Street, Cedar Falls, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair), Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair), Sun-Hee Park, Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz, Roland Buchbinder, Tariq Al-Rashid

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priya Nakashima-Reyes, Esq. (outside counsel); Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price (consultant, Prairie Sector Partners); Corinne Baptiste-Lindqvist, CPA (independent auditor engaged to review pension and tax matters); Wendell Ashworth (recording secretary)

---

### Record of Business

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call.** Kimani called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. All six members were present. The minutes of the January 14, 2026, meeting were approved on motion of Park, seconded by Feldstein-Ruiz, carried unanimously without amendment.

**2. Introduction of Auditor.** Kimani introduced Corinne Baptiste-Lindqvist, CPA, engaged by Okonkwo-Price's firm to conduct a focused review of retirement plan and tax filing matters for both organizations following irregularities identified in the first weeks of document production.

**3. Due Diligence Report — Financial.** Okonkwo-Price presented his preliminary due diligence report. He reported three material findings requiring committee attention:

  (a) An unrecorded pension withdrawal liability of approximately $310,000 attributable to Hawkeye Youth Bridge's 2019 withdrawal from a multi-employer retirement plan, which had not been reflected as a liability on Hawkeye's financial statements or disclosed in its most recent audit. Baptiste-Lindqvist confirmed the estimate based on plan actuary correspondence provided by Buchbinder's office on February 4, 2026, and stated that the final assessed amount could range between $280,000 and $340,000 depending on interest calculations.

  (b) A lapsed lease on Hawkeye's satellite office at 1140 Prospect Boulevard, Waterloo, which Okonkwo-Price stated had continued on a month-to-month holdover basis without a signed renewal since August 2024, exposing Hawkeye to termination on short notice and complicating any post-merger real estate consolidation planning.

  (c) Unfiled unrelated business income tax returns (Form 990-T) for Hawkeye Youth Bridge covering tax years 2022 through 2024, arising from rental income Hawkeye received from a commercial tenant subletting a portion of its Cedar Falls building. Baptiste-Lindqvist stated that penalties and interest, if assessed, could reach an estimated $18,000 to $26,000, and recommended voluntary disclosure to the IRS at the earliest opportunity.

Buchbinder stated that he had not been aware of the pension withdrawal liability until it was raised in due diligence and disputed the $310,000 figure as "worst case, not most likely." Baptiste-Lindqvist responded that she had used the actuary's own mid-point estimate. Feldstein-Ruiz asked whether these liabilities would have been disclosed in Hawkeye's audited financials had a full audit, rather than a review, been performed. Baptiste-Lindqvist stated they should have been, at minimum, disclosed in footnotes.

**4. Legal Counsel Presentation.** Nakashima-Reyes advised the committee that the pension liability, lease lapse, and unfiled returns did not, in her opinion, constitute legal bars to continuing exploration, but that all three needed to be remediated or specifically addressed in any definitive agreement's representations and warranties, and disclosed to both boards in writing. She recommended the committee direct Hawkeye to retain tax counsel for the 990-T voluntary disclosure and to pursue a lease renewal or exit strategy for the Prospect Boulevard property within 60 days.

**5. Motion to Pause the Process.** Al-Rashid moved that the committee suspend all further merger exploration activity for a period of not less than 90 days to allow Hawkeye to remediate the identified financial and compliance issues before proceeding further. Buchbinder seconded. Discussion followed. Marchetti stated that a pause of that length would jeopardize the Black Hawk Community Foundation's June 30 deadline and the state contract reprocurement timeline. Park stated that the remediation items, while serious, were "the kind of things due diligence is supposed to find" and did not require halting the calendar. Kimani stated he was torn but ultimately could not support an open-ended pause without a defined remediation plan already in hand. Feldstein-Ruiz opposed the motion, stating the parties could remediate concurrently with continued exploration. The vote was recorded as follows: In favor — Buchbinder, Al-Rashid, Kimani. Opposed — Marchetti, Park, Feldstein-Ruiz. The motion failed on a tie vote of three to three and did not carry.

**6. Remediation Directive.** In lieu of a pause, Marchetti moved that Hawkeye Youth Bridge, through Buchbinder, retain qualified tax counsel within 15 days to prepare and file the delinquent Form 990-T returns and pursue voluntary disclosure, and separately pursue resolution of the Prospect Boulevard lease within 60 days, with written status reports to the committee at each subsequent meeting. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. The motion carried five to one, with Kimani opposed on the basis that he preferred the longer pause but would not obstruct a remediation timeline once the pause failed.

**7. Legal Structuring Options.** Nakashima-Reyes presented three preliminary legal structures for consideration: (i) a full statutory merger with Cedar Valley as surviving entity; (ii) a member substitution model in which Hawkeye would become a controlled affiliate with Cedar Valley as sole corporate member; and (iii) a joint operating agreement short of merger. She stated her office would prepare a comparative memorandum for the March meeting.

**8. Funder and Contract Update.** Feldstein-Ruiz reported that the joint acknowledgment letter to the Black Hawk Community Foundation had been sent January 20, 2026, and that Ingrid Vasquez-Thorne had responded confirming the grant terms remained open through June 30, 2026. Park reported no new information on the state contract reprocurement timeline.

**9. Scheduling.** The next meeting was confirmed for March 11, 2026, location to be determined, with a request that Buchbinder provide a written remediation status update in advance.

**10. Adjournment.** Marchetti adjourned the meeting at 6:45 p.m. on motion of Park, seconded by Al-Rashid, carried unanimously.

Respectfully submitted, Wendell Ashworth, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING THREE

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge

**Date:** March 11, 2026

**Time:** 4:00 p.m. to 7:10 p.m.

**Place:** Cedar Valley Family Services Administrative Offices, Board Conference Room, 815 Sycamore Street, Waterloo, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair), Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair), Sun-Hee Park, Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz, Roland Buchbinder, Tariq Al-Rashid

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priya Nakashima-Reyes, Esq.; Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price; Corinne Baptiste-Lindqvist, CPA (by telephone); Marisol Odumosu (Hawkeye Youth Bridge program director, presenting on staff town hall); Wendell Ashworth (recording secretary)

---

### Record of Business

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call.** Marchetti called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. All six members present. Minutes of the February 11, 2026, meeting were approved on motion of Feldstein-Ruiz, seconded by Kimani, carried unanimously.

**2. Remediation Status Report.** Buchbinder reported that Hawkeye had retained tax counsel Priyanka Deshmukh-Foster on February 20, 2026, who had prepared the delinquent Form 990-T returns for filing under the IRS voluntary disclosure practice; Buchbinder stated the returns would be filed by March 20, 2026. He further reported that the Prospect Boulevard landlord had offered a one-year lease renewal at a 14 percent rent increase, which Hawkeye's board had not yet acted upon. Nakashima-Reyes advised that the pension withdrawal liability remained unresolved pending a formal demand letter from the plan administrator, expected in April. Park asked that the pension matter be tracked as a standing agenda item until resolved. No objection was raised.

**3. Staff Town Hall Report.** Marisol Odumosu presented a report on a staff town hall held at Hawkeye on March 3, 2026, attended by 34 of Hawkeye's 41 employees. Odumosu stated that staff expressed concern about job security, program continuity, and the pace of the process. She reported that a letter signed by 22 employees had been delivered to the committee that morning, addressed jointly to Marchetti and Kimani, requesting (i) written assurance against involuntary layoffs for 12 months following any merger, (ii) a staff liaison seat in future due diligence discussions, and (iii) clarity on which programs would be retained. Marchetti read the letter into the record in full. Feldstein-Ruiz stated Cedar Valley had not held a comparable town hall and committed to scheduling one before the April meeting. Kimani moved that the committee formally acknowledge receipt of the staff letter and direct the co-chairs to prepare a written response within 15 days addressing each of the three requests. Al-Rashid seconded. The motion carried six to zero. Buchbinder asked that the response explicitly state that no final decision had been made, which the co-chairs agreed to include.

**4. Financial Update — Hawkeye Reserves.** Park, reporting in her capacity as Cedar Valley treasurer with review access to Hawkeye's monthly financials under the due diligence scope, reported that Hawkeye's unrestricted operating reserves had fallen to approximately 1.2 months of operating expenses as of February 28, 2026, down from 2.1 months at the start of the process, attributable to a delayed state reimbursement and increased legal and accounting costs associated with the remediation work. Buchbinder confirmed the figure but stated the delayed reimbursement was expected within 30 days and would restore reserves to approximately 1.8 months. Feldstein-Ruiz stated the reserve level "underscores the urgency, not the wisdom, of moving carefully." Kimani requested that reserve levels be reported at every subsequent meeting.

**5. Legal Structuring Memorandum.** Nakashima-Reyes presented her comparative memorandum on merger structures previously outlined in February. She recommended the member substitution model as most administratively efficient given Hawkeye's corporate form, while preserving Hawkeye's separate 501(c)(3) status, board seats, and program identity for a transition period. She noted this structure was also most consistent with the state contract's apparent preference for a single controlling entity while allowing continuity of Hawkeye's existing grant agreements during transition.

**6. Discussion of Surviving Name.** The committee took up the question of the surviving organization's name for use in the definitive agreement drafting and funder communications. Buchbinder stated that "Hawkeye Youth Bridge" carried 19 years of community recognition in Cedar Falls and should be preserved at minimum as a program brand. Marchetti stated Cedar Valley's board had expressed strong preference for retaining the "Cedar Valley Family Services" corporate name given its size and the scale of the combined budget. Al-Rashid suggested a combined name such as "Cedar Valley Family and Youth Services." Kimani stated he was open to a combined name but would not accept the Hawkeye name disappearing entirely from public use. No motion was made; Marchetti stated the naming question would be referred to a joint communications subcommittee for recommendation, to be appointed by the co-chairs before the April meeting. Feldstein-Ruiz and Odumosu were asked to gather comparable naming examples from other recent Iowa nonprofit mergers for reference.

**7. Funder Update.** Feldstein-Ruiz reported a check-in call with Ingrid Vasquez-Thorne on March 5, 2026, in which Vasquez-Thorne asked for a written progress update by April 15, 2026, given the June 30 deadline. The committee directed Feldstein-Ruiz and Kimani to prepare that update jointly.

**8. State Contract Update.** Park reported that the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services had issued an informal notice indicating the reprocurement solicitation would be released in early July 2026 as previously expected, with proposals due 45 days thereafter.

**9. Scheduling.** The next meeting was set for April 8, 2026, at Hawkeye's offices in Cedar Falls.

**10. Adjournment.** Marchetti adjourned the meeting at 7:10 p.m. on motion of Al-Rashid, seconded by Park, carried unanimously.

Respectfully submitted, Wendell Ashworth, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING FOUR

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge

**Date:** April 8, 2026

**Time:** 4:00 p.m. to 7:35 p.m.

**Place:** Hawkeye Youth Bridge Administrative Offices, Conference Room A, 220 Main Street, Cedar Falls, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair), Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair), Sun-Hee Park, Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz, Roland Buchbinder

**Members Absent:** Tariq Al-Rashid (resigned from committee effective April 8, 2026; see Item 9)

**Others Attending:** Priya Nakashima-Reyes, Esq.; Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price; Corinne Baptiste-Lindqvist, CPA; Wendell Ashworth (recording secretary)

---

### Record of Business

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call.** Kimani called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. Five of six members present; Al-Rashid's absence was noted, with Kimani stating he had received Al-Rashid's resignation letter that morning. Minutes of the March 11, 2026, meeting were approved on motion of Park, seconded by Buchbinder, carried unanimously.

**2. Remediation Status — Final.** Buchbinder reported that Hawkeye's delinquent Form 990-T returns for tax years 2022 through 2024 had been filed on March 19, 2026, under IRS voluntary disclosure procedures, and that Deshmukh-Foster estimated total penalties and interest at approximately $21,400, consistent with Baptiste-Lindqvist's February estimate. Buchbinder further reported that Hawkeye's board had approved the one-year lease renewal at Prospect Boulevard on March 25, 2026, resolving the lapsed-lease finding. Nakashima-Reyes reported that the pension plan administrator had issued a formal withdrawal liability assessment of $317,650, payable over a 20-quarter installment schedule beginning September 2026, which she recommended be expressly addressed as an assumed liability in any definitive agreement. Park moved that the committee accept the remediation status as substantially resolved for purposes of continuing the process, with the pension liability to be carried as a disclosed and assumed obligation. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. The motion carried five to zero.

**3. Financial Update — Reserves.** Park reported Hawkeye's unrestricted reserves had recovered to 1.7 months of operating expenses following receipt of the delayed state reimbursement referenced in March, still below the 2.1-month level recorded at the start of the process.

**4. Staff Communications Follow-Up.** Marchetti reported that the co-chairs' written response to the March 11 staff letter had been delivered to Hawkeye staff on March 24, 2026, committing to (i) a 12-month no-layoff assurance for direct service staff contingent on a signed agreement, (ii) a non-voting staff liaison role in future due diligence sessions beginning with this meeting, and (iii) a commitment that program continuity decisions would be made through a joint program committee rather than unilaterally. Feldstein-Ruiz reported that Cedar Valley had held its own staff town hall on March 28, 2026, attended by 61 of 118 employees, generating similar questions but no written petition.

**5. Consultant Presentation — Integration Structure.** Okonkwo-Price presented his final integration recommendation, proposing a member substitution structure in which Cedar Valley Family Services would become the sole corporate member of Hawkeye Youth Bridge, with Hawkeye retaining its separate 501(c)(3) status, its own board, and its executive leadership for a minimum three-year transition period, subject to reserved powers held by Cedar Valley's board over budget approval, executive hiring, and major asset transactions. He recommended a combined governing board structure during transition with a seven-to-four split of seats favoring Cedar Valley, reflecting the relative size of the two organizations' budgets and staff, with the option to move to a fully unified board structure at the end of the transition period.

**6. Motion — Recommended Structure.** Marchetti moved that the committee recommend to both parent boards the member substitution structure as presented by Okonkwo-Price, including the seven-to-four board composition during a three-year transition period, for further board-level review and negotiation of definitive agreement terms. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. Buchbinder stated he could support the structure only if Hawkeye's program identity and the Hawkeye name were preserved in some public-facing form, consistent with the unresolved naming question from March. Kimani stated he would support the motion on the understanding that the naming subcommittee recommendation would be brought back before any definitive agreement was signed. The motion carried four to one, with Buchbinder opposed and Kimani in favor with the stated understanding recorded.

**7. Transition Budget.** Nakashima-Reyes and Okonkwo-Price jointly presented a proposed transition budget of $265,000 to cover legal fees, integration consulting, systems harmonization, and a contingency reserve for the pension liability's first-year installment payments. Park moved to recommend the $265,000 transition budget to both parent boards for approval, with funding to be shared proportionally based on each organization's operating budget, resulting in an approximate 75/25 split between Cedar Valley and Hawkeye. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. The motion carried five to zero.

**8. Referral to Parent Boards.** The committee agreed that its recommendations — the member substitution structure with a seven-to-four board split, the $265,000 transition budget on a 75/25 cost share, and the outstanding naming question to be resolved before final agreement execution — would be transmitted in a joint written report to both parent boards no later than April 22, 2026, for review at each board's respective May meeting, with the goal of enabling a signed definitive agreement in advance of the June 30, 2026, Black Hawk Community Foundation deadline. Marchetti and Kimani were directed to co-sign the report.

**9. Committee Membership — Resignation.** Kimani reported that Tariq Al-Rashid had submitted a letter of resignation from the committee that morning, citing disagreement with the pace and direction of the process and stating he would continue to raise concerns through Hawkeye's full board rather than the committee. Kimani stated Hawkeye's board would consider a replacement appointment before the May meeting. Marchetti thanked Al-Rashid for his service on the record.

**10. Funder Update.** Feldstein-Ruiz reported the joint progress update had been delivered to Ingrid Vasquez-Thorne on April 6, 2026, and that Vasquez-Thorne had acknowledged receipt and reiterated the June 30 deadline with no indication of flexibility.

**11. State Contract Update.** Park reported no change to the anticipated July reprocurement timeline.

**12. Scheduling.** The committee agreed to reconvene May 13, 2026, location to be determined, pending each parent board's action on the April 22 referral report.

**13. Adjournment.** Kimani adjourned the meeting at 7:35 p.m. on motion of Park, seconded by Feldstein-Ruiz, carried unanimously.

Respectfully submitted, Wendell Ashworth, Recording Secretary.

## MEETING FIVE

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge

**Date:** May 13, 2026

**Time:** 4:00 p.m. to 7:50 p.m.

**Place:** Cedar Valley Family Services Administrative Offices, Board Conference Room, 815 Sycamore Street, Waterloo, Iowa

**Members Present:** Delphine Marchetti (Co-Chair), Obadiah Kimani (Co-Chair), Sun-Hee Park, Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz, Roland Buchbinder, Genevieve Okafor-Lindstrom (Hawkeye Youth Bridge board member, appointed to fill vacancy)

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Priya Nakashima-Reyes, Esq.; Thaddeus Okonkwo-Price; Corinne Baptiste-Lindqvist, CPA; Marisol Odumosu (staff liaison, non-voting); Wendell Ashworth (recording secretary)

---

### Record of Business

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call.** Marchetti called the meeting to order at 4:00 p.m. Kimani introduced Genevieve Okafor-Lindstrom, appointed by Hawkeye's board on April 30, 2026, to fill the vacancy left by Al-Rashid's resignation. Marchetti welcomed Okafor-Lindstrom and noted for the record that Marisol Odumosu now joined the committee as non-voting staff liaison pursuant to the March 24 staff commitment letter. Minutes of the April 8, 2026, meeting were approved on motion of Park, seconded by Feldstein-Ruiz, carried unanimously, with Okafor-Lindstrom abstaining as she had not been present.

**2. Report on Parent Board Actions.** Marchetti reported that Cedar Valley's full board met on May 4, 2026, and voted eleven to two to approve, in principle, the member substitution structure, the seven-to-four board composition, and the $265,000 transition budget on the proposed 75/25 cost share, subject to final review of definitive agreement language by Cedar Valley's separately retained counsel, Holbrook Ferris Danvers LLP. Kimani reported that Hawkeye's full board met on May 6, 2026, and voted six to three to approve the same structure in principle, subject to resolution of the naming question and confirmation of the twelve-month staff no-layoff assurance in binding contractual form. Kimani noted that the three dissenting Hawkeye board votes included Al-Rashid, who had addressed the board directly and urged rejection of the structure as "governance in name only." Buchbinder stated he had abstained from Hawkeye's board vote given his role as executive director and committee member.

**3. Naming Subcommittee Recommendation.** Feldstein-Ruiz, who had chaired the naming subcommittee with Odumosu and Okafor-Lindstrom's predecessor, presented the subcommittee's recommendation: that the surviving corporate entity be named "Cedar Valley Family Services," with Hawkeye Youth Bridge preserved as a permanent program brand and division name for youth-specific services delivered from the Cedar Falls location, and with Hawkeye's logo retained on program materials and signage for a minimum of five years. Buchbinder stated this was an improvement over earlier discussion but asked that the five-year minimum be extended to ten years. Okafor-Lindstrom, in her first substantive comment to the committee, stated she believed a fixed term of any length would eventually become a flashpoint and suggested the agreement instead commit to retaining the Hawkeye brand "for so long as the program area itself continues to operate," removing the fixed-term question entirely. Kimani stated he found that formulation preferable. Park moved to recommend the naming structure as modified by Okafor-Lindstrom — surviving corporate name Cedar Valley Family Services, with the Hawkeye Youth Bridge brand retained for the program's operational life — to both parent boards for inclusion in the definitive agreement. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded. The motion carried six to zero.

**4. Legal Counsel Presentation — Definitive Agreement Draft.** Nakashima-Reyes presented a first working draft of the definitive Agreement and Plan of Member Substitution, running 44 pages, and reviewed its major provisions section by section: representations and warranties, including specific disclosure schedules for the pension withdrawal liability and the remediated tax filings; assumption of liabilities; the reserved powers matrix for the transitional Cedar Valley board majority; the staff protection provisions incorporating the twelve-month no-layoff assurance; and a termination clause permitting either party to withdraw prior to the state's contract award determination if the reprocurement outcome materially altered the financial premise of the merger. Nakashima-Reyes stated that Cedar Valley's separate counsel, Holbrook Ferris Danvers LLP, had reviewed the draft and proposed 14 comments, primarily technical, none of which she characterized as deal-threatening. She recommended the committee target a signing-ready draft by June 10, 2026, to allow both boards time for final ratification votes before the June 30 foundation deadline.

**5. Pension Liability — Payment Schedule Confirmation.** Baptiste-Lindqvist confirmed that the plan administrator's formal assessment of $317,650 remained on the 20-quarter installment schedule previously reported, with the first payment of $15,882 due September 30, 2026. She confirmed this had been incorporated into the transition budget's contingency reserve as directed at the April meeting. Park asked that the payment schedule be attached as a disclosure schedule to the definitive agreement rather than left to a side letter. Nakashima-Reyes agreed to incorporate it directly.

**6. Financial Update — Reserves.** Park reported Hawkeye's unrestricted reserves stood at 2.0 months of operating expenses as of April 30, 2026, continuing the recovery trend reported in April.

**7. Staff Liaison Report.** Odumosu reported that staff reaction to the March 24 commitment letter had been "cautiously positive," and that a second, smaller staff meeting held May 1, 2026, produced no further written petitions, though she relayed continuing questions from Hawkeye case management staff about supervisory reporting lines post-transition. Feldstein-Ruiz stated that question would be addressed in the integration workplan to be developed after signing.

**8. Funder Update.** Feldstein-Ruiz reported a call with Ingrid Vasquez-Thorne on May 8, 2026, in which Vasquez-Thorne asked for the signed agreement or, at minimum, board ratification votes, to be reported to the Foundation no later than June 25, 2026, five days ahead of the formal deadline, to allow the Foundation's own grant committee to process the disbursement.

**9. State Contract Update.** Park reported that the Department of Health and Human Services had confirmed the reprocurement solicitation would be posted July 6, 2026, with proposals due August 20, 2026, and stated this timeline remained compatible with the merger schedule.

**10. Scheduling.** The committee set its next meeting for June 10, 2026, to review the final definitive agreement draft in advance of both boards' ratification votes.

**11. Adjournment.** Marchetti adjourned the meeting at 7:50 p.m. on motion of Buchbinder, seconded by Okafor-Lindstrom, carried unanimously.

Respectfully submitted, Wendell Ashworth, Recording Secretary.
