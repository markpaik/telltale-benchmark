# Pine Hollow Area School District
## Minutes of the Regular Meeting of the Board of Education

**Date:** September 15, 2025
**Time:** 6:00 p.m., called to order
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, 410 Hollow Road, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Loretta Feddema; Director Mai Xiong; Director Owen Skrypek

**Members Absent:** Director Denise Marchetti (excused, family medical matter)

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Recording Secretary Priya Anand; demographer Dr. Sylvia Marsh of the West Central Educational Cooperative; facilities consultant Owen Radtke of Kestrel Education Partners; approximately twenty-five members of the public

---

### Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and led the pledge of allegiance. Clerk Feddema called the roll and recorded six members present and one absent, as noted above, establishing a quorum.

### Approval of Agenda

Vice Chair Villalpando moved to approve the agenda as printed. Treasurer Halvorsen seconded. The motion carried 6-0.

### Consent Agenda

Clerk Feddema read the consent agenda, consisting of: the minutes of the August 18, 2025 regular meeting; the bills and claims register for August 2025 in the amount of $1,142,318.44; the personnel report reflecting the resignation of paraprofessional Dana Wielenga effective August 29, the hiring of elementary teacher Kristen Loeffler at Birchdale Elementary, and the granting of a medical leave to custodian Ray Tuttle; the August activity fund report; and the acceptance of a gift of $2,500 from the Pine Hollow Booster Club for weight-room equipment. Director Xiong asked that the personnel item regarding the Birchdale hire be pulled for separate discussion. Director Skrypek moved to approve the remainder of the consent agenda as read. Clerk Feddema seconded. The motion carried 6-0. On the pulled item, Superintendent Baldacci clarified that the Birchdale hire filled an existing vacancy and was not affected by the facilities discussion scheduled later in the meeting. Director Xiong moved to approve the personnel item as presented. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 6-0.

### Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 6:14 p.m. Three individuals addressed the board. Resident Carla Whitfield of Birchdale Township asked the board to be transparent about any building closure discussions before they advanced further. Parent Dwight Nash asked whether the enrollment study to be presented that evening would be made public in full. Teacher Marjorie Ostlund, speaking as a district employee and not on behalf of the union, urged the board to consider the impact of any consolidation on class sizes at the receiving buildings. Chair Achterberg closed public comment at 6:26 p.m., noting that written comments could be submitted to the district office through October 1.

### Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported that the district opened the 2025-2026 school year on September 3 with 1,340 students enrolled across the four elementary buildings, the middle school, and the high school, compared to 1,910 students in the fall of 2014. He stated that the tenth-day count would be finalized by September 22 and reported to the Minnesota Department of Education. He introduced Dr. Sylvia Marsh of the West Central Educational Cooperative, who had been retained in June to prepare an enrollment projection.

Dr. Marsh presented a ten-year enrollment projection showing continued decline to approximately 1,180 students by the 2029-2030 school year, driven by regional birth-rate trends and out-migration of families with school-age children. She stated that Birchdale Elementary, with 187 students, had the highest per-pupil operating cost of the district's four elementary buildings due to its age and low enrollment relative to capacity. Director Xiong asked whether the projection accounted for a proposed housing development near Birchdale. Dr. Marsh responded that she had reviewed preliminary plat filings with the township but that the development was not far enough along to include in the model with confidence. Superintendent Baldacci stated that the full projection report would be posted to the district website by September 19.

### Business Manager's Report

Business Manager Kalinowski reported that the general fund balance stood at $23.6 million as of June 30, 2025, but that current budget trends, including declining enrollment-driven state aid and unresolved labor costs, projected a $2.9 million operating shortfall by June 30, 2027. She stated that a shortfall of that magnitude would place the district in statutory operating debt under Minnesota Statutes section 123B.83, triggering a required deficit reduction plan to the Department of Education. She reviewed monthly cash flow and stated that without corrective action, the district's unassigned fund balance would fall below the ten percent threshold recommended by the district's financial advisor by fiscal year 2027. Treasurer Halvorsen asked for a comparison of per-pupil costs across the four elementary buildings, which Kalinowski said she would prepare for the October meeting.

### Old Business

There was no old business.

### New Business

**Facilities Study, First Reading.** Facilities consultant Owen Radtke of Kestrel Education Partners presented the findings of a building-by-building facilities audit commissioned by the board in May 2025. Radtke reported that Birchdale Elementary, built in 1962, required an estimated $3.1 million in deferred maintenance over the next five years, including roof, boiler, and accessibility upgrades, and operated at 58 percent of building capacity. He recommended that the board consider consolidating Birchdale's students into the district's three remaining elementary buildings, which he stated had sufficient combined capacity to absorb the students without additional construction. Radtke's report was presented as a first reading only; no action was requested or taken. Chair Achterberg stated that a second reading and opportunity for board discussion would occur at a future meeting, and that the administration would first bring forward a process for community input.

**Boundary Committee.** Superintendent Baldacci recommended that the board seat a boundary committee to study attendance-area boundaries in the event of any consolidation, composed of two board members, two parents from each elementary attendance area, one teacher representative, and the elementary principals. Vice Chair Villalpando moved to seat a boundary committee with himself and Director Xiong as the board representatives, with the administration to solicit parent and staff volunteers and report a full roster at the October meeting. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 6-0.

### Closed Session

At 7:38 p.m., Vice Chair Villalpando moved, pursuant to Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03, to close the meeting to discuss the board's strategy in advance of negotiations with the Pine Hollow Education Association, whose contract had expired June 30, 2025. Treasurer Halvorsen seconded. The motion carried 6-0. The board entered closed session at 7:41 p.m. Present in closed session were the six board members present, Superintendent Baldacci, Business Manager Kalinowski, and district negotiator Wendell Osei. The board discussed its initial economic position and authorized Superintendent Baldacci to communicate a preliminary offer to the union's bargaining team. No votes were taken in closed session. The board reconvened in open session at 8:12 p.m.

### Adjournment

There being no further business, Director Skrypek moved to adjourn. Clerk Feddema seconded. The motion carried 6-0. The meeting adjourned at 8:15 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Loretta Feddema, Clerk

# Pine Hollow Area School District
## Minutes of the Regular Meeting of the Board of Education

**Date:** October 20, 2025
**Time:** 6:00 p.m., called to order
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, 410 Hollow Road, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Loretta Feddema; Director Mai Xiong; Director Owen Skrypek; Director Denise Marchetti

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Recording Secretary Priya Anand; district negotiator Wendell Osei; approximately forty members of the public

---

### Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and led the pledge of allegiance. Clerk Feddema called the roll and recorded all seven members present.

### Approval of Agenda

Director Marchetti moved to approve the agenda as printed, with the addition of an item under new business to set a mediation date in the teacher contract matter. Director Xiong seconded. The motion carried 7-0.

### Consent Agenda

Clerk Feddema read the consent agenda: the minutes of the September 15, 2025 regular meeting; the bills and claims register for September 2025 in the amount of $1,098,672.10; the personnel report reflecting the retirement of high school custodian Alden Frye effective November 1 and the hiring of a long-term substitute at the middle school; the September activity fund report; and approval of the high school FFA chapter's overnight trip to the state leadership conference in November. Treasurer Halvorsen moved to approve the consent agenda as read. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 7-0.

### Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 6:11 p.m. Six individuals addressed the board. Parent and referendum committee volunteer Sandra Yun urged voters to support the November 4 operating levy referendum, stating that failure would accelerate the need for building closures. Birchdale parent Todd Ferrante stated his continued opposition to any Birchdale closure and asked the board to commit to a vote no earlier than after a full public hearing process. Teacher and union bargaining team member Patricia Loeb spoke about the difficulty of retaining staff without a competitive settlement, noting neighboring districts had settled at 3.5 to 4 percent. Retiree and former board member Walt Anholt spoke in support of fiscal caution and asked the board not to be swayed by "one loud meeting." Two additional residents, Faith Ojeda and Herman Dietz, spoke generally in favor of the levy. Chair Achterberg closed public comment at 6:34 p.m.

### Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported on the campaign in support of the November 4 operating levy referendum, which would ask voters to approve a $425 per-pupil increase in local optional revenue. He stated that the citizens' committee "Pine Hollow Forward" had held four community information sessions in September and October, distributed mailers to approximately 2,100 households, and secured newspaper endorsements from the Pine Hollow Sentinel. He cautioned that internal polling shared informally with the district suggested the outcome was uncertain, with rural turnout patterns historically unfavorable to levy questions in the district. He reminded the board that state law prohibited the district itself from advocating for or against the referendum and that his report was limited to factual campaign activity conducted by the independent citizens' committee. He also reported that the boundary committee authorized in September had been seated with eight parent volunteers, two teachers, and the four elementary principals, and would hold its first meeting October 28.

### Business Manager's Report

Business Manager Kalinowski presented the requested comparison of per-pupil operating costs across the district's four elementary buildings, showing Birchdale Elementary at $11,240 per pupil compared to a range of $8,150 to $8,900 at the other three buildings, driven primarily by facility age and lower enrollment relative to fixed staffing costs. She reiterated the projected $2.9 million shortfall by June 2027 and stated that passage of the operating levy would reduce, but not eliminate, the projected gap, closing an estimated $1.1 million of the $2.9 million shortfall. She stated that the remaining gap would require either additional revenue, expenditure reductions including staffing and facilities, or both. Director Marchetti asked what the shortfall would look like if the levy failed and no other action were taken. Kalinowski responded that the district would be required to file a deficit reduction plan with the Department of Education and could face statutory operating debt designation as early as the 2026-2027 fiscal year audit.

### Old Business

Vice Chair Villalpando, as boundary committee co-chair, reported that the committee's first substantive meeting was scheduled for October 28 and that no recommendations would come before the board until at least December.

### New Business

**Mediation Date.** Superintendent Baldacci reported that negotiations with the Pine Hollow Education Association had reached impasse, with the union's bargaining team seeking a 4.5 percent salary increase and the district's offer standing at 1.75 percent. He stated that both parties had jointly petitioned the Bureau of Mediation Services and that a mediator had proposed November 12, 2025, for a formal mediation session. Treasurer Halvorsen moved to authorize the superintendent and district negotiator to proceed to mediation on November 12, 2025, or the next available date offered by the Bureau of Mediation Services. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 7-0.

### Closed Session

At 7:52 p.m., Director Marchetti moved, pursuant to Minnesota Statutes section 13D.03, to close the meeting to discuss the district's position in advance of mediation with the Pine Hollow Education Association. Clerk Feddema seconded. The motion carried 7-0. The board entered closed session at 7:55 p.m. Present were all seven board members, Superintendent Baldacci, Business Manager Kalinowski, and negotiator Wendell Osei. The board reviewed the financial parameters for a potential settlement in light of the pending shortfall and discussed contingencies depending on the outcome of the November 4 referendum. No votes were taken. The board reconvened in open session at 8:30 p.m. Chair Achterberg stated for the record that no action was taken in closed session and that the topics discussed were limited to negotiation strategy.

### Adjournment

Director Xiong moved to adjourn. Director Marchetti seconded. The motion carried 7-0. The meeting adjourned at 8:32 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Loretta Feddema, Clerk

# Pine Hollow Area School District
## Minutes of the Regular Meeting of the Board of Education

**Date:** November 17, 2025
**Time:** 6:00 p.m., called to order
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, 410 Hollow Road, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Vice Chair Hector Villalpando; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Loretta Feddema; Director Mai Xiong; Director Owen Skrypek; Director Denise Marchetti

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Recording Secretary Priya Anand; Birchdale Elementary Principal Regina Okonkwo; approximately two hundred ten members of the public

---

### Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and led the pledge of allegiance. Clerk Feddema called the roll and recorded all seven members present. Chair Achterberg noted the unusually large attendance and stated that the meeting had been relocated from the board room to the media center and that overflow seating with audio had been arranged in the adjoining commons.

### Approval of Agenda

Chair Achterberg stated that, given the anticipated volume of public comment, the board would take up public comment before the consent agenda by unanimous consent. Vice Chair Villalpando moved to approve the agenda with public comment moved ahead of the consent agenda. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 7-0.

### Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 6:07 p.m. and stated that, given the number of individuals who had signed the comment sheet, each speaker would be limited to three minutes and the period would run until the sign-up sheet was exhausted or 9:00 p.m., whichever came first. Forty-two individuals ultimately addressed the board over approximately three hours on the subject of the proposed closure of Birchdale Elementary School. The following is a representative record of the comments received; the full sign-in sheet and any written statements submitted were entered into the record as Attachment A to these minutes.

Birchdale parent-teacher organization president Louise Kettering opened the comment period, presenting a petition signed by 640 residents opposing closure and asking the board to delay any vote until spring. Birchdale Elementary Principal Regina Okonkwo, speaking in a personal capacity as a district employee, described the school's role as a community anchor and stated that six staff members had indicated they would not follow their positions to another building if consolidated. Kindergarten teacher Anne Provost spoke about the difficulty of transitioning young children mid-year and urged an effective date no earlier than the end of a full school year. Birchdale Township Supervisor Douglas Ames stated that the township's tax base depended in part on the school's presence and asked the board to consider the economic impact on the community. Parent Yolanda Restrepo, whose child receives special education services at Birchdale, expressed concern about transportation times to other buildings. Bus driver Curt Halvorsen (no relation to Treasurer Halvorsen) stated that some routes from the Birchdale area to the next-nearest elementary building would exceed fifty minutes one way. Retired teacher Phyllis Nordgren recalled the 1962 construction of the building and asked the board to weigh sentiment alongside spreadsheets. Parent and volunteer firefighter Skip Journeau stated that closing the school would remove the only public building in the township available for emergency shelter. Fourth-grade student Maren Okafor read a letter, with parental permission, asking the board "not to make my friends go far away." High school senior and Birchdale alumnus Trey Ballenger spoke about his own experience at the building. Additional speakers, including Priscilla Wamsley, George Ledoux, Ana Beltran, Marcus Overby, and thirty others, offered statements largely echoing the themes of community identity, transportation burden, transition timing, and requests for delay, with several speakers also questioning the accuracy of the enrollment projections presented in September and asking that the board commission an independent second study. A small number of speakers, including former board candidate Arliss Tobin, spoke in support of consolidation on fiscal grounds, stating that the district could not continue to fund four small elementary buildings given the enrollment decline and levy failure. Chair Achterberg closed public comment at 9:04 p.m., thanking the speakers for their civility, and noted that written comments would remain open through November 21.

### Consent Agenda

Clerk Feddema read the consent agenda: the minutes of the October 20, 2025 regular meeting; the bills and claims register for October 2025 in the amount of $1,167,904.55; the personnel report reflecting the resignation of middle school paraprofessional Joel Frentz; and the October activity fund report. Director Marchetti moved to approve the consent agenda as read. Director Xiong seconded. The motion carried 7-0.

### Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported the results of the November 4, 2025 operating levy referendum, which failed with 46.2 percent voting in favor and 53.8 percent voting against, on turnout of approximately 31 percent of registered voters. He stated that the failure of the referendum removed the $1.1 million in projected revenue that Business Manager Kalinowski had described in October, leaving the full $2.9 million shortfall unaddressed by new revenue. He reported that the mediation session held November 12 with the Pine Hollow Education Association did not produce a settlement, and that the mediator had scheduled a follow-up session for December 3. He stated that the union's position remained a 4.5 percent increase and the district's offer remained 1.75 percent as of that date. He also reported that the boundary committee had met twice and had asked the administration for additional transportation data before making any recommendation.

### Business Manager's Report

Business Manager Kalinowski reported that, with the referendum's failure, the projected $2.9 million shortfall by June 2027 remained unchanged from October's projection and that the district's unassigned fund balance would decline to approximately 6.8 percent of expenditures by that date absent corrective action, below the ten percent guideline. She stated that closure of one elementary building, combined with associated staff reductions, had been modeled by the administration as reducing the projected shortfall by approximately $1.4 million annually, and that the administration's recommendation, to be presented for board discussion under new business, reflected that modeling.

### Old Business

**Facilities Study, Recommendation.** Superintendent Baldacci presented the administration's recommendation, following the September first reading of the Kestrel Education Partners facilities study, that the board approve the closure of Birchdale Elementary School effective at the end of the current school year, June 2026, with students reassigned to the district's three remaining elementary buildings according to boundaries to be finalized by the boundary committee, and an associated reduction of 14.5 full-time equivalent positions. Following the extensive public comment received earlier in the meeting, Vice Chair Villalpando moved to table the recommendation to allow the boundary committee to complete its transportation analysis and to allow the administration to respond in writing to questions raised about the enrollment projection. Director Marchetti seconded. Discussion followed. Director Xiong stated she could not support a vote that evening given the volume and substance of public concern. Treasurer Halvorsen stated he was inclined to support closure but agreed additional transportation data was warranted before a final vote. The motion to table carried 7-0. Chair Achterberg stated that the item would return to the board no later than the December meeting and directed the administration to distribute written responses to community questions by December 5.

### New Business

There was no additional new business.

### Adjournment

Director Skrypek moved to adjourn. Treasurer Halvorsen seconded. The motion carried 7-0. The meeting adjourned at 9:41 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Loretta Feddema, Clerk

# Pine Hollow Area School District
## Minutes of the Regular Meeting of the Board of Education

**Date:** December 15, 2025
**Time:** 6:00 p.m., called to order
**Place:** Pine Hollow High School Media Center, 410 Hollow Road, Pine Hollow, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Rosalind Achterberg; Treasurer Grant Halvorsen; Clerk Loretta Feddema; Director Mai Xiong; Director Owen Skrypek; Director Denise Marchetti

**Members Absent:** Vice Chair Hector Villalpando (excused, out-of-state family obligation)

**Others Attending:** Superintendent Terrence Baldacci; Business Manager Nadia Kalinowski; Recording Secretary Priya Anand; District Legal Counsel Marcus Wente of Wente & Ostrander; approximately one hundred sixty members of the public

---

### Call to Order and Pledge of Allegiance

Chair Achterberg called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. and led the pledge of allegiance. Clerk Feddema called the roll and recorded six members present and one member, Vice Chair Villalpando, excused and absent.

### Approval of Agenda

Treasurer Halvorsen moved to approve the agenda as printed. Director Skrypek seconded. The motion carried 6-0.

### Consent Agenda

Clerk Feddema read the consent agenda: the minutes of the November 17, 2025 regular meeting; the bills and claims register for November 2025 in the amount of $1,041,225.80; the personnel report reflecting no new hires or separations; and the November activity fund report. Director Marchetti moved to approve the consent agenda as read. Director Xiong seconded. The motion carried 6-0.

### Public Comment

Chair Achterberg opened public comment at 6:13 p.m. and stated that, in light of the number of speakers at the November meeting, comments would again be limited to three minutes per speaker, with a total period of ninety minutes. Nineteen individuals addressed the board. Louise Kettering again asked the board to delay a vote and stated that the boundary committee's transportation data, released December 5, still showed unacceptable ride times for Birchdale-area students. Principal Regina Okonkwo reiterated her concerns about staff retention. Parent Todd Ferrante asked the board to consider a phased closure over two years rather than immediate action. Teacher's union president Patricia Loeb noted that 14.5 positions represented a significant share of the bargaining unit and asked the board to consider the effect on the ongoing contract mediation. Several speakers, including Faith Ojeda, Herman Dietz, and Arliss Tobin, spoke in support of proceeding with closure, citing the district's finances and the failed referendum. Chair Achterberg closed public comment at 7:38 p.m.

### Superintendent's Report

Superintendent Baldacci reported that the administration had distributed written responses to community questions from the November meeting on December 5, including a revised transportation analysis showing that the longest projected route from the Birchdale attendance area to Prairie View Elementary, the nearest receiving building, would be approximately forty-two minutes, a reduction from the fifty-minute estimate cited in November following route redesign by the district's transportation contractor. He reported that the December 3 mediation session with the Pine Hollow Education Association had again failed to produce a settlement, with the union's position remaining at 4.5 percent and the district's offer remaining at 1.75 percent, and that the mediator had scheduled a further session for January 14, 2026. He stated that the administration's recommendation to close Birchdale Elementary effective June 2026, with an associated reduction of 14.5 full-time equivalent positions, remained before the board for action that evening, and introduced District Legal Counsel Marcus Wente to be available for procedural questions.

### Business Manager's Report

Business Manager Kalinowski restated the district's financial position: a general fund balance of $23.6 million, a projected shortfall of $2.9 million by June 2027, and the risk of statutory operating debt designation absent corrective action. She stated that the administration's modeling showed the proposed closure and staff reduction would reduce the projected shortfall by approximately $1.4 million annually beginning in fiscal year 2027, and that the remaining gap would need to be addressed through the outcome of labor negotiations and further budget review in spring 2026. Treasurer Halvorsen asked whether a phased two-year closure, as suggested by a public commenter, had been modeled. Kalinowski responded that a phased closure would delay realization of savings by approximately one year and reduce the two-year cumulative benefit by an estimated $900,000, but confirmed it remained an option available to the board.

### Old Business

**Facilities Study, Action.** Chair Achterberg reopened the tabled recommendation from the November 17 meeting to close Birchdale Elementary School effective June 2026 and reduce staffing by 14.5 full-time equivalent positions. Treasurer Halvorsen moved to approve the closure of Birchdale Elementary School effective June 30, 2026, adoption of revised elementary attendance boundaries as recommended by the boundary committee on December 9, 2025, and a reduction of 14.5 full-time equivalent positions as identified in the administration's staffing plan dated December 5, 2025. Clerk Feddema seconded the motion. Discussion followed. Director Xiong stated that she believed the district's financial pressures were real but that the process had moved too quickly given community opposition and unresolved transportation concerns, and that she intended to vote against the motion. Director Marchetti stated she shared those concerns and would also vote no. Director Skrypek stated that, as a resident of the Birchdale attendance area himself, he did not believe he could vote for or against the closure without a direct conflict of interest affecting his constituents and stated his intention to abstain. Chair Achterberg and Treasurer Halvorsen spoke in favor of the motion, citing the financial projections and the failed referendum. Clerk Feddema stated she would support the motion reluctantly, citing the statutory operating debt risk.

Clerk Feddema called the roll on the motion:

- Chair Rosalind Achterberg: Aye
- Treasurer Grant Halvorsen: Aye
- Clerk Loretta Feddema: Aye
- Director Mai Xiong: Nay
- Director Denise Marchetti: Nay
- Director Owen Skrypek: Abstain
- Vice Chair Hector Villalpando: Absent

The motion carried 3-2, with one abstention and one member absent. Chair Achterberg declared the motion adopted and directed the administration to notify affected staff and families in writing by December 22, 2025, and to bring an implementation timeline to the January meeting.

### New Business

There was no additional new business.

### Resignation of Director Xiong and Vacancy Process

Following the vote on the Birchdale closure, Director Xiong asked to be recognized and stated that she was resigning her position on the board of education, effective immediately, citing disagreement with the manner and pace of the closure decision and a desire to allow the board to fill the seat with a member who could more fully support its implementation. She submitted a written resignation letter to Clerk Feddema, which was entered into the record as Attachment B to these minutes. Chair Achterberg thanked Director Xiong for her service and accepted the resignation on behalf of the board.

Superintendent Baldacci advised the board that, under Minnesota Statutes section 123B.09, the remaining board members were required to appoint a qualified individual to fill the vacancy for the remainder of the unexpired term, which ran through December 2026, and that the appointment was to be made within thirty days if practicable. District Legal Counsel Wente confirmed that the board could establish an application process at its next meeting and was not required to act that evening. Treasurer Halvorsen moved to direct the administration to publish a notice of vacancy and application procedure by December 19, 2025, with applications due to the district office by January 5, 2026, and appointment of a successor to be considered at the January 20, 2026 regular meeting. Director Marchetti seconded. The motion carried 6-0, with Director Xiong abstaining from the vote as the departing member.

### Adjournment

Director Skrypek moved to adjourn. Treasurer Halvorsen seconded. The motion carried 6-0. The meeting adjourned at 8:47 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Loretta Feddema, Clerk
