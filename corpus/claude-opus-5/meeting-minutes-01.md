Dale Rautio of Halvorson & Meech, Ltd., presented the audited financial statements for the fiscal year ended September 30, 2025. He reported an unmodified ("clean") opinion on the statements. Key figures reported:

- Sales, $18,412,600, an increase of 0.4 percent over fiscal 2024;
- Gross margin, 34.1 percent, down from 36.8 percent in fiscal 2024;
- Personnel expense, 28.4 percent of sales, up from 26.1 percent;

- Net loss, $364,800, after net interest expense of $24,600;

### 6. General Manager's Report

General Manager Haugen presented his written report of January 14, 2026, and summarized the following.

**Margin.** Analysis of the 2.7-point margin decline attributed approximately 1.1 points to unrecovered cost increases in the wellness and grocery departments, 0.8 points to shrink in produce and prepared foods, 0.5 points to a wider discount program adopted in March 2024, and 0.3 points to competitive price matching on a 240-item key value list. A department-by-department repricing was underway, with the grocery reset completed and produce scheduled for February.

**Shrink.** A shrink reduction initiative launched November 3, 2025, produced a reduction in produce shrink from 6.4 percent to 4.9 percent of department sales over ten weeks.

**Labor.** Headcount stood at 112, of whom 71 were scheduled at thirty hours or more. Scheduled hours had been reduced 3.2 percent since October without layoffs, through attrition in the front end. Mr. Dielen was recognized and stated that Local 1189 had been notified of the schedule changes and had no grievance pending.

**First quarter results.** For the quarter ended December 31, 2025: sales $4,487,000, a comparable-store decrease of 2.3 percent; gross margin 34.9 percent; operating loss $63,700 against a budgeted loss of $88,000.

**Remediation.** Mr. Haugen reported that segregation-of-duties changes responsive to the material weakness would be documented by February 13, 2026.

### 4. Presentation: Superior, Wisconsin Trade Area Market Study

Priya Venkataraman of Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors presented the market study dated February 11, 2026, prepared under the engagement authorized January 20 at a cost of $16,400.

Ms. Venkataraman summarized the methodology: primary trade area defined by a twelve-minute drive time containing 21,400 households; competitive audit of eleven food retailers including two supermarkets and one supercenter; intercept surveys of 402 shoppers; analysis of the cooperative's own transaction data identifying 780 active owner households with Wisconsin addresses.

Findings presented:

- Projected first-year sales of $5,900,000, rising to $7,400,000 in year three and $8,050,000 in year four;
- Projected transfer of approximately 9 percent of existing Duluth store sales, or roughly $1,650,000 annually, in the first full year;
- Projected combined operating break-even in **year four**, with the second location itself reaching store-level break-even in month thirty-one;
- Peak cumulative negative cash flow of $1,900,000 occurring in month thirty;
- Required pre-opening capital of $5,600,000, of which not less than $1,200,000 should be owner-sourced;
- Sensitivity analysis indicating that a 10 percent shortfall in first-year sales would push combined break-even beyond year six.

Ms. Venkataraman stated that the site was viable but that her firm's standard readiness criteria — twenty-four months of positive operating results at the existing store, a current ratio above 1.10, and no lender waiver in effect — were **not met** at present. Asked directly by Treasurer Kozlowski whether she recommended proceeding in 2026, she replied that she did not, and that she would recommend a decision point in the first quarter of 2027.

Directors questioned Ms. Venkataraman for thirty-five minutes. Director Fitzgerald asked whether the exclusivity deadline changed her recommendation; she said a site is not a strategy. Director Beaulieu-Cadotte asked whether a smaller footprint would alter the capital requirement materially; Ms. Venkataraman said a 6,000- to 7,000-square-foot market format would reduce project cost by roughly seventy percent and reduce projected sales by roughly half, and that her firm had not modeled it.

### 4. General Manager's Report

General Manager Haugen presented his final written report, dated April 15, 2026.

March period sales were $1,551,400, a comparable increase of 0.4 percent, the first positive comparable month since April 2025; gross margin 35.9 percent, against the B-2 benchmark of 35.5 percent; **operating income of $6,800**, the first positive operating month in eleven. ### 5. Treasurer's Report; Fiscal 2024 Patronage Cash Distribution

Treasurer Kozlowski reported that the covenant waiver extension with North Shore Bank of Commerce had been executed April 8, 2026, running through September 30, 2026, and containing the reported condition restricting distributions to members while the waiver is in effect.

She reported that the fiscal 2024 patronage allocation of $431,000 had been notified to member-owners in January 2025, of which 20 percent, or $86,200, was scheduled for cash distribution in June 2026, the remaining 80 percent retained as allocated equity. She stated that Bylaws Article X, Section 4 authorizes the board to defer or suspend the cash portion of any allocation upon a determination that distribution would impair the cooperative's financial condition, and that the loan documents now independently prohibited it.

**Motion 2026-27** (Kozlowski / Beaulieu-Cadotte): That the board find that distribution of the cash portion of the fiscal 2024 patronage allocation would impair the financial condition of the cooperative and is prohibited under the covenant waiver executed April 8, 2026; that the cash distribution of $86,200 scheduled for June 2026 be **suspended**, the underlying allocation remaining credited to member-owner equity accounts without reduction; that no patronage program be declared for fiscal 2026 in light of projected results; and that the Secretary and the Owner Linkage Committee prepare a written notice to all member-owners explaining the suspension, the reasons for it, and the conditions under which distributions would resume, to be mailed and posted not later than May 8, 2026.

Discussion continued twenty-two minutes. Director Tallbear asked whether the suspension could be characterized as a deferral rather than a cancellation; Treasurer Kozlowski said the allocation remained on the members' books and that "suspended" was the accurate word. Director Warsame asked that the notice state plainly that the co-op had lost money. Director Lindstrom asked that it also state the March result.

**Motion 2026-27 carried 8–0** on a roll call vote requested by President Ramanathan.
*Aye:* Beaulieu-Cadotte, Kozlowski, Lindstrom, Okonkwo, Ramanathan, Suh, Tallbear, Warsame.

### 7. Alternative Growth Strategies Report and Revised Expansion Proposal

Vice President Okonkwo presented the report of the Expansion Feasibility Task Force dated April 14, 2026, prepared pursuant to Motion 2026-13 and posted to the owner portal April 17. The task force evaluated four options: the purchased 14,800-square-foot Superior store as previously proposed; a leased small-format store; expanded online ordering and delivery from the Duluth store; and no expansion.

The task force recommended the second option in a revised form: a **6,500-square-foot leased market-format store** in the Superior trade area, carrying produce, bulk, dairy, grab-and-go prepared foods, and a curated grocery set, with no service deli or bakery. Estimated project cost was $1,450,000, comprising leasehold improvements of $720,000, equipment of $560,000, and pre-opening expense of $170,000, with no real estate purchase. Ms. Venkataraman, joining by telephone, stated that her firm had prepared a preliminary sales estimate of $2,900,000 in year one and $3,600,000 in year three, with store-level break-even projected in month nineteen and a peak cumulative negative cash flow of $610,000. She reiterated her readiness criteria and stated that a decision in early 2027 remained her recommendation but that the smaller format materially reduced the downside case.

Treasurer Kozlowski noted that $1,450,000 could not be financed under the lender's $250,000 debt limitation without prior written consent, and that a member loan campaign would be required in any event.

**Motion 2026-31** (Okonkwo / Lindstrom): To receive the Expansion Feasibility Task Force report of April 14, 2026; to adopt the revised small-format leased store as the cooperative's sole expansion concept under active consideration, superseding the purchase proposal at 1802 Tower Avenue, which is hereby withdrawn from consideration; to refer the concept to the Finance Committee for the preparation of a capital plan, including a member loan campaign feasibility assessment and a lender consent strategy, reporting not later than the August 18, 2026 meeting; to direct the Owner Linkage Committee to conduct not fewer than three owner engagement sessions on the concept before any board vote; and to provide that **no board vote to proceed shall be taken before the seating of a permanent General Manager and the receipt of audited fiscal 2026 results.**

Discussion continued thirty-three minutes. Director Tallbear said she would vote no because the petitioners had asked for a stop and this was a continuation. Director Warsame said she agreed with the concept but not with the timing and would vote no for that reason. Director Beaulieu-Cadotte said the final clause answered her February objection. Secretary Suh said the withdrawal of the $4.2 million purchase was itself the board's substantive answer to the ownership.

**Motion 2026-31 carried 6–2** on a roll call vote.
*Aye:* Beaulieu-Cadotte, Kozlowski, Lindstrom, Okonkwo, Ramanathan, Suh.
*Nay:* Tallbear, Warsame.

**Motion 2026-32** (Tallbear / Warsame): To approve the written response to the member-owner petition of March 17, 2026, drafted by the Owner Linkage and Member Engagement Committee, as amended in discussion to recite the withdrawal of the Tower Avenue purchase, the suspension of the patronage cash distribution, the March operating result, and the commitment that no expansion vote will occur before a permanent General Manager is seated; and to direct that it be mailed to all petition signatories and posted to the owner portal not later than May 8, 2026. **Carried 8–0.**
