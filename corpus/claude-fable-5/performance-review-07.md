# Zia Mercantile — Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Dev Ramchandani
**Title:** Store Manager, Store 07 — Rio Rancho
**Review Period:** July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager, District 3
**Date of Review:** August 9, 2026

**Rating Scale:** 5 — Exceptional | 4 — Exceeds Expectations | 3 — Meets Expectations | 2 — Needs Improvement | 1 — Unsatisfactory

---

## Summary of Role and Context

Dev has managed the Rio Rancho store for four years. Rio Rancho is the second-largest store in District 3 by square footage and carries the district's broadest contractor-facing assortment. This review covers a year in which chainwide comparable sales fell 1.8 percent, the district bonus did not pay because district shrink finished at 2.4 percent against a 1.6 percent target, and the company confirmed that two District 3 stores will convert to the smaller neighborhood format in March 2027, eliminating one store manager position in the district. Those facts frame every review this cycle, and I address what they mean for Dev specifically in the compensation and career sections below.

## Goal 1: Comparable Sales — Target: +1.0 percent. Result: +7.2 percent.

Dev delivered the best comparable sales result in the district and, to my knowledge, the best in the chain this year. Against a chain that declined 1.8 percent, Rio Rancho grew 7.2 percent. That is a nine-point spread over the chain and it was not an accident of market. Rio Rancho's trade area added housing, but the two competing big-box locations in that trade area were flat by our syndicated data, so the growth is share capture, not tide.

The engine was the contractor sales desk pilot. Dev volunteered for the pilot when merchandising offered it to the district in July, staffed it with two associates he trained personally, built a call list of 140 licensed contractors from permit records, and instituted a standing 6:30 a.m. will-call window so crews could load before job sites opened. The desk finished its first year at 1.1 million dollars in revenue, roughly 60 percent of it incremental by the pilot team's attach analysis, at a gross margin about two points below store average but with basket sizes four times the store norm. The pilot is now the template merchandising is using for the rollout to eight additional stores next year, and the rollout playbook is substantially Dev's own operating document with the company logo on it.

I want to be precise about what impressed me beyond the number. Dev ran the pilot without letting the core store slip: DIY-segment comps excluding the contractor desk were still up 2.9 percent, so this was growth on top of a healthy base, not cannibalization or neglect of the retail floor.

**Rating on this goal: 5 — Exceptional.**

## Goal 2: Shrink — Target: 1.6 percent. Result: 1.7 percent.

Rio Rancho finished at 1.7 percent shrink, a tenth over target and the second-best result in the district. Given that the contractor desk introduced high-value, high-velocity SKUs and a pre-dawn will-call process — both classic shrink exposures — holding to 1.7 percent reflects real control discipline. Dev's cycle count compliance ran at 96 percent, receiving audits were clean on all four unannounced checks, and he caught and terminated an internal theft situation in November through his own register-exception review before loss prevention flagged it.

The district as a whole finished at 2.4 percent, which killed the bonus for everyone, including Dev. I will say plainly in this document what I have said to him in person: Dev's store did not cause the miss, and it is a hard thing to run the best store in the district and take home no bonus because of the district aggregate. The structure is what it is this year. I have advocated to regional HR that the bonus design weight store-level shrink more heavily, and Dev's year is Exhibit A in that argument.

**Rating on this goal: 4 — Exceeds Expectations.**

## Goal 3: Staffing and People Development — Target: fill within 21 days; turnover at or below district average.

Time to fill averaged 17 days against the 21-day standard. Hourly turnover was 34 percent against a district average of 51 percent. Dev promoted two associates to department lead and sent one assistant manager, Carla Reyes, through the regional management development program; she is now on the succession slate for a store of her own. His practice of delegating hiring approvals to his assistant managers, with a weekly review rather than a pre-approval gate, is the model I have asked other managers in the district to adopt.

Employee survey engagement at Rio Rancho was 81 percent favorable, first in the district. Verbatim comments repeatedly cited schedule predictability and "knowing where I stand." No employee relations cases were filed against store management this year.

**Rating on this goal: 4 — Exceeds Expectations.**

## Goal 4: Safety — Target: pass all quarterly safety walks; recordable incident rate below 3.0.

Rio Rancho passed all four quarterly safety walks. The recordable incident rate was 1.9. One lost-time injury occurred in September — a forklift-adjacent strain in receiving — and Dev's incident review and corrective action (restriped pedestrian lanes, retraining, spotter requirement) were completed within the ten-day standard and held up under regional safety's audit.

**Rating on this goal: 4 — Exceeds Expectations.**

## Employee Self-Assessment (excerpted; employee's own words in quotation)

> "I set out this year to prove the contractor desk could work in a store our size and I believe we did that. The number I'm proudest of isn't the 1.1 million — it's that DIY comps were positive too, because it means we didn't rob the floor to feed the desk. My biggest miss was shrink at 1.7 against 1.6; I own the tenth. On my own development, I've been direct with Ximena: I've done four years in this chair, I applied for the regional operations manager role, and I don't see myself running a store next year. I'd rather say that in writing than have it be a surprise."

**Manager response:** I appreciate the directness, in May and again here. It matches what Dev told me in our May one-on-one, and it is the responsible way to handle ambition. My assessment of his candidacy is below.

## Incidents and Feedback

Customer survey score: 74, second in the district behind Santa Fe. Contractor-desk customers scored 88 on the pilot's separate survey. One escalated customer complaint reached the district office this year (a special-order delay); Dev resolved it within 48 hours and the customer's follow-up note was complimentary. No policy or conduct incidents.

## Overall Rating: 4 — Exceeds Expectations

I considered a 5 and want the file to show why I landed at 4. Dev's sales performance was exceptional and I rated it so. The overall rating reflects the shrink target miss, however narrow, and my standard that an overall 5 requires all goals met or exceeded. This is the highest overall rating I am issuing in the district this year.

## Compensation, Bonus, and the Format Conversion

The district merit pool is 2 percent. I am recommending Dev at 3.5 percent, the maximum individual allocation the pool structure permits, funded by differentiation within the district. The district bonus did not pay; see Goal 2 for my position on that. Rio Rancho is not one of the two stores converting to the smaller format in March, and Dev's position is not the one being eliminated. That said, the conversion is directly relevant to Dev's situation: if he is not selected for the regional operations manager role, the district will have a surplus manager and a manager who has said he will not stay in a store role. I am not going to pretend those facts are unrelated.

## Career and Succession

Dev is one of three internal candidates for the open regional operations manager position. I have submitted my endorsement: he is my strongest candidate on operating results and the only one who has built something the chain is now scaling. My honest note of caution, which I have shared with him, is that the regional role is 70 percent influence without authority, and Dev's default mode is to fix things himself; the panel should probe that, and Dev should be ready for it. If he is not selected, I have committed to working with him on alternatives — including a chain-level contractor program role, which merchandising has informally floated — rather than assuming a fifth year in Rio Rancho, which he has told me clearly he will not do. Carla Reyes is his identified successor and could step up on 60 days' notice.

## Goals for Fiscal 2027

1. **Transition readiness:** Regardless of the regional decision, complete a documented transition file for Rio Rancho and certify Carla Reyes as ready-now by October 31, 2026.
2. **Contractor rollout support:** Serve as the peer coach for the eight-store contractor desk rollout; each rollout store to reach 400,000 dollars in desk revenue in year one.
3. **Shrink:** Bring Rio Rancho to 1.5 percent or better, with specific controls on contractor will-call.
4. **Sales (if in role):** Comparable sales of +3.0 percent on top of this year's base, which I acknowledge is a demanding two-year stack.
5. **Development:** Complete the influence-and-stakeholder module of the leadership curriculum by December 31, 2026, aimed squarely at the regional-role gap identified above.

# Zia Mercantile — Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Pauline Villanueva-Estrada
**Title:** Store Manager, Store 03 — Santa Fe
**Review Period:** July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager, District 3
**Date of Review:** August 9, 2026

**Rating Scale:** 5 — Exceptional | 4 — Exceeds Expectations | 3 — Meets Expectations | 2 — Needs Improvement | 1 — Unsatisfactory

---

## Summary of Role and Context

Pauline has managed the Santa Fe store for nine years, the longest tenure of any manager in District 3. Santa Fe is a high-visibility location with a loyal customer base that Pauline has personally cultivated over nearly a decade. This review covers a year in which chainwide comparable sales fell 1.8 percent, the district bonus did not pay because district shrink finished at 2.4 percent against the 1.6 percent target, and two District 3 stores will convert to the smaller neighborhood format in March 2027, which eliminates one store manager position in the district. Santa Fe is one of the two converting stores. I address the implications directly in this review because Pauline deserves candor, not hints.

This is a difficult review to write and I expect a difficult one to receive. It contains genuine and earned praise alongside the most serious performance concerns I have documented for Pauline in the three years I have been her district manager. Both halves are true at the same time.

## Goal 1: Comparable Sales — Target: +1.0 percent. Result: 0.0 percent (flat).

Santa Fe finished flat against a chain that declined 1.8 percent, so on a relative basis the store outperformed the chain by nearly two points. On an absolute basis, the store missed its target and has now been flat or slightly negative for three consecutive years while its trade area's retail spending, per our syndicated data, grew modestly each of those years. The pattern I see in the walk-throughs and the category data is a store that executes its existing model very well and has stopped adding to it. Santa Fe was offered the contractor desk pilot alongside Rio Rancho last July and declined it; that pilot generated 1.1 million dollars in its first year elsewhere in the district. Pauline's stated reason at the time — that the store lacked the staffing bandwidth — was, as this review will show, a real constraint but also a self-inflicted one.

**Rating on this goal: 2 — Needs Improvement.** Relative outperformance of the chain keeps this from a 1; a third consecutive flat year against a growing trade area keeps it from a 3.

## Goal 2: Shrink — Target: 1.6 percent. Result: 2.9 percent.

Santa Fe finished at 2.9 percent shrink, the worst result in the district and nearly double target. This is the single largest driver of the district finishing at 2.4 percent, which in turn is why no manager in District 3 — including managers who beat their own shrink targets — received a bonus this year. I have told Pauline this face to face and I am recording it here because accountability requires that the file reflect it.

The root causes, per the loss prevention deep-dive completed in April, were not primarily external theft. They were operational: cycle count compliance at 61 percent against a 90 percent standard, a receiving process running with chronic short-staffing that led to unverified deliveries, and a backlog of unprocessed damage and return-to-vendor product that sat unreconciled for months. Every one of those causes traces back to the staffing situation described under Goal 3. Shrink at Santa Fe is not a mystery; it is the operational bill for running a store thin for a year.

I want to be fair about one point: Pauline accepted the LP findings without defensiveness, and the corrective plan she built in May is sound on paper. It has not yet run long enough to show results, so the plan earns credit for responsiveness, not for outcome.

**Rating on this goal: 1 — Unsatisfactory.**

## Goal 3: Staffing and People Development — Target: fill within 21 days; retain assistant manager bench.

Time to fill at Santa Fe averaged 46 days against the 21-day standard, more than double, and the worst in the district. The cause is structural and it is a choice: Pauline holds every hiring approval personally, at every level down to seasonal part-time. Candidates routinely waited a week or more between an assistant manager's recommendation and Pauline's interview slot, and the store lost documented candidates to competitors during those waits on at least six occasions this year. I directed Pauline in my October and again in my February one-on-ones to delegate hiring approval for hourly roles to her assistant managers. She did not. Her stated rationale — that her personal hiring standard is why the store's culture and customer scores are what they are — is not without merit, but it does not survive contact with a 46-day fill time and the shrink consequences documented above.

More serious: two assistant managers transferred out of Santa Fe this year, and both named Pauline in their exit interviews. The consistent themes, which HR has shared with Pauline in summary form, were that decisions of any consequence required her personal sign-off, that development conversations did not happen, and that — in one exit interview's words as recorded by HR — there was "no version of growing at that store that doesn't wait for Pauline to retire." Losing one assistant manager is turnover. Losing two, both citing the same cause, is a leadership finding, and I am treating it as one.

**Rating on this goal: 1 — Unsatisfactory.**

## Goal 4: Safety — Target: pass all quarterly safety walks; recordable rate below 3.0.

Santa Fe passed all four quarterly safety walks and finished with a recordable incident rate of 2.1, with no lost-time injuries. Housekeeping and hazard-communication standards at Santa Fe are consistently the best I see in the district. This is genuine and I do not want it lost in the rest of the document.

**Rating on this goal: 3 — Meets Expectations.**

## Customer and Employee Feedback

Santa Fe leads the district in customer survey scores at 78, and has led the district on that measure for five straight years. Verbatim comments name Pauline personally more often than any other manager in the district is named at their store — customers know her, trust her, and come back because of her. Two local contractors wrote unsolicited letters to the district office this year praising the store. This is a real asset and it is Pauline's personal creation. Employee engagement, by contrast, was 58 percent favorable, second-lowest in the district, with the least favorable scores on "I have opportunities to grow" and "decisions are made at the right level."

## Employee Self-Assessment (excerpted; employee's own words in quotation)

> "I have given nine years to this store and its customers, and the 78 score says what kind of store we run. I acknowledge shrink was unacceptable and I own it. On hiring, I hear the criticism, but I have seen what happens when standards slip on who we put on the floor. My standard is high because the store's reputation is high. I am concerned that the format conversion decision has already been made about me and that this review is the paperwork for it."

**Manager response:** I want to answer the last sentence directly, because it deserves a direct answer. No decision has been made about which manager position is eliminated in the March conversion. This review reflects this year's documented results, most of which predate any conversion announcement, and the rating below is the rating the results earn. What is true — and I will not pretend otherwise — is that the conversion decision will be made partly on this year's performance records across the district, and Pauline's record this year does not help her. The customer franchise she has built is her strongest argument, and it is a real one. The path to strengthening her position is the improvement plan below, executed visibly and fast.

## Overall Rating: 2 — Needs Improvement

The customer result is district-best and the safety record is clean. Against that stand the district's worst shrink result, the district's worst fill time, two assistant manager departures naming the manager, a third flat sales year, and a documented instruction to delegate hiring that was not followed. A 3 requires meeting core expectations; two goals rated Unsatisfactory forecloses that. A 1 would ignore a genuinely excellent customer franchise and a clean safety record. The rating is 2.

## Compensation, Bonus, and the Format Conversion

The merit pool is 2 percent; consistent with a Needs Improvement rating, I am recommending 0 percent for Pauline this cycle. The district bonus did not pay for anyone; Santa Fe's shrink result was the largest single contributor to that outcome. Santa Fe converts to the smaller format in March 2027. The converted store will still have a manager role, at a reduced grade, and one manager position in the district will be eliminated. Selection criteria will include this year's performance record, and Pauline should read this review with that plainly in view.

## Performance Improvement Plan and Goals for Fiscal 2027

This review is accompanied by a formal 90-day performance improvement plan, with checkpoints at 30, 60, and 90 days, on the following:

1. **Shrink:** Execute the May corrective plan fully. Cycle count compliance at 90 percent within 60 days; store shrink at 2.0 percent or better by the mid-year inventory and 1.7 percent by year-end.
2. **Delegation of hiring:** Effective within two weeks of this review, hiring approval for all hourly roles moves to the assistant managers, with Pauline reviewing weekly after the fact. Time to fill at 25 days within 90 days, 21 by year-end. This item is a direct instruction, and compliance is itself a PIP measure.
3. **Bench rebuilding:** Hire or develop two assistant managers; complete documented monthly development conversations with each, verified by HR.
4. **Sales:** Comparable sales of +1.5 percent, with a written plan for one new revenue program by October 31.
5. **Sustain strengths:** Hold the customer survey score at 75 or above and maintain the clean safety record through the format conversion, where Pauline's customer relationships will matter most.

I will meet with Pauline every two weeks through the PIP period. I want to close with what I said in our conversation: nine years of customer loyalty is not something I discount, and this plan is written to be achievable by the manager who built it.

# Zia Mercantile — Annual Performance Review

**Employee:** Keisha Amankwaa
**Title:** Store Manager, Store 14 — Farmington
**Review Period:** October 6, 2025 – June 30, 2026 (partial year; promoted into role October 2025)
**Reviewer:** Ximena Robles, District Manager, District 3
**Date of Review:** August 9, 2026

**Rating Scale:** 5 — Exceptional | 4 — Exceeds Expectations | 3 — Meets Expectations | 2 — Needs Improvement | 1 — Unsatisfactory

---

## Summary of Role and Context

Keisha was promoted from assistant manager to store manager of Farmington in October 2025, after the prior manager resigned mid-season and left the store with 40 percent of its positions open going into the fourth-quarter peak. She had never run a store. This review covers roughly nine months in role, and I have calibrated targets and ratings to that partial year and to the condition of the store she inherited, which was — I will say it for the record — the worst handoff I have seen in my time as a district manager. It also covers a year in which chain comparable sales fell 1.8 percent, the district bonus did not pay because district shrink finished at 2.4 percent against the 1.6 percent target, and two district stores convert to the smaller format in March 2027, eliminating one manager position. Farmington is not a converting store.

The honest headline of this review: Keisha performed a genuine rescue on staffing and stabilized a store in crisis, and she has two serious operational deficiencies — safety walk failures and the January inventory result — that she must close quickly. Neither the rescue nor the deficiencies cancels the other out. Both are in this file.

## Goal 1: Staffing Recovery — Target (set at promotion): full staffing by end of Q4 peak; stabilize hourly turnover. Result: full staffing by February.

This was the goal the store's survival depended on, and Keisha delivered it. She took a store at 40 percent open positions in October — including no receiving lead and one functioning key-holder besides herself — and reached full staffing by February. She did it during peak season, in a Farmington labor market where we compete with oil-field wages, and she did it without emergency pay premiums beyond the approved seasonal rates. Her methods bear recording because they are the district's new playbook for distressed staffing: she ran open hiring events on three consecutive Saturdays, made same-day conditional offers, delegated first interviews to her two most experienced associates, and personally called every applicant who declined to find out why, then fixed what she could (the biggest fix: moving to published three-week schedules, which candidates had told her was the differentiator against the competing employers).

Of the hires made since October, 82 percent were still employed at fiscal year-end, so this was not a warm-body exercise; the hires stuck. Time to fill from October forward averaged 19 days against the 21-day standard — from a store that had effectively stopped hiring. Two associates she hired in November are already department leads.

**Rating on this goal: 5 — Exceptional.**

## Goal 2: Sales Stabilization — Target: hold comparable sales decline to no worse than −3.0 percent given store condition. Result: −2.1 percent.

Farmington's comparable sales finished down 2.1 percent for the fiscal year, against a stabilization target of −3.0 percent and a chain at −1.8 percent. The trajectory matters more than the full-year number: the store was running down 6 to 8 percent in the October–December period she inherited, when there were not enough people to keep shelves faced and registers open, and it ran roughly flat in the final quarter once staffing recovered. That trajectory is the direct product of Goal 1. I am not going to hold a first-year manager accountable for a hole dug before she took the chair; I am going to note that she filled most of it.

**Rating on this goal: 3 — Meets Expectations** (target met; chain-relative gap noted).

## Goal 3: Shrink and Inventory Control — Target: 1.6 percent. Result: January physical inventory finished 84,000 dollars short; annualized shrink rate of 2.6 percent.

The January inventory came in 84,000 dollars short, and Farmington finished the year at an annualized 2.6 percent shrink rate against the 1.6 percent target — the second-worst result in the district and a contributor to the district-level miss that cost every manager the bonus. Some of this shortfall was inherited: the loss prevention review attributed a substantial portion to the pre-October period, when receiving ran unverified for weeks and back-stock records were, in LP's word, "unreliable." But not all of it. The LP review also found that from October through January, Keisha did not implement cycle counts (34 percent compliance) and did not re-establish receiving verification until December. Her explanation — that every available hour went to hiring and floor coverage — is true and I have weighed it. It is also the case that I raised cycle counts with her in our November one-on-one and the controls did not come until after the January count made the cost visible.

Since February, the picture has changed: cycle count compliance ran at 91 percent in the final quarter, receiving audits passed both unannounced checks, and the trend-line shrink indicators for the final quarter project below 1.9 percent. The recovery is real; the January number is also real.

**Rating on this goal: 2 — Needs Improvement.**

## Goal 4: Safety — Target: pass all quarterly safety walks; recordable rate below 3.0. Result: failed two of three walks.

Keisha's store failed two of the three quarterly safety walks conducted during her tenure. The Q3 (January) failure cited blocked emergency exits in the stockroom, overstock stacked above safe heights, and an expired fire extinguisher inspection. The Q4 (April) failure cited repeat findings on stockroom stacking plus an unguarded panel saw. A repeat finding is the part I weigh most heavily, because it means the first walk's corrective actions were not sustained. The recordable incident rate was 2.8 — under the 3.0 threshold, but the highest in the district, including a laceration on the panel saw cited in the April walk. That injury and that citation are connected, and I have told Keisha that this is the finding in her file that concerns me most: safety failures are the one category where "the store was in crisis" is not an acceptable trade-off, because the currency at risk is not dollars.

To her credit, her response to the April failure was immediate and complete: saw guard replaced within 48 hours, a stockroom reset over two weekends, a weekly self-audit using the corporate walk checklist, and a follow-up walk in June that she requested herself, which the store passed cleanly.

**Rating on this goal: 2 — Needs Improvement.**

## Customer and Employee Feedback

Farmington's customer survey score finished at 66, up from 58 in the October trough, still below the district's mid-70s norm and clearly correlated with the staffing recovery. Employee engagement was 72 percent favorable — remarkable for a store that nearly emptied out a year ago — with the strongest scores in the district on "my manager listens to me." Three associates independently mentioned Keisha by name in survey verbatims, all positively. One customer complaint escalated to the district office in November regarding checkout waits; it was a fair complaint about a store that had four open register positions at the time.

## Employee Self-Assessment (excerpted; employee's own words in quotation)

> "When I took the store in October I made a decision to put everything into people, because without people nothing else was fixable. I stand by that decision, but I made it too absolutely. I should have protected two hours a week for cycle counts and I didn't, and the January inventory is partly the price. The safety walks are on me with no asterisk — the repeat finding especially. I don't want credit for the recovery without owning what I let slip to get it. What I'd ask for is a full year with a fully staffed store, because I haven't had one yet."

**Manager response:** This is one of the most accurate self-assessments I have received, and the sentence about owning the safety findings "with no asterisk" is exactly the standard I want. I agree with her analysis of her own error — total prioritization instead of proportional prioritization — and the goals below are built around giving her the full, fully staffed year she asks for, with the controls in place from day one.

## Overall Rating: 3 — Meets Expectations

The rating question here is how to weigh an exceptional rescue against two significant control failures, in a partial year, from a first-time manager handed a store in crisis. A 4 is foreclosed by the safety walk failures and the inventory result; I do not give Exceeds Expectations in a year with a repeat safety finding. A 2 would erase a staffing recovery that no other manager in this district — and I mean that literally — was in a position to attempt, and would ignore that both deficient areas showed strong, verified fourth-quarter correction. The record supports a 3: core expectations met in the aggregate, on a steep trajectory, with clearly identified deficiencies now under corrective action.

## Compensation, Bonus, and the Format Conversion

The merit pool is 2 percent. I am recommending Keisha at 2.5 percent, modestly above pool, in recognition of the staffing result and her below-market promotion salary, which HR has confirmed sits at the 25th percentile of the store manager band. The district bonus did not pay because district shrink finished at 2.4 percent against the 1.6 percent target; Farmington's 2.6 percent contributed to that miss, and Keisha should understand that her store's controls affect every manager's bonus, not only her own. Farmington is not one of the two stores converting to the smaller format in March 2027, and Keisha's position is not at risk in the associated manager-position elimination. I have told her directly not to spend attention on it; her security in this role rests on next year's safety and shrink results, which are fully in her hands.

## Goals for Fiscal 2027

1. **Safety — top priority:** Pass all four quarterly safety walks with zero repeat findings; recordable rate below 2.5; maintain the weekly self-audit with results submitted to me monthly. A failed walk next year is a serious matter and I have said so explicitly.
2. **Shrink:** Full-year shrink at 1.9 percent or better, with cycle count compliance at 90 percent every month, not on average.
3. **Sales:** Comparable sales of +2.0 percent — an aggressive target that reflects a fully staffed store lapping a depressed base.
4. **Staffing:** Hold time to fill at or below 21 days and first-year retention at or above 75 percent; develop one assistant manager to promotion-ready by year-end.
5. **Customer recovery:** Customer survey score of 72 or better.
6. **Development:** Complete the new-manager operations certification, which the mid-season promotion bypassed, by November 30, 2026 — the safety and inventory-control modules first.
