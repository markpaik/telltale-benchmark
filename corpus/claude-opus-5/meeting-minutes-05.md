## 4. Serious Safety Event Review

Dr. Sethi presented two events for review under the committee's standing charge.

**Event 2026-001.** On January 9, a patient at 34 weeks gestation presented to the emergency department with contractions and vaginal bleeding. The on-call obstetrician was contacted and directed transfer to a tertiary center in Sioux Falls. The patient was placed in a ground ambulance without examination by a qualified medical person as defined by federal regulation, without fetal monitoring, and without documentation of the physician certification required for transfer of an unstabilized patient. The patient delivered en route at approximately mile marker 168. Mother and infant survived. The infant required neonatal intensive care for eleven days and was discharged home.

Dr. Sethi reported that root cause analysis, completed January 26, identified four contributing causes: absence of a written protocol governing obstetric presentations to the emergency department; ambiguity as to whether the on-call obstetrician or the emergency physician held responsibility for the stabilization determination; a transfer form in use since 2019 that did not capture the required certification elements; and no documented competency validation for emergency department nursing staff in fetal monitoring, which Ms. Amankwah confirmed had lapsed in 2023 when the last trained educator resigned.

Ms. Grabowski was invited to address the committee. She stated that emergency department staff had escalated the absence of an obstetric protocol in writing on three occasions in 2024 and 2025 and that the requests had not resulted in action. Dr. Solheim asked her to provide those written escalations to Ms. Ojeda for the record. Ms. Grabowski agreed.

Mr. Vandenberg asked whether any individual had been disciplined. Ms. Lindstrom answered that a just culture review had been completed, that the review found system causes predominated, and that no individual discipline had been imposed, but that the emergency physician's file carried a documented coaching entry regarding transfer documentation.

**Event 2026-002.** On January 22, a patient admitted for pneumonia received a dose of metoprolol that had been discontinued on admission but remained active in the home medication list. The error was caught by the receiving nurse on the following shift. The patient experienced transient bradycardia to 48 and required no intervention beyond monitoring. Dr. Sethi identified the cause as failure of medication reconciliation at admission, the same defect underlying the condition-level survey finding.

Dr. Solheim directed that both events remain open on the committee's tracking log until the corresponding corrective actions were validated as effective, not merely as completed.

## 7. Obstetrics Service Line — Deliberation and Recommendation

The committee received the five analyses delivered March 26.

**Clinical risk analysis** (Dr. Sethi). Continuation with two obstetricians and locum coverage carries risk from discontinuity, unfamiliarity with local systems, and variable cesarean response times. Closure carries risk of unplanned deliveries in the emergency department, in transport, and at home. Dr. Sethi estimated, from the hospital's own data, that 12 to 18 unplanned presentations annually would be expected following closure, of which 3 to 5 would involve delivery on site.

**Transfer analysis** (Ms. Ojeda). Nearest alternative delivering facilities: Sisseton, 58 miles; Brookings, 61 miles; Aberdeen, 92 miles; Sioux Falls, 101 miles. Median winter transport time to Brookings, from three years of ambulance data, 74 minutes.

**Community impact statement** (Ms. Lindstrom). 188 deliveries in the preceding year, of which 41 percent originated outside Codington County. Estimated 6 to 9 percent of prenatal patients would seek all care outside the community, with consequent loss of associated volume.

**Recruitment assessment** (Ms. Steenhoek). Two searches active since October 2025 with no acceptances; four candidates declined, three citing call burden. Estimated time to fill, 11 to 17 months. Loan repayment and rural incentive programs available.

**Financial analysis** (Mr. Ferraro). Scenario A, full continuation with locums: incremental cost $780,000 to $960,000 annually above current. Scenario B, closure of inpatient delivery with retention of prenatal and gynecologic services: net improvement of $1.3 million with one-time closure costs of $340,000. Scenario C, continuation for a defined bridge period of eighteen months with locum coverage and active recruitment: incremental cost $1.2 to $1.5 million over the period.

Deliberation ran ninety minutes. Mr. Vandenberg argued for Scenario B on the ground that the hospital could not honestly promise a safe service. Sister Doyle argued for Scenario C, stating that the community had asked for time and that eighteen months was a reasonable answer to that request. Mr. Waldner asked whether Scenario C was affordable alongside the nursing market adjustment; Mr. Ferraro answered that both together would consume the hospital's projected operating margin and require draw on reserves. Ms. Balasubramanian stated that the committee's duty was to state the safety conditions under which the service could continue and to let the full board weigh the money. Mr. Reinhardt asked whether Scenario C could be structured with objective checkpoints such that the service closed automatically if coverage failed.

**Motion.** Ms. Balasubramanian moved that the committee recommend to the full board the adoption of Scenario C, a bridge period of eighteen months from May 1, 2026, subject to the following conditions, each of which if unmet for thirty consecutive days would trigger mandatory reconsideration by this committee within fifteen days: continuous obstetric physician coverage with no gap in the call schedule; obstetric anesthesia available within thirty minutes at all hours; decision-to-incision capability for emergency cesarean within thirty minutes documented by drill not less than monthly; and no use of mandatory overtime in the obstetric unit exceeding twelve percent of scheduled hours in any month. Sister Doyle seconded.

Discussion. Mr. Vandenberg stated he would vote no, that the conditions were correct but that he did not believe they could be met, and that the committee was deferring a decision it had the information to make. Dr. Solheim stated that the conditions were the substance of the recommendation and that a bridge without them would be a promise the hospital could not keep.

**The motion carried 5–1.** Voting aye: Solheim, Doyle, Reinhardt, Balasubramanian, Waldner. Voting nay: Vandenberg. Mr. Vandenberg asked that his dissent be recorded with his stated reason, which the Chair granted.

**Motion.** Mr. Reinhardt moved that the committee recommend to the full board that the five analyses, redacted only as necessary to protect individual identities, be published on the hospital's website within ten days of full board action. Mr. Waldner seconded. **The motion carried 6–0.**

**Motion.** Mr. Waldner moved that the committee transmit its recommendation, the petition, the public comment record, and the recorded dissent to the full board for action at its April 16, 2026 meeting. Ms. Balasubramanian seconded. **The motion carried 6–0.**

## 7. Committee Charter and Reporting Calendar

Dr. Solheim presented a revised charter, circulated April 24, prepared by herself, Ms. Lindstrom, and Mr. Pfeiffer following the year's experience.

Principal revisions:

1. **Membership** expanded from six to seven, with the added seat reserved for a member with clinical or regulatory background, appointed by the board chair.
2. **Standing agenda** fixed by charter to include, at every meeting: quality dashboard with run charts; serious safety event review including all events classified level 3 or above; regulatory and accreditation status; open corrective action plans; and vacancy report for quality, safety, infection prevention, and compliance positions.
3. **Direct access.** The compliance officer and the quality director shall have unrestricted direct access to the committee chair, and shall meet with the chair without management present not less than quarterly.
4. **Escalation standard.** Any condition-level regulatory finding, any immediate jeopardy declaration, any serious safety event resulting in death or permanent harm, and any vacancy exceeding ninety days in a named quality, safety, infection prevention, or compliance leadership position shall be reported to the committee chair within two business days of management's knowledge, without waiting for the next meeting.
5. **Reporting calendar.** The committee shall report to the full board at every regular board meeting, in writing, in a fixed format including dashboard, open findings, and open serious events.
6. **Annual self-assessment** and annual review of the charter.
7. **Executive session** authority stated expressly, with the practice of reporting out that a session occurred and whether action was taken.

Discussion. Sister Doyle asked whether the two-business-day escalation standard was realistic. Mr. Threadgill answered that it was, and that had it been in force in August 2025 the infection prevention vacancy would have reached the committee seven months earlier. Mr. Reinhardt asked that the charter state that the committee has no operational authority and does not direct staff outside the chief executive; Dr. Solheim accepted the amendment and language was inserted at section 2.4.

**Motion.** Sister Doyle moved that the committee adopt the revised charter as amended and recommend its approval by the full board at the May 21, 2026 meeting. Ms. Balasubramanian seconded. **The motion carried 5–0.**

**Motion.** Mr. Reinhardt moved adoption of the 2026–2027 reporting calendar as presented, fixing meetings on the first Thursday of each month at 7:30 a.m., with the November meeting moved to November 12 to avoid a holiday conflict, and setting a full-day committee retreat for September 24, 2026.
