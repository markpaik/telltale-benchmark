## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the requirements for the control of hazardous energy during servicing and maintenance of machines and equipment at Teton Fabrication Works, Idaho Falls, Idaho. It exists to prevent injury from the unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy, including the type of incident that occurred in March 2026, when an operator suffered a partial amputation of two fingers during a die change on a 200-ton press brake after a second worker restored power at the disconnect.

This procedure implements the requirements of 29 CFR 1910.147 (The Control of Hazardous Energy), satisfies the terms of the informal settlement agreement with OSHA following Inspection citations issued in 2026, and closes the gaps identified in the internal audit, specifically: 61 of 148 machine-specific procedures missing or outdated, three lock boxes with unaccounted keys, and no documented periodic inspections for either shift in the preceding two years.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Covered Activities

This procedure applies to all servicing and maintenance of machines and equipment in the 90,000 square foot facility where the unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy could cause injury. Covered activities include but are not limited to:

- Die changes and tooling changes on press brakes
- Blade, lens, nozzle, and bed maintenance on fiber lasers
- Programming, teaching, and maintenance inside robotic weld cell perimeters
- Maintenance on the powder coat line, including ovens, booths, and overhead conveyor
- Clearing jams that require removal of guards or placing any body part in a point of operation
- Electrical, pneumatic, and hydraulic system repair
- Cleaning, lubricating, and adjusting where guards are removed or bypassed

### 2.2 Covered Equipment

All 148 pieces of powered equipment listed in the Equipment-Specific Procedure Register (maintained in the maintenance system, register EHS-REG-004-A). Energy sources present in this facility include:

- **Electrical:** 480 VAC three-phase, 240 VAC, 120 VAC, and control voltage circuits
- **Pneumatic:** Plant air at 110 psi, including downstream receivers and actuators
- **Hydraulic:** System pressure and hydraulic accumulators that retain pressure after shutdown (press brakes and select weld cell fixtures)
- **Gravity:** Suspended loads on overhead conveyor sections of the powder coat line, elevated rams, and counterweights
- **Thermal:** Powder coat oven surfaces and laser cutting beds (residual heat)

### 2.3 Exclusions

This procedure does not apply to:

- Normal production operations where guards remain in place and no body part enters a danger zone
- Cord-and-plug equipment meeting the conditions of Section 6.13
- Minor servicing meeting all conditions of Section 6.14
- Hot tap operations (not performed at this facility; any future hot tap work requires a separate approved procedure)

## 3. Definitions

**Affected Employee** — An employee whose job requires them to operate or use a machine on which servicing is being performed under lockout/tagout, or whose job requires them to work in an area in which such servicing is being performed. Approximately 200 employees at this facility are affected employees.

**Authorized Employee** — An employee who locks out or tags out machines in order to perform servicing or maintenance. Currently 74 employees, listed on the Authorized Employee Roster (EHS-REG-004-B).

**Energy-Isolating Device** — A mechanical device that physically prevents the transmission or release of energy: circuit breakers, disconnect switches, line valves, blocks, and blank flanges. Push buttons, selector switches, and other control-circuit devices are **not** energy-isolating devices.

**Equipment-Specific Energy Control Procedure (ESECP)** — A written, machine-specific document identifying every energy source, isolation point, and verification step for one piece of equipment.

**Group Lockout** — A method used when servicing is performed by a crew of more than one authorized employee, providing each individual a level of protection equivalent to a personal lock.

**Lockout Device** — A device (padlock, hasp, valve cover, breaker lockout, plug cap) that holds an energy-isolating device in the safe position.

**Primary Authorized Employee (PAE)** — The authorized employee with overall responsibility for a group lockout.

**Stored Energy** — Energy retained in a system after shutdown: hydraulic accumulator pressure, compressed air in receivers and lines, gravity on suspended loads or raised components, capacitor charge, spring tension, and residual heat.

**Tagout Device** — A warning tag attached to an energy-isolating device stating that the equipment may not be operated. Tags alone are used only where a device cannot accept a lock, with the additional measures of Section 6.5.4.

**Verification (Zero Energy Check)** — The act of confirming, after isolation and lock application, that the equipment is actually de-energized, by attempted start, test instrument, gauge reading, or physical inspection as specified in the ESECP.

**Zero Energy State** — The condition in which all energy sources are isolated, all stored energy is dissipated or restrained, and verification is complete.

## 5. Equipment and Materials

- Individually keyed red safety padlocks, one key per lock, assigned by serial number to a named authorized employee. **No master keys and no duplicate keys exist.** Spare-key inventory is prohibited; a lost key is handled under Section 6.12.
- Standardized red "DANGER — DO NOT OPERATE" tags with employee name, date, and work order number
- Multi-lock hasps, group lock boxes (serialized, keyed individually, registered in EHS-REG-004-C)
- Breaker lockouts, disconnect lockouts, ball valve and gate valve covers, plug caps, cable lockouts
- Safety blocks and die blocks rated for press brake ram loads; conveyor load restraints
- Calibrated voltage tester (verified on a known live source before and after each use), pressure gauges

Lockout stations are located at: press brake bay, laser bay, weld cell corridor, powder coat line entry, and the maintenance shop. Station contents are inventoried monthly by the EHS Manager.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Writing and Revising Equipment-Specific Energy Control Procedures

6.1.1 Every piece of equipment on the Equipment-Specific Procedure Register shall have a current ESECP before any covered servicing is performed on it. Equipment without a current ESECP is red-tagged out of service for maintenance work until the ESECP is written and approved.

6.1.2 The EHS Manager assigns ESECP development to a maintenance technician familiar with the equipment. The technician physically walks down the machine and documents:

1. Equipment ID, name, and location
2. Every energy source (type and magnitude, e.g., "480 VAC, 60 A disconnect DS-114")
3. The exact location and labeling of every energy-isolating device
4. Stored energy hazards and the dissipation or restraint method (e.g., "Bleed accumulator via valve HV-3 until gauge PG-1 reads 0 psi; allow 2 minutes")
5. The verification method for each source (test instrument, gauge, attempted start, physical check)
6. The lockout devices required
7. Step-by-step shutdown, isolation, verification, and restoration sequence
8. A photograph of each isolation point, attached to the ESECP

6.1.3 The EHS Manager reviews the draft against the machine on the floor, then approves it. The approved ESECP is loaded into the maintenance system, linked to the equipment record, and a laminated copy is posted at or on the machine.

6.1.4 ESECPs are reviewed during the annual periodic inspection and revised within 30 days whenever equipment is modified, relocated, or a deficiency is found. Revision requires the same walkdown and approval as Section 6.1.2–6.1.3. Superseded versions are archived, not deleted.

6.1.5 **Backlog closure:** The 61 missing or outdated ESECPs identified in the 2026 audit shall be completed on the schedule in the OSHA settlement corrective action plan, tracked weekly by the EHS Manager and reported monthly to the VP of Manufacturing Operations until closed.

### 6.2 Preparation for Shutdown

6.2.1 Before starting, the authorized employee shall:

1. Open or confirm the work order in the maintenance system and verify the correct ESECP is attached and current (check version and effective date against the register).
2. Read the ESECP in full, even if the task is routine.
3. Identify the type and magnitude of every energy source, the hazards, and the control method. If any observed condition does not match the ESECP (e.g., an isolation point has moved), **stop** and notify the EHS Manager before proceeding.
4. Gather the locks, tags, and devices specified in the ESECP.

### 6.3 Notification

6.3.1 The authorized employee notifies the shift supervisor of the equipment to be locked out, the expected duration, and the work order number.

6.3.2 The shift supervisor notifies all affected employees in the work area, verbally and in person, before shutdown begins. For the powder coat line and overhead conveyor, notification includes all downstream and upstream stations, because a conveyor stop affects the full line.

6.3.3 Notification is repeated at restoration (Section 6.8).

### 6.4 Machine Shutdown and Energy Isolation

6.4.1 Shut down the equipment using its normal stopping procedure (operator controls, e-stop is not a substitute for the normal stop sequence unless the ESECP says otherwise).

6.4.2 Operate every energy-isolating device listed in the ESECP, in the sequence listed:

- **Electrical:** Open the disconnect or breaker. Visually confirm blade or handle position where visible.
- **Pneumatic:** Close the isolation valve and bleed downstream air to atmosphere via the bleed valve; confirm 0 psi on the downstream gauge. Where no bleed exists, actuate the equipment per the ESECP to exhaust trapped air.
- **Hydraulic:** Close the isolation valve. **Bleed accumulators per the ESECP until the gauge reads zero.** Accumulators on press brakes and select weld fixtures retain pressure after shutdown; assuming the pump-off condition equals zero energy is prohibited.
- **Gravity:** Lower suspended conveyor loads and elevated components to their lowest position where possible. Where lowering is not possible, install the safety blocks, pins, or restraints specified in the ESECP. **Press brake rams shall be blocked with rated die blocks before any hand enters the die space, without exception, including during die changes.**
- **Thermal:** Observe the cool-down time in the ESECP before contacting oven or laser bed surfaces.

### 6.5 Lockout / Tagout Device Application

6.5.1 Each authorized employee performing work applies their **own personally assigned lock** to every energy-isolating device (or to the group lock box under Section 6.10). No employee may work under another person's lock.

6.5.2 A completed danger tag (name, date, work order number) is attached with each lock.

6.5.3 Where multiple employees lock the same isolation point, a multi-lock hasp is used.

6.5.4 **Tagout only:** If an energy-isolating device cannot accept a lock, attach the tag at the device and take at least one additional measure providing equivalent protection, as specified in the ESECP: removal of an isolating circuit element, blocking of a controlling switch, removal of a valve handle, or opening of an extra disconnect. Tagout-only isolation requires EHS Manager approval noted on the work order.

### 6.6 Verification of Zero Energy

6.6.1 After all locks are applied, and before any body part enters a danger zone, the authorized employee verifies isolation for **every** energy source:

1. **Attempted start:** Confirm no personnel are exposed, then operate the normal start controls and every mode selector. The machine must not respond. **Return all controls to OFF/neutral** after the test — leaving a control in the run position caused the March 2026 incident sequence to be survivable-by-luck only; a control left on will restart equipment the instant power is restored.
2. **Electrical:** Where the ESECP requires it (all 480 V work and any exposure to conductors), a qualified person tests for absence of voltage with a tester verified live-dead-live.
3. **Pneumatic/Hydraulic:** Confirm zero on the downstream gauges; attempt to actuate a cylinder if the ESECP specifies.
4. **Gravity:** Physically confirm blocks and restraints are seated and pinned.

6.6.2 If any verification fails, stop, re-isolate, and re-verify. If the failure cannot be resolved, notify the EHS Manager and red-tag the equipment.

### 6.7 Performing the Work

6.7.1 Work is limited to the scope of the work order. If the scope expands to expose a new energy source, stop, update the isolation per the ESECP, and re-verify.

6.7.2 If the employee leaves the immediate work area (break, lunch, parts run), their locks **remain in place**. On return, the employee re-verifies zero energy per Section 6.6 before resuming work in a danger zone.

### 6.8 Release from Lockout and Restart

6.8.1 Before removing locks:

1. Inspect the work area: tools, parts, and rags removed; guards reinstalled; blocks and restraints removed (blocks are removed **only** after all personnel are clear of gravity hazards).
2. Confirm all employees are clear of the equipment and pinch points. Walk the full perimeter of large equipment (weld cells, conveyor sections) — a visual sweep from one position is not sufficient.
3. The shift supervisor notifies affected employees that the equipment is about to be re-energized.

6.8.2 Each authorized employee removes **only their own** lock and tag. The last lock removed is that of the employee (or PAE) responsible for the work.

6.8.3 Restore energy in the reverse of the isolation sequence in the ESECP. Perform the function test specified in the ESECP before releasing the equipment to production.

6.8.4 The authorized employee closes the lockout section of the work order in the maintenance system, recording lock-on and lock-off times.

### 6.9 Shift or Personnel Change

6.9.1 Lockout protection shall be continuous across shift changes. The off-going and on-coming authorized employees meet **at the equipment** and conduct a face-to-face transfer:

1. Off-going employee briefs work status, hazards, isolation points, and any temporary conditions.
2. On-coming employee applies their personal locks **before** the off-going employee removes theirs (lock-to-lock transfer). At no moment is the equipment without an authorized employee's lock.
3. On-coming employee re-verifies zero energy per Section 6.6 before entering any danger zone.
4. Both sign the Shift Transfer Log section of the work order.

6.9.2 If the on-coming employee is not available at shift change, the off-going employee's lock remains, the supervisor is notified, and the transfer occurs when the on-coming employee arrives, following 6.9.1. The off-going employee's lock is **never** removed to "clear the board" between shifts; an absent employee's lock is handled only under Section 6.12.

### 6.10 Group Lockout

6.10.1 When more than one authorized employee services the same equipment, a group lockout is used with a serialized group lock box:

1. The supervisor designates a Primary Authorized Employee (PAE) on the work order.
2. The PAE performs shutdown, isolation, and verification per Sections 6.4–6.6, applying a lock to each energy-isolating device and placing **all keys to those isolation locks inside the group lock box**.
3. The PAE locks the box with their personal lock. Every member of the crew then applies their own personal lock to the box before beginning work.
4. Each crew member visually confirms the isolation points or receives a documented briefing from the PAE, and signs the group lockout roster attached to the work order.
5. Crew members remove their personal box locks when their portion of work is complete. The PAE's lock is removed last, after Section 6.8.1 checks, and only then are the isolation locks removed.

6.10.2 **Lock box key control:** Every lock box and every box lock is serialized and registered in EHS-REG-004-C with the assigned keyholder's name. The EHS Manager audits the register quarterly. Any lock box with an unaccounted key is removed from service immediately, and its lock cylinder is replaced before return to service. The three boxes with unaccounted keys identified in the 2026 audit are retired and destroyed.

### 6.11 Testing or Positioning Requiring Temporary Re-energization

When equipment must be energized to test or position it (e.g., jogging a press brake ram during die setup, cycling a robot for teach verification):

1. Clear all tools and materials from the equipment.
2. Ensure all personnel are clear; the PAE or authorized employee accounts for every crew member by name.
3. Remove locks and tags per Section 6.8.2.
4. Energize and perform the test or positioning. During the energized interval, no body part enters a point of operation; jog and teach functions are used at reduced speed per the equipment ESECP.
5. De-energize, re-isolate, re-lock, and **re-verify zero energy per Section 6.6** before servicing resumes.

Skipping the re-verification step after temporary re-energization is a serious violation subject to Section 6.17.

### 6.12 Removal of an Absent Employee's Lock

6.12.1 A lock may be removed by someone other than the employee who applied it **only** under the direction of the EHS Manager (or, in her absence, the VP of Manufacturing Operations), and only after all of the following are completed and documented on form EHS-FRM-004-1:

1. Verify the lock owner is not in the facility (check time records, page overhead, check parking lot).
2. Make all reasonable attempts to contact the employee by phone and text; document each attempt with time.
3. If contacted, obtain the employee's verbal confirmation that they are clear of the equipment.
4. Physically inspect the equipment and work area to confirm no one is exposed and the equipment is safe to energize.
5. The EHS Manager authorizes removal in writing; the lock is cut off (never opened with a spare key — none exist).
6. The employee is informed **before** they resume work on their next shift that their lock was removed, and they sign the form acknowledging this.

6.12.2 A lost personal key is treated the same way: the lock is cut, the lock is destroyed, and a new serialized lock is issued.

### 6.13 Cord-and-Plug Equipment Exception

Lockout is not required for cord-and-plug equipment (portable grinders, small bench tools, corded welders) when **all** of the following are true:

1. The plug is the only energy source (no stored energy hazard, no capacitors, no springs under load).
2. The plug is unplugged before servicing begins.
3. The plug remains under the **exclusive control** of the employee performing the work — in their hand, in their pocket, or within arm's reach and in their direct line of sight at all times.

If the plug cannot remain under exclusive control, apply a plug cap lockout with the employee's personal lock.

### 6.14 Minor Servicing Exception

6.14.1 Routine, repetitive tasks integral to production — such as clearing a sheet jam at a fiber laser, minor powder gun adjustments, or clearing a part hang-up at a weld cell fixture — may be performed without full lockout **only if all** of the following are met:

1. The task is listed on the Approved Minor Servicing Task List (EHS-REG-004-D) for that specific machine, approved by the EHS Manager.
2. Effective alternative protection is used exactly as specified: interlocked guard doors verified functional, key-controlled mode selectors, e-stop engaged with control under exclusive control of the employee, or light curtains verified per the ESECP.
3. No guard is removed and no bypass or defeat of any safety device occurs.
4. The task takes minutes, not hours, and requires no tools beyond those listed.

6.14.2 If any condition cannot be met — including an interlock found faulty — the task reverts to full lockout under this procedure. **A die change on a press brake is never minor servicing** and always requires full lockout with ram blocking.

### 6.15 Contractor Coordination

6.15.1 Before any contractor (including the conveyor vendor servicing the powder coat line) performs covered work:

1. The EHS Manager and the contractor's supervisor exchange and review each other's energy control procedures at a documented pre-job meeting.
2. The contractor is given the applicable ESECPs and a facility orientation covering isolation point locations, lockout stations, and this procedure's group lockout and shift-change rules.
3. Teton employees are informed of the contractor's lockout activities by their shift supervisor.
4. Contractors use their own locks and tags, identifiable by company name, meeting the standardization requirements of Section 5.
5. Where Teton and contractor employees work on the same equipment, a group lockout under Section 6.10 is used, with the PAE designated by mutual agreement and documented on the work order. Overhead conveyor work always requires gravity load restraint verification by both parties.

6.15.2 The EHS Manager audits at least one contractor lockout per contractor visit and records the audit on the work order.

### 6.16 Annual Periodic Inspection

6.16.1 The EHS Manager schedules inspections so that **every ESECP is inspected at least once per calendar year, on both first and second shift over the course of the cycle**, correcting the two-year documentation gap identified in the audit.

6.16.2 Each inspection is performed by an authorized employee **who is not using the procedure being inspected at that time**, accompanied by the EHS Manager or designee. The inspector:

1. Observes an authorized employee performing (or walking through) the ESECP on the actual equipment.
2. Reviews the ESECP against the machine for accuracy.
3. For lockout: holds a documented review with each authorized employee involved, covering their responsibilities. For any tagout-only isolation: extends the review to affected employees, covering tag limitations.
4. Records findings on form EHS-FRM-004-2: equipment ID, ESECP number and version, date, inspector, employees included, deviations found, and corrective actions with due dates.

6.16.3 Deviations trigger: correction of the ESECP within 30 days (Section 6.1.4), and retraining of involved employees within 30 days.

6.16.4 The EHS Manager compiles an annual summary; the VP of Manufacturing Operations reviews and signs it by December 15 each year. Completed inspection certifications are filed in the maintenance system against each equipment record and retained per Section 7.

### 6.17 Enforcement

Violations of this procedure — including operating locked equipment, removing another's lock, restoring power without authorization, or bypassing verification — are subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination under the plant work rules. All violations are reported to the EHS Manager the same day and investigated within 5 business days.

## 7. Training and Authorization Records

### 7.1 Training Requirements

**Authorized employees:** Initial 8-hour classroom and hands-on course covering energy types and magnitudes at this facility, ESECP use, lock application, verification, group lockout, shift transfer, and the exceptions in Sections 6.11–6.14, followed by a supervised practical demonstration on at least two machine types (one must include hydraulic accumulator bleed-down). Authorization is granted by the EHS Manager only after passing a written test (minimum 80%) and the practical.

**Affected employees:** 1-hour training covering the purpose of the program, recognition of locks and tags, and the absolute prohibition on operating or energizing locked equipment or touching devices.

**Other employees:** Awareness briefing at orientation covering lock/tag recognition and the restart prohibition.

**Maintenance technicians and shift supervisors:** Authorized-employee training plus shift-transfer and group-lockout facilitation.

### 7.2 Retraining

Retraining is required when: job assignment changes; equipment, process, or ESECP changes present a new hazard; a periodic inspection or incident reveals deviations or inadequate knowledge; or an employee has not performed lockout in 12 months. Retraining includes a repeat practical demonstration.

### 7.3 Records

The Training Coordinator maintains in the maintenance system, for each employee: name, training type, dates, trainer, test score, practical evaluation, and authorization status. The Authorized Employee Roster (EHS-REG-004-B) is updated within 2 business days of any authorization change and posted at each lockout station. Training records are retained for duration of employment plus 3 years.

## Appendix F — Quick Reference: The Eight Non-Negotiables

Posted at every lockout station and issued to every authorized employee at training.

1. **No ESECP, no work.** If the machine-specific procedure is missing or doesn't match the machine, stop and call the EHS Manager.
2. **Your lock, your key, your life.** Never work under someone else's lock. Never touch someone else's lock.
3. **Bleed the accumulator every time.** Pump off does not mean pressure off. Gauge reads zero or you don't reach in.
4. **Block the ram.** No hand enters a press brake die space without rated blocks in place. Die changes are never "minor servicing."
5. **Try to start it.** After locking out, attempt a start — then return every control to OFF.
6. **Lock-to-lock at shift change.** The new lock goes on before the old lock comes off, at the machine, face to face.
7. **Re-verify after any re-energization.** Testing or jogging the machine ends only when you have re-isolated, re-locked, and re-verified.
8. **Only the EHS Manager removes an absent employee's lock** — by cutting it, after the documented contact process. There are no spare keys in this plant, and there never will be.

*In March 2026, a coworker restored power at a disconnect because he believed the job was done. It cost an operator part of two fingers. Every rule on this card exists so that never happens again.*
