## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE WILLOW BEND PLANNING COMMISSION

**Body:** Planning Commission, City of Willow Bend, Minnesota
**Date:** Monday, February 2, 2026
**Time:** 7:00 p.m.
**Place:** Council Chambers, Willow Bend City Hall, 200 Civic Center Drive
**Members Present:** Chair Beatriz Ocampo-Reyes; Vice Chair Douglas Wetherbee; Commissioners Nnamdi Eze, Solveig Brandt, Hassan Farooqi, Priya Raghunathan, and Tomás Villanueva
**Members Absent:** None
**Others Attending:** Erica Bakke, Planning Director; Peter Dahlquist, Assistant City Attorney; Tom Radke, City Engineer; Janelle Sorum, Recording Secretary; Margaret C. Voss, Attorney, Halloran & Voss LLP, representing Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Craig Stenerson, Vice President of Development, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; approximately 85 members of the public

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. Recording Secretary Sorum called the roll. All seven members were present. A quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Agenda

Commissioner Brandt moved, seconded by Commissioner Eze, to approve the agenda as published. The motion carried 7–0 by voice vote.

### 3. Approval of Minutes

Vice Chair Wetherbee moved, seconded by Commissioner Raghunathan, to approve the minutes of the regular meeting of January 5, 2026, as drafted. The motion carried 7–0 by voice vote.

### 4. Public Comment on Non-Agenda Items

Karen Lindqvist, 8811 Township Line Road, speaking on behalf of Neighbors for the Rural Edge, stated that the group had gathered signatures opposing large-scale industrial rezoning on the City's eastern edge and intended to file a formal petition with the City Clerk prior to any public hearing. No other members of the public addressed the Commission.

### 5. Staff Report: Status of the 2045 Comprehensive Plan Update

Planning Director Bakke presented a written status report on the 2045 Comprehensive Plan Update. She reported that the update, originally scheduled for adoption in May 2025, was approximately nine months behind schedule due to consultant turnover and delays in the regional water supply chapter. She stated that the land use chapter, including the future designation of the agricultural transition area on the City's eastern edge, remained in draft form and had not been reviewed by the Commission or the City Council. She stated that staff had received a complete application from Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners on January 12, 2026, requesting rezoning of 148 acres of farmland at 4200 County Road 9 East from A-1 Agricultural Preserve to I-2 General Industrial for a 1.1 million square foot food distribution warehouse, together with a preliminary request for $14.6 million in tax increment financing assistance. She stated that the applicant's traffic study projected 640 truck trips per day.

Director Bakke recommended that, given the unresolved status of the land use chapter, the Commission consider recommending that the City Council adopt an interim ordinance under Minn. Stat. § 462.355, subd. 4, imposing a six-month moratorium on rezonings of agricultural land to industrial classifications while the comprehensive plan study was completed.

### 6. Consideration of Recommended Interim Ordinance (Industrial Rezoning Moratorium)

Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened discussion on the recommended interim ordinance.

Attorney Margaret C. Voss, appearing for Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners, addressed the Commission. Ms. Voss asserted that her client held vested rights arising from its complete application filed January 12, 2026, and from substantial expenditures on due diligence, engineering, and option payments. She further asserted that the City was bound by the sixty-day agency decision deadline in Minn. Stat. § 15.99 and stated that a moratorium adopted after receipt of a complete application would, in her client's view, constitute an unlawful attempt to change the rules mid-application. She stated that Halcyon Ridge reserved all legal remedies.

Assistant City Attorney Dahlquist advised the Commission that the City had, by letter dated January 28, 2026, exercised its statutory right to extend the initial sixty-day review period by an additional sixty days, and that the question of whether an interim ordinance could lawfully apply to a pending application was a legal question reserved to the City Council and its counsel. He advised that the Commission's role that evening was advisory.

Commissioner Eze stated that recommending industrial rezoning before the land use chapter was complete would put the cart before the horse. Commissioner Brandt concurred and noted the scale of the proposal relative to the City's existing industrial inventory. Vice Chair Wetherbee stated that he opposed a moratorium, expressing concern about litigation exposure and the message it would send to prospective employers. Commissioner Villanueva stated that he shared Vice Chair Wetherbee's concerns and preferred to process the application on its merits. Commissioner Farooqi stated that a pause of limited duration was a reasonable planning tool. Commissioner Raghunathan agreed, provided the moratorium was tied to a defined study scope.

**Motion:** Commissioner Eze moved, seconded by Commissioner Brandt, that the Planning Commission recommend that the City Council adopt an interim ordinance pursuant to Minn. Stat. § 462.355, subd. 4, imposing a six-month moratorium on the rezoning of land classified A-1 Agricultural Preserve to any industrial zoning classification, for the purpose of protecting the planning process while the 2045 Comprehensive Plan land use study was completed, with staff directed to transmit the recommendation and the Commission's findings to the City Council for its February 17, 2026 meeting.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Eze, Brandt, Farooqi, Raghunathan. Nays — Wetherbee, Villanueva. **The motion carried 5–2.**

### 7. Scheduling of Public Hearing — Application PC-2026-004 (Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners)

Director Bakke stated that, irrespective of the Council's action on the interim ordinance, the application remained pending and statutory notice obligations continued to run.

**Motion:** Vice Chair Wetherbee moved, seconded by Commissioner Farooqi, to schedule the public hearing on Application PC-2026-004 for the regular meeting of March 2, 2026, and to direct staff to publish and mail notice as required by ordinance and statute. The motion carried 7–0 by voice vote.

### 8. Staff and Commissioner Reports

Director Bakke reported that the draft 2026 annual work plan would be presented in the second quarter. Chair Ocampo-Reyes reminded members of the state land use training webinar on February 19, 2026.

### 9. Adjournment

Commissioner Villanueva moved, seconded by Commissioner Raghunathan, to adjourn. The motion carried 7–0. Chair Ocampo-Reyes adjourned the meeting at 8:42 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Janelle Sorum, Recording Secretary
Approved: March 2, 2026

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE WILLOW BEND PLANNING COMMISSION

**Body:** Planning Commission, City of Willow Bend, Minnesota
**Date:** Monday, March 2, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m.
**Place:** Council Chambers, Willow Bend City Hall, 200 Civic Center Drive
**Members Present:** Chair Beatriz Ocampo-Reyes; Vice Chair Douglas Wetherbee; Commissioners Nnamdi Eze, Solveig Brandt, Hassan Farooqi, and Priya Raghunathan
**Members Absent:** Commissioner Tomás Villanueva (excused)
**Others Attending:** Erica Bakke, Planning Director; Peter Dahlquist, Assistant City Attorney; Tom Radke, City Engineer; Janelle Sorum, Recording Secretary; Margaret C. Voss, Attorney, Halloran & Voss LLP; Craig Stenerson, Vice President of Development, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Lisa Truong, PE, Meridian Traffic Group; approximately 310 members of the public (overflow seating provided in the Community Room with audio feed)

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Recording Secretary Sorum called the roll. Six members were present; Commissioner Villanueva was absent with prior notice. A quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Agenda and Minutes

Commissioner Farooqi moved, seconded by Commissioner Brandt, to approve the agenda as published and the minutes of the February 2, 2026 regular meeting as drafted. The motion carried 6–0 by voice vote.

### 3. Procedural Statement

Chair Ocampo-Reyes reviewed hearing procedures: sworn testimony, a three-minute limit per speaker, and no repetition of prior testimony except by exception of the Chair. Assistant City Attorney Dahlquist noted for the record that the City Council had adopted the interim ordinance on February 17, 2026, on a 4–1 vote, but had directed that Application PC-2026-004 continue through the hearing process while the Council obtained a legal opinion on the ordinance's applicability to the pending application. He noted that the City's extended statutory review period under Minn. Stat. § 15.99 ran through May 12, 2026. Director Bakke stated for the record that the City Clerk had received a petition bearing 1,300 signatures from Neighbors for the Rural Edge on February 20, 2026, and that the petition was entered into the hearing record as Exhibit H-14.

### 4. Public Hearing — Application PC-2026-004, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Rezoning of 148 Acres at 4200 County Road 9 East from A-1 to I-2; Related Tax Increment Financing Referral

Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened the public hearing at 6:41 p.m. All persons intending to testify were sworn by the Recording Secretary.

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke, having been sworn, presented the staff report. She described the subject property as 148 acres of row-crop farmland with frontage on County Road 9, adjacent to the Maple Slough drainageway on the north and rural residential parcels on the south and east. She summarized the proposal: a 1.1 million square foot food distribution warehouse, 212 loading docks, projected 640 daily truck trips per the applicant's traffic study, and a request for $14.6 million in tax increment financing to fund road, water, and sewer extensions. She reviewed the rezoning criteria in City Code § 152.28 and stated that staff found the application premature pending completion of the comprehensive plan land use chapter, and inconsistent with the current A-1 designation. Staff recommended denial without prejudice.

**Applicant Presentation.** Craig Stenerson, having been sworn, presented for the applicant. He described projected employment of 425 full-time positions at an average wage of $24.60 per hour, a projected market value at completion of $118 million, and the applicant's willingness to fund a signalized intersection at County Road 9 and Township Line Road. Lisa Truong, PE, having been sworn, summarized the traffic impact study, stating that trip generation was based on ITE Trip Generation Manual, 11th Edition, Land Use Code 154 (High-Cube Fulfillment Center) and that the projected 640 daily truck trips would operate at Level of Service C or better at all study intersections with the proposed improvements. Attorney Voss reiterated the applicant's position regarding vested rights and the statutory deadline.

**Public Testimony.** Sixty-three members of the public, each having been sworn, testified between 7:12 p.m. and 11:08 p.m. The Recording Secretary maintained a speaker log, incorporated into the record as Exhibit H-22. Testimony is summarized as follows:

- Forty-nine speakers testified in opposition, citing truck traffic on County Road 9 and Township Line Road, nighttime noise and lighting, loss of prime farmland, groundwater drawdown, drainage into Maple Slough, diesel emissions near the Prairie Crossing neighborhood, and the appropriateness of $14.6 million in public subsidy.
- Eight speakers testified in support, citing employment, tax base diversification, and the site's proximity to the county highway network.
- Six speakers testified as neutral, requesting additional study.

Testimony of particular note included the following. Dr. Alan Petrovich, PE, 7420 Ridgeline Court, a retired Minnesota Department of Transportation traffic engineer, having been sworn, testified that the applicant's study understated trip generation. He testified that the study applied a fulfillment-center land use code to what was functionally a cross-dock food distribution facility, that comparable facilities of similar dock count generated between 850 and 1,000 daily truck trips, that the study's directional distribution assumed 70 percent of trucks would use County Road 9 westbound without any enforceable routing mechanism, and that no weekend or harvest-season counts had been taken. He submitted a nine-page technical memorandum, entered as Exhibit H-31. Karen Lindqvist, having been sworn, presented the Neighbors for the Rural Edge petition and testified that the group did not oppose industry categorically but opposed industrial rezoning ahead of the comprehensive plan. Ms. Truong, recalled, testified in rebuttal that the land use code selection was defensible but acknowledged that no facility-specific counts from comparable sites had been included in the study.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed public testimony at 11:08 p.m. and asked whether the Commission wished to close or continue the hearing.

### 5. Commission Discussion and Action

Commissioner Eze stated that the trip generation dispute was material and unresolved. Commissioner Raghunathan requested an independent peer review of the traffic study. Vice Chair Wetherbee stated that the applicant should be given the opportunity to respond to Exhibit H-31 with revised analysis. Commissioner Farooqi asked staff to confirm the wetland status of the Maple Slough margin; City Engineer Radke stated that the applicant had submitted a desktop (Level 1) wetland determination only.

**Motion:** Commissioner Raghunathan moved, seconded by Commissioner Eze, to continue the public hearing on Application PC-2026-004 to the regular meeting of April 6, 2026; to keep the hearing record open for written submissions through March 27, 2026; to direct staff to retain an independent traffic engineering peer review of the applicant's traffic study, at the applicant's expense pursuant to City Code § 152.06, with the peer review report due to the Commission by April 1, 2026; and to invite the applicant to submit any revised site plan or traffic materials by March 23, 2026.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Farooqi, Raghunathan. Nays — none. **The motion carried 6–0.**

### 6. Adjournment

Commissioner Brandt moved, seconded by Commissioner Farooqi, to adjourn. The motion carried 6–0. Chair Ocampo-Reyes adjourned the meeting at 11:26 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Janelle Sorum, Recording Secretary
Approved: April 6, 2026

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE WILLOW BEND PLANNING COMMISSION

**Body:** Planning Commission, City of Willow Bend, Minnesota
**Date:** Monday, April 6, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m.
**Place:** Council Chambers, Willow Bend City Hall, 200 Civic Center Drive
**Members Present:** Chair Beatriz Ocampo-Reyes; Vice Chair Douglas Wetherbee; Commissioners Nnamdi Eze, Solveig Brandt, Hassan Farooqi, Priya Raghunathan, and Tomás Villanueva
**Members Absent:** None
**Others Attending:** Erica Bakke, Planning Director; Peter Dahlquist, Assistant City Attorney; Tom Radke, City Engineer; Janelle Sorum, Recording Secretary; Margaret C. Voss, Attorney, Halloran & Voss LLP; Craig Stenerson, Vice President of Development, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Lisa Truong, PE, Meridian Traffic Group; David Aldous, PE, Northstar Transportation Consultants (City peer reviewer); approximately 240 members of the public

### 1. Call to Order and Roll Call

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Recording Secretary Sorum called the roll. All seven members were present. A quorum was established.

### 2. Approval of Agenda and Minutes

Commissioner Villanueva moved, seconded by Commissioner Farooqi, to approve the agenda as published and the minutes of the March 2, 2026 regular meeting as drafted. The motion carried 7–0 by voice vote. Commissioner Villanueva stated for the record that he had reviewed the recording and full record of the March 2, 2026 hearing and was prepared to participate in the continued item.

### 3. Continued Public Hearing — Application PC-2026-004, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners

Chair Ocampo-Reyes reconvened the continued public hearing at 6:37 p.m. Persons offering new testimony were sworn by the Recording Secretary.

**Staff Report on Revised Submittal.** Director Bakke, having been previously sworn, reported that the applicant had submitted a revised site plan and revised traffic materials on March 23, 2026. The revisions included: (a) a landscaped earthen berm, twenty feet in height and 2,400 feet in length, along the southern and eastern property lines, with a double row of coniferous plantings; (b) a proposed truck routing agreement, in recordable form, restricting all truck ingress and egress to County Road 9 west of the site and prohibiting truck traffic on Township Line Road, enforceable by liquidated damages of $500 per violation; (c) a reduction in dock count from 212 to 164 and elimination of the third-shift outbound operation, reducing projected daily truck trips by 320, from 640 to 320; and (d) full cutoff lighting fixtures with a 0.1 footcandle limit at the property line. She stated that staff continued to note the tension with the interim ordinance and the incomplete land use chapter, but that the revised plan substantially mitigated the operational impacts identified at the hearing, and that staff took no position on the rezoning, deferring to the Commission's findings.

**Peer Review Report.** David Aldous, PE, having been sworn, presented the independent peer review commissioned by the City. He testified that the original study's land use code selection likely understated truck generation by 25 to 40 percent, substantially corroborating Exhibit H-31; that the revised submittal's projection of 320 daily truck trips was reasonable for the reduced dock count and shift structure, provided that the routing agreement and dock cap were made enforceable conditions; and that the intersection of County Road 9 and Township Line Road would operate at Level of Service C or better under the revised projections with the applicant-funded signal.

**Applicant Presentation.** Mr. Stenerson, previously sworn, presented the revised plan and confirmed that the applicant would accept a recorded dock-count cap, the routing agreement, and annual traffic count reporting as conditions of approval. Attorney Voss stated that the applicant maintained its legal positions but was prepared to accept reasonable conditions.

**Additional Public Testimony.** Eleven persons, having been sworn, offered new testimony between 7:24 p.m. and 8:03 p.m. Dr. Petrovich testified that the revised trip projection was credible if the conditions were enforceable, but stated continuing concern regarding the adequacy of the Level 1 wetland determination for the Maple Slough margin. Ms. Lindqvist testified that Neighbors for the Rural Edge remained opposed on comprehensive-plan-timing grounds. Three speakers testified in support of the revised plan.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed the public hearing at 8:11 p.m.

### 4. Commission Deliberation, Findings, and Action

Commissioner Eze stated that the revisions were meaningful but that rezoning 148 acres of A-1 land ahead of the comprehensive plan remained unsupportable and inconsistent with the moratorium the Commission itself had recommended on February 2. Commissioner Brandt concurred, adding concerns regarding the unresolved wetland status and the $14.6 million tax increment request. Commissioner Raghunathan stated that the trip generation dispute had been resolved but that the plan-consistency criterion had not been satisfied. Vice Chair Wetherbee stated that the revised plan, with enforceable conditions, met the rezoning criteria and that the site's highway access made it the City's logical industrial reserve regardless of the plan's timing. Commissioners Villanueva and Farooqi expressed agreement with Vice Chair Wetherbee. Chair Ocampo-Reyes stated that she found the conditions sufficient and that the sixty-day extension deadline of May 12, 2026, counseled a decision on the merits.

**Motion to Recommend Denial:** Commissioner Eze moved, seconded by Commissioner Brandt, to recommend that the City Council deny Application PC-2026-004, based on findings that the rezoning was inconsistent with the A-1 designation and premature pending completion of the 2045 Comprehensive Plan land use chapter.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Eze, Brandt, Raghunathan. Nays — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Farooqi, Villanueva. **The motion failed 3–4.**

**Motion to Recommend Approval with Conditions:** Vice Chair Wetherbee moved, seconded by Commissioner Villanueva, to recommend that the City Council approve Application PC-2026-004, rezoning the subject 148 acres from A-1 Agricultural Preserve to I-2 General Industrial, and to refer the $14.6 million tax increment financing request to the Council and the Economic Development Authority for independent analysis, subject to adoption of the following findings and the following seventeen conditions:

*Findings of Fact:* (1) the revised application, as conditioned, was consistent with the general goals of the existing Comprehensive Plan for employment growth along the county highway corridor; (2) the peer-reviewed projection of 320 daily truck trips, as capped by condition, would not create undue congestion at study intersections; (3) the berm, lighting, and routing conditions mitigated impacts on adjacent rural residential uses; and (4) municipal utilities could be extended to serve the site.

*Conditions:* (1) recorded restriction capping loading docks at 164; (2) recorded truck routing agreement prohibiting truck traffic on Township Line Road, with liquidated damages of $500 per violation; (3) daily truck trips not to exceed 320, verified by (4) annual classified traffic counts submitted to the City Engineer each October; (5) construction of the twenty-foot berm and landscape plan prior to certificate of occupancy; (6) full cutoff lighting not exceeding 0.1 footcandle at property lines; (7) no outbound dock operations between 10:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m.; (8) applicant funding of the County Road 9/Township Line Road signal, subject to county approval; (9) dedication of right-of-way for the County Road 9 turn lanes; (10) completion of a Level 2 wetland delineation of the Maple Slough margin, reviewed and approved by the Technical Evaluation Panel prior to grading permit issuance; (11) stormwater management to infiltrate the ten-year event on site with no net increase in discharge to Maple Slough; (12) a groundwater appropriation permit from the Department of Natural Resources prior to building permit if daily use exceeded 10,000 gallons; (13) architectural treatment of all facades visible from public rights-of-way; (14) a development agreement executed prior to the rezoning ordinance taking effect; (15) any tax increment assistance subject to a separate "but-for" finding by the Council after independent financial review; (16) a phasing plan limiting initial construction to 700,000 square feet, with the balance subject to administrative site plan review; and (17) all conditions binding on successors and assigns and recorded against the property.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Farooqi, Villanueva. Nays — Eze, Brandt, Raghunathan. **The motion carried 4–3.**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes directed staff to transmit the recommendation, findings, conditions, and complete hearing record to the City Council for its meeting of April 21, 2026.

### 5. Staff Reports

Director Bakke reported that the draft 2026 annual work plan and a proposed housing overlay study scope would appear on the May 4, 2026 agenda.

### 6. Adjournment

Commissioner Raghunathan moved, seconded by Commissioner Brandt, to adjourn. The motion carried 7–0. Chair Ocampo-Reyes adjourned the meeting at 9:47 p.m.

Commissioner Eze moved, seconded by Commissioner Villanueva, to approve the agenda as published and the minutes of the April 6, 2026 regular meeting as drafted. The motion carried 7–0 by voice vote.

### 3. Recusal — Commissioner Farooqi

Commissioner Farooqi stated for the record that, subsequent to the April 6, 2026 meeting, he had learned that his employer, Gopher State Mechanical Contractors, had entered into a design-assist contract with Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners for the proposed facility. On the advice of the Assistant City Attorney, he announced his recusal from all further proceedings on Application PC-2026-004 and stated that he would leave the dais and the chamber during Item 4. Commissioner Farooqi left the chamber at 7:09 p.m.

### 4. Remand from City Council — Application PC-2026-004 (Wetland Delineation)

Director Bakke reported that on April 21, 2026, the City Council, rather than acting on the Commission's April 6 recommendation, voted 5–0 to remand Application PC-2026-004 to the Planning Commission. The Council's remand resolution, entered into the record as Exhibit R-1, directed the Commission to receive and evaluate a completed Level 2 wetland delineation of the Maple Slough margin and a Technical Evaluation Panel review before returning a final recommendation, citing correspondence from the Wetland Conservation Act local government unit staff indicating that the applicant's Level 1 desktop determination may have understated the extent of jurisdictional wetland along the site's northern 30 acres. She reported that the applicant had executed a further extension of the statutory review period under Minn. Stat. § 15.99 through August 15, 2026, entered into the record as Exhibit R-2.

Sarah Okafor of Prairie Hydrology LLC, retained by the City as wetland reviewer, having been sworn, testified that a compliant delineation required growing-season field work, that field investigation could begin in mid-May, and that a delineation report and Technical Evaluation Panel concurrence could reasonably be completed by early July 2026.

Attorney Voss stated that the applicant would commission the Level 2 delineation immediately, would fund the City's review consultant per City Code § 152.06, and consented to the schedule described by Ms. Okafor.

**Motion:** Commissioner Raghunathan moved, seconded by Vice Chair Wetherbee, that the Commission: (a) accept the City Council's remand of Application PC-2026-004; (b) direct the applicant to complete a Level 2 wetland delineation of the subject property, with Technical Evaluation Panel review, and to submit the delineation report to the Planning Director by July 6, 2026; (c) direct staff to schedule the remanded item, with a reopened public hearing limited to wetland and drainage evidence, for the regular meeting of July 13, 2026 (the July meeting having been moved from July 6 due to the holiday week); and (d) direct staff to report the Commission's schedule to the City Council at its May 19, 2026 meeting.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Raghunathan, Villanueva. Nays — none. Recused — Farooqi. **The motion carried 6–0.**

Commissioner Farooqi returned to the chamber and resumed his seat at 7:44 p.m.

Commissioner Brandt requested that the eastern edge study explicitly evaluate an agricultural buffer overlay; Director Bakke accepted the amendment as a friendly revision to the work plan text.

Nays — none. **The motion carried 7–0.**

Nays — none. **The motion carried 7–0.**
