**TO:** Deshawn Whitfield, President; Executive Committee
**FROM:** Yuki Tanabe-Ortiz, Senior Vice President of Merchandising
**DATE:** June 15, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Six-Store Digital Loyalty Pilot — Results and Recommendation

This memo summarizes the results of the twelve-week digital loyalty and targeted-pricing pilot conducted at six stores — Northampton, Amherst, Keene, Rutland, Springfield-Boston Road, and Williamstown — and recommends that we proceed to a company-wide rollout on the timeline the executive committee approved in March, adjusted for the vendor delay noted below.

**Pilot Design**

The pilot replaced the paper circular at the six stores with app-based and card-linked offers personalized to purchase history, supported by weekly targeted pricing on roughly 200 SKUs per store. Customers who did not enroll in the loyalty program continued to see a reduced-page printed circular so we could isolate the effect of personalization rather than simply the removal of print. Enrollment reached 61 percent of transacting households by week eight and held there through the close of the test.

**Results**

Average basket size across the six stores rose 4.1 percent versus the trailing thirteen-week baseline, with the strongest lift in Rutland and Williamstown, both of which had the lowest starting loyalty penetration and therefore the most room to move. Trip frequency was roughly flat, which tells us the basket gain is coming from incremental items per trip rather than additional visits — consistent with the targeted-pricing mechanic doing what it is designed to do. Enrolled-household spend outpaced non-enrolled spend by 6.8 percent, a gap wide enough that I am comfortable attributing most of the basket lift to the program rather than to seasonal or competitive noise, though I will note the pilot ran through a period with no major competitor promotional events, so we should expect some softening once we are live everywhere and competitors respond.

**Vendor Funding Impact**

The offsetting finding, which Padraig and I have already discussed and which his memo will address in financial terms, is that vendor promotional funding tied to circular placement fell materially at pilot stores. Under our current vendor agreements, co-op and slotting dollars are largely contracted against guaranteed circular impressions — a full-page ad, a front-page placement, a certain number of weeks of feature pricing. A personalized digital offer to a subset of shoppers does not satisfy those guarantee terms under most of our current contracts, so vendors have been unwilling to fund it at the same rate. Extrapolated company-wide, we estimate this at a $1.2 million annual reduction in promotional funding unless we renegotiate the underlying contracts, which our vendor relations team has begun doing but which will not be complete before full rollout. I want to be direct that this is a real cost of the transition, not a modeling artifact, and it is not fully offset by the basket gain in dollar terms — the basket lift improves gross sales and, we believe, gross margin quality, but the funding loss hits margin dollars directly and immediately.

**Recommendation**

I recommend we proceed to full rollout. The basket and margin-quality signal is strong enough, and the format works well operationally — store teams in all six pilot locations reported the in-aisle digital tag readers and the app performed reliably, with only minor complaints about older-model Android phones not scanning cleanly, which our vendor says is fixable in the next firmware release. My primary caution is timing: the vendor has told us implementation across the remaining 22 stores will run into scheduling constraints that push completion later than the original March target. I understand Padraig's memo will address the revised capital and timeline picture directly. From a merchandising standpoint, I would rather launch correctly in August than launch on the original date with degraded functionality, and I do not believe the delay changes the underlying case for moving ahead.

I am available to walk the committee through store-by-store pilot detail before the July board update if that would be useful.

**TO:** Store Directors; Corporate Staff, All Departments
**FROM:** Deshawn Whitfield, President
**DATE:** June 29, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Digital Loyalty Program Launch and Store Network Changes

I am writing to tell you directly, before you hear it secondhand, that Larkspur is making two significant changes to how we operate: we are replacing our paper circular with a digital loyalty and targeted-pricing program across all 28 stores, and we are closing four stores that have not been performing, two of which we intend to reopen next year in a smaller format better suited to their markets.

**Why We Are Doing This**

Our gross margin has fallen from 26.8 percent to 24.1 percent over the past two years. That is not a one-quarter problem and it is not explained by any single line item — it reflects rising occupancy and labor costs against a promotional model, the printed circular, that has become less effective at driving traffic while remaining just as expensive to produce and distribute. Four of our stores lost money in every quarter of 2026, and I do not believe that pattern reverses on its own. Yuki's team has spent the last three months piloting a digital alternative to the circular at six stores, and the results — a 4.1 percent increase in average basket size among participating households — tell us this is the right direction, even though it comes with real costs I will not minimize, including a reduction in vendor promotional funding that Padraig's team is working through.

**Store Closures**

Effective with this notice, we are closing our North Adams and Bennington locations permanently. Both stores have lost money every quarter for two years, both operate in trade areas with declining population and a competitor within four miles that has invested heavily in the last eighteen months, and neither has a path to profitability we can identify at their current size and lease terms.

We are also closing our Pittsfield and Brattleboro stores, but with a different plan for those two. Both sit in markets that can support a grocery store, just not one of our current 30,000-plus-square-foot format at our current cost structure. We intend to reopen both as approximately 12,000-square-foot stores focused on a tighter, faster-turning assortment, and we are targeting a reopening in the first half of 2027, pending construction and permitting timelines Renata's team will manage.

I know this is difficult news for the people who work at all four locations, and Estelle will be sending a separate memo this week detailing transfer rights, recall rights, and severance for affected employees. I want to be clear that our intent with Pittsfield and Brattleboro is to bring back as many of the current team as the smaller format can support, and that recall rights will extend to all 28 stores, not just the two reopening.

**What This Means for the Rest of the Network**

For the 24 stores not affected by closure, the digital loyalty rollout will proceed store by store beginning in August, on a schedule Yuki's team will share directly with store directors. I recognize August is later than the March date some of you may have heard discussed internally earlier this year; the vendor implementation has slipped, and Padraig's forthcoming memo will address the capital and financial implications of that delay along with the revised FY28 outlook.

I will be visiting each region over the next three weeks to answer questions in person. In the meantime, direct questions to your district manager, and do not speculate with employees about matters not yet decided — if you do not have an answer, tell people it is coming.

**TO:** Store Directors; District Managers; Human Resources Business Partners
**FROM:** Estelle Nwachukwu, Vice President of Human Resources
**DATE:** July 8, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Transfer, Severance, and Scheduling Policy — North Adams, Bennington, Pittsfield, and Brattleboro Closures

This memo sets the policy that will govern the 310 employees affected by the closure of the North Adams, Bennington, Pittsfield, and Brattleboro stores, effective with the schedules distributed alongside this memo. Please review this in full before fielding employee questions, and route anything not covered here to your HR business partner rather than improvising an answer.

**Notice and Timeline**

Affected employees will receive individual written notice by July 15, consistent with our obligations under state plant-closing statutes and, for our unionized locations, the collective bargaining agreement with UFCW Local 1459. Last working day at each of the four stores will be staggered between August 24 and September 11 to allow for orderly inventory drawdown, equipment removal, and, at Pittsfield and Brattleboro, preservation of fixtures we intend to reuse in the smaller-format rebuild. I recognize the union has raised concerns about the adequacy of this notice period; President Whitfield is responding to that objection directly in a separate memo, and store directors should not attempt to negotiate notice terms independently.

**Recall Rights**

Every employee separated by these closures retains recall rights to any open position at any of our 28 stores for eighteen months from their separation date, in order of seniority within job classification, consistent with the contract for our represented employees and as company policy for non-represented employees. When Pittsfield and Brattleboro reopen at the smaller format, employees from those two stores will have first recall rights to the new positions, again by seniority, before we recruit externally, though I want to be candid that a 12,000-square-foot store will support fewer positions than the stores being closed, so not everyone will be recalled to their former location even with full seniority protection honored.

District managers should maintain active contact with separated employees whose recall clock is running and should flag openings proactively rather than waiting for the corporate posting system to surface them, particularly for employees who may not be checking the internal job board regularly.

**Severance**

Employees not recalled within 90 days of separation, or who decline an offered transfer to another store within a reasonable commuting distance (defined as 25 miles, consistent with past practice), become eligible for severance calculated at one week of pay per year of service, with a minimum of two weeks and a maximum of sixteen. Severance is paid in a lump sum upon separation for employees who decline a qualifying transfer offer, or upon expiration of the 90-day recall window for employees who are not offered a position. Health benefits continue through the end of the month in which separation or the recall window expiration occurs, and COBRA information will be provided at that time.

**Scheduling During the Transition**

Between now and each store's last working day, we ask store directors to maintain normal scheduling to the extent inventory and traffic support it, rather than front-loading hour reductions. I recognize this creates some inefficiency in the final weeks of operation, but abrupt hour cuts ahead of a closure that employees did not choose creates avoidable hardship and legal exposure, and Padraig's office has already accounted for this cost in the closure budget. Any deviation from normal scheduling in the final four weeks requires district manager sign-off.

Please direct individual employee questions about their specific situation to your HR business partner. General policy questions can come to my office directly.

**TO:** Deshawn Whitfield, President; Executive Committee; Board Finance Committee
**FROM:** Padraig Malloy, Chief Financial Officer
**DATE:** July 20, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Revised Capital Plan and FY28 Margin Forecast — Digital Loyalty Program and Store Closures

This memo updates the capital plan and FY28 margin forecast the board approved in March in light of the vendor implementation delay, the resulting cost overrun, the loss of vendor promotional funding identified in the pilot, and the store closure and lease termination costs now confirmed for North Adams, Bennington, Pittsfield, and Brattleboro.

**Capital Plan Revision**

The digital loyalty program capital estimate has increased from $5.4 million to $7.9 million, a $2.5 million overrun. Roughly $1.4 million of that increase reflects the vendor's revised scope for in-store hardware — the digital shelf-tag and reader infrastructure across 22 remaining stores requires a hardware revision the vendor did not disclose at the time of the original bid, driven by connectivity limitations at several of our older locations that were not adequately assessed during vendor scoping. The remaining $1.1 million reflects extended project management and integration costs associated with the schedule slipping from March to August, including a fee the vendor is charging for extended staffing of the implementation team and additional costs to run our existing circular production in parallel for a longer period than originally planned, since we cannot cut over store by store as quickly as intended.

I have reviewed this with the vendor's account team and do not believe further scope creep is likely, but I want the board to understand that $7.9 million should now be treated as the working estimate rather than a ceiling with meaningful cushion in it.

**FY28 Margin Forecast**

The March forecast assumed the digital program would be fully live by mid-2026 and would contribute a full year of benefit in FY28. Under the revised August start and store-by-store rollout schedule, we now expect full deployment by the end of the first quarter of FY27, which does still give us a full FY28 benefit year, so the delay itself is a timing cost, not a lost-benefit cost, and I do not want to overstate its severity.

The more significant revision is the $1.2 million annual reduction in vendor promotional funding that Yuki's pilot memo identified, which our vendor relations team's ongoing contract renegotiations may recover only partially by the time FY28 begins. I am building the FY28 forecast on the assumption that we recover $400,000 of that funding through renegotiated terms and treat the remaining $800,000 as a permanent reduction in promotional income. Combined with the $2.5 million capital overrun, which affects depreciation and financing cost rather than margin directly, and the store closure costs below, I am revising the FY28 gross margin forecast from the previously communicated 25.6 percent to 25.1 percent. I want to be clear this still represents meaningful recovery from our current 24.1 percent, driven primarily by the basket lift and by the elimination of four underperforming stores from the base, but it is a half-point below what the board approved in March, and I did not want that gap to surface for the first time in a board meeting.

**Store Closure and Lease Costs**

Two landlords, at North Adams and Bennington, have asserted lease termination payment claims totaling $2.1 million, arguing our leases do not contain early-termination provisions that permit exit without payment. Legal is reviewing both leases and believes we have a reasonable, though not certain, argument that the underperformance clause in the North Adams lease limits our exposure there to roughly $600,000 rather than the $1.3 million claimed. We have no comparable argument at Bennington and are budgeting the full $800,000 claim there. I have included $1.4 million in the FY26 closure reserve against this exposure and will update the board as negotiations proceed.

The Pittsfield and Brattleboro rebuild costs, estimated at $2.2 million combined for the smaller-format construction, are budgeted separately in the FY27 capital plan and are not reflected in the figures above.

I am available to walk through the full model in advance of the August board meeting.

**TO:** Rosa Delgado, President, UFCW Local 1459
**FROM:** Deshawn Whitfield, President, Larkspur Markets
**CC:** Estelle Nwachukwu, Vice President of Human Resources; Renata Kovalenko, Director of Store Operations
**DATE:** July 29, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Response to Local 1459's Objection to Closure Notice Period

Thank you for your letter of July 22 setting out the local's formal objection to the notice period provided for the closures of our North Adams, Bennington, Pittsfield, and Brattleboro stores. I want to respond directly and address each point you raised rather than route this through counsel first, because I think these questions deserve a plain answer from me.

Your central objection is that the notice provided, running from July 15 through staggered last working days between August 24 and September 11, does not meet the 60-day standard the local believes applies under Article 14 of our agreement for closures affecting represented employees at a covered location. Our position, which counsel has reviewed, is that Article 14's 60-day provision applies to closures without an operational successor at the same location, and that Pittsfield and Brattleboro do not fall under that provision because we intend to reopen both locations and have committed in writing, in Ms. Nwachukwu's July 8 memo to affected employees, to first recall rights for separated employees at those two stores. We recognize this reading is not the only possible one, and I do not want to resolve it by simply asserting it. I am proposing that for Pittsfield and Brattleboro specifically, we extend the effective last-working-day dates by two weeks, to September 8 and September 25 respectively, which brings total notice to 61 and 60 days from the July 15 notice date. I believe this resolves the dispute for those two locations without requiring us to litigate the interpretation question, and I would like your agreement to that adjustment by August 5 so we can communicate revised dates to employees with certainty.

For North Adams and Bennington, where there is no reopening and therefore no ambiguity about which contract provision applies, I do not agree that the 60-day standard applies, and our notice there — 40 to 58 days depending on individual last working day — reflects a closure, not a successor-location transition. I recognize the local disagrees, and I do not want to pretend this disagreement is resolved by this letter. If the local wishes to file a grievance on this specific point, we will engage through the grievance procedure in good faith, and I would rather do that than have this dispute sit unresolved through the contract reopener discussions beginning in January, which I do not think serves either of us.

I want to also respond to the local's related point about recall rights being illusory given the smaller footprint of the rebuilt stores. That concern is fair on its face — a 12,000-square-foot store will not support the staffing of the stores being closed — but I do not think it changes the notice-period analysis, since the recall right itself, not its ultimate scope, is what determines whether Article 14 or the closure provisions apply. I have asked Ms. Nwachukwu to provide the local with detailed projected staffing levels for both rebuilt stores as soon as store design is finalized, currently expected in September, so the local has real numbers rather than estimates when evaluating the practical value of recall rights for its members.

I recognize the January contract reopener will likely be the venue where the local wants to press for clearer language on successor-location notice obligations going forward, and I have no objection to that being on the agenda. In the meantime, I would like a response on the Pittsfield and Brattleboro date adjustment by August 5, and I am available to meet in person before then if that would help.

I also want to address a smaller point in your letter that I do not want to lose amid the larger notice-period dispute: the local's request that we provide bumping rights within each affected store's job classifications before recall rights to other locations take effect. Our current practice, and Ms. Nwachukwu's July 8 memo, treats recall as a cross-store right exercised by seniority within classification, which is consistent with how we handled the Adams Street distribution consolidation in 2023. I understand the local's preference would be to allow a senior employee at, say, North Adams to displace a more junior employee in the same classification at a nearby operating store before that store's own employees are affected. We have not operated that way in the past, and I am reluctant to introduce it now without discussing the operational consequences, since it could mean junior employees at unaffected stores lose shifts or positions to make room for employees from closing locations, which creates a second wave of disruption at stores that did nothing to cause this situation. I would rather discuss this at the table in January, with real data from this closure in hand, than decide it unilaterally in this letter. If the local believes this is urgent enough to require resolution before January, tell me and we will find time sooner.

On timing more generally, I want to acknowledge that the union's frustration is not solely about the calendar. I recognize that some of this frustration is about the pace of information — the local has told Ms. Nwachukwu's office more than once that members are hearing about scheduling changes from store directors before shop stewards are notified, and that this has happened at Bennington twice in the last three weeks. That should not be happening, and I have asked Ms. Kovalenko to confirm with every affected store director that shop stewards receive schedule and staffing information on the same day as employees, not after. If it happens again, I want to hear about it directly rather than through a grievance filing, because I would rather fix it than litigate it.

Finally, on the broader question of whether this closure package reflects good faith toward the workforce generally: I understand skepticism is fair given that this is our second store consolidation in three years. I do not have a way to prove good faith in a letter, but I can tell you what we are doing that goes beyond what the contract strictly requires — the eighteen-month recall window is six months longer than the contract minimum, the severance schedule Ms. Nwachukwu outlined caps at sixteen weeks rather than the twelve-week contract ceiling, and we have committed to first recall rights at the rebuilt stores in writing rather than leaving that to informal practice. None of that resolves the notice-period dispute, and I am not offering it as a substitute for a real answer on Article 14. I am offering it because I think it is relevant context for how we intend to handle the rest of this transition, including the reopener in January.

I will plan to be in Greenfield the week of August 3 and can meet at the union hall or at our offices, whichever is more convenient for your members who want to attend. Please let me know by August 5 on the Pittsfield and Brattleboro date proposal, and separately let me know if you want to schedule the in-person meeting before or after that date.

Sincerely,

Deshawn Whitfield
President, Larkspur Markets
