# Tule Bend Unified School District
## Board of Trustees Agenda Item — April 8 Meeting
## Proposal to Terminate Contracted Transportation Services and Establish a District-Operated Transportation Department

**Prepared for:** Board President Leilani Kahananui and Members of the Board of Trustees
**Prepared by:** Guadalupe Serrato, Superintendent; Nathan Okonkwo, Chief Business Official
**Date:** April 8

---

## Executive Summary

Tule Bend Unified School District transports approximately 6,400 of its 9,760 students daily across fourteen school sites in a service area that spans roughly 210 square miles of rural and semi-rural Tulare County. For the past six years that service has been delivered under contract by First Route Student Transportation. That contract has degraded to a point that the Superintendent and Chief Business Official can no longer recommend renewal. Last year First Route missed 1,140 scheduled runs and, in January alone, logged 22 arrivals more than 45 minutes late. These failures land hardest on the 78 percent of our students who are socioeconomically disadvantaged and the 31 percent who are English learners — students for whom a missed bus is often not an inconvenience but the difference between attending school and not. Districtwide chronic absenteeism stands at 34.1 percent, and site administrators, attendance staff, and the Multi-Tiered System of Supports team have identified unreliable transportation as a material, documentable contributor.

The district faces a decision point independent of this history: absent Board action by May 15, the current contract's evergreen clause triggers automatically, extending First Route for one additional year at a 14 percent rate increase, raising the annual contract cost from $7.6 million to approximately $8.66 million with no corresponding performance guarantees or service remedies beyond what already exists and has already failed to produce results.

Staff recommend the Board terminate the First Route contract effective at the close of the current extension period, or upon the negotiated transition date, and direct staff to establish a district-operated Transportation Department under a newly created Director of Operations position. The proposed department would acquire 46 buses, lease and retrofit a corporation yard, and hire 58 drivers and six mechanics, built out over an eighteen-month period beginning this summer, with 22 of the district's routes converting to district operation in August and the remaining routes converting the following January.

The capital cost of stand-up is $14.2 million. Ongoing annual operating cost is projected at $6.9 million, a $700,000 annual reduction from the current $7.6 million contract cost and a $1.76 million annual reduction from the $8.66 million the district would pay under the automatic extension. Capital funding is proposed from three sources: $9.1 million from capital facilities funds, $3.4 million from a state clean school bus allocation for which the district has been notified of eligibility but has not yet received a formal award letter, and $1.7 million from unrestricted general fund reserve. The reserve draw would reduce the district's unrestricted reserve from its current level to 4.2 percent of general fund expenditures, above the 3 percent state minimum reserve requirement but below the 5 percent level the Board has historically targeted as a working buffer.

This proposal is not without risk, and staff address those risks directly below, including the contingency on state funding, the tight recruitment timeline for 58 CDL-holding drivers in a competitive regional labor market, and the reserve impact. Staff also address, and do not recommend, two alternatives: rebidding the contract to another vendor, and pursuing a joint powers transportation arrangement with a neighboring district.

The specific action requested of the Board on April 8 is set out in the final section of this document.

## 1. Service Record and Effects on Attendance

### 1.1 Contract Performance

First Route Student Transportation has held the district's transportation contract for six years. Performance has declined steadily over the last three, coinciding with driver turnover at First Route that district staff estimate, based on route substitutions logged by site staff, at over 60 percent annually at the local depot level.

In the last complete school year, First Route:

- Missed 1,140 scheduled runs districtwide, an average of more than six missed runs per school day across the district's route network.
- Logged 22 arrivals of more than 45 minutes late in January alone, a single month in which a cold-weather mechanical failure at First Route's regional depot took four buses out of service for over two weeks.
- Failed to meet the contract's 95 percent on-time performance standard in nine of twelve months, according to First Route's own monthly reporting, which district staff have independently spot-checked against school-site sign-in logs and found to understate the frequency of lateness.
- Substituted drivers unfamiliar with routes, stops, and student needs — including students with IEPs specifying particular boarding assistance — on a frequency that site special education staff describe as routine rather than exceptional.

District staff have raised these failures with First Route management on a documented, recurring basis, including a formal notice of contract deficiency issued in October and a follow-up corrective action meeting in December. First Route's response has been to cite regional driver shortages affecting the entire transportation contracting industry in the Central Valley, a shortage staff do not dispute but which does not relieve the district of its obligation to get students to school.

### 1.2 Attendance and Instructional Effects

Districtwide chronic absenteeism — students missing 10 percent or more of enrolled school days — stands at 34.1 percent, well above the statewide average. While transportation failures are not the sole driver of chronic absenteeism, site principals and the district's attendance review team report a consistent pattern: schools with the highest proportion of bus-dependent students show correlated spikes in both tardiness and full-day absence on days and weeks when First Route service disruptions were logged. Elementary sites in the district's most rural attendance areas — where a missed morning run leaves no practical alternative for many working families — report the sharpest correlation.

For the 78 percent of students who are socioeconomically disadvantaged, many in households without a second vehicle or with parents working shifts that do not allow for ad hoc school drop-off, an unreliable bus is frequently determinative of whether a student attends school that day. For the 31 percent of students who are English learners, many of whom are also transported to programs at sites other than their neighborhood school, route unreliability compounds effects on both attendance and instructional access, since late arrivals routinely mean students miss the first period of core instruction or targeted language support blocks that are not repeated later in the day.

Staff do not present district-operated transportation as a single solution to chronic absenteeism, which has multiple causes documented elsewhere in the district's attendance improvement plan. Staff do present reliable transportation as a necessary, though not sufficient, condition for attendance recovery, and note that the current vendor relationship has not delivered that reliability despite six years of contract administration, escalating corrective notices, and, this year, a rate increase already built into the base contract.

## 2. Proposed Department: Structure and Staffing

### 2.1 Organizational Placement

Staff propose establishing a Transportation Department reporting through the Chief Business Official's office, consistent with the district's existing organizational structure for Facilities and Maintenance & Operations. The department would be led by a newly created Director of Operations position. Superintendent Serrato has identified Wesley Frayne, currently the district's Assistant Director of Maintenance & Operations with prior experience standing up a transportation department at a comparably sized district in Kern County, as the recommended appointee to this role, subject to Board approval of the position and separate action on the appointment.

### 2.2 Staffing Plan

The department's staffing plan, to be built out over eighteen months, consists of:

- **1 Director of Operations** — overall department leadership, vendor and parts procurement, safety compliance, and reporting to the CBO.
- **1 Operations Supervisor** — day-to-day dispatch, route assignment, and driver scheduling, hired in the department's first six months.
- **1 Safety and Training Coordinator** — responsible for CDL training pipeline, defensive driving certification, and state and federal compliance recordkeeping, hired concurrent with the Operations Supervisor.
- **58 Bus Drivers** — hired in two cohorts corresponding to the August and January route conversions (see Section 4).
- **6 Mechanics** — hired in a single cohort ahead of the August conversion, to establish preventive maintenance capacity before the fleet is in daily service districtwide.
- **2 Dispatch/Clerical staff** — supporting route sheets, parent communication, and daily attendance-linked reporting to school sites.

Total department headcount at full build-out is 69 positions. All driver and mechanic positions are proposed as CSEA-represented bargaining unit positions, consistent with the district's existing classified employee structure, and staff have engaged in informal discussion with CSEA chapter leadership regarding wage placement (addressed in Section 4.3).

### 2.3 Facilities

The department requires a corporation yard sufficient to store, fuel, and maintain 46 buses, with covered bay space for the maintenance operation. Staff have identified a candidate site — a vacant former agricultural equipment yard approximately 2.3 miles from the district office — currently available on a five-year lease with options, requiring retrofit for fuel storage, bus washing, covered maintenance bays, and driver break facilities. Lease and retrofit costs are included in the capital budget in Section 3.

### 2.4 Fleet

The proposed fleet of 46 buses is sized to the district's current route structure of 37 routes plus spares for maintenance rotation and growth capacity, and includes a mix of standard and lift-equipped vehicles matched to the district's special education transportation caseload. Staff have applied for inclusion in the state's clean school bus allocation program specifically to fund a portion of this fleet with zero-emission or near-zero-emission vehicles; the funding contingency associated with that application is addressed in Section 3.2 and Section 6.

### 3.4 Reserve Impact

The proposed $1.7 million reserve draw reduces the district's unrestricted general fund reserve from its current balance to a projected 4.2 percent of general fund expenditures. This remains above the state-required minimum reserve of 3 percent for districts of Tule Bend's size, but below the 5 percent level the Board has, in past budget study sessions, identified as its informal working target for fiscal stability given the district's declining enrollment trend.

Staff want to be direct with the Board about this tradeoff. Board President Kahananui and two trustees have previously and publicly expressed reluctance to draw down reserves for capital projects, a position staff take seriously given the district's enrollment decline of 11 percent over five years and the associated pressure on future revenue. Staff's judgment is that the annual operating savings of $700,000 against the current contract, and $1.76 million against the automatic extension the district faces without action, will restore reserve capacity within approximately two and a half years even without accounting for the avoided cost of continued service failures — missed instructional time, attendance recovery staffing, and the administrative cost of the corrective-action cycle with First Route that has consumed senior staff time for the past two years. Staff nonetheless present the rebid and joint-arrangement alternatives in Section 5 in part because they draw less, or not at all, on reserve funds, and ask the Board to weigh that difference explicitly.

## 4. Schedule and Driver Recruitment

### 4.1 Implementation Timeline

| Milestone | Timing |
|---|---|
| Board action authorizing contract termination and department stand-up | April 8 |
| Notice of non-renewal delivered to First Route | Within 5 business days of Board action |
| Director of Operations appointment finalized | May |
| Corporation yard lease executed; retrofit begins | May–July |
| First driver cohort recruitment opens | May |
| Mechanic cohort hired; preventive maintenance shop operational | July |
| First bus deliveries (Phase 1 fleet) | June–July |
| **Phase 1: 22 routes convert to district operation** | **August** (start of school year) |
| Driver cohort 2 recruitment opens | September |
| Remaining fleet deliveries (Phase 2) | October–December |
| **Phase 2: remaining routes convert to district operation** | **January** |
| Full department operational review | Following spring |

This phased approach is deliberate. Converting 22 routes in August allows the department to build operational experience, refine dispatch and maintenance workflows, and identify problems at a scale the Director of Operations and initial supervisory staff can manage directly, before taking on the full route network in January. It also spreads driver recruitment across two hiring cycles rather than requiring the district to source and train 58 drivers simultaneously against a single start date, which staff and CSEA both regard as unrealistic given current regional labor conditions.

### 4.2 Driver Recruitment Plan

Recruitment of 58 CDL-holding drivers is the single largest execution risk in this proposal, and staff treat it as such. The plan includes:

- A district-sponsored CDL training partnership with the regional community college's transportation training program, allowing the district to recruit candidates without an existing CDL and train them into the role, rather than competing solely for already-licensed drivers.
- Referral incentives for current classified staff and community members, administered through the district's HR office beginning in May.
- A hiring target of 30 drivers for the August cohort (staffing 22 routes plus spares and training backfill) and 28 for the January cohort.
- Background check, physical, and drug-testing processing built into the timeline with a minimum six-week lead time per candidate, based on current county-level processing times.

### 4.3 Wage Rate and CSEA Position

The proposed driver wage of $24.10 per hour sits $2.65 below the county average of $26.75 identified by CSEA chapter president Dolores Ybarra in her review of comparable districts' current driver pay scales. Staff do not dispute Ms. Ybarra's figure. The $24.10 rate was built into the operating budget in Section 3.3 as a starting placement, consistent with the district's existing classified salary schedule progression for comparable driving classifications, and is subject to negotiation with CSEA as part of establishing the new job classifications this proposal creates.

Staff acknowledge directly that recruiting 58 drivers in a competitive labor market at a below-average wage is a material risk to the recruitment timeline in Section 4.1, and that this issue must be resolved, not merely noted, before the August conversion date. Staff recommend the Board authorize, as part of the April 8 action, a supplemental negotiation with CSEA regarding driver wage placement, with any resulting increase to be absorbed within the $6.9 million operating budget's existing contingency margin where possible, and returned to the Board for consideration if it is not. This is addressed further in Section 6.

## 5. Alternatives Considered

Staff evaluated two alternatives to district operation and do not recommend either as the Board's April 8 action, for the reasons below. The Board may direct staff to pursue either in place of, or as a contingency to, the recommended action.

### 5.1 Rebid the Contract to Another Vendor

Staff solicited informal interest from three regional student transportation contractors in advance of preparing this proposal. Two indicated they lacked capacity to take on a district of Tule Bend's route scale within the district's timeline; one indicated interest but at a preliminary quoted rate of approximately $8.1 million annually, above the current contract and only modestly below the automatic extension rate, reflecting the same regional driver shortage that has degraded First Route's performance. A formal RFP process would take four to six months to complete and would not resolve before the May 15 extension deadline, meaning the district would incur at least one year under the extended First Route contract, or an equivalent short-term arrangement, regardless of a rebid's eventual outcome. Staff's judgment is that rebidding transfers the district's reliability risk to a different vendor operating in the same constrained labor market, without a demonstrated cost advantage over district operation and without a clear path to avoiding the May 15 deadline.

### 5.2 Joint Transportation Arrangement with a Neighboring District

Staff held two exploratory conversations with the neighboring Rocha Valley Unified School District regarding a joint powers transportation arrangement, under which the two districts would share a corporation yard, maintenance staff, and dispatch infrastructure while operating separate route networks. Rocha Valley staff expressed conditional interest but noted their own board has not taken up the question and would require a minimum of two to three months of internal review before any joint powers authority could be formed, again exceeding the May 15 window for this year's decision. A joint arrangement could offer meaningful capital cost-sharing on facility and maintenance infrastructure in a future year and staff recommend it remain under active exploration as a longer-term option, potentially informing the district's second-phase facility decisions, but it is not available as a solution to the immediate May 15 deadline and should not be treated as an alternative to the current proposal so much as a possible refinement to pursue in parallel.

### 5.3 Do Nothing (Accept the Automatic Extension)

Staff include this for completeness though it is not a genuine alternative given the service record documented in Section 1. Accepting the automatic one-year extension commits the district to $8.66 million in transportation cost with no new performance remedies, while deferring — not avoiding — the underlying decision to a point one year from now when the district will have spent an additional $1 million above current contract cost and lost a year of lead time on facility and recruitment work that the district-operation timeline in Section 4 requires regardless of when it begins.

## 6. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Clean school bus allocation award letter does not arrive, or arrives at a reduced amount | Staff will bring a fallback funding plan to the Board within 60 days of a negative or reduced determination, drawing on capital facilities fund capacity or phasing bus purchases to match confirmed funding; no bus purchase commitments will be finalized against the $3.4 million allocation until the award letter is received. |
| Driver recruitment fails to reach 58 hires on the Section 4.1 timeline | Phased route conversion (22 routes in August, remainder in January) is structured specifically to allow a recruitment shortfall in the first cohort to be absorbed by delaying the Phase 2 conversion date rather than by understaffing routes; CDL training partnership with the community college is intended to widen the applicant pool beyond already-licensed drivers. |
| CSEA wage negotiation is not resolved before August, affecting recruitment | Staff recommend Board authorization on April 8 to begin wage negotiation with CSEA immediately, in parallel with recruitment, rather than sequencing it after the department is otherwise built; staff will return to the Board if a resolved wage rate exceeds the operating budget's contingency capacity. |
| Reserve falls to 4.2%, reducing the district's fiscal cushion against further enrollment decline | Annual operating savings of $700,000 to $1.76 million (Section 3.3) are projected to rebuild reserve capacity over approximately two and a half years; staff will report reserve status at each quarterly budget update following implementation. |
| Corporation yard retrofit is delayed, affecting fleet delivery or maintenance readiness | Lease agreement includes a retrofit completion deadline with penalty provisions; staff have identified a secondary temporary parking and fueling arrangement at the district's existing maintenance yard as a bridge if retrofit is delayed past the August conversion date. |
| First department year experiences its own reliability problems during the buildout, similar in kind to current issues | Phased conversion limits first-year exposure to 22 of 37 routes, allowing the Director of Operations to identify and correct operational problems at reduced scale before Phase 2; the department reports monthly on-time performance to the Board beginning with the August conversion. |
| Fuel and parts cost volatility affects the $6.9 million operating projection | Operating budget contingency margin and mid-year budget review process, consistent with the district's existing budget monitoring practice, will surface variance early; staff do not propose this as a fixed-price guarantee but as a budgeted projection subject to normal monitoring. |

## Appendix D: Parent and Community Communication Plan

Recognizing that any transportation transition creates uncertainty for families, particularly those with students in special education requiring consistent boarding support, staff propose the following communication timeline, to begin immediately following Board action on April 8:

- **April:** District-wide notification letter, in English and Spanish, to all families explaining the transition, the reason for it, and the phased timeline, distributed through the district's existing parent notification system and posted at all fourteen sites.
- **May:** Site-level meetings at each of the nine Phase 1 elementary schools to review specific route and stop information as it becomes available, with special education families invited to a separate meeting with the Director of Operations designee to address individual transportation needs.
- **July:** Confirmed Phase 1 route and stop assignments distributed to families at Phase 1 sites, minimum three weeks before the start of school.
- **November:** Phase 2 site-level meetings at the five secondary and remaining elementary sites, following the same model as the May meetings.
- **December:** Confirmed Phase 2 route and stop assignments distributed, minimum three weeks before the January conversion date.

This communication plan is designed to give families at each phase a minimum of three weeks' notice of their specific route assignment, consistent with the district's existing practice for annual route adjustments, and to ensure families with special education transportation needs have a direct point of contact with the Director of Operations designee rather than routing those concerns through general district office channels during the transition period.

## Appendix E: Insurance, Liability, and Risk Transfer

Transitioning from a contracted to a district-operated model shifts liability exposure for vehicle accidents, student injury during transport, and workers' compensation claims from First Route's insurer to the district's own coverage. Staff have reviewed this shift with the district's joint powers insurance authority and confirmed the following:

- The district's existing property and liability coverage pool can absorb the additional fleet and driver exposure with an estimated premium increase of $180,000 annually, already included in the $6.9 million operating cost figure in Section 3.3.
- Workers' compensation experience modification for the new driver and mechanic classifications will be rated initially at the pool's standard new-district-operation rate, with adjustment after the first full claims year; staff have budgeted conservatively at the higher initial rate rather than assuming an early favorable adjustment.
- The district will carry excess liability coverage consistent with the levels currently required of First Route under the existing contract, so that the district's liability exposure per incident does not increase relative to the status quo, only the party bearing first-dollar responsibility changes.
- First Route's insurer will remain responsible for any claims arising from incidents occurring before the transition date of each route's conversion; the district's coverage attaches only upon each route's actual conversion to district operation, consistent with the phased schedule in Section 4.1, so there is no gap or overlap in coverage responsibility.

Staff do not consider insurance and liability transfer a material risk to this proposal, but include this detail because trustees have asked, in prior discussions of contracted-versus-in-house service models for other district functions, how liability exposure is handled during a phased transition.

## Appendix F: Safety and Regulatory Compliance Program

A district-operated transportation department assumes direct responsibility for compliance obligations currently managed by First Route, including California Highway Patrol terminal inspections, driver medical certification and drug and alcohol testing consortium participation, and California Department of Education pupil transportation safety requirements. The Safety and Training Coordinator position described in Section 2.2 is structured specifically to carry this compliance function, with the following elements built into the department's first-year operating plan:

- Enrollment in a federally compliant drug and alcohol testing consortium prior to the first driver cohort's start date, a prerequisite to operating any route.
- Scheduling of the district's first CHP terminal inspection to occur before the August conversion date, so that any corrective findings can be addressed before routes go live rather than after.
- A defensive driving and student management training curriculum for all drivers, delivered in partnership with the community college training program referenced in Section 4.2, with a minimum 40-hour pre-service training requirement before any driver is assigned an unsupervised route.
- Monthly safety committee meetings, chaired by the Safety and Training Coordinator and including CSEA representation, beginning with the first driver cohort's hire date.

Staff note that First Route's own compliance record, while not the primary basis for this proposal, has itself been inconsistent: the district's contract file includes two CHP inspection deficiency notices issued to First Route's regional depot in the past eighteen months, both eventually corrected but neither surfaced to the district proactively by the vendor. A district-operated department places compliance responsibility, and compliance visibility, directly within district administration rather than dependent on a vendor's self-reporting.

## Appendix H: Glossary of Terms

**Chronic absenteeism** — the percentage of enrolled students who miss 10 percent or more of the school days for which they are enrolled, whether excused or unexcused.

**Clean school bus allocation** — a state grant program providing funding toward the purchase of zero-emission or near-zero-emission school buses and associated infrastructure.

**Evergreen clause** — a contract provision that automatically renews or extends an agreement unless one party affirmatively acts to terminate it before a specified deadline.

**Joint powers authority (JPA)** — a legal entity formed by two or more public agencies to jointly exercise powers common to each, used here to describe a potential shared transportation arrangement with a neighboring district.

**On-time performance** — the percentage of scheduled bus runs arriving at their destination within the contractually defined window, typically measured against a threshold such as 45 minutes.

**Unrestricted general fund reserve** — the portion of a district's general fund not designated for a specific restricted purpose, available for general operating use and subject to a state-mandated minimum percentage of expenditures.
