# REVIEW ONE

**Employee Name:** Lourdes Manzanares
**Title:** Utility Billing Supervisor
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Reginald Ashcroft, Deputy Director of Utilities
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

---

## I. Goals Set for the Review Period

At the start of this review period, three goals were established jointly with Ms. Manzanares: (1) stabilize the new customer information and billing system following its November conversion, (2) reduce call center abandonment rate below 20 percent, and (3) maintain billing accuracy and staffing coverage in the billing office despite two unfilled clerk vacancies carried over from the hiring freeze.

## II. Narrative Assessment Against Goals

The billing system conversion did not go smoothly. The first billing cycle after go-live misapplied rate codes and meter-read intervals to approximately 4,100 of the utility's 34,000 accounts, generating a wave of erroneous bills, refund requests, and council inquiries in September. Ms. Manzanares was not responsible for the vendor's data migration errors, which originated before the conversion in the legacy system export. However, she owns the recovery, and the recovery has been the defining achievement of her year.

Working with the vendor's implementation team, she built a manual reconciliation process, prioritized accounts by dollar exposure and hardship risk, and cleared the misbilled population to a residual error rate below 0.3 percent by January, four months ahead of the vendor's own remediation timeline. She personally briefed the council utilities subcommittee twice on remediation status, and I received no complaints about her conduct or communication in either session, only about the underlying system failure, which predates her authority.

Call center performance improved substantially. Abandonment rate fell from 31 percent at the start of the period to 9 percent by June, well past the 20 percent target, achieved while the office operated two clerk positions short of authorized strength because those vacancies were frozen and not backfilled under the citywide hiring freeze, now in its fourteenth month. Ms. Manzanares absorbed the workload of both vacant clerk positions into her own schedule and that of the two remaining billing clerks, restructuring queue coverage and cross-training staff on collections and payment plan functions that had previously been siloed.

**Self-assessment (Ms. Manzanares, submitted July 18, 2026):** *"I want to be evaluated on what the office delivered, not just on the fire we put out. We turned a system failure into the most accurate billing cycle this office has run in five years, and we did it two people short the whole time. I'm proud of that. I also want it on the record that I asked for the reclassification study to be completed on its own timeline, not tied to the budget calendar, because the work already changed. I'm doing manager-level work with a supervisor title and supervisor pay, and I've been doing it since November."*

I find her self-assessment accurate. The scope of her role has functionally expanded to include vendor management, subcommittee-level reporting, and cross-departmental coordination with IT and Finance on the billing conversion — none of which were part of the billing supervisor position when it was last classified.

## III. Compliance History and Specific Incidents

No compliance violations attach to the billing function. The September misbilling event was reported transparently to the council utilities subcommittee and to affected customers with a same-cycle credit-and-correction process; the state does not regulate municipal billing accuracy, so this was not a regulatory matter, but it is documented here because of its material effect on the year's workload and public perception of the utility.

## IV. Feedback

**Council:** Two council members who sit on the utilities subcommittee commented favorably on Ms. Manzanares's September and December briefings, describing her as "the only person in that building who gave us a straight answer" during the billing crisis. No adverse council feedback was received.

**Coworkers:** Both remaining billing clerks describe her as demanding but fair, and credit her with shielding the front-line staff from the worst of the September call volume by taking escalated calls herself. The IT project lead assigned to the conversion praised her account-level troubleshooting as "better than what the vendor gave us."

**Public:** Complaints logged against the billing office dropped from 214 in September to 11 in June. Several customers who escalated hardship cases to my office specifically named Ms. Manzanares as having resolved their accounts personally and promptly.

## V. Overall Rating

**Rating: Exceeds Expectations**

This is the highest rating available on the four-tier civil service form (Does Not Meet / Meets / Exceeds / Outstanding is not used in this system; the applicable scale tops at Exceeds Expectations absent a director-level Outstanding designation, which I am not authorized to award at the deputy director level and am therefore forwarding to the Director with a recommendation that it be considered).

## VI. Application of Pay Structure and Hiring Freeze

Ms. Manzanares is eligible for the 2 percent across-the-board increase provided to all civil service employees under the current pay plan, effective with the next pay cycle following council ratification. Based on this rating, I am also recommending her for an award from the 1 percent performance pool, the maximum discretionary amount available under the current plan for a supervisor-level position, subject to Human Resources' pool allocation across the department.

Her application for reclassification from Billing Supervisor to Billing Manager, filed in March, was tabled by council pending the classification and compensation study now scheduled for the 2027 budget cycle. I want the record to reflect that this delay is a council budgeting decision, not a reflection on her performance or my assessment of the duties she is currently performing. I have recommended to the Director that her file be flagged for priority review when the classification study resumes, and that interim recognition be considered through the performance pool in the meantime, given that the pool is the only compensation lever presently available outside the frozen classification process.

The clerk vacancies she has absorbed remain frozen. I have submitted a freeze exception request for at least one of the two positions, citing the sustained overtime-equivalent burden on her office; that request is pending with the City Manager's office as of this writing.

## VII. Goals for the Coming Year

1. Complete full system stabilization, including automated reconciliation reporting, by the end of the first quarter, and document standard operating procedures for the new platform so recovery from any future vendor errors does not depend solely on institutional memory.
2. Maintain call abandonment below 12 percent through the coming year, with a stretch target of 8 percent contingent on at least one clerk vacancy being unfrozen.
3. Partner with Human Resources on the 2027 classification study, including preparing a documented duties inventory to support the Billing Manager reclassification request.
4. Cross-train a second staff member to the point of backup capability on vendor escalation and subcommittee reporting, to reduce single-point dependency on the supervisor role.

# REVIEW TWO

**Employee Name:** Gustavo Delgado-Marin
**Title:** Plant Operations Lead
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Reginald Ashcroft, Deputy Director of Utilities
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

---

## I. Goals Set for the Review Period

Goals for this period were: (1) maintain full compliance with all discharge permit limits, (2) run the apprentice operator rotation to advance at least one operator toward Class C or B licensure, (3) manage overtime within budget guidance despite frozen operator positions, and (4) support the environmental compliance analyst function in reestablishing reliable state reporting following the August notice of violation.

## II. Narrative Assessment Against Goals

On the technical core of the job, Mr. Delgado-Marin's year was excellent. Every discharge permit parameter — ammonia, biochemical oxygen demand, total suspended solids, fecal coliform, and dissolved oxygen — was met in every reporting period, with no exceedances and no permit violations of any kind attributable to plant operations. As the utility's only Class A licensed operator, he carries legal responsibility for process control decisions that no one else on staff is currently certified to make, and the plant's compliance record this year, apart from the reporting lapse addressed in Ms. Achterberg's review, is a direct reflection of his competence.

The apprentice rotation is where this review must be direct. Mr. Delgado-Marin declined, for the third consecutive year, to run the formal apprentice rotation that would allow the two operators currently awaiting license sign-off to accumulate the supervised hours and documented competencies needed to sit for their Class C exams. He has cited workload as the reason in each of the past two cycles, and workload is real: the plant logged 480 hours of overtime in the third quarter alone, largely because two operator positions have sat frozen since the hiring freeze began fourteen months ago, and he has been covering shift gaps personally rather than leave the plant under-licensed at night. That is a legitimate operational tension, and I do not discount it.

But the effect of his refusal is that the plant remains a single point of failure. If Mr. Delgado-Marin is unavailable for any extended period — illness, injury, family emergency — the utility has no second Class A or advancing Class B operator to step into his role, and that is a compliance risk the council and the state would rightly view as unacceptable. Two operators have now filed formal grievances over the lack of sign-off progress, and both grievances name the absence of the apprentice rotation as their basis. I have reviewed both grievances; they are procedurally valid, and I expect at least one to proceed to a civil service hearing if the underlying issue is not resolved this year.

**Self-assessment (Mr. Delgado-Marin, submitted July 21, 2026):** *"I run one plant with one Class A operator, and that operator is me. Every hour I spend on paperwork for the apprentice program is an hour I'm not on the process. I said no to the rotation because I don't trust that if I train someone up and they leave for a better-paying job in Amarillo, this city will backfill the position — they haven't backfilled anything else in fourteen months. I met every permit limit this year with the staff I actually have. I'd rather be graded on that than on a training program I don't believe the city is committed to supporting."*

I take his self-assessment seriously, and it identifies a real structural problem: the freeze creates a disincentive to invest in training operators who may leave, because there is no assurance the vacancy would be filled with a replacement rather than absorbed. That said, his position carries formal responsibility for operator development as a core job function, not a discretionary add-on, and three consecutive years of declining it is a performance shortfall regardless of the reasons behind it. Both things are true at once, and this review reflects both.

On the fourth goal, supporting Ms. Achterberg's reporting function, his cooperation was good. He provided her sampling data promptly and correctly identified the laboratory identifier error in the March report before it reached the state a second time, which helped limit the scope of the corrective filing.

## III. Compliance History and Specific Incidents

The plant achieved full discharge permit compliance for the review period, with zero exceedances. The August notice of violation from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality concerned missed monthly disinfectant residual reports, not effluent quality, and it predates Ms. Achterberg's hiring; it is addressed fully in her review, but I note here that Mr. Delgado-Marin's own residual monitoring at the plant level was never in question — the violation was a reporting failure, not a process failure, and his log data was consistently complete and available when the corrected filings were prepared.

The two pending grievances (Operator M. Villareal, filed April 2026; Operator T. Okonkwo-Reyes, filed May 2026) are the significant compliance-adjacent matters of the year from a labor relations standpoint and are noted for the record as unresolved as of this review date.

## IV. Feedback

**Council:** No council member has raised Mr. Delgado-Marin's name directly in a public session this year, which in a utility operations context I read as a positive sign; plant operations that stay off the council agenda are, in this business, plant operations that are working.

**Coworkers:** Feedback is sharply divided. Junior operators describe him as an excellent technical mentor when he is engaged one-on-one, but both grievants specifically state in their filings that they feel "blocked" and "stuck" by the absence of a formal path to advancement. The two other senior staff who work with him describe him as reliable and calm under pressure, particularly during a June chlorine feed equipment failure that he resolved without any permit impact.

**Public:** No direct public feedback specific to Mr. Delgado-Marin was received; plant operations are not customer-facing in the way billing is.

## V. Overall Rating

**Rating: Meets Expectations**

I want to be explicit about why this is not "Exceeds," given the strength of the compliance record. The apprentice rotation and licensing sign-off are formal, written job responsibilities of the Plant Operations Lead position, and three years of non-performance on a core responsibility caps the overall rating regardless of technical excellence elsewhere. This is a "Meets" with an unusually strong technical component and an unresolved structural deficiency, not a middling year across the board.

## VI. Application of Pay Structure and Hiring Freeze

Mr. Delgado-Marin is eligible for the 2 percent across-the-board increase. I am not recommending an award from the 1 percent performance pool this cycle, specifically because of the unresolved apprentice rotation issue and the two active grievances tied to it; I would not be able to defend a discretionary award to council or in a subsequent grievance hearing while that issue is open. I have told him this directly and in writing, separate from this document, so that it does not arrive as a surprise.

The two frozen operator positions referenced throughout this review are the same vacancies driving both the overtime totals and the grievance filings. I have submitted a freeze exception request for these positions on public health and safety grounds, given that they are licensed operator roles directly tied to a facility already under one open state notice of violation; that request is pending. I have also recommended that any exception granted be paired with a binding requirement that the apprentice rotation run as a condition of backfilling the positions, so the structural incentive problem he identified in his self-assessment is addressed at the same time as the staffing problem.

## VII. Goals for the Coming Year

1. Run the apprentice rotation for at least one full cycle with both Villareal and Okonkwo-Reyes, with documented hours and sign-off milestones tracked monthly, regardless of the freeze exception outcome.
2. Reduce quarterly overtime to no more than 300 hours, contingent on freeze exception approval; if the exception is denied, we will jointly revisit this target and I will escalate the staffing risk to the Director and council directly.
3. Maintain full discharge permit compliance.
4. Resolve the pending grievances through direct engagement with the affected operators and HR before either proceeds to a civil service hearing.

# REVIEW THREE

**Employee Name:** Simone Achterberg
**Title:** Environmental Compliance Analyst
**Review Period:** February 9, 2026 – July 31, 2026 (initial partial-year review; hired mid-cycle)
**Reviewer:** Reginald Ashcroft, Deputy Director of Utilities
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

---

## I. Goals Set for the Review Period

Ms. Achterberg was hired in February specifically in response to the August 2025 notice of violation from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality for missed monthly disinfectant residual reports. Her goals, set at hire, were: (1) clear the backlog of unfiled and incomplete sampling records identified in the notice of violation, (2) build a reporting calendar and tracking system to prevent recurrence, and (3) establish the utility's ongoing compliance posture with the state as stable and current.

## II. Narrative Assessment Against Goals

Ms. Achterberg's first ninety days were, without qualification, strong. She inherited fourteen months of backlogged sampling records — the direct legacy of the gap that produced the notice of violation — and cleared the entire backlog by early May, reconstructing missing residual data from plant logs, cross-referencing lab results, and filing the corrected historical reports the state required as a condition of resolving the notice of violation. This is exactly the outcome the position was created to produce, and she produced it faster than I expected from someone new to both the utility and, by her own account, new to TCEQ-specific reporting formats.

She also built a reporting calendar that did not exist before her arrival: a shared, dated tracking system that flags every monthly, quarterly, and annual submission deadline across the plant's permit obligations, with automated reminders to both herself and Mr. Delgado-Marin. This tool is, in my judgment, the most durable contribution of her tenure so far, because it addresses the systemic gap — the lack of any tracking mechanism — that allowed fourteen months of missed reports to accumulate before anyone caught it.

Against that record, the March incident has to be addressed plainly. A monthly disinfectant residual report submitted to the state in March listed an incorrect laboratory identification number, associating the plant's sample results with the wrong certified laboratory. This required a corrected filing, which Ms. Achterberg identified and initiated herself after cross-checking the submission against Mr. Delgado-Marin's plant logs, and which was corrected within the state's allowed window without further enforcement action or a second notice of violation. I want to be precise about the weight of this: it is a real error, on a report tied to the exact category of failure that triggered her hiring, and it is fair that it tempers an otherwise outstanding first review period. It is also true that she caught and corrected it herself before it became a compliance escalation, which is a materially different outcome than the original fourteen-month gap she was hired to fix.

**Self-assessment (Ms. Achterberg, submitted July 22, 2026):** *"I'm not going to pretend the March error didn't happen or that it doesn't matter — it does, and I built a second-check step into the calendar system specifically because of it, so every submission now gets reviewed against source lab documentation before it goes out, not after. I'd ask that this review weigh the fact that I found my own mistake before the state did, against a backdrop where the previous fourteen months of reports weren't checked by anyone at all. I came in and cleared four-teen months of backlog in ninety days while learning the job. I think that's the headline of my first six months, not the footnote."*

I agree that this is a fair framing, and I have weighted the review accordingly. A single self-caught, self-corrected error in an otherwise complete and timely reporting record over a five-and-a-half-month period, arriving from a new hire brought in specifically to fix a much larger systemic failure, does not offset the scale of what she accomplished. It does mean I am not rating this period at the top of the scale, because the position exists precisely to prevent errors in this exact report type, and one occurred.

## III. Compliance History and Specific Incidents

The utility's standing with TCEQ has materially improved during this period. The original notice of violation, issued August 2025 for missed monthly disinfectant residual reports over the prior fourteen months, was substantively resolved through the backlog filing Ms. Achterberg completed by May 2026; I have not yet received formal written closure from the state's regional office, which remains outstanding and which I am tracking separately. The March laboratory identifier error generated a single corrected filing and no additional enforcement action, penalty, or second notice of violation. No other compliance incidents occurred during this period.

## IV. Feedback

**Council:** One council member specifically asked at the June utilities subcommittee meeting whether the notice of violation had been resolved and was told, accurately, that the backlog was cleared and formal closure was pending; the member described this as "the kind of update I actually want to hear" relative to prior briefings on the same subject.

**Coworkers:** Mr. Delgado-Marin's feedback on her, noted in his own review, is positive; he describes her as responsive and describes the reporting calendar as something that "should have existed years ago." Billing and administrative staff who interact with her peripherally describe her as organized and easy to work with.

**Public:** No direct public feedback has been received regarding Ms. Achterberg; her role is not customer-facing, and environmental compliance reporting of this kind does not typically generate public comment absent an active violation, which is itself an indirect positive signal.

## V. Overall Rating

**Rating: Meets Expectations, with note of exceptional early performance**

Because this is a partial-year, first review following a February hire, the civil service form calls for a standard rating without eligibility for the "Exceeds" distinction, which under current policy requires a full twelve-month review period; I am flagging this limitation for the record because, on substance, her work through May would have supported a higher rating had the full-year eligibility rule not applied, and the March correction is the reason I would not have pushed for the top rating even if it had been available.

## VI. Application of Pay Structure and Hiring Freeze

Ms. Achterberg's position was created and filled as a direct response to the notice of violation and was therefore treated as an exception to the hiring freeze at the time of her recruitment; no freeze issue applies to her position going forward, and it is fully funded and authorized.

As a new hire with less than one full year of service at the time of this review, she is not yet eligible for consideration for the 1 percent performance pool under current Human Resources policy, which restricts pool eligibility to employees who have completed a full annual review cycle; she will become eligible at her next review, covering August 2026 through July 2027. She is eligible for the 2 percent across-the-board increase, which under the pay plan is not service-length restricted and applies to all current civil service employees regardless of hire date within the fiscal year, prorated per HR's standard new-hire calculation.

## VII. Goals for the Coming Year

1. Obtain formal written closure of the August 2025 notice of violation from TCEQ's regional office and add the closure documentation to the permanent compliance file.
2. Complete one full annual cycle with zero reporting errors, using the source-document cross-check step she built into the calendar following the March incident.
3. Cross-train a second staff member — proposed as one of the operators currently in the apprentice rotation under Mr. Delgado-Marin's revised goals — on the reporting calendar system, so compliance tracking does not depend on a single employee.
4. Conduct a full audit of all permit reporting obligations beyond disinfectant residuals, to confirm no other category of required report carries an undetected backlog similar to the one she was hired to resolve.

I am forwarding the attached three completed annual reviews for your countersignature and for placement in the respective personnel files. Two matters within these reviews require action above my authority level and I am flagging them here for your direct attention rather than leaving them solely embedded in the narrative text.

First, Ms. Manzanares's reclassification request remains tabled pending the 2027 classification and compensation study. I am available to discuss any of the three reviews or the two flagged items at your convenience before the files are closed out for this cycle.
