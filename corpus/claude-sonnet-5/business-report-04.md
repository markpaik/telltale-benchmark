# Network Operations Review — First Half, Fiscal Year 2026

**Report Period:** January 1, 2026 – June 30, 2026
**Prepared by:** Sanjay Bhatt, Director of Network Analytics
**Prepared for:** Wabash Crossing Logistics Executive Committee; Joint Business Review with Beaumont Home Brands
**Distribution:** Rhonda Piotrowski, Chief Operating Officer; Cesar Villanueva, Vice President of Operations
**Date Issued:** July 29, 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Wabash Crossing Logistics operated five distribution centers during the first half of fiscal 2026, totaling roughly 3.16 million square feet of contract warehousing capacity and supporting transportation services for a customer base anchored by Beaumont Home Brands, which represents 31 percent of company revenue. Against a backdrop of generally stable throughput, the first half produced a mixed operational picture: two sites performed at or ahead of plan, one site is managing a capacity constraint that is beginning to affect service, one site is under a formal customer-driven improvement plan, and one recently opened site continues to ramp well below contracted volume.

Indianapolis again carried the largest share of network volume and held accuracy steady at 99.4 percent, but is running above budgeted cost per unit, driven substantially by warehouse labor turnover of 62 percent. Groveport shipped strong unit volume but slipped to 98.1 percent order accuracy against a 99.5 percent service-level commitment, generating $214,000 in customer chargebacks during the period. Louisville is operating at 96 percent of storage capacity with overtime consuming 18 percent of labor hours, and refused two inbound trailers in June because no dock space was available — an early warning sign that requires network attention before peak season. Carlisle, Pennsylvania, our largest facility at 1.1 million square feet, remains on a performance improvement plan initiated in April after on-time shipping fell to 91.3 percent against a 98 percent contractual commitment; the site's warehouse management system upgrade, originally scheduled for March, has slipped to September. Winchester, Virginia, which opened in August 2025, is receiving 68 percent of contracted volume and losing approximately $180,000 per month.

This report addresses each site's volume, cost, quality, and labor performance; details the Carlisle improvement plan and the WMS delay; assesses the Winchester ramp shortfall; summarizes network-wide financial and safety results; and closes with second-half priorities and peak season readiness.

The central message for the executive committee and for our colleagues at Beaumont Home Brands is this: the network is fundamentally sound and growing, but four of five sites currently carry a distinct and identifiable risk to cost, service, or both. None of these risks is beyond correction inside the second half, and none requires abandoning our current capital or staffing plans, but each requires sustained management attention between now and the peak shipping season that begins in October.

## 3. Site-by-Site Performance

### 3.1 Indianapolis

Indianapolis remains the network's volume leader and its most consistent quality performer. The site shipped 41.2 million units in the first half against a facility footprint of 610,000 square feet, translating to among the highest units-per-square-foot productivity in the network. Order accuracy held at 99.4 percent for the sixth consecutive reporting period, and the site has not triggered a customer chargeback since Q3 fiscal 2025.

The cost picture is less favorable. Actual cost per unit came in at $0.42 against a $0.39 budget, a variance of approximately 7.7 percent, or roughly $1.24 million over budget on the half's volume. The primary driver is labor: warehouse staff turnover reached 62 percent on an annualized basis, well above the network average of 49 percent and more than double the 28 percent turnover the site recorded in fiscal 2024. Elevated turnover has increased overtime and training costs, both to cover open shifts and to onboard replacement associates fast enough to hold the accuracy line. Site leadership attributes the turnover increase to wage competition from two new distribution facilities that opened within a 20-mile radius of the Indianapolis campus in late 2025, both offering starting wages $1.10–$1.40 above our current pay band for entry-level warehouse associates.

Site management has requested a wage-band adjustment effective Q3 and is piloting a referral bonus and a 90-day retention bonus for new hires. Human resources estimates the wage adjustment would add approximately $2.1 million in annualized labor cost network-wide if applied only to Indianapolis, but modeling suggests it would reduce turnover-related overtime and re-training costs by a comparable or greater amount within two quarters. This request is included in the second-half recommendations in Section 7.

### 3.2 Groveport

Groveport shipped 28.7 million units against its 900,000-square-foot footprint, a healthy volume performance, but quality metrics moved in the wrong direction. Order accuracy fell to 98.1 percent against a customer service-level commitment of 99.5 percent, and the accuracy gap generated $214,000 in customer chargebacks during the first half — the largest chargeback total of any site in the network this period.

Root-cause analysis points to two contributing factors. First, Groveport onboarded a new inbound customer program in February that added roughly 4 million units of incremental monthly volume without a proportional increase in trained pick staff; the resulting mix of new associates working unfamiliar SKUs coincided with the accuracy decline beginning in March. Second, a slotting reconfiguration intended to improve pick-path efficiency was implemented in April without a full accuracy validation cycle, and an internal audit in May identified mislabeled bin locations in two of the facility's twelve pick zones as a direct contributor to mis-picks.

Corrective actions are underway. The mislabeled zones were corrected in June, and accuracy in those two zones has since recovered to 99.3 percent. Site leadership has extended new-hire training from five days to eight days for the affected customer program and added a second-pass quality check on the highest-value SKU categories. July accuracy data, not yet finalized for this report, is trending toward 98.9 percent — improved but not yet back to the 99.5 percent commitment. We do not expect Groveport to fully close the gap before the September reporting cycle, and the site should be watched closely given the accuracy pattern's resemblance, in its early stages, to what preceded Carlisle's decline in calendar 2025.

Cost performance at Groveport was close to budget, at $0.37 actual versus $0.36 budgeted, a variance attributable mostly to the extended training hours described above. Turnover at 41 percent is above the network's historical norm but below the levels seen at Indianapolis and Carlisle.

### 3.3 Louisville

Louisville is the smallest facility in the network at 450,000 square feet and is beginning to show the effects of sustained volume growth against a fixed footprint. The site ran at 96 percent of rated storage capacity for the first half, and overtime consumed 18 percent of total labor hours, both figures that have increased steadily over the past three quarters. In June, the site refused two inbound trailers because no dock space was available to receive them — the first such refusals recorded at any Wabash Crossing facility in at least two years.

Order accuracy and on-time performance at Louisville remain solid, at 98.8 percent and within one point of service-level commitments, which is the encouraging part of an otherwise concerning trend. The concern is structural rather than operational: Louisville has effectively run out of room. Storage utilization above 95 percent materially reduces a facility's ability to absorb receiving spikes, rotate slow-moving inventory efficiently, or stage outbound freight without congestion, and the trailer refusals in June are a direct symptom.

Site and network engineering have identified three options, none of which is without cost or lead time. The first is a short-term overflow lease of approximately 60,000 square feet of nearby third-party warehouse space, available on 90-day notice at an estimated $340,000 annualized cost, which would relieve immediate pressure but does not solve the underlying constraint. The second is a permanent volume reallocation of one Louisville customer program, representing roughly 3 million units annually, to Indianapolis or Groveport, both of which have modest available capacity; this option requires customer consent and a minimum 120-day transition. The third is a capital project to add roughly 85,000 square feet of adjacent warehouse space to the existing Louisville site, for which a feasibility estimate is in progress but which would not be operational before fiscal 2027 at the earliest. We recommend pursuing the overflow lease immediately as a bridge into peak season while evaluating the volume reallocation and capital options in parallel; this is addressed further in Section 7.

### 3.4 Carlisle

Carlisle is the network's largest facility at 1.1 million square feet and is managed by General Manager Tanya Broussard. It is also the site under a formal customer performance improvement plan, initiated by Beaumont Home Brands in April 2026, and its performance is the single most material item in this review given Beaumont's status as our largest customer relationship. Section 4 below covers the improvement plan in full detail; this section summarizes the underlying operating metrics.

Carlisle's on-time shipping performance for the first half was 91.3 percent against a contractual commitment of 98 percent, a shortfall of nearly 7 points that has been the primary trigger for the improvement plan. Order accuracy at Carlisle also softened over the period, averaging 97.6 percent against an internal target of 99.0 percent, though accuracy is a secondary concern relative to the on-time shipping gap. Cost per unit came in at $0.46 against a $0.40 budget, a variance driven by expedited outbound freight used to partially offset late shipments, additional overtime, and temporary labor brought in to stabilize throughput during the second quarter.

Warehouse staff turnover at Carlisle reached 55 percent for the half, the second-highest in the network after Indianapolis, and site leadership has identified turnover-driven inexperience as a contributing factor to both the on-time and accuracy shortfalls. The facility's warehouse management system, which governs wave planning, labor allocation, and outbound scheduling, is running on a version that is now three release cycles behind current, and the planned upgrade — originally scheduled to go live in March 2026 — has slipped to September 2026. The delay is discussed in Section 4.2.

### 3.5 Winchester

Winchester, Virginia opened in August 2025 as the network's newest facility, sized at 520,000 square feet and built specifically to support anticipated growth in the mid-Atlantic customer base, including planned expansion of Beaumont Home Brands' East Coast distribution footprint. Eleven months into operation, Winchester is receiving 68 percent of contracted volume and is losing approximately $180,000 per month, a run rate that, if unchanged, would produce roughly $2.16 million in annualized site-level losses.

The volume shortfall traces primarily to the pace of customer onboarding rather than to any operational deficiency at the site itself. Of the four customer programs contracted to move into Winchester on the original opening schedule, two are fully transitioned and operating at or near forecast volume, one is roughly 40 percent transitioned with the remainder delayed by the customer's own inventory system migration, and one has not yet begun transition pending a contract amendment currently in legal review. Site-level quality metrics are strong for the volume the facility does handle — order accuracy of 98.9 percent and on-time shipping within one point of commitment — which supports the conclusion that Winchester's shortfall is a demand problem, not a capability problem.

Cost per unit at $0.61 against a $0.44 budget reflects the basic economics of underutilized fixed capacity: labor, utilities, and facility costs are largely fixed regardless of throughput, so the per-unit cost rises sharply when volume runs well below the level the site was built and staffed to handle. Site leadership has taken two mitigating actions. First, warehouse labor has been right-sized twice since opening, most recently in May, to better match current volume, though this has to be balanced against the need to retain enough trained staff to absorb the pending volume transitions without a hiring scramble. Second, the site has begun accepting limited short-term storage and pick-pack work from smaller regional customers to generate incremental contribution margin while the anchor programs complete their transitions; this incremental business contributed approximately $95,000 in the second quarter and is expected to grow modestly in the third quarter.

## 4. Carlisle Performance Improvement Plan and System Upgrade

### 4.1 Improvement Plan Status

Beaumont Home Brands placed the Carlisle facility on a formal performance improvement plan on April 14, 2026, following three consecutive months of on-time shipping performance below the 98 percent contractual commitment, culminating in a March result of 89.7 percent. The plan, developed jointly by Wabash Crossing operations leadership and Beaumont's supply chain team, established four corrective milestones and a 180-day review period ending in mid-October 2026.

The milestones are: (1) restore on-time shipping to 95 percent by end of Q2 fiscal 2026 as an interim target; (2) complete the delayed warehouse management system upgrade and stabilize the site on the new platform; (3) reduce warehouse staff turnover to below 40 percent through a revised retention program; and (4) restore order accuracy to 99.0 percent. Progress against each is summarized below.

| Milestone | Target | Status as of June 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| On-time shipping (interim) | 95.0% by end of Q2 | 91.3% — not met |
| WMS upgrade completion | Originally March 2026 | Slipped to September 2026 |
| Warehouse staff turnover | Below 40% | 55% — not met |
| Order accuracy | 99.0% | 97.6% — not met |

None of the four milestones has been met on the original timeline. General Manager Broussard and site leadership implemented interim measures beginning in May, including additional temporary staffing during peak shift windows, a revised wave-planning schedule designed to work around the current system's known scheduling limitations, and a dedicated Beaumont account team embedded at the site to triage service issues in real time. These measures produced a modest improvement in June, when on-time shipping reached 93.1 percent for the month, up from an April low of 88.9 percent, but the site remains well short of both the interim and final targets.

Beaumont's supply chain leadership has indicated, through the account team, that they view the June trend positively but expect to see sustained improvement through the third quarter before revisiting the plan's status. A formal 90-day check-in with Beaumont is scheduled for the joint session referenced in the title block of this report, and a further review is scheduled for mid-October at the plan's conclusion. Failure to demonstrate clear trajectory toward the 98 percent commitment by that date carries risk to the broader commercial relationship, given Carlisle's role as the primary fulfillment site for a substantial share of Beaumont's housewares volume.

### 4.2 Warehouse Management System Upgrade

The Carlisle WMS upgrade was originally scheduled to go live in March 2026 and has slipped to September 2026, a six-month delay. The upgrade project is being delivered by our enterprise systems team in partnership with the software vendor, and the delay has three principal causes.

First, data migration testing in February uncovered inconsistencies between Carlisle's legacy location-mapping data and the new system's data model, requiring a data-cleansing effort that took approximately ten weeks longer than planned. Second, the vendor's implementation team experienced staffing turnover of its own during the first quarter, which slowed configuration and testing support on two occasions. Third, given Carlisle's status on the improvement plan, site and network leadership made a deliberate decision in May to push the go-live date rather than cut over during a period of active service instability, on the judgment that introducing a new, unfamiliar system into an already-strained operation would create unacceptable risk of a further service decline during the transition.

The revised go-live date is September 14, 2026, with a two-week hypercare period staffed jointly by the vendor and our systems team running through the end of September. This timing places the cutover roughly six weeks before the traditional start of peak season volume ramp in late October, which the project team believes is sufficient runway to stabilize the new system before peak demand arrives, though it leaves less margin than we would prefer. Network leadership has asked the systems team to prepare a contingency plan in the event the September date slips further, including a fallback option of delaying cutover until January 2027, after peak season concludes, rather than risk a system transition during the highest-volume period of the year. We will bring that contingency plan to the executive committee no later than the end of August so a decision can be made with adequate lead time.

## 5. Winchester Volume Ramp and Path to Breakeven

Winchester's financial performance is the most straightforward of the network's four flagged issues, in the sense that its cause and eventual resolution are both clearly understood: the site is losing money because it is running well below the volume it was built and staffed to handle, and it will approach breakeven as contracted customer programs complete their transitions.

Site leadership and the sales team have modeled three scenarios for the second half. In the base case, the customer program currently 40 percent transitioned completes its migration by the end of Q3, and the program pending contract amendment begins transition in Q4, bringing Winchester to approximately 82 percent of contracted volume by calendar year-end and to full contracted volume by Q2 fiscal 2027. Under this scenario, monthly losses narrow from the current $180,000 to approximately $60,000 by December and the site reaches monthly breakeven in the second quarter of fiscal 2027. In the delayed case, the pending contract amendment is not finalized until Q1 fiscal 2027, pushing full breakeven out by one additional quarter and adding an estimated $400,000 to cumulative site losses. In the accelerated case, both pending transitions complete ahead of schedule and the site reaches breakeven by the end of Q4 fiscal 2026; sales leadership assesses this as the least likely of the three scenarios but not implausible given the customer's stated urgency to exit its current facility.

We are treating the base case as the planning assumption for the remainder of this report and for the second-half financial forecast in Section 6. The most important lever we control directly is continued cost discipline at the site — holding labor and overhead as tightly as possible to actual volume without compromising the site's ability to absorb the anticopated Q3 and Q4 transitions without a disruptive hiring surge. Site leadership's twice-executed labor right-sizing this fiscal year reflects that discipline, and a further modest adjustment is planned for August pending final confirmation of the Q3 transition timeline.

## 6. Network Financial Summary

Network revenue for the first half of fiscal 2026 totaled approximately $158.4 million, tracking modestly ahead of the prior-year first half and consistent with the low-single-digit annual growth implied by last year's full-year revenue of $310 million. Beaumont Home Brands' volume across Carlisle and other sites accounted for approximately $49.1 million of first-half revenue, or 31 percent, consistent with its full-year share.

Network operating cost performance was mixed, consistent with the site-level detail above. Three of five sites — Indianapolis, Carlisle, and Winchester — ran above budgeted cost per unit, while Groveport ran close to budget and Louisville ran modestly above budget, driven by the overtime described in Section 3.3. On a blended basis, network cost per unit came in at approximately $0.42 against a $0.39 budget, a variance of roughly 7.7 percent that, applied across total network volume, represents an estimated $5.4 million unfavorable variance for the half. The largest single contributors to this variance are Carlisle's expedite and overtime costs associated with the improvement plan (estimated $2.3 million), Indianapolis's turnover-driven overtime (estimated $1.24 million), and Winchester's underabsorbed fixed costs (approximately $1.08 million across the half, consistent with the $180,000 monthly run rate described in Section 3.5).

This is a smaller gap than the site-level detail might suggest in isolation, but it should not be read as a signal that the underlying issues are minor; each of the four flagged sites carries a distinct trajectory that, left uncorrected, would widen materially in the second half, particularly if any of the flagged issues intersects with peak season volume, as discussed in Section 8.

Total company headcount stood at 2,900 employees as of June 30, consistent with the figure referenced in company-wide reporting. Blended warehouse staff turnover across the five sites subject to this review averaged approximately 49 percent on an annualized basis, above the company's internal target of 35 percent and above the prior-year comparable period's 38 percent. Turnover is a network-wide cost and quality risk, not merely a site-specific one, and is addressed further in the second-half recommendations.

## 7. Network Safety Performance

Safety performance across the network was stable during the first half, with no fatalities and no OSHA-reportable incidents that triggered a facility shutdown or regulatory citation. The network's total recordable incident rate (TRIR) for the first half was 3.2 per 200,000 hours worked, modestly above the company's internal target of 2.8 and roughly in line with the industry benchmark for contract warehousing and transportation operations. The days-away-restricted-or-transferred (DART) rate was 1.9, within the company's target range.

Louisville and Carlisle recorded the highest site-level incident rates in the network, at 4.1 and 3.8 respectively, both above the network average. At Louisville, the elevated rate correlates with the capacity and congestion issues described in Section 3.3; safety leadership has specifically flagged an increase in minor forklift and material-handling incidents in the receiving and staging areas during the second quarter, consistent with a more congested working environment. At Carlisle, the elevated rate correlates with the turnover and temporary-staffing pressures described in Section 4; new and temporary associates, who have less facility-specific experience, account for a disproportionate share of the site's incidents this period.

No incident at either site rose to a level requiring escalation beyond standard site and regional safety review, and both sites remain in compliance with all applicable OSHA and state workplace safety requirements. We view the elevated rates at Louisville and Carlisle as consistent with, and to some degree explained by, the operational pressures already under corrective action at both sites, and we expect improvement at both sites to follow as the underlying capacity and staffing issues are addressed. Safety leadership will continue monthly site-level reviews and will bring any material change in trend to the executive committee outside the normal reporting cycle if warranted.

## 8. Second-Half Outlook and Peak Season Readiness

The second half of fiscal 2026 carries the network's highest-volume months, with peak season demand typically building from late October through December and representing, in a normal year, approximately 34 to 38 percent of full-year unit volume across the customer base. Four factors specific to this year's second half require particular attention heading into that period.

First, Carlisle's WMS cutover is now scheduled for mid-September, roughly six weeks ahead of the typical start of the peak volume ramp. This timing is deliberate, intended to allow a stabilization window before demand builds, but it leaves less margin than we would prefer, and the contingency plan described in Section 4.2 — including the option to defer cutover until after peak season — will be presented to the executive committee by the end of August. Whichever path is chosen, Carlisle enters peak season either on a newly stabilized system or on a legacy system already known to constrain wave planning and labor scheduling; neither path is risk-free, and the site's improvement plan milestones must be assessed with that reality in mind.

Second, Louisville's capacity constraint will be under its greatest stress during peak season, when inbound receiving volume typically increases before outbound shipments follow. The overflow lease option described in Section 3.3 should be executed, if approved, in time to be operational no later than early October; absent additional capacity, we should expect further trailer refusals and a higher risk of missed inbound appointments during the fourth quarter, with downstream effects on outbound service levels.

Third, Winchester's base-case volume ramp assumes the pending customer transitions proceed on the schedules described in Section 5. If the Q3 transition slips into Q4, as occurred with a comparable program at Groveport's 2025 opening, Winchester will enter peak season further below capacity than planned, which is a less severe outcome operationally — an underutilized site handles a volume increase more easily than a capacity-constrained one — but would extend the site's losses further into fiscal 2027.

Fourth, network-wide labor availability during peak season remains a standing risk given current turnover levels, particularly at Indianapolis and Carlisle. Both sites' peak staffing plans assume a seasonal hiring surge beginning in September; given this year's elevated baseline turnover, both sites have increased their seasonal hiring targets by approximately 15 percent over the prior year's plan to provide a larger buffer against attrition during the hiring and training window.

Network leadership's second-half priorities, in order, are: stabilize Carlisle's on-time shipping performance and complete the WMS cutover without further slippage; secure interim capacity relief at Louisville ahead of peak season; hold Groveport's accuracy recovery on its current trajectory and confirm the improvement is durable, not seasonal; and continue Winchester's disciplined cost management while sales completes the pending customer transitions. We will also bring the Indianapolis wage-band adjustment request to the executive committee for a decision in August, given its direct bearing on that site's ability to control turnover before its own peak-season hiring push begins.

## 9. Recommendations for Executive Committee Action

1. **Approve or decline the Louisville overflow lease** (approximately $340,000 annualized) in time for an October operational start, to relieve the capacity constraint before peak season volume arrives.
2. **Decide the Carlisle WMS cutover contingency** — proceed with the September 14 go-live or defer to January 2027 — no later than the end of August, based on the systems team's contingency plan.
3. **Approve the Indianapolis wage-band adjustment** (estimated $2.1 million annualized) to address the site's above-target turnover before its peak-season hiring window opens in September.
4. **Confirm continued support for Winchester's base-case ramp plan** through Q2 fiscal 2027, recognizing the site's near-term losses as a planned cost of building the mid-Atlantic footprint rather than a standalone performance failure.
5. **Direct network analytics to prepare a focused Beaumont Home Brands briefing** on the Carlisle improvement plan status ahead of the joint session referenced in this report's title block, incorporating June's improving trend alongside a candid assessment of the distance remaining to the 98 percent commitment.

This report and its recommendations are offered for discussion at the executive committee meeting and, where appropriate, for the joint session with Beaumont Home Brands referenced above. Network analytics will provide updated site-level data at the Q3 close and will bring any material change in the status of Carlisle, Louisville, or Winchester to the executive committee's attention outside the normal quarterly cycle if conditions warrant.
