## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes how Cedar Line Markets receives, classifies, and acts on product recall and market withdrawal notices so that affected product is removed from sale, quarantined, accounted for, and — where required — reported and disclosed to customers within defined time clocks. It replaces the informal, email-driven process used through February 2026, under which a Class I recall on ready-to-eat chicken salad left product on shelves at 11 of 34 stores 31 hours after notice, was documented as a deficiency by a Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA) inspector, and was followed by a mock recall that recovered only 71 percent of affected units against a four-hour, 100 percent target. This procedure exists to close that gap and to give any employee performing a recall role a step they can follow without needing to ask a supervisor what comes next.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all product sold or distributed by Cedar Line Markets across all 34 stores, the Airway Heights distribution center (DC), and the commissary, regardless of whether the recall originates from a supplier, a regulatory agency, or Cedar Line's own quality assurance findings. It covers:

- Class I, II, and III recalls as defined by FDA/USDA, and WSDA-issued withdrawal directives
- Warehouse-distributed product moving through the Airway Heights DC
- Direct-store-delivery (DSD) product from the 46 approved DSD vendors that never touches the DC
- Commissary-produced deli and prepared-food items, including those without a supplier lot code
- Voluntary market withdrawals initiated by Cedar Line for quality reasons that do not rise to a safety recall

This procedure does not cover general product quality complaints handled through the vendor scorecard process, nor does it cover foodborne illness investigations that have not yet resulted in a recall or withdrawal notice (those are governed by FSQA-SOP-009, Illness Complaint Intake).

## 3. Definitions

**Recall** — Removal from sale of a product that FDA, USDA-FSIS, WSDA, or the manufacturer/supplier has determined to be misbranded or to present a health or safety risk.

**Class I Recall** — A situation in which there is a reasonable probability that use of the product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death.

**Class II Recall** — A situation in which use of the product may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or the probability of serious harm is remote.

**Class III Recall** — A situation in which use of the product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences.

**Market Withdrawal** — Removal of product initiated voluntarily by Cedar Line for a quality defect that does not violate FDA/USDA law and does not warrant regulatory reporting.

**Recall Coordinator** — The designated FSQA staff role, staffed 24/7 on an on-call rotation, responsible for intake, classification, and cascading notice within the required clock.

**Register Block** — An item-file flag pushed to every point-of-sale lane in all 34 stores that prevents a UPC from scanning or tendering.

**Shelf Pull** — Physical removal of affected product from sales floor, cooler, freezer, or DSD display by store personnel.

**DC Quarantine** — Physical segregation and system hold of affected lots at the Airway Heights DC pending disposition instruction.

**Reconciliation** — The comparison of quantity received (per DC receiving records or DSD invoice) against quantity recovered (pulled, quarantined, or confirmed destroyed) for a given lot.

**Effectiveness Check** — A documented verification, at 24 hours and again at 72 hours after notice, confirming that register blocks are active and shelf pulls are complete at every affected location.

**Direct-Store-Delivery (DSD) Vendor** — Any of the 46 approved vendors who deliver product directly to stores without the product passing through the Airway Heights DC or appearing in DC receiving records.

**Commissary Item** — A store-made deli or prepared-food product produced at the Cedar Line commissary, typically carrying an internal production lot code rather than a supplier lot code.

**Loyalty Database** — The 1.2 million-cardholder customer database used to identify purchasers of an affected item by loyalty card transaction for direct notification purposes.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Notice Receipt and Classification

1. Any recall or withdrawal notice — from a supplier, FDA, USDA-FSIS, WSDA, or an internal FSQA finding — must be directed immediately to the recall coordinator's dedicated intake line, which is staffed 24 hours a day, 7 days a week through an on-call rotation. The intake line accepts phone, email, and the supplier notification platform's automated alert feed simultaneously.

2. Upon receipt, the recall coordinator logs the notice in the master recall log within 15 minutes, capturing: date/time received, source, product description, UPC(s), lot code(s), production/best-by dates, hazard described, and whether the notice originates from a supplier, regulator, or internal finding.

3. The recall coordinator classifies severity within 30 minutes of receipt, in consultation with the Director of FSQA when reachable, using FDA/USDA class definitions (Section 3). If the notice itself already states a class (e.g., a USDA-FSIS Class I recall notice), that classification governs unless FSQA determines the risk to Cedar Line's distribution differs materially.

4. Classification determines the notification clock:
   - **Class I** — full store and DC notification cascade begins within 1 hour of receipt; register block live within 2 hours; shelf pull confirmed complete at all affected locations within 4 hours.
   - **Class II** — cascade begins within 2 hours; register block live within 4 hours; shelf pull confirmed within 8 hours.
   - **Class III** — cascade begins within 8 hours; register block live and shelf pull confirmed within 24 hours.
   - **Voluntary Market Withdrawal** — handled per Section 5.10; no regulatory clock applies, but internal target mirrors Class III.

5. If classification cannot be determined with confidence within 30 minutes (ambiguous hazard, incomplete lot data), the recall coordinator defaults to the next more severe class until clarified, and documents the reason for the default in the master recall log.

### 5.2 Notification Cascade

6. The recall coordinator initiates the cascade using the supplier notification platform's broadcast function, which pushes a standardized recall alert simultaneously to: all 34 store directors, category manager(s) for the affected item, DC inventory control, receiving leads, customer care, and marketing. The alert includes UPC, lot code(s), hazard, classification, and the applicable clock from Section 5.1.4.

7. Each store director must acknowledge receipt of the alert within 15 minutes for Class I, 30 minutes for Class II, and 1 hour for Class III, by replying through the notification platform. The recall coordinator tracks acknowledgment status on a live dashboard; any store that has not acknowledged within the window is called directly by the recall coordinator or a designated FSQA staff member.

8. The Director of FSQA briefs the SVP Retail Operations within 2 hours of receipt for any Class I or Class II event, and within 8 hours for Class III. For Class I events, the SVP Retail Operations briefs the CEO within 4 hours of receipt.

9. Category managers confirm, within 1 hour of the cascade alert, whether the affected UPC(s) moved through the Airway Heights DC, arrived via DSD, or were produced at the commissary, and route the notice accordingly to DC inventory control (Section 5.4) or the relevant DSD vendor contact (Section 5.9).

### 5.3 Register Block and Shelf Pull at Store Level

10. Upon receiving the cascade alert, the category manager submits a register block request to the IT/item file administrator, identifying every UPC affected, including all size and pack variants sharing the recalled lot.

11. The IT/item file administrator pushes the register block to the item file, which propagates to all point-of-sale lanes at all 34 stores. The administrator confirms the block is live by running a test scan against the affected UPC(s) at a minimum of three stores selected at random, and reports confirmation time back to the recall coordinator.

12. Simultaneously, each store director assigns a shelf-pull team (minimum two employees for Class I/II) to physically remove the affected product from sales floor, cooler, freezer, and any DSD display area, regardless of whether the register block is yet confirmed live — the block must never be the sole control.

13. The shelf-pull team completes the store-level recall checklist, which requires: location(s) checked (sales floor, backroom, cooler, freezer, DSD rack), quantity pulled by lot code, whether any units were sold prior to pull (estimated from register/loyalty data where available), and signature of the store director.

14. The store director reports the completed checklist to the recall coordinator by the deadline in Section 5.1.4. Product pulled is placed in a clearly labeled "DO NOT SELL — RECALL HOLD" bin in the store's designated quarantine area (backroom cage or walk-in) pending pickup or destruction instruction from category management.

15. If a store cannot complete the shelf pull within the clock (e.g., staffing gap, store closed), the store director immediately escalates to the recall coordinator, who redeploys a district-level or FSQA field resource to complete the pull in person. This escalation, and its resolution time, is logged in the master recall log as a deviation.

### 5.4 DC Quarantine and Reconciliation

16. DC inventory control places any affected lot(s) still on hand at the Airway Heights DC into physical quarantine (a locked, signed cage or designated quarantine zone) and applies a system hold in the warehouse management system (WMS) blocking the lot from further outbound picks, within the same clock that applies to the register block (Section 5.1.4).

17. DC inventory control pulls the lot-level receiving record for the affected lot(s) from the WMS, documenting total quantity received at the DC and total quantity subsequently shipped to each of the 34 stores.

18. DC inventory control reconciles quantity received against quantity recovered by summing: (a) quantity remaining in DC quarantine, (b) quantity confirmed pulled and held at each store per the store-level checklist (Section 5.3.13), and (c) quantity confirmed sold prior to pull, where estimable from register or loyalty transaction data.

19. Any variance between quantity received and quantity accounted for (sum of a, b, c) that exceeds 2 percent of total quantity received is flagged in the master recall log as an open reconciliation gap, and DC inventory control, together with the relevant store receiving leads, investigates the gap until it is closed or explained (e.g., product used in commissary production, damaged and previously destroyed under separate log).

20. Reconciliation status — full, partial with explained variance, or open gap — is reported by DC inventory control to the recall coordinator no later than the 24-hour effectiveness check (Section 5.6).

### 5.5 Customer Notification

21. For Class I and Class II recalls, customer care and marketing determine, within 4 hours of the cascade alert, whether direct customer notice is warranted in addition to in-store signage. Direct notice is required whenever the affected item can be tied to specific purchasers through loyalty card transaction data.

22. Marketing queries the loyalty database for all cardholder transactions matching the affected UPC(s) and lot code(s) (where lot-level POS capture exists) within the product's sale window, and generates a purchaser list.

23. Customer care sends direct notice (email and, where a phone number is on file, SMS) to every identified purchaser within 8 hours of the cascade alert for Class I, and within 24 hours for Class II. The notice states the product, the hazard, the lot code(s), and instructions to discard or return the product for a full refund without receipt.

24. Marketing simultaneously posts a public recall notice on the company website and in-store signage at all 34 stores (posted at the entrance and at the shelf location) within the same window, regardless of whether direct loyalty notice is possible, since not all purchasers are loyalty cardholders.

25. Customer care logs every inbound customer call related to the recall, including any report of illness or adverse reaction, and escalates any illness report to the Director of FSQA within 1 hour of receipt, regardless of time of day.

### 5.6 Effectiveness Checks at 24 and 72 Hours

26. The recall coordinator conducts a formal effectiveness check at 24 hours after notice receipt, verifying for every one of the 34 stores: register block confirmed live, shelf pull checklist received and signed, and no re-stocking of affected product has occurred. Any store not meeting all three criteria is recorded as an open item and a follow-up call is placed to that store director within 1 hour of the check.

27. A second effectiveness check is conducted at 72 hours, verifying that all open items from the 24-hour check are closed, that DC reconciliation (Section 5.4) is complete or has a documented explained variance, and that no new units of affected product have been identified in any store, at the DC, or through DSD channels.

28. The Director of FSQA reviews both effectiveness checks and assigns an overall grade: **Effective** (100 percent of stores compliant at 72 hours, reconciliation closed), **Partially Effective** (compliance gaps closed but reconciliation variance remains open), or **Ineffective** (any store still has affected product accessible for sale at 72 hours, or reconciliation gap exceeds 5 percent unexplained). Any grade of Ineffective triggers a mandatory review of this procedure per the Document Control Block review cycle.

29. Results of both checks, including store-by-store compliance status, are recorded in the master recall log and summarized in a written effectiveness report to the SVP Retail Operations within 24 hours of the 72-hour check.

### 5.7 Product Disposition and Credit

30. Once reconciliation is complete for a given location (store or DC), the category manager issues written disposition instructions: destroy on-site with witnessed destruction log, return to supplier for credit, or hold for regulatory pickup (if requested by FDA/USDA/WSDA).

31. Store directors and DC inventory control execute disposition within 5 business days of receiving instruction, and document destruction (or return shipment) with date, quantity, method, and signature of the employee performing it plus a second witness for Class I/II events.

32. The category manager reconciles supplier credit against the destroyed/returned quantity and confirms credit has been issued or invoiced within 30 days of disposition, closing the financial loop in the master recall log.

### 5.8 Reporting to State and Federal Regulators

33. For any Class I or Class II recall, the Director of FSQA notifies WSDA and the applicable federal agency (FDA or USDA-FSIS, depending on product category) of Cedar Line's response actions within 24 hours of notice receipt, using the agency's designated reportable food or recall response channel, even where the originating notice came from the supplier or the agency itself.

34. The notification to regulators includes: classification, number of stores affected, quantity received/recovered/outstanding per the reconciliation in Section 5.4, customer notification actions taken, and the effectiveness check schedule.

35. If a WSDA or FDA/USDA inspector requests an on-site verification of recall execution, the Director of FSQA or her designee makes the master recall log, store-level checklists, and reconciliation records available within 4 hours of the request, and accompanies the inspector during any store visit.

36. Any regulatory finding of deficiency (as occurred in February 2026) is logged as a corrective action item, assigned an owner and closure date by the Director of FSQA, and reviewed at the next scheduled FSQA leadership meeting.

### 5.9 Exceptions

**5.9.1 After-Hours Notices.** Notices received between 10:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m. Pacific time are still logged and classified by the on-call recall coordinator within the standard 30-minute window. For Class I events, the on-call recall coordinator has authority to wake and deploy an after-hours store response using each store's posted emergency contact list; a store need not be open for the register block to be pushed, but the physical shelf pull must be completed by the start of that store's next business day and confirmed within 1 hour of opening. The overnight clock does not pause the notification cascade (Section 5.2), only the physical shelf-pull deadline for closed locations.

**5.9.2 Direct-Store-Delivery Vendors.** Because DSD inventory never appears in the DC's WMS or lot-level receiving records, reconciliation for DSD product relies on vendor-supplied delivery records and store-level DSD rack checks. Upon a DSD-item recall, the category manager contacts the vendor's designated recall contact directly (contact list maintained in the vendor master file) and requires the vendor to supply a store-by-store delivery quantity within 4 hours. Store directors physically check DSD racks as part of the standard shelf pull (Section 5.3.12) regardless of whether vendor delivery data has yet arrived. Reconciliation for DSD product is considered complete when vendor-supplied delivery quantities are matched against store-confirmed pull quantities; any store where the DSD vendor's own route driver is contractually responsible for pulling product is still independently verified by the store director, who does not rely solely on the vendor's confirmation.

**5.9.3 Commissary Items With No Supplier Lot Code.** Commissary-produced deli and prepared-food items are tracked by internal production lot/batch code rather than a supplier lot code. When a recall or withdrawal affects a commissary item — whether due to a recalled raw ingredient or an internal quality finding — the commissary production manager identifies all internal batch codes that used the affected ingredient lot, cross-referenced against the commissary's daily production log, and reports the affected batch code(s) and production date range(s) to the recall coordinator within 2 hours of notice. The register block and shelf pull are executed against the commissary's internal batch code exactly as they would be against a supplier lot code; DC quarantine does not apply since commissary product is delivered directly to stores, so store-level pull and quarantine in the store's backroom cooler serves as the equivalent control.

**5.9.4 Voluntary Market Withdrawals.** When Cedar Line initiates a withdrawal for a quality defect that does not meet the threshold for a regulatory recall (e.g., underweight packaging, cosmetic defect, non-hazardous mislabeling), the Director of FSQA authorizes the withdrawal and the recall coordinator runs the same cascade, register block, and shelf-pull steps as a Class III recall, but the regulatory reporting step (Section 5.8) is not required unless WSDA or FDA/USDA requests documentation. Customer notification (Section 5.5) is discretionary and decided jointly by marketing and the Director of FSQA based on customer-facing risk to brand trust rather than health and safety. All voluntary withdrawals are still logged in the master recall log and subject to the same effectiveness checks and closure sign-off as a regulatory recall.

### 5.10 Closure

37. The recall coordinator declares an event closed only after: register block confirmed removed or converted to permanent discontinuation (per category manager instruction), reconciliation closed or variance explained and accepted by the Director of FSQA, disposition and credit completed, and both effectiveness checks graded Effective or Partially Effective with all open items resolved.

38. Closure is documented with a signed closure memo from the Director of FSQA, filed with the master recall log, and copied to the SVP Retail Operations.

## 6. Records

The following records are generated and retained for each recall or withdrawal event:

- Master recall log (intake, classification, cascade timestamps, acknowledgment tracking)
- Store-level recall checklists (all 34 stores, signed)
- Register block confirmation record (IT/item file administrator)
- DC quarantine log and lot-level receiving reconciliation
- DSD vendor delivery confirmation records (where applicable)
- Commissary batch code cross-reference (where applicable)
- Loyalty database query and customer notification send log
- Customer care inbound call log, including any illness reports
- 24-hour and 72-hour effectiveness check reports and Director of FSQA grading
- Disposition and destruction/return records with witness signatures
- Supplier credit reconciliation
- Regulatory notification correspondence and any inspection findings
- Closure memo

All records above are retained for a minimum of 3 years from closure, or longer if required by an active regulatory inquiry, litigation hold, or insurance claim, whichever is longest. Records are stored in the FSQA document management system with restricted access limited to FSQA staff, the recall coordinator, and the SVP Retail Operations.

## Appendix E: Recall Drill and Training Program

Following the February 2026 mock recall that recovered only 71 percent of affected units against a four-hour, 100 percent target, Cedar Line Markets institutes a standing drill and training program to keep this procedure current in practice rather than only on paper.

1. **Drill Frequency.** The recall coordinator, with the Director of FSQA, runs a full-scale mock recall no less than twice per calendar year, and a tabletop exercise (cascade and classification only, no physical shelf pull) at least once per quarter. At least one full-scale drill per year must simulate a Class I event to test the after-hours exception (Section 5.9.1).

2. **Drill Design.** Each full-scale drill selects a real, currently stocked UPC (not an actual recall) and issues a simulated notice through the supplier notification platform, timestamped identically to a live event. All 34 stores, DC inventory control, and at least six of the 46 DSD vendors (rotating selection so that all vendors are tested within a 24-month period) are included in the simulation.

3. **Drill Scoring.** Drills are scored against the same clocks defined in Section 5.1.4 and the same effectiveness grading defined in Section 5.6.28. A drill scoring below 90 percent store compliance at the four-hour mark for a simulated Class I event triggers a mandatory root-cause review, store-level retraining at any non-compliant location within 10 business days, and a follow-up drill at that location within 60 days.

4. **New Employee Training.** Every newly hired store director, receiving lead, and DC inventory control employee completes recall procedure training, including a walkthrough of the Appendix A checklist and a live system demonstration of the register block confirmation process, before being scheduled for an unsupervised closing or overnight shift.

5. **Annual Refresher.** All roles named in Section 4 complete an annual refresher covering any revisions made to this procedure since the prior training cycle, tracked in the FSQA learning management system with completion records retained per Section 6.

6. **Vendor Drill Participation.** DSD vendors are contractually required, per the Cedar Line Supplier Quality Agreement Section 9, to participate in at least one drill per 24-month cycle and to respond to the simulated vendor contact request within the 4-hour window specified in Section 5.9.2; repeated non-response is escalated to category management for vendor scorecard action.

## Appendix G: Recall Classification Decision Tree

The recall coordinator uses the following sequence, in order, to reach a classification within the 30-minute window required by Section 5.1.3:

**Step 1 — Does the notice already carry an FDA, USDA-FSIS, or WSDA classification?**
- Yes → Adopt that classification unless FSQA has independent information suggesting Cedar Line's distribution presents a materially different risk profile (e.g., product held at improper temperature in transit). If independent information exists, escalate to the Director of FSQA before finalizing.
- No → Proceed to Step 2.

**Step 2 — Does the hazard description indicate a pathogen, allergen non-disclosure, foreign material capable of causing serious injury, or any condition with a reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequence or death?**
- Yes → Classify as Class I. Proceed to cascade under the Class I clock.
- No → Proceed to Step 3.

**Step 3 — Does the hazard description indicate a condition that may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or where serious harm is remote but not eliminated?**
- Yes → Classify as Class II. Proceed to cascade under the Class II clock.
- No → Proceed to Step 4.

**Step 4 — Is this a labeling, weight, cosmetic, or non-hazardous quality defect with no plausible adverse health consequence?**
- Yes → Determine whether the issue violates FDA/USDA law (mislabeling, unauthorized ingredient, etc.).
  - If it violates law → Classify as Class III.
  - If it does not violate law and is purely a quality decision → Classify as Voluntary Market Withdrawal per Section 5.9.4.
- No → Escalate immediately to the Director of FSQA for manual classification; default to Class II pending her determination, per Section 5.1.5.

**Step 5 — Regardless of outcome above, confirm distribution channel** (DC-distributed, DSD, or commissary-produced) to route the notice to the correct reconciliation path (Sections 5.4, 5.9.2, 5.9.3) before the cascade alert is sent.
