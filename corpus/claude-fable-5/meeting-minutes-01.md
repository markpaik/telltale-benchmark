## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**Body:** Board of Directors, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative
**Date:** Tuesday, January 20, 2026
**Time:** 6:02 p.m. – 8:47 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Linnea Sorensen, Hassan Farah, Colleen Rasmussen

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen (General Manager); Renata Voss (Finance Manager, staff); eleven member-owners in the gallery

### 1. Call to Order and Determination of Quorum

President Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:02 p.m. Secretary Suh confirmed that all nine directors were present and that a quorum existed.

### 2. Approval of Agenda

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded approval of the agenda as distributed. The motion carried unanimously by voice vote.

### 3. Approval of Minutes

Ms. Rasmussen moved and Mr. Farah seconded approval of the minutes of the December 16, 2025 regular meeting. The motion carried unanimously, with Mr. Fitzgerald abstaining because he had been absent from that meeting.

### 4. Member-Owner Comment Period

Three member-owners spoke during the fifteen-minute comment period. Member-owner Gail Pettersen expressed concern about rumors of a second store and asked whether member-owners would be given a vote. Member-owner Thomas Bruneau spoke in support of expansion, citing the lack of a natural foods store in Superior, Wisconsin. Member-owner Dana Kivimäki asked whether the patronage dividend would be paid given the fiscal 2025 results. President Ramanathan thanked the speakers and noted that the dividend decision would be made after the audited financial statements were received.

### 5. Treasurer's Report — Fiscal 2025 Close

Treasurer Kozlowski presented the preliminary fiscal 2025 year-end financial statements, prepared with Finance Manager Voss. She reported the following:

- Total sales of $18.4 million, an increase of 1.1 percent over fiscal 2024, but below the budgeted 4 percent growth.
- An operating loss of $340,000 for the fiscal year, against a budgeted operating surplus of $95,000.
- Gross margin of 34.1 percent, down from 36.8 percent in fiscal 2024. Ms. Kozlowski attributed the erosion principally to price competition from two conventional grocers, increased shrink in the produce and prepared foods departments, and wage increases implemented in July 2025 that were not fully offset by pricing adjustments.
- Cash on hand of $1.42 million, equivalent to approximately 28 days of operating expenses, down from 41 days one year earlier.
- Compliance with all covenants on the cooperative's term loan with Northcountry Cooperative Development Fund, though Ms. Kozlowski cautioned that the debt-service coverage ratio of 1.28 was near the covenant floor of 1.25.

Ms. Kozlowski stated that the independent audit by Wipfli LLP was underway and that audited statements were expected by the March meeting. In discussion, Mr. Fitzgerald asked whether the loss was concentrated in any quarter; Ms. Voss responded that the fourth quarter accounted for roughly $190,000 of the loss. Ms. Sorensen asked about the effect of the margin decline on dividend capacity; Ms. Kozlowski responded that, in her professional judgment, the cooperative could not prudently declare a patronage dividend on a loss year, but that the formal recommendation would await the audit.

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded acceptance of the Treasurer's report. The motion carried unanimously.

### 6. General Manager's Report

General Manager Haugen reported on operations for December 2025 and early January 2026:

- December sales of $1.71 million, up 0.4 percent over the prior December.
- Customer count down 2.3 percent year over year; average basket size up 2.8 percent.
- Staffing at 112 employees; turnover for calendar 2025 was 31 percent, an improvement from 38 percent in 2024.
- A margin recovery plan comprising category-by-category price reviews, renegotiation with the cooperative's primary distributor, and tightened shrink controls, with a target of restoring gross margin to 35.5 percent by the end of the second quarter of fiscal 2026.
- Completion of the refrigeration compressor replacement approximately $14,000 under budget.

Mr. Farah asked whether the margin recovery plan risked pricing the co-op out of reach for lower-income shoppers; Mr. Haugen responded that the basics program of 120 price-held staple items would be maintained. The report was received without a motion.

### 7. Proposed Second Store — Superior, Wisconsin

Mr. Haugen presented a proposal to acquire and fit out a 14,500-square-foot former retail building at 1804 Tower Avenue, Superior, Wisconsin, at a total project cost of $4.2 million, comprising $1.6 million for the property, $2.1 million for renovation and equipment, and $500,000 for inventory, pre-opening costs, and contingency. The proposed financing structure combined a $2.5 million term loan, a $1.0 million member-owner loan campaign, and $700,000 of cooperative cash and equity.

Extended discussion followed. Mr. Fitzgerald spoke in favor, stating that the site was well priced and that delay risked losing it. Ms. Kozlowski expressed serious reservations about undertaking a $4.2 million project in the immediate aftermath of a $340,000 loss year with 28 days of cash. Ms. Tallbear stated that the board had not seen independent market data and should not rely solely on management projections. Mr. Okonkwo and Ms. Rasmussen concurred that an independent market study was a prerequisite.

Mr. Fitzgerald moved and Mr. Farah seconded that the board authorize the General Manager to negotiate a purchase agreement for the Tower Avenue property, contingent on financing.

Ms. Tallbear then moved and Ms. Kozlowski seconded that the main motion be tabled pending receipt of an independent market study. The motion to table carried 7–2, with Directors Ramanathan, Okonkwo, Kozlowski, Suh, Tallbear, Sorensen, and Rasmussen voting in favor and Directors Fitzgerald and Farah voting against.

Ms. Rasmussen then moved and Mr. Okonkwo seconded that the board engage Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors to conduct a market study of the Superior, Wisconsin trade area at a cost not to exceed $22,000, with the report to be delivered before the February regular meeting. The motion carried 8–1, with Mr. Fitzgerald voting against on grounds of cost.

**Action items:** Mr. Haugen to execute the engagement letter with Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors by January 23 and to request a 90-day extension of the seller's letter of intent. Ms. Kozlowski to provide the consultant with financial data.

### 8. Committee Referrals

President Ramanathan referred the question of member-owner communication regarding the potential expansion to the Member Engagement Committee (chair: Ms. Tallbear), with a report due at the February meeting. She referred review of the board's capital expenditure policy to the Finance Committee (chair: Ms. Kozlowski), with a report due in March.

### 9. Adjournment

Mr. Suh moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded adjournment. The motion carried, and the meeting adjourned at 8:47 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Jae-Won Suh, Secretary
Approved: February 17, 2026

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**Body:** Board of Directors, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative
**Date:** Tuesday, February 17, 2026
**Time:** 6:04 p.m. – 9:21 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Linnea Sorensen, Hassan Farah, Colleen Rasmussen

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen (General Manager); Renata Voss (Finance Manager, staff); Priya Venkataraman (Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors); approximately thirty-five member-owners in the gallery

### 1. Call to Order and Determination of Quorum

President Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:04 p.m. Secretary Suh confirmed that all nine directors were present and that a quorum existed. Noting the unusually large gallery, President Ramanathan reviewed the meeting ground rules.

### 2. Approval of Agenda

Ms. Kozlowski moved and Mr. Okonkwo seconded approval of the agenda as distributed. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Approval of Minutes

Ms. Sorensen moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded approval of the minutes of the January 20, 2026 regular meeting. The motion carried unanimously.

### 4. Member-Owner Comment Period

Six member-owners spoke, and President Ramanathan extended the comment period to twenty minutes. Four speakers — Gail Pettersen, Dana Kivimäki, Robert Lindqvist, and Anne-Marie Chartier — spoke against expansion, citing the fiscal 2025 loss, the suspended state of the dividend question, and concern that a second store would divide management attention. Two speakers — Thomas Bruneau and Yusuf Adan — spoke in favor, citing food access in Superior and the risk of a competitor taking the site. Ms. Pettersen announced that member-owners were circulating a petition on the question. President Ramanathan thanked all speakers.

### 5. Treasurer's Report

Treasurer Kozlowski reported January 2026 results: sales of $1.44 million, down 0.6 percent from January 2025; gross margin of 34.4 percent, a modest improvement attributable to early actions under the margin recovery plan; an operating loss for the month of $21,000, better than the budgeted loss of $38,000 for the seasonally slow month; and cash of $1.37 million, or 27 days. She reported that the Wipfli audit fieldwork was complete and that the draft audit confirmed the preliminary $340,000 operating loss with no material adjustments. Mr. Okonkwo moved and Mr. Farah seconded acceptance of the report; the motion carried unanimously.

### 6. General Manager's Report

Mr. Haugen reported that the distributor renegotiation had concluded with an estimated annualized cost saving of $85,000; that the shrink task force had reduced produce shrink from 6.1 percent to 5.2 percent of department sales in six weeks; and that two department manager positions (Deli and Wellness) had been filled internally. He reported one lost-time workplace injury in the meat department, with corrective measures reviewed with the cooperative's insurer. The report was received without a motion.

### 7. Presentation of Market Study — Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors

Ms. Priya Venkataraman presented the Superior, Wisconsin market study, distributed to directors on February 12. Principal findings included:

- An estimated trade-area population of 41,000 with below-average but growing demand for natural and organic foods.
- Projected first-year sales for a store at the Tower Avenue site of $4.6 million, growing to $6.9 million in year four.
- Projected operating losses of $410,000 in year one, $240,000 in year two, and $90,000 in year three, with **break-even projected in year four** and a cumulative cash requirement, including losses, of approximately $5.0 million against the $4.2 million project budget.
- A sensitivity analysis showing that a 10 percent shortfall in sales would push break-even beyond year six.
- A finding that the parent store's current balance sheet provided "thin but not prohibitive" support for the project, conditioned on restoration of the parent store's margin to at least 35.5 percent before commitment.

Directors questioned Ms. Venkataraman for approximately forty minutes. Ms. Kozlowski asked whether the study had assumed a successful $1.0 million member loan campaign; Ms. Venkataraman confirmed it had, and noted that comparable single-store co-ops had raised such amounts in six to nine months. Ms. Tallbear asked what happened to the model if the parent store's recovery stalled; Ms. Venkataraman responded that the combined entity would face a covenant breach by mid-year-two. Mr. Fitzgerald asked whether the site was likely to remain available; Ms. Venkataraman said she could not opine on the seller's intentions.

### 8. Motion to Proceed with the Superior Expansion

Mr. Fitzgerald moved and Mr. Farah seconded: *That the board remove from the table and adopt the resolution authorizing the General Manager to negotiate a purchase agreement for the property at 1804 Tower Avenue, Superior, Wisconsin, at a total project cost not to exceed $4.2 million, contingent on financing commitments and a successful member loan campaign of not less than $1.0 million.*

The motion to take from the table carried 9–0. Debate on the main motion followed for approximately one hour. Mr. Fitzgerald, Mr. Farah, Ms. Sorensen, and Mr. Suh spoke in favor, emphasizing the study's confirmation of long-run viability and the strategic cost of remaining a single store in a consolidating market. Ms. Kozlowski, Ms. Tallbear, Mr. Okonkwo, Ms. Rasmussen, and President Ramanathan spoke against or expressed reservations, emphasizing the consultant's own precondition that parent-store margin be restored before commitment, the 27-day cash position, and the absence of demonstrated member-owner support for a loan campaign.

On a roll-call vote requested by Ms. Kozlowski, **the motion failed 5–4**: Directors Fitzgerald, Farah, Sorensen, and Suh voted in favor; Directors Ramanathan, Okonkwo, Kozlowski, Tallbear, and Rasmussen voted against.

Following the vote, Ms. Rasmussen moved and Mr. Okonkwo seconded that the board direct the General Manager to report by the April regular meeting on lower-cost alternatives for serving the Superior trade area, including a smaller-format store, and that no further negotiation on the Tower Avenue property occur in the interim. The motion carried 7–2, with Directors Fitzgerald and Farah voting against.

**Action items:** Mr. Haugen to prepare an alternatives analysis by April 14 for distribution before the April 21 meeting. Ms. Venkataraman to deliver her final written report by February 27.

### 9. Committee Reports and Referrals

Ms. Tallbear reported for the Member Engagement Committee that a member forum on the expansion question would be scheduled regardless of the evening's vote, and recommended a written survey of member-owners. President Ramanathan referred design of the survey to the Member Engagement Committee with a report due in March. Ms. Kozlowski reported that the Finance Committee's capital expenditure policy review remained on schedule for March.

### 10. Adjournment

Mr. Suh moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded adjournment. The motion carried, and the meeting adjourned at 9:21 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Jae-Won Suh, Secretary
Approved: March 17, 2026

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**Body:** Board of Directors, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative
**Date:** Tuesday, March 17, 2026
**Time:** 6:01 p.m. – 9:05 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Linnea Sorensen, Colleen Rasmussen

**Members Absent:** Hassan Farah (excused; travel)

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen (General Manager); Renata Voss (Finance Manager, staff); Erik Wipfli engagement partner Susan Trelawney (by video, item 6); approximately fifty member-owners in the gallery

### 1. Call to Order and Determination of Quorum

President Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:01 p.m. Secretary Suh confirmed that eight of nine directors were present and that a quorum existed.

### 2. Approval of Agenda

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded approval of the agenda, amended at President Ramanathan's request to add "General Manager Transition" as item 8. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Approval of Minutes

Ms. Kozlowski moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded approval of the minutes of the February 17, 2026 regular meeting. The motion carried unanimously.

### 4. Member-Owner Comment Period — Presentation of Petition

The entire twenty-minute comment period, extended by President Ramanathan to thirty minutes by unanimous consent, was devoted to member-owner comment on expansion. Member-owner Gail Pettersen, speaking for a group styling itself "Members for One Strong Store," formally presented a petition bearing **512 verified member-owner signatures** opposing any expansion project until the cooperative had recorded two consecutive profitable quarters and restored the patronage dividend. Ms. Pettersen read the petition text into the record; the petition was received by Secretary Suh and appended to these minutes as Attachment A.

Eight additional member-owners spoke, all in support of the petition: Dana Kivimäki, Robert Lindqvist, Anne-Marie Chartier, Priscilla Whitefeather, Joan Aho, Marcus Delray, Helen Sundstrom, and Peter Vukovich. Recurring themes were the fiscal 2025 loss, staff wages, and a desire for a binding member vote on any project exceeding $1 million. No speakers in favor of expansion addressed the board. President Ramanathan thanked the petitioners and stated that the board would formally respond in writing within thirty days.

Ms. Tallbear moved and Mr. Okonkwo seconded that the petition be referred to the Member Engagement Committee and the Governance Committee jointly, with a recommended board response due at the April 21 meeting, including analysis of whether the bylaws require or permit a member referendum on major capital projects. The motion carried unanimously.

### 5. Treasurer's Report

Treasurer Kozlowski reported February 2026 results: sales of $1.39 million, up 1.9 percent over February 2025; gross margin of 34.9 percent, continuing the recovery trend; an operating surplus for the month of $9,000, the first monthly surplus since September 2025; and cash of $1.41 million, or 28 days. Personnel costs ran at 24.6 percent of sales, slightly above the 24.0 percent target. Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded acceptance of the report; the motion carried unanimously.

### 6. Presentation of the Audited Financial Statements

Susan Trelawney of Wipfli LLP presented the audited financial statements for fiscal 2025 by video conference. The audit rendered an unmodified opinion, confirming the operating loss of $340,000 and a net loss after non-operating items of $296,000. Ms. Trelawney noted one significant deficiency in internal control relating to inventory count procedures in the prepared foods department, with a management response already drafted. Ms. Kozlowski moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded acceptance of the audited financial statements and the management letter. The motion carried unanimously.

**Action item:** Ms. Voss to implement the revised inventory count procedure by April 30 and report completion to the Finance Committee.

### 7. General Manager's Report

Mr. Haugen reported that the margin recovery plan remained on track; that the deli reset had been completed; and that a proposed staffing restructure would be presented to the incoming management. He then indicated he wished to defer further remarks to item 8. The report was received without a motion.

### 8. General Manager Transition — Resignation of Nils Haugen

President Ramanathan announced that on March 13, 2026 she had received General Manager Haugen's written **resignation, effective May 1, 2026**. Mr. Haugen addressed the board, stating that he had accepted a position with a food distribution firm in the Twin Cities, that his decision predated and was not caused by the February expansion vote, and that he was committed to an orderly transition. Directors thanked Mr. Haugen for eleven years of service.

Ms. Rasmussen moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded: *That the board accept the resignation of General Manager Nils Haugen effective May 1, 2026, with thanks for his service.* The motion carried 8–0.

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Kozlowski seconded: *That the board establish a General Manager Transition Committee consisting of Directors Ramanathan, Okonkwo, Kozlowski, and Tallbear, charged with (a) recommending an interim general manager to the board at the April 21 meeting, (b) drafting a search plan and position description for a permanent general manager, and (c) engaging counsel to review the transition agreement.* The motion carried 8–0.

**Action items:** President Ramanathan to convene the Transition Committee by March 24. Mr. Haugen to deliver a written transition memorandum covering vendor contracts, banking authorities, and personnel matters by April 10. Ms. Kozlowski to review signatory authority changes with the cooperative's bank effective May 1.

### 9. Committee Reports

Ms. Tallbear reported for the Member Engagement Committee that the member survey had been distributed on March 2 with 1,146 responses received to date; full results would be reported in April. Preliminary tabulation showed 61 percent of respondents opposed to expansion "at this time," 24 percent in favor, and 15 percent undecided. Ms. Kozlowski presented the Finance Committee's revised capital expenditure policy, which would require an independent feasibility study and a two-thirds board majority for any project exceeding $500,000. Ms. Kozlowski moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded adoption of the revised capital expenditure policy as presented. The motion carried 7–1, with Mr. Fitzgerald voting against.

### 10. Adjournment

Mr. Suh moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded adjournment. The motion carried, and the meeting adjourned at 9:05 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Jae-Won Suh, Secretary
Approved: April 21, 2026

## MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**Body:** Board of Directors, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative
**Date:** Tuesday, April 21, 2026
**Time:** 6:03 p.m. – 9:32 p.m.
**Place:** Community Room, Lake Superior Community Grocery Cooperative, Duluth, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Aditi Ramanathan (President), Dwayne Okonkwo (Vice President), Marta Kozlowski (Treasurer), Jae-Won Suh (Secretary), Bernadette Tallbear, Owen Fitzgerald, Linnea Sorensen, Hassan Farah, Colleen Rasmussen

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Nils Haugen (General Manager); Renata Voss (Finance Manager, staff); Simone Delacroix (Store Operations Manager, staff, item 7); approximately twenty-five member-owners in the gallery

### 1. Call to Order and Determination of Quorum

President Ramanathan called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m. Secretary Suh confirmed that all nine directors were present and that a quorum existed.

### 2. Approval of Agenda

Ms. Kozlowski moved and Mr. Farah seconded approval of the agenda, amended to add "Director Resignation and Board Vacancy" as item 10. The motion carried unanimously.

### 3. Approval of Minutes

Ms. Tallbear moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded approval of the minutes of the March 17, 2026 regular meeting. The motion carried unanimously, with Mr. Farah abstaining because he had been absent.

### 4. Member-Owner Comment Period

Four member-owners spoke. Gail Pettersen thanked the board for its written response to the petition and asked that the promised member forum be held before any new expansion decision. Thomas Bruneau urged the board not to abandon Superior entirely. Joan Aho asked about the status of the patronage dividend. Marcus Delray asked how the board vacancy would be filled. President Ramanathan responded that both the dividend and the vacancy were on the evening's agenda.

### 5. Treasurer's Report

Treasurer Kozlowski reported March 2026 results: sales of $1.56 million, up 2.7 percent over March 2025; gross margin of 35.2 percent; an operating surplus for the month of $27,000; and cash of $1.49 million, or 29 days. Year-to-date fiscal 2026 results showed a cumulative operating surplus of $15,000, ahead of the budgeted year-to-date loss of $30,000. Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded acceptance of the report; the motion carried unanimously.

### 6. Patronage Dividend for Fiscal 2025

Ms. Kozlowski presented the Finance Committee's recommendation that no patronage dividend be declared for fiscal 2025, given the audited net loss of $296,000 and the cooperative's cash position, and that retention of all fiscal 2025 results be applied to the accumulated deficit. She noted the bylaws vest dividend declaration in the board's discretion based on financial results.

Ms. Kozlowski moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded: *That the patronage dividend be suspended for fiscal 2025, that no allocation be made to member-owner patronage accounts for the year, and that management communicate the decision and its rationale to member-owners in the May newsletter and by e-mail.* Discussion followed; Mr. Suh asked that the communication commit to revisiting the dividend upon a return to profitability, and the movers accepted this as a friendly amendment. The motion as amended carried 9–0.

**Action item:** Ms. Voss and the Member Engagement Committee to issue the member communication by May 8.

### 7. General Manager's Report and Operations Update

Mr. Haugen, in his final report to the board, summarized the state of operations: gross margin recovery ahead of schedule; the inventory control deficiency remediated as of April 15; staffing stable at 112 employees; and the transition memorandum delivered April 9. Store Operations Manager Simone Delacroix briefed the board on the summer merchandising plan. The board thanked Mr. Haugen, and President Ramanathan presented a resolution of appreciation, adopted by acclamation.

### 8. Appointment of Interim General Manager

President Ramanathan reported for the General Manager Transition Committee. The committee had interviewed two internal candidates and one external candidate and unanimously recommended **Simone Delacroix**, Store Operations Manager and a nine-year employee of the cooperative, as Interim General Manager effective May 1, 2026, at an interim salary adjustment of $14,000 annualized, with a term through the appointment of a permanent general manager. The committee further recommended engaging Columinate to conduct the permanent search at a cost not to exceed $18,000, with a target appointment by October 2026. Ms. Delacroix left the room during deliberation.

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Tallbear seconded: *That Simone Delacroix be appointed Interim General Manager effective May 1, 2026, on the terms recommended by the Transition Committee, and that the President be authorized to execute the interim employment agreement.* The motion carried 9–0. Ms. Delacroix returned and accepted the appointment.

Ms. Kozlowski moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded engagement of Columinate for the permanent search on the recommended terms. The motion carried 9–0.

**Action items:** President Ramanathan to execute the interim agreement by April 28. Ms. Kozlowski to update banking signatory authorities effective May 1. The Transition Committee to finalize the position description with Columinate by May 15.

### 9. Superior Trade Area — Smaller-Format Proposal

Pursuant to the board's February 17 direction, Mr. Haugen presented an alternatives analysis, developed with input from Northern Tier Cooperative Advisors. The recommended alternative was a **smaller-format store of approximately 5,000 square feet in leased space in Superior**, at a total project cost of $1.15 million (leasehold improvements $650,000; equipment $350,000; inventory and pre-opening $150,000), with projected break-even in year two under the consultant's demand model. The analysis recommended that no commitment be made before (a) two consecutive profitable quarters at the Duluth store, (b) completion of the permanent general manager appointment, and (c) a member-owner advisory vote, consistent with the Governance Committee's response to the March petition.

Discussion followed. Mr. Fitzgerald stated that the smaller format was inferior to the Tower Avenue opportunity but preferable to inaction. Ms. Tallbear and Ms. Kozlowski supported the staged conditions. Mr. Farah asked that a food-access analysis be incorporated.

Ms. Rasmussen moved and Mr. Okonkwo seconded: *That the board adopt the smaller-format concept as the cooperative's working expansion framework; that management be directed to develop a full business plan, site options, and pro forma financials for board review no earlier than the September 2026 meeting; and that any commitment be conditioned on two consecutive profitable quarters, a permanent general manager in place, compliance with the capital expenditure policy adopted March 17, and a member-owner advisory vote.* The motion carried 8–1, with Mr. Fitzgerald voting against, stating for the record that he supported expansion but opposed relinquishing the Tower Avenue site.

**Action item:** Interim General Manager Delacroix to schedule the member forum on expansion, jointly with the Member Engagement Committee, by June 30.

### 10. Director Resignation and Board Vacancy

President Ramanathan reported that on April 17, 2026 she had received the written **resignation of Director Owen Fitzgerald, effective at the close of this meeting**. Mr. Fitzgerald stated that he was resigning because his commercial property interests on Superior Street and his continuing business interest in the Superior, Wisconsin market created a potential conflict of interest he preferred to remove entirely, and that he wished the cooperative well. Directors thanked Mr. Fitzgerald for four years of service.

Ms. Tallbear moved and Mr. Suh seconded acceptance of the resignation with thanks. The motion carried 8–0, with Mr. Fitzgerald abstaining.

Secretary Suh reported the Governance Committee's advice under Article V, Section 7 of the bylaws: with the annual member-owner election scheduled for **June 16, 2026**, the board could either appoint a member-owner to serve until the election or leave the seat vacant and add it to the ballot. The committee recommended leaving the seat vacant and placing the remaining one year of the term on the June ballot alongside the three regularly expiring seats, so that member-owners would fill it directly.

Mr. Suh moved and Ms. Sorensen seconded: *That the board leave the vacated seat unfilled and direct the Elections Committee to place the one-year unexpired term on the June 16, 2026 ballot, with candidate filings due May 15.* The motion carried 8–0.

**Action items:** Mr. Suh to notify the Elections Committee and publish the amended election notice by April 27. The Governance Committee to update the candidate packet by May 1.

### 11. Committee Reports

Ms. Tallbear presented the final member survey results (1,402 responses: 58 percent opposed to expansion at this time, 27 percent in favor, 15 percent undecided) and the joint Member Engagement/Governance Committee written response to the March petition, which the board endorsed by consensus for publication. Ms. Kozlowski reported that the Finance Committee would present the fiscal 2027 budget calendar in May.

### 12. Adjournment

Mr. Okonkwo moved and Ms. Rasmussen seconded adjournment. The motion carried, and the meeting adjourned at 9:32 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Jae-Won Suh, Secretary
Approved: May 19, 2026
