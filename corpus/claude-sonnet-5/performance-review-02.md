# Performance Review: Jerome Castellanos

**Title:** Eighth-Grade Mathematics Teacher, Mathematics Department Chair
**Review Period:** August 2025 – June 2026
**Reviewer:** Nadia Haddad, Principal
**School:** Saguaro Bend Middle School, Canyon Vista Unified School District

### I. Review of Prior-Year Goals

At the start of the year, Mr. Castellanos set three goals in consultation with me: (1) raise eighth-grade math median growth percentile above the district median of 52; (2) expand Saturday academy access to at-risk students identified through fall benchmark data; (3) mentor two second-year math teachers toward independent lesson design, in preparation for a possible department-lead succession plan.

Mr. Castellanos exceeded the growth target by a wide margin. His students' median growth percentile finished the year at 68, the highest of any tested grade or subject in the district, in a building where 62 percent of students qualify for free or reduced-price meals and where the school as a whole entered state targeted support after three years of declining math proficiency. This result stands out further because it was achieved without any softening of the tested population; his rosters included the full range of prior proficiency levels assigned to eighth grade.

The Saturday academy goal was met and surpassed. Mr. Castellanos organized and largely staffed a Saturday program that served 96 students across the year, with attendance data showing the heaviest concentration among students flagged in the fall benchmark as at risk of non-proficiency. This is his own initiative on top of contracted duties; the district provided a modest stipend for the first ten sessions and none thereafter, a point he raised directly with me in April.

On mentoring, progress was partial. He worked informally with one of the two assigned second-year teachers through co-planning during his contracted department-chair release period; the second mentee relationship did not develop consistently, in part because Mr. Castellanos's own instructional load and Saturday programming left limited additional time. I count this goal as met for one of two intended mentees.

**Self-Assessment (Castellanos):** *"I hit the growth number and then some, and I think the Saturday program is the best work I've done here — but I want to be honest that I did not get consistent time with both new teachers, and I don't think I can sustain the Saturday program at this level again without either more staffing or clearer support from the district. I also want to say plainly that I applied for the AP residency stipend in January because I want to grow here, not leave, but I need to know there's an actual path, and right now I don't."*

### II. Observation Record and Incidents

Mr. Castellanos was observed formally three times (October, January, April) and informally on eleven additional walkthroughs. All three formal observations rated Highly Effective on instructional delivery, with particular strength noted in questioning technique and use of formative data to regroup students mid-lesson. The April observation, conducted during a Saturday academy session at his invitation, showed the same rigor and pacing as his contracted classes.

No disciplinary or compliance incidents are on record for Mr. Castellanos this year. His department completed all required data submissions on time, and he represented the school at two district curriculum council meetings without being asked twice.

The one recurring concern raised by department members in my end-of-year chair check-ins is availability: two teachers noted that Mr. Castellanos's Saturday commitment reduced his responsiveness to routine department questions on Fridays. I raised this with him directly in May; he acknowledged it and proposed shifting some Saturday prep to a weekday block next year.

### III. State Rubric Rating

| Domain | Rating |
|---|---|
| Instructional Practice | Highly Effective |
| Student Growth | Highly Effective |
| Professional Responsibilities | Effective |
| Learning Environment | Highly Effective |
| **Overall Rating** | **Highly Effective** |

This is one of a limited number of Highly Effective ratings I am able to assign this year under the district instruction capping such ratings at 20 percent of the building's teaching staff. I want to be direct in this document that the data supports this rating without qualification, and that assigning it here means declining to assign it elsewhere among strong candidates. I am comfortable with that tradeoff given the growth data and the Saturday program outcomes, but the underlying cap is a constraint I am flagging for the record, not one I regard as reflecting Mr. Castellanos's performance.

### IV. Staffing and Compensation Context

Two points bear directly on this evaluation and on retention risk, and I am documenting both plainly.

First, the assistant-principal residency stipend Mr. Castellanos applied for in January remains frozen by board action, with no announced date for reconsideration. I have no authority to unfreeze it at the building level, and I told him so in April when he raised the issue.

Second, the school will lose two positions in the spring due to a projected 6 percent enrollment decline. I do not anticipate this affecting the math department directly given current staffing ratios, and I have told Mr. Castellanos as much, but I recognize that a hiring freeze context alongside a stipend freeze reasonably reads to him as an absence of any advancement path in this building. He told me in April that he will look outside the district absent a path forward. I consider this a serious retention risk given that he is, by measurable outcomes, the strongest teacher in the building's highest-need subject.

I am forwarding a written recommendation to the district that the AP residency stipend freeze be reconsidered for Mr. Castellanos specifically, or that an alternative compensated leadership pathway (district math coach, mentor-teacher stipend) be created before the fall semester. This recommendation is outside my unilateral authority to grant but is, in my judgment, necessary to retain him.

### V. Goals for 2026–2027

1. Sustain median growth percentile at or above 60 while absorbing any roster changes from the spring position reductions.
2. Formalize the Saturday academy into a documented, transferable model — schedule, staffing needs, and materials list — so it does not depend entirely on Mr. Castellanos's personal availability and can survive staffing changes.
3. Complete structured mentoring with both assigned second-year teachers, with a fixed weekly meeting block protected on the master schedule.
4. In partnership with the principal, pursue a concrete leadership pathway conversation with the district by October, independent of the frozen stipend, including documentation of the growth and program outcomes above as the basis for that conversation.

# Performance Review: Yolanda Begay

**Title:** Seventh-Grade Language Arts Teacher, Master Schedule and State Testing Coordinator
**Review Period:** August 2025 – June 2026
**Reviewer:** Nadia Haddad, Principal
**School:** Saguaro Bend Middle School, Canyon Vista Unified School District

### I. Review of Prior-Year Goals

Ms. Begay's goals for the year were: (1) maintain consistently effective classroom observation ratings while piloting a new argumentative-writing unit; (2) manage the state assessment materials order and testing calendar without disruption; (3) turn around routine schedule-correction requests from department chairs within five instructional days.

On classroom instruction, Ms. Begay met her goal. Her observation ratings were effective across every formal and informal visit this year, and the argumentative-writing pilot she developed is being adopted building-wide next year at her recommendation and with my support. Students in her sections showed strong engagement with the unit, and two colleagues cited her materials directly when planning their own units this spring.

On testing coordination, Ms. Begay did not meet the goal. The February order for state assessment materials was missed. As a direct consequence, 210 students across the building were routed into a make-up testing window in the spring, which compressed the testing calendar for other grade levels, required additional proctor coverage on short notice, and drew a formal inquiry from the district assessment office regarding the cause of the delay. Ms. Begay identified the cause as a missed vendor deadline she did not calendar correctly and did not have a backup check in place to catch.

On schedule corrections, the record does not support that the five-day goal was met. Three of four department chairs reported to me independently this spring that routine correction requests — course-level swaps, period conflicts, roster duplications — regularly took several weeks to resolve, with two chairs describing having to escalate the same request more than once. This is a substantive operational gap given that Ms. Begay holds this responsibility as a defined part of her assignment, separate from her classroom teaching duties, and it affected instructional planning time for the affected chairs.

**Self-Assessment (Begay):** *"The writing unit is the best work I've done, and I'm proud of it. I own the testing materials miss completely — I did not have a second check on that calendar and I should have. I think the schedule-correction backlog is real but I also want to note that I'm doing that job on top of a full teaching load with no additional release time, and the volume of requests this year, especially with the enrollment changes already being discussed for spring, was higher than in prior years. I'm not saying that as an excuse for the testing miss, which was mine alone, but I think it's relevant to the schedule turnaround issue."*

### II. Observation Record and Incidents

Ms. Begay was observed formally three times (September, December, March) and rated effective on all three, with strength consistently noted in student discourse structures and formative check-ins during independent writing time. No instructional concerns are on record.

Two incidents are documented outside the classroom observation record. The first is the February materials-order miss described above, documented in an email exchange with the district assessment office dated February 19 and a follow-up corrective action memo I issued March 2, which Ms. Begay signed without dispute. The second is the pattern of schedule-correction delays, documented through written notes from three department chairs (math, science, and social studies) submitted to me between March and May at my request after I began hearing informal complaints.

I met with Ms. Begay on May 14 to review both matters. She did not dispute either record and proposed, on her own initiative, a written intake log for schedule requests with target turnaround dates, which I have approved for implementation in the fall.

### III. State Rubric Rating

| Domain | Rating |
|---|---|
| Instructional Practice | Effective |
| Student Growth | Effective |
| Professional Responsibilities | Developing |
| Learning Environment | Effective |
| **Overall Rating** | **Effective** |

The Professional Responsibilities rating of Developing reflects the testing-materials incident and the documented pattern of delayed schedule corrections, both of which fall within Ms. Begay's coordination duties rather than her instructional duties. Her instructional practice remains solidly effective and is not in question. The overall rating of Effective is a composite that reflects strong teaching alongside a coordination role that did not meet expectations this year; it does not place her among the building's Highly Effective ratings this cycle, a designation for which the district cap of 20 percent leaves limited room in any case, but I want to be clear this rating stands on the record independent of that cap.

### IV. Staffing Context

The projected two-position reduction in the spring, driven by the 6 percent enrollment decline, does not put Ms. Begay's teaching position at risk given seventh-grade section counts. However, I want to note for the record that the master-schedule and testing-coordination duties will become more complex, not less, as the school absorbs a position reduction — course offerings will need to be consolidated and rosters rebalanced, which increases the coordination workload at precisely the time this year's performance record shows strain in that role. I am recommending to the district that this coordination role be reconsidered for a defined release-period allocation next year given the added complexity; this is not currently funded and I do not control that allocation.

### V. Goals for 2026–2027

1. Complete the state assessment materials order and calendar with a documented second-check process, confirmed in writing to the principal by January 15.
2. Implement the written schedule-request intake log with a five-instructional-day turnaround target, tracked monthly, with a mid-year check-in with all four department chairs.
3. Lead building-wide rollout of the argumentative-writing unit, including one shared planning session per grade-level team by October.
4. Absorb spring master-schedule consolidation resulting from the projected staffing reduction with a documented rebalancing plan submitted to the principal no later than April, given the added complexity this will introduce to the coordination role.

# Performance Review: Dustin Marchetti

**Title:** Eighth-Grade Science Teacher
**Review Period:** August 2025 – June 2026
**Reviewer:** Nadia Haddad, Principal
**School:** Saguaro Bend Middle School, Canyon Vista Unified School District

### I. Review of Prior-Year Goals

Mr. Marchetti entered this year on an improvement plan opened in the prior spring following two developing-level observations. The plan, which closed successfully in March, set three goals: (1) raise the science proficiency rate on the fall-to-spring benchmark from its prior 19 percent baseline; (2) resolve the specific instructional gaps identified in the two developing observations, principally pacing and checks for understanding; (3) maintain attendance and communication practices consistent with district expectations, including timely parent contact regarding grading.

On the primary academic goal, Mr. Marchetti made real and documented progress. His proficiency rate rose from 19 percent to 31 percent over the course of the year, a 12-point gain that is a significant improvement and reflects genuine instructional growth, particularly in the second semester after the improvement plan's coaching cycles began showing results in classroom practice.

On the instructional gaps identified in the improvement plan, the record shows sufficient improvement to close the plan in March. Pacing and checks for understanding, both cited as deficiencies in his two prior developing observations, showed measurable improvement in the two observations conducted after the plan opened, discussed further below.

On the third goal, the record is mixed. A parent complaint regarding grading practices was escalated to the district office in October; the complaint concerned inconsistent application of a late-work policy and unclear communication about make-up assignments. The district's review found the underlying policy application inconsistent but did not find it warranted disciplinary action; it directed a corrective conversation, which I held with Mr. Marchetti in November, and he adjusted his syllabus language and grading calendar afterward without further complaints this year. Separately, Mr. Marchetti missed nine days of instruction in the second semester. I do not have documentation classifying all nine absences, and I want to note that some fell within protected leave categories; however, the frequency during a semester in which he was also working to close an improvement plan is a factor I am recording here because of its effect on instructional continuity, particularly given that substitute coverage in science lab settings is difficult to secure reliably in this building.

**Self-Assessment (Marchetti):** *"I know this was a hard year to prove myself in, and I think the numbers show real growth — I worked hard on pacing especially, because that was the clearest feedback I got. The parent complaint stung, but I understand where the confusion came from and I fixed my syllabus. On the absences, some of that was medical and I don't think it's fair to treat all nine days the same, but I also know missing that much time hurt continuity for my students, especially in a year I was trying to show consistency. I want to be evaluated on where I ended the year, not just where I started it."*

### II. Observation Record and Incidents

Mr. Marchetti's improvement plan file includes two developing-level observations from the prior spring (cited in the plan's opening but outside this review period) and four observations conducted this year: October, December (improvement-plan checkpoint), January, and April.

The October observation, conducted shortly after the plan opened, rated Developing, consistent with the pacing and questioning concerns already on record. The December checkpoint observation, conducted jointly with the district instructional coach assigned under the plan, showed improvement in pacing but continued inconsistency in checks for understanding, and rated Developing with documented growth noted in the narrative. The January observation rated Effective for the first time, with clear improvement in formative questioning and lesson transitions. The April observation, conducted after the plan's formal close, rated Effective, sustaining the January gains.

Two non-instructional incidents are on the record for this period: the October parent complaint regarding grading, resolved as described above with a corrective conversation and no further incidents; and the pattern of nine absences in the second semester, documented in attendance records, with substitute coverage notes indicating three of the nine days required lab activities to be canceled or substantially modified rather than covered as planned.

### III. State Rubric Rating

| Domain | Rating |
|---|---|
| Instructional Practice | Developing |
| Student Growth | Effective |
| Professional Responsibilities | Developing |
| Learning Environment | Effective |
| **Overall Rating** | **Developing** |

This overall rating reflects a teacher who has made real, documented gains and closed an improvement plan successfully, but who has not yet sustained a full year of effective-level practice across all domains. The two most recent observations rated Effective, which is the basis for optimism reflected in next year's goals below, but the overall annual rating necessarily accounts for the full period, including the earlier developing-level observations and the professional-responsibilities concerns tied to the parent complaint and attendance pattern. This rating is not affected by the district's 20 percent cap on Highly Effective ratings, as it is well below that threshold; I note this only to be clear that the cap played no role in this determination.

### IV. Staffing Context

The projected loss of two positions in the spring, driven by the 6 percent enrollment decline, creates direct uncertainty for Mr. Marchetti given his position in his third year and his rating this cycle. I want to state plainly what I told him in our May conference: a Developing rating alone does not place him first in line for position reduction under district seniority-and-performance guidelines, particularly given the documented growth trajectory and the two most recent Effective observations, but a third-year teacher exiting an improvement plan is more exposed than a tenured colleague with a stronger overall-year record if the district's reduction process weighs this year's composite rating. I am recommending to the district, in writing, that Mr. Marchetti's placement determination give weight to the trajectory reflected in the January and April observations rather than the full-year composite alone, given the documented improvement. This recommendation is advisory; the final placement decision rests with the district staffing office.

### V. Goals for 2026–2027

1. Sustain Effective-level instructional practice across all formal observations for the full year, building on the January and April gains, with a particular focus on checks for understanding as the remaining area of inconsistency.
2. Raise proficiency rate to 40 percent or higher, continuing the current growth trajectory.
3. Implement and maintain a written grading and late-work policy, distributed to families in the first two weeks of the year and reviewed with the principal before distribution, to prevent recurrence of the prior grading complaint.
4. Meet with the principal by September to establish an attendance and continuity plan, including advance lab-coverage arrangements for any anticipated absences, given the documented impact of missed lab days on instructional continuity this year.

He was given the opportunity to respond in writing; his response is incorporated above as the self-assessment excerpt and is otherwise attached in full to the personnel file.
