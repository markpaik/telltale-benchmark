We run 140 beds a night at Ustick and Fairview combined. Last night was 138. We've been at or above 132 every night since March 9. We currently serve 394 PSH households with 19 case managers. That's 1:20.7. We have three case manager vacancies we've been slow-walking since February for exactly the reasons Roberto describes.

The ratio breaks at 18 case managers. At 18 we're at 1:21.9, which is technically compliant and practically not, because that assumes nobody takes vacation, nobody is out sick, and nobody quits. At 17 we're at 1:23.2 and we're in breach.

What happens in breach: the contract's remedy section gives HUD and the local CoC lead the option to require a corrective action plan — another one — and if uncured within 60 days, to reduce or terminate the award. I have never seen them terminate over a caseload ratio. I have seen them reduce. And I'd point out that we would be asking HUD to accept a corrective action plan on documentation while simultaneously generating a second compliance failure on service delivery. I don't think that's a good posture.

Furloughs don't help the way people think they do. If I furlough a case manager for two weeks, the households don't go anywhere. The ratio counts assigned households against filled positions. A furloughed position is not a filled position for compliance purposes — I checked this with the CoC lead in 2021 during the last budget scare and got it in writing. So a furlough of even two case managers puts us at 17 and in breach.

Where there is actual flex: I have 4 FTE in shelter operations that are funded entirely from unrestricted and private dollars — two overnight monitors, a kitchen coordinator, and a part-time intake specialist that we round to 0.5. Cutting those saves roughly $19,000 a month and does not touch a federal compliance ratio.
