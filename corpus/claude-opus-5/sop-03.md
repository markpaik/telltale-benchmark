## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure establishes how CACS qualifies shippers, accepts, rejects, plans, loads, and reports dangerous goods offered for air transport, and how it maintains the training and records that support those activities.

It exists because the company's prior practice did not meet the standard required of it. In 2025 the FAA assessed a civil penalty of $178,000 following an undeclared lithium-ion battery pack that vented in the Anchorage warehouse. The internal audit that followed found errors on 14 of 50 shipper's declarations sampled, three acceptance checklists signed by agents whose recurrent dangerous goods training had lapsed beyond the 24-month interval, and no written procedure covering hidden or undeclared goods found at the counter. This document closes those three findings and establishes a single controlled method across all seventeen points at which CACS touches a dangerous goods shipment.

---

## 2. SCOPE

**2.1 Applies to.** All dangerous goods offered to CACS for carriage by air, on scheduled or charter service, on any of the company's nine aircraft, at the Anchorage cargo counter or at any of the eight village stations that accept freight. It applies to freight tendered by commercial shippers, government agencies, tribal health organizations, air taxi interline partners, and walk-up individual shippers.

**2.2 Also applies to.** Company material (COMAT) offered by CACS itself, dangerous goods carried on medevac charters, and dangerous goods discovered in cargo already accepted as general freight.

**2.3 Does not apply to.** Dangerous goods carried by passengers or crew as baggage on any CACS combi or charter passenger operation; those are governed by CACS-DG-004. Fuel, oil, and hydraulic fluid in aircraft systems. Items forming part of the aircraft's required equipment.

**2.4 Precedence.** Where this procedure is more restrictive than 49 CFR or the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR), this procedure governs. Where a regulation is more restrictive than this procedure, the regulation governs and the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager must be notified within one business day so the procedure can be corrected. No employee may waive a step in this procedure. Only the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager, with the written concurrence of the Vice President of Operations, may authorize a deviation, and only in writing, on form DG-215, retained for three years.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Acceptance agent.** An employee trained and qualified under 14 CFR 121 Subpart Z / IATA DGR 1.5 to perform the first acceptance check of a dangerous goods shipment.

**Acceptance check.** The two-person verification of documents, packaging, marks, labels, and quantity performed before a dangerous goods shipment is taken into CACS custody.

**CAO.** Cargo Aircraft Only. A shipment or package that may not be carried aboard an aircraft carrying passengers.

**DG checker.** An employee qualified to perform the second, independent acceptance check. A DG checker holds current dangerous goods training and has completed CACS internal qualification DG-Q2.

**Duty officer.** The operations supervisor on call at all times, reachable at (907) 555-0147, who has authority to stop a shipment, stop a loading operation, or recall an aircraft prior to departure.

**Hermes.** The CACS cargo management system of record for bookings, acceptance, load planning, and document imaging.

**Hidden dangerous goods.** Articles or substances meeting the criteria of a dangerous good that are not evident from the shipping description, packaging, or shipper's statement.

**NOTOC.** Notification to Captain. The document listing dangerous goods aboard, their location, quantity, and emergency response information, provided to the pilot-in-command before departure.

**Overpack.** An enclosure used by one shipper to contain one or more packages and form one handling unit.

**Pre-alert.** Advance electronic notice from a shipper of an intended dangerous goods tender, submitted before the shipment arrives at the counter.

**Qualified shipper.** A shipper listed on the CACS Approved Dangerous Goods Shipper Register, having met the requirements of Section 5.

**Section II lithium batteries.** Lithium cells and batteries meeting the smaller-quantity provisions of IATA DGR Packing Instructions 965–970 Section II, as further limited by CACS in Section 8.4.

**Shipper's Declaration.** The Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods, required by 49 CFR 172.200–205 and IATA DGR 8.1.

**Undeclared dangerous goods.** Dangerous goods presented as general cargo, without declaration, documentation, marking, or labeling.

**Village station.** A CACS-staffed facility at Bethel, Nome, Kotzebue, Dillingham, Aniak, Unalakleet, St. Mary's, or Emmonak.

## 5. PROCEDURE — SHIPPER QUALIFICATION AND PRE-ALERT

**5.1 Approved Shipper Register.** CACS accepts dangerous goods only from shippers listed on the Approved Dangerous Goods Shipper Register maintained in Hermes under Compliance → Shippers, except walk-up shippers handled under 5.7.

**5.2 To add a shipper to the Register**, the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager collects and files the following:

1. A completed form DG-100, Shipper Qualification Application, signed by an officer or authorized agent of the shipper.
2. Copies of dangerous goods training certificates for each individual who will prepare declarations, showing a completion date within the previous 24 months and the name of the training provider.
3. A sample Shipper's Declaration prepared by the shipper for a commodity they intend to ship.
4. A written acknowledgment that the shipper will notify CACS within five business days of any change in trained personnel.

**5.3 Review.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager reviews the application within 10 business days. The sample declaration is checked against IATA DGR 8.1.6 for all required elements. If the sample contains any error, the manager returns it with the specific citation and the shipper resubmits. Two consecutive rejected samples require the shipper's personnel to complete retraining before reapplying.

**5.4 Approval and expiry.** Approved shippers receive a Register number in the format SHP-YYYY-NNN. Registration expires 24 months from the date of the shipper's most recent training certificate. Hermes flags registrations 60 days before expiry and blocks booking on the expiry date.

**5.5 Pre-alert.** Approved shippers submit a pre-alert through the Hermes shipper portal at least four hours before tender for scheduled service, and at least two hours before tender for same-day charter. The pre-alert states: proper shipping name, UN number, class or division, packing group, number of packages, net quantity per package, packing instruction, whether the shipment is CAO, intended flight date, and origin and destination.

**5.6 Pre-alert screening.** Load planning reviews each pre-alert within one hour of receipt and confirms in Hermes that the commodity and quantity can be accommodated on the intended aircraft type under Section 8. If it cannot, load planning notifies the shipper and offers the next feasible flight. A shipment tendered without a pre-alert is not refused outright, but it goes to the back of the acceptance queue and may not be accepted within 90 minutes of a scheduled departure.

**5.7 Walk-up shippers.** An individual or small business not on the Register may tender dangerous goods only if all of the following apply: the shipment is a single package; it is a commodity listed in the CACS Walk-Up Commodity List posted at each counter (Section II lithium batteries in equipment, dry ice under 2.5 kg, small arms ammunition in Division 1.4S under 5 kg net, and consumer commodities); a qualified acceptance agent prepares no part of the shipper's documentation; and the DG checker performs the full second check. Walk-up tenders of any other commodity are refused with a copy of the Register application form.

**5.8 Prohibition on preparation.** No CACS employee may prepare, complete, correct, or dictate the content of a Shipper's Declaration. An employee may point out that a field is blank or that an entry does not match the package. An employee may not tell a shipper what to write. This prohibition is absolute and is a terminable offense.

## 6. PROCEDURE — DOCUMENT VERIFICATION

**6.1 Sequence.** Documents are verified before packages are examined. If documents fail, the packages are not touched further and the shipment is rejected under Section 9.

**6.2 Documents required at tender.**

1. Air waybill, with the dangerous goods handling information in the Nature and Quantity of Goods box and the statement "Dangerous Goods as per attached Shipper's Declaration."
2. Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods, two copies, signed in ink or bearing a compliant electronic signature, with red hatched borders.
3. For shipments not requiring a declaration (Section II lithium batteries, dry ice as a refrigerant, excepted quantities), the applicable document or marking substitute per IATA DGR.
4. Any required state or operator approval, competent authority approval, or written permission, in original or certified copy.

**6.3 Line-by-line check of the Shipper's Declaration.** The acceptance agent verifies each of the following against form DG-201 Part A, ticking each line. A blank line is not a completed check.

1. Shipper name and address, complete, matching the air waybill.
2. Consignee name and address, complete, matching the air waybill.
3. Air waybill number present and matching.
4. Page numbering ("Page 1 of 1" or correct sequence).
5. Aircraft type box: the inapplicable option deleted, not both left standing. This was the single most common error in the 2025 audit and is checked first.
6. Airport of departure and airport of destination, spelled out.
7. Shipment type: non-radioactive or radioactive, inapplicable deleted.
8. UN or ID number, with prefix.
9. Proper shipping name, matching the current IATA list exactly, with technical name in parentheses where the entry requires it.
10. Class or division, with subsidiary risk in parentheses where applicable.
11. Packing group, where the entry has one.
12. Number and type of packages, and net quantity per package, with units.
13. Packing instruction number, matching the entry and quantity.
14. Authorizations, where required, quoted in full.
15. Additional handling information, including the 24-hour emergency response telephone number with country code, monitored, and the name of the responsible person.
16. Shipper's certification statement, unaltered.
17. Name, title, place, date, and signature of the signatory, all present.
18. No alteration or erasure in the description block. Amendments are permitted only if signed by the shipper in the same ink and dated.

**6.4 Quantity cross-check.** The agent computes the total net quantity per package and confirms it does not exceed the maximum net quantity per package for the stated packing instruction and aircraft type. For Q-value shipments (multiple dangerous goods in one package), the agent computes the Q value and confirms it does not exceed 1.

**6.5 Compatibility check.** The agent confirms the declared commodity is permitted on the intended aircraft, is not forbidden, and is not on the CACS Embargoed Commodity List posted at each counter.

**6.6 Second check.** The DG checker independently repeats 6.3 through 6.5 without reference to the agent's completed Part A. The DG checker completes Part B. Where the two checks disagree on any line, the shipment is held and the duty officer is called. The duty officer contacts the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager for adjudication. The resolution is recorded in the remarks block of DG-201.

**6.7 Imaging.** Both copies of the Shipper's Declaration, the air waybill, and the completed DG-201 are scanned into Hermes against the air waybill number before the shipment leaves the acceptance area. Acceptance is not complete until the scan is confirmed in Hermes.

## 7. PROCEDURE — PACKAGE MARKING, LABELING, AND CONDITION INSPECTION

**7.1 Physical inspection.** After documents pass, the acceptance agent inspects every package in the shipment. For shipments of more than 20 identical packages, the agent inspects a minimum of 20 packages plus 10 percent of the remainder, selected at random, and inspects 100 percent for external damage.

**7.2 Package condition.** The agent confirms each package is:

1. Free of leakage, staining, dampness, crystalline deposit, odor, bulging, or frost patterning inconsistent with the declared contents.
2. Structurally sound, with no crushed corners, punctures, or failed closures.
3. Of a type authorized by the stated packing instruction.
4. Bearing a legible UN specification marking where the packing instruction requires performance packaging, with the marking consistent with the packing group and substance.

**7.3 Marks.** The agent confirms the presence, legibility, durability, and correctness of:

1. Proper shipping name and UN number, adjacent to each other, on the outer package.
2. Full name and address of shipper and consignee.
3. Net quantity marking where required.
4. Package orientation arrows on two opposite vertical sides for combination packagings containing liquids, where required.
5. Overpack marking, where an overpack is used, reading "Overpack" and repeating the marks and labels of the inner packages unless they are visible.
6. Specification marking for dry ice: "Dry ice" or "Carbon dioxide, solid," UN1845, and net mass in kilograms.
7. Lithium battery mark, with the correct UN number, for Section II shipments where required.

**7.4 Labels.** The agent confirms:

1. Hazard labels of the correct class and division, diamond shape, minimum 100 mm per side, on the same surface as and near the proper shipping name mark.
2. Subsidiary risk labels present where required, without the class number in the lower corner where IATA so requires.
3. Cargo Aircraft Only label present, orange, on the same surface as the hazard label, for any CAO package.
4. Handling labels present as required: Cryogenic Liquid, Magnetized Material, Keep Away From Heat, Lithium Battery.
5. No irrelevant, obsolete, or conflicting labels remaining on the package. Reused boxes with old labels are the second most common defect at village stations. Old labels must be removed or completely obliterated by the shipper, not by CACS staff.

**7.5 Reconciliation.** The agent confirms the number of packages presented equals the number declared, and that each package's marks match the corresponding line of the declaration.

**7.6 Second check.** The DG checker independently inspects the packages under 7.2 through 7.5 and completes Part B of DG-201.

**7.7 Radioactive material.** Shipments of Class 7 require the additional checks on form DG-203 (transport index, criticality safety index, surface radiation level measured with a calibrated survey meter, category label, and package identification). Only DG checkers holding CACS qualification DG-Q3 may accept Class 7. Anchorage and Bethel are the only stations authorized to accept Class 7.

## 8. PROCEDURE — QUANTITY AND SEGREGATION LIMITS BY AIRCRAFT TYPE

**8.1 Aircraft categories.** For the purposes of this procedure, the CACS fleet is grouped as:

| Category | Aircraft | Configuration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Beech 1900C (3) | Cargo only | No accessible cargo compartment in flight |
| B | Cessna 208B Caravan (4) | Cargo only | Single pilot, no fire suppression in cargo area |
| C | SAAB 340B (2) | Combi, forward passenger section | Passenger-carrying limits apply forward of the barrier |

**8.2 General limits.**

1. Category A and B aircraft may carry CAO packages. Category C aircraft may carry CAO packages **only** when operated in all-cargo configuration with no passengers and with the configuration recorded on the load sheet by the load planner and countersigned by the pilot-in-command.
2. Total net quantity of Class 3 flammable liquids on any Category B aircraft is limited to 60 litres per flight. On Category A, 220 litres. On Category C in all-cargo configuration, 220 litres. On Category C with passengers, Class 3 in Packing Group I is not carried.
3. Division 1.4S explosives (ammunition) are limited to 25 kg net explosive mass on Category B, 75 kg on Category A and C. No other explosives division is accepted on any CACS aircraft.
4. Dry ice is limited to 200 kg per flight across all aircraft categories, and to 100 kg per compartment on Category A and B. Dry ice is not carried on Category C with passengers in excess of 50 kg. Ventilation must be confirmed by the load planner against the aircraft flight manual supplement before dispatch.
5. Aviation fuel in drums (UN1863, Fuel, aviation, turbine engine, or UN1203 Gasoline) is CAO only, accepted in UN-specification steel drums of no more than 220 litres, no more than two drums per Category A flight, no more than one per Category B flight, and never on Category C.
6. Section II lithium batteries: no more than one Section II package per consignment, and no more than 10 Section II packages per flight on Category B. Section II lithium ion batteries and cells shipped alone (PI 965 Section II) are **not accepted by CACS on any aircraft**; only batteries contained in or packed with equipment (PI 966, 967, 969, 970 Section II) are accepted. This restriction is more stringent than IATA and follows directly from the 2025 venting event.

**8.3 Segregation.** Load planners apply the IATA DGR Table 9.3.A separation table. In addition, CACS requires:

1. Class 3 and Division 5.1 packages separated by not less than 2 metres or by an intervening solid barrier.
2. Class 8 packages loaded below and outboard of all foodstuffs, and never above any other package.
3. Division 6.2 infectious substances and diagnostic specimens loaded so they are not in contact with foodstuffs and are readily accessible for removal on the ground.
4. Dry ice loaded away from live animals by not less than 1 metre and never in the same closed compartment as live animals unless the compartment is ventilated per the flight manual.
5. Radioactive material segregated per the transport index table and never within 1 metre of a crew station.
6. Lithium batteries not loaded adjacent to Class 1 or Class 3 packages.

**8.4 Aggregate quantity check.** Before releasing a load plan, the load planner runs the Hermes DG Aggregate Report for the flight and confirms every limit in 8.2 is satisfied. The report is printed and attached to the load sheet. If the report shows an exceedance, the load planner removes packages beginning with the lowest-priority booking until the report clears, and notifies the affected shippers.

## 9. PROCEDURE — ACCEPTANCE OR REJECTION

**9.1 Acceptance.** A shipment is accepted only when every applicable line of DG-201 Parts A and B is ticked, both signatures are present with printed names and employee numbers, the scan is confirmed in Hermes, and the Hermes acceptance status reads ACCEPTED. The agent applies the CACS acceptance stamp to both copies of the Shipper's Declaration, retains one, and returns one to the shipper.

**9.2 No conditional acceptance.** There is no such thing as accepting a shipment pending correction. A shipment either passes or is rejected. Packages may not be taken into the warehouse, staged, or moved airside before acceptance is complete.

**9.3 Rejection.** If any line of DG-201 cannot be ticked, the acceptance agent rejects the shipment. The agent:

1. Stops the check at the point of failure. Further checks are not required.
2. Completes form DG-205, Dangerous Goods Rejection Notice, recording the air waybill number, shipper name and Register number, date and time, station, the specific defect, and the exact regulatory citation (for example, "IATA DGR 8.1.6.9.2 — aircraft type box, both options left undeleted").
3. Marks the shipment status REJECTED in Hermes, which locks the booking against reacceptance without a new DG-201.
4. Gives the shipper the pink copy of DG-205 and retains the white copy.
5. Returns all packages to the shipper. Packages are not stored, held for correction, or placed in the warehouse.
6. Notifies the station manager before the end of shift.

**9.4 Resubmission.** A rejected shipment may be re-tendered after correction by the shipper. Re-tender starts a completely new acceptance check with a new DG-201. The prior DG-205 number is entered in the remarks block of the new DG-201.

**9.5 Rejection tracking.** Hermes compiles rejections by shipper. Three rejections by the same shipper within any rolling 90 days trigger a review by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager, who may require retraining of the shipper's personnel, place the shipper on a 100 percent second-check regime, or recommend suspension to the Vice President of Operations. Five rejections within 90 days require suspension from the Register until the shipper's personnel present new training certificates.

**9.6 Shipper dispute.** A shipper who disputes a rejection may ask for review by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager. The shipment remains rejected during the review. No station employee reverses a rejection. Only the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager may reverse one, in writing, on the DG-205 remarks block, with the reason stated.

## 10. PROCEDURE — LOAD PLANNING AND NOTIFICATION TO CAPTAIN

**10.1 Load plan build.** After acceptance, the load planner assigns each dangerous goods package a loading position in Hermes, applying Section 8. The planner records compartment, position, orientation where required, and securing method.

**10.2 Accessibility.** Where practicable, dangerous goods are loaded so they are accessible for inspection and removal on the ground. Packages of Division 6.2, Class 7, and any package requiring orientation are loaded last and outermost.

**10.3 Securing.** Ramp handlers secure all dangerous goods packages with cargo nets, straps, or tie-downs so they cannot shift. Orientation-marked packages are loaded upright and secured to prevent inversion. Drums are loaded upright, chocked, and strapped.

**10.4 Pre-load inspection.** Before loading, the ramp handler inspects each package for damage or leakage. A package showing any defect is not loaded. The handler stops, does not touch the package further, and calls the duty officer. See Section 15.3.

**10.5 NOTOC generation.** The load planner generates the NOTOC in Hermes after the load plan is final and before the aircraft doors close. The NOTOC lists, for each dangerous goods item:

1. Air waybill number.
2. Proper shipping name and UN or ID number.
3. Class or division, and subsidiary risk.
4. Packing group.
5. Number of packages and net quantity per package.
6. Loading location by compartment and position.
7. CAO designation where applicable.
8. Whether the item is a radioactive material, with transport index.
9. Airport of loading and airport of unloading.
10. Confirmation that no damaged or leaking package has been loaded.
11. Emergency response drill code or reference to the onboard emergency response guidance.

**10.6 NOTOC verification.** The load planner physically walks the load and confirms the NOTOC matches what is aboard, position by position. The planner signs the NOTOC. A NOTOC is never signed from the desk.

**10.7 Delivery to the pilot-in-command.** The load planner hands the NOTOC to the pilot-in-command before departure and obtains a signature or, at village stations without a load planner present, transmits it by the Hermes tablet and obtains electronic acknowledgment. The pilot-in-command retains one copy. One copy remains on the ground at the point of departure, accessible until the flight arrives, and is retained in the station file.

**10.8 Changes after NOTOC issue.** Any change to the dangerous goods aboard after the NOTOC is signed requires a new NOTOC. Amendments by pen are not permitted. The superseded NOTOC is marked VOID, initialed, and retained.

**10.9 Ground copy retention.** The ground copy is scanned into Hermes within 24 hours of departure and retained for three months, or for a longer period where an incident is involved.

## 11. PROCEDURE — DISCREPANCY AND INCIDENT REPORTING

**11.1 Categories.** CACS distinguishes:

1. **Discrepancy.** A dangerous goods shipment found not to comply after acceptance, or dangerous goods found in cargo not declared, with no release, damage, or injury.
2. **Dangerous goods incident.** An occurrence associated with the transport of dangerous goods that results in injury, property damage, fire, breakage, spillage, leakage, radiation, or other evidence that the integrity of the packaging was not maintained.
3. **Dangerous goods accident.** An occurrence associated with the transport of dangerous goods resulting in fatal or serious injury or major property damage.

**11.2 Immediate actions for any incident or accident.**

1. Stop work. Evacuate the immediate area to at least 25 metres, or 100 metres for a Class 1, Class 2 flammable, or lithium battery event.
2. Call 911 if there is fire, injury, or a release requiring emergency services.
3. Call the duty officer at (907) 555-0147.
4. Do not attempt to clean up a release without the station spill kit and appropriate personal protective equipment, and not at all for Class 6.2 or Class 7.
5. Preserve the scene and all packaging, documents, and labels. Photograph before moving anything.

**11.3 Duty officer actions.** The duty officer notifies the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager immediately, opens an incident record in Hermes, assigns an incident number, and confirms whether an immediate telephone report to the National Response Center is required.

**11.4 Immediate telephone notice.** For any incident meeting the criteria of 49 CFR 171.15 — including a fatality, an injury requiring hospitalization, evacuation of the public for one hour or more, closure of a major transport artery for one hour or more, fire, breakage, spillage, or suspected radioactive contamination, or a release of a marine pollutant in a quantity exceeding 450 L — the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager, or the duty officer in the manager's absence, telephones the National Response Center at 1-800-424-8802 **at the earliest practicable moment and in no case later than 12 hours** after the occurrence.

**11.5 Written report.** A written report on DOT Form F 5800.1 is filed **within 30 days** of the date of discovery for any incident described in 49 CFR 171.16, and a copy is retained for three years. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager files this report.

**11.6 Undeclared and misdeclared shipments.** Any undeclared or misdeclared dangerous goods discovered in cargo is reported in writing to the FAA at the earliest practicable moment, and in any event **within 30 days** on Form F 5800.1, together with a copy of the air waybill and any documentation. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager also notifies the FAA Alaska Certificate Management Office by email within one business day of discovery.

**11.7 Internal discrepancy report.** For every discrepancy, incident, or accident, the discovering employee completes form DG-208, Dangerous Goods Discrepancy Report, before the end of shift. The form records what was found, where, when, by whom, the air waybill number, the shipper, and the immediate action taken. DG-208 is not a disciplinary document. Employees who report in good faith are not disciplined for the report.

**11.8 Investigation.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager investigates every DG-208 within 10 business days, determines root cause, assigns corrective action with a named owner and due date, and closes the record in Hermes. Corrective actions overdue by more than 15 days are escalated to the Vice President of Operations.

**11.9 Shipper consequences.** A confirmed undeclared or misdeclared tender results in immediate suspension of the shipper from the Register pending investigation. Reinstatement requires new training certificates and the written approval of the Vice President of Operations.

## 12. PROCEDURE — TRAINING AND QUALIFICATION RECORDS

**12.1 Who must be trained.** Every acceptance agent, DG checker, load planner, ramp handler, duty officer, station manager, and any employee who handles, offers, or accepts cargo, in the function-specific category applicable to their duties.

**12.2 Initial training.** Before performing any dangerous goods function unsupervised, an employee completes initial function-specific training and passes a written examination at 80 percent or better. Training covers general awareness, function-specific content, safety, security awareness, and CACS procedures including this document.

**12.3 Recurrent training.** Recurrent training is completed **within 24 months of the previous training completion date**. CACS schedules recurrent training at 21 months to build in margin. There is no grace period beyond 24 months.

**12.4 Automatic lock-out.** This is the control that addresses the 2025 audit finding of checklists signed by lapsed agents. Hermes holds each employee's training expiry date. At 23 months, Hermes emails the employee, the station manager, and the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager daily. At 24 months plus one day, Hermes **removes the employee's authority to sign a DG-201**, and the employee's number is rejected by the acceptance screen. There is no override at station level. Only the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager can restore authority, and only after a completed training record is filed.

**12.5 Paper backup.** Village stations operating on paper during a Hermes outage use the Station Qualification Roster, form DG-212, printed and posted at the counter, reissued monthly by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager and showing each employee's expiry date. During an outage, no employee whose name does not appear on the current roster may sign a DG-201.

**12.6 Records content.** For each employee the training record contains: name, employee number, function, date of most recent training, description of training, name and address of the training provider, copy of the examination result, and a copy of the certificate. Records are retained for the duration of employment plus 36 months, and in all cases for the current and preceding 24-month period as required by 49 CFR 172.704(d).

**12.7 Records location.** Electronic records in Hermes under Compliance → Training. Signed originals in the training records office, Anchorage. The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager verifies quarterly that the electronic and paper records agree.

**12.8 Contractor and interline staff.** Any contractor performing an acceptance, loading, or handling function provides current training records before commencing work. The station manager verifies them against 12.6 and files a copy. Contractors without records do not touch dangerous goods.

## 14. PROCEDURE — SELF-AUDIT AND MONITORING

**14.1 Quarterly acceptance audit.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager samples a minimum of 50 accepted dangerous goods shipments per quarter, drawn proportionally across Anchorage and the eight village stations, and re-performs the document and checklist review against Section 6. Findings are recorded on DG-220.

**14.2 Target.** The declaration error rate found at audit shall be below 2 percent. The 2025 baseline was 28 percent (14 of 50). Any quarter above 5 percent triggers a station-level corrective action plan approved by the Vice President of Operations.

**14.3 Station visits.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager visits each village station at least annually and observes at least one live acceptance, inspects the spill kit, emergency response guidance, DG-201 stock, current binder version, and the posted DG-212 roster.

**14.4 Monthly dashboard.** Hermes produces a monthly dashboard for the Vice President of Operations showing: acceptances by station, rejections by reason, training expiries in the next 90 days, open DG-208 records, and overdue corrective actions.

## 15. PROCEDURE — EXCEPTION PATHS

**15.1 Village station without a certified scale.**

1. Stations at Aniak, Unalakleet, St. Mary's, and Emmonak do not hold a scale certified to a tolerance adequate for net quantity verification.
2. At these stations, dangerous goods are accepted only where net quantity can be verified without weighing: by manufacturer's marked net quantity on an unopened package, by counted units of identical marked packages, or by the marked capacity of a sealed drum or container.
3. Where net quantity cannot be verified by these means, the agent calls the duty officer. The duty officer may authorize acceptance only if the shipper provides a weight certificate from a certified scale dated within 24 hours, transmitted electronically and attached in Hermes.
4. Dry ice may be accepted at these stations against the shipper's marked net mass only if the package bears a durable manufacturer's or shipper's net mass marking and the total for the flight, computed by load planning, remains at or below 200 kg.
5. Aviation fuel in drums is **not accepted** at stations without a certified scale.
6. Every acceptance under this exception is annotated in the DG-201 remarks block with "No certified scale — verification method: [method]" and is included in the quarterly audit sample at 100 percent.

**15.2 Medevac charters.**

1. Medevac charters may carry medical oxygen, portable medical equipment containing lithium batteries, diagnostic specimens, and dry ice, tendered by a qualified medical provider under the operator's dangerous goods provisions.
2. The requesting medical provider transmits an equipment manifest to the duty officer before departure listing each dangerous good, quantity, and packaging.
3. The duty officer verifies the manifest against the CACS Medevac Permitted Items List and confirms the aircraft category limits in Section 8 are not exceeded.
4. A NOTOC is required and is prepared by the duty officer where no load planner is available. Section 10.5 applies in full. The NOTOC is transmitted to the pilot-in-command and acknowledged before departure.
5. Cylinders of medical oxygen are secured upright, valve protected, and restrained against movement in all directions.
6. Urgency does not waive the NOTOC. No medevac departs without one.
7. The duty officer files a DG-208 within 24 hours recording the medevac dangerous goods carried, for audit purposes only.

**15.3 Damaged package found on the ramp.**

1. The finder stops immediately, does not lift, tilt, or move the package, and clears personnel to 25 metres, or 100 metres for Class 1, Class 2 flammable, or lithium batteries.
2. The finder calls the duty officer and reports the air waybill number, proper shipping name from the package marking, and the nature of the defect.
3. If the package is already aboard the aircraft, it is removed only under the direction of the duty officer, using appropriate personal protective equipment, and only if removal can be done without damaging the package further. If it cannot, the aircraft is not loaded further and the area is isolated.
4. The load planner voids the NOTOC and issues a new one reflecting the removal.
5. The damaged package is moved to the isolation area (Anchorage, Bay 7; village stations, the marked outdoor isolation pad), placed in an overpack or salvage packaging if a release is present and the commodity permits, and not returned to the general warehouse.
6. The aircraft compartment is inspected for contamination and cleaned before further loading. A compartment contaminated by a Class 6.2, Class 7, or Class 8 release is placed out of service until decontaminated and released by the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager.
7. A DG-208 is filed before end of shift. Section 11 reporting applies.

**15.4 Undeclared dangerous goods discovered in a package already accepted.**

This path did not exist before this revision and is the direct closure of the third 2025 audit finding.

1. **Stop.** Do not reseal, repack, tape, or move the package. Do not place it on a vehicle or aircraft. Do not hand it back to the shipper.
2. **Isolate.** Move personnel back. If the item is a lithium battery, an aerosol, a fuel container, or shows heat, swelling, odor, or leakage, clear to 100 metres and call 911 in addition to the duty officer.
3. **Call the duty officer** at (907) 555-0147 immediately. Report the air waybill number, station, what was found, and the condition of the item.
4. **Do not attempt to classify or declare the item.** CACS employees do not prepare shipper documentation (Section 5.8). The item is not "fixed" at the counter.
5. **Photograph** the package as found, the item, and any markings, and attach the images in Hermes.
6. **Remove the shipment from the flight.** The load planner voids any NOTOC and rebuilds the load plan.
7. **Contact the shipper** by the duty officer, not by the acceptance agent, informing them the shipment is being held pending investigation, that it will not be transported, and that the matter is reportable to the FAA.
8. **Segregate the shipment** in the isolation area under the shipper's name and the air waybill number. It is not returned to the shipper until the Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager authorizes release in writing, and it is never returned by air.
9. **Report.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager notifies the FAA Alaska Certificate Management Office by email within one business day and files Form F 5800.1 within 30 days per Section 11.6.
10. **Shipper action.** The shipper is suspended from the Register under Section 11.9.
11. **Screen the balance.** The Dangerous Goods Compliance Manager reviews all shipments from the same shipper accepted in the preceding 30 days and inspects any still in CACS custody.
12. **Hidden dangerous goods aids.** Each counter displays the CACS Hidden Dangerous Goods Poster listing common concealments: aircraft parts, camping equipment, chemicals, drilling equipment, electrical apparatus, frozen foods with dry ice, machinery, medical supplies, outboard motors, passenger baggage shipped as cargo, photographic supplies, refrigerators, repair kits, samples for testing, swimming pool chemicals, tool boxes, and vehicle parts. Agents ask the shipper the two counter questions on every general cargo tender: "Does this shipment contain any batteries, aerosols, fuels, chemicals, or compressed gases?" and "Was this item ever used to hold fuel or chemicals?" The answers are recorded on the air waybill.
