I need to put the credit facility in the middle of this conversation rather than at the end of it, because it constrains the answer more than anyone on this thread has assumed.

The Cascadia facility is $45M committed. We're drawn $28.4M as of Friday. The borrowing base is 85 percent of eligible accounts receivable plus 60 percent of eligible inventory at the lower of cost or net realizable value, capped by a 65 percent NOLV appraisal sublimit. The certificate is due March 31 for the February 28 measurement date, and it is certified by me personally.

Three mechanics matter here.

First, the aging haircut. Inventory over 270 days from receipt is excluded from eligible inventory entirely. Not haircut — excluded. I misspoke to Lorena last week when I said haircut and I want to correct it on the record. Our snowsports goods were received between July 14 and October 30, 2025. The July and August receipts cross 270 days between April 10 and May 20. So the February 28 certificate is fine on aging. The May 31 certificate is where roughly $2.9M at cost falls out of the base, which at a 60 percent advance rate is $1.74M of availability.

Second, and this is the one I'm more worried about, the facility defines eligible inventory at the lower of cost or net realizable value. The moment we take a markdown and hold goods at a price that implies a lower NRV, the carrying value in the base drops even if the units haven't moved. If we mark $3.4M of goods to 55 percent off, the auditors will not let me carry that at cost.

Third, Cascadia has the right to order a field exam and an updated NOLV appraisal at our expense if inventory turns fall below a threshold, which is defined in the agreement as trailing twelve-month COGS over average inventory below 3.0x. We ran 3.4x last year. Sitting on $3.4M of dead-weight snowsports through the summer takes us to approximately 3.05x by the July measurement. That's uncomfortably close and the exam alone runs $40-55K plus a lot of my team's time.

The unhelpful summary is that pack and hold is worse for the credit facility than liquidation, and liquidation is worse for the P&L. Somebody's going to have to actually decide.
