## 5. STAFF REPORT — STATUS OF THE 2045 COMPREHENSIVE PLAN UPDATE

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented a written memorandum dated January 27, 2026. She reported that the 2045 Comprehensive Plan update, originally scheduled for adoption in June 2025, was approximately nine months behind schedule. She attributed the delay to three causes: the withdrawal of the original consultant, Sundberg Planning Group, in March 2025; the Metropolitan Council's revised forecast release in August 2025, which required the land use and transportation chapters to be recalculated; and staff vacancies in the planning department totaling eleven months of unfilled position time during 2025.

Director Bakke stated that the current land use chapter guided the eastern edge of the city as "Agricultural Preserve / Long-Term Urban Reserve," a designation adopted in 2009 and not revisited since. She said that staff anticipated the draft land use chapter would be released for commission review in July 2026, with adoption no earlier than December 2026.

**Commission Discussion.** Commissioner Brandt asked what legal effect the outdated plan had on pending applications. City Attorney Ridder responded that under Minnesota Statutes § 462.357, subdivision 2, the zoning ordinance controlled where it conflicted with the comprehensive plan, but that the commission was obligated to make findings addressing consistency with the plan as adopted.

Commissioner Nyquist asked whether the delay exposed the city to liability. Attorney Ridder said it did not by itself, but that approving development inconsistent with the guided land use without adequate findings created appellate risk.

No action was taken on this item.

## 6. PUBLIC HEARING — PROPOSED INTERIM ORDINANCE No. 2026-03, TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON INDUSTRIAL REZONINGS

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented the staff report dated January 27, 2026, recommending adoption of an interim ordinance under Minnesota Statutes § 462.355, subdivision 4, imposing a six-month moratorium on the acceptance and processing of applications to rezone property to any I-1, I-2, or I-3 industrial district. She stated the purpose was to allow completion of the land use and transportation chapters of the comprehensive plan update before the city committed 148 acres or more to industrial development. Staff recommended a term of six months from the effective date, with the possibility of extension to a total of not more than eighteen months.

Director Bakke noted that a rezoning application had been filed by Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners on January 14, 2026, for 148 acres located at the eastern edge of the city, that the application had been deemed complete on January 22, 2026, and that staff took no position at that meeting on whether the interim ordinance would or could apply retroactively to that application.

**Applicant Comment.** Attorney Gerald Vosberg addressed the commission on behalf of Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners. He asserted three positions for the record: first, that the application filed January 14 and deemed complete January 22 preceded any moratorium and was therefore governed by the ordinance in effect on the date of filing; second, that his client had acquired vested rights through the expenditure of approximately $1.9 million in engineering, environmental, and option costs incurred in reliance on the city's pre-application guidance of August through December 2025; and third, that Minnesota Statutes § 15.99 required the city to approve or deny the application within sixty days of January 22, 2026, absent a written extension, and that the sixty-day period expired March 23, 2026. He stated that his client would not consent to an extension that ran past the statutory deadline and that failure to act would result in automatic approval.

Attorney Ridder responded for the record that the city had provided written notice of extension on January 26, 2026, extending the review period by an additional sixty days to May 22, 2026, as permitted by § 15.99, subdivision 3(f), and that the city disputed the assertion of vested rights on the ground that no permit had been issued and no construction had commenced.

**Public Hearing.** Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened the public hearing at 7:52 p.m. and administered the oath to all persons intending to testify.

**Marisol Quintanilla-Reed, 8802 Old Barn Road**, testified in support of the moratorium, stating that the city had no adopted policy on truck routing and that adopting one after approving a warehouse was backward.

**Dale Bergquist, 512 Fifth Avenue South**, testified in opposition, stating that the moratorium would signal to employers that Willow Bend was closed for business and that the city had lost the Ferndale packaging plant in 2023 for similar reasons.

**Yolanda Prentice-Okafor, 3300 Sagebrush Circle**, speaking as chair of Neighbors for the Rural Edge, testified in support and stated that her organization had gathered 1,300 signatures on a petition opposing industrial rezoning at the eastern edge. She submitted the petition to the Recording Secretary, who marked it Exhibit A.

**Curtis Molnar-Whitfield, 7719 Highway 61**, testified in opposition and identified himself as a member of Local 638. He stated the project represented approximately 400 construction jobs.

Eleven additional persons testified: seven in support of the moratorium and four in opposition. Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed the public hearing at 8:41 p.m.

**Findings of Fact.** Upon motion of Commissioner Eze, seconded by Commissioner Brandt, the commission adopted the following findings:

1. The city's 2040 Comprehensive Plan land use chapter was adopted in 2009 and had not been amended to reflect the Metropolitan Council's 2025 forecast revisions.
2. The 2045 Comprehensive Plan update was approximately nine months behind its adopted schedule and the land use chapter was not expected before July 2026.
3. The city had no adopted truck route plan, no adopted industrial design standards, and no adopted policy governing the interface between industrial and agricultural land uses.
4. The study period requested was reasonable in duration and reasonably related to the planning process underway.

**Vote on findings:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Delacroix-Vang (5). Nays — Farooqi, Nyquist (2). **Findings adopted 5–2.**

**Motion.** Commissioner Eze moved that the Planning Commission recommend to the City Council adoption of Interim Ordinance No. 2026-03 imposing a six-month moratorium on industrial rezoning applications, with the express recommendation that the ordinance not be applied to any application deemed complete before its effective date. Commissioner Brandt seconded.

**Discussion.** Commissioner Farooqi stated he would vote no because a moratorium adopted three weeks after a major application was filed invited litigation regardless of the carve-out. Commissioner Nyquist stated he would vote no because the delay was the city's own doing and should not be borne by an applicant. **Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Delacroix-Vang (5). Nays — Farooqi, Nyquist (2). **Motion carried 5–2.**

## 5. PUBLIC HEARING — CITY FILE No. RZ-2026-001, HALCYON RIDGE INDUSTRIAL PARTNERS, REZONING OF 148 ACRES FROM A-1 AGRICULTURAL TO I-2 GENERAL INDUSTRIAL

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented the staff report dated February 24, 2026, consisting of 94 pages with appendices. She described the subject property as 148.3 acres located south of County Road 19 and east of Torvald Avenue, in agricultural production under a cash rent arrangement since 1978, owned by the Haugesund Family Limited Partnership and under purchase option to the applicant.

Director Bakke identified the following issues for the commission's consideration:

- **Comprehensive plan consistency.** The property was guided Agricultural Preserve / Long-Term Urban Reserve. The requested I-2 designation was not consistent with the guided use. An amendment to the land use plan would be required and had been applied for concurrently as City File No. CPA-2026-002.
- **Traffic.** The applicant's study projected 640 truck trips per day and 1,240 passenger vehicle trips per day at full build-out and full staffing across three shifts. County Road 19 was a two-lane rural section rated for 4,800 vehicles per day and presently carrying 3,100. The study identified the need for a signal at County Road 19 and Torvald Avenue, left turn lanes at both site entrances, and a five-hundred-foot southbound right turn lane.
- **Wetlands.** A delineation performed October 2025 identified 4.7 acres of Type 2 and Type 3 wetland in three basins. The delineation had been submitted to the Technical Evaluation Panel but had not yet received concurrence.
- **Infrastructure.** Municipal sanitary sewer terminated 3,400 feet west of the property. Water service required a trunk main extension and a booster station.
- **Fiscal.** The applicant requested $14,600,000 in tax increment financing over a proposed twenty-five year district. The estimated market value at completion was $96,000,000.

Staff did not offer a recommendation of approval or denial at that meeting, instead identifying twenty-two areas in which the record was incomplete and recommending that the commission receive testimony and continue the item.

**Applicant Presentation.** Amanda Kirchhoff presented for the applicant for twenty-two minutes. She stated that Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners had developed eleven distribution facilities in five states, that the end user was a national grocery cooperative under a twenty-year lease, and that the facility would employ 610 full-time equivalent positions at an average wage of $24.40 per hour with benefits. She described a proposed building footprint of 1,100,000 square feet with 142 dock doors, 340 trailer stalls, and 720 employee parking spaces.

Roland Kellerman, P.E., presented the traffic impact study. He testified that the 640-truck figure was derived from the Institute of Transportation Engineers Trip Generation Manual, 11th Edition, Land Use Code 155, High-Cube Fulfillment Center Warehouse, using the independent variable of 1,000 square feet of gross floor area, and cross-checked against operating data from three comparable facilities.

Dr. Simone Adeyemi-Hart summarized the wetland delineation and stated that the proposed site plan avoided all three basins with buffers of not less than fifty feet.

Attorney Vosberg reserved the applicant's position on vested rights and the sixty-day rule and confirmed the applicant's written agreement to the extension through May 22, 2026.

**Public Hearing.** Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened the public hearing at 8:04 p.m. and swore all persons intending to testify, en masse and individually as they came forward. Testimony continued, with two recesses, until 11:14 p.m. **Sixty-three persons testified.** The complete testimony is preserved in the verbatim transcript, incorporated by reference. Testimony is summarized as follows.

**Yolanda Prentice-Okafor, 3300 Sagebrush Circle**, chair of Neighbors for the Rural Edge, testified under oath for nine minutes on yielded time. She stated the group's 1,300 signatures represented residents of the Sagebrush, Meadowlark, and Old Barn neighborhoods. She testified that the group did not oppose industrial development generally but opposed its placement at the eastern edge without a truck route plan, and requested denial.

**Anders Lindqvist-Boone, P.E., 8815 Old Barn Road**, testified under oath. He identified himself as a licensed professional engineer in Minnesota, License No. 44182, with nineteen years of experience in transportation engineering, and stated he was testifying as a resident and not on behalf of any employer. He challenged the trip generation figures on four grounds: that ITE Land Use Code 155 understated truck percentages for food-grade cold chain distribution, which he testified operated on a different replenishment cycle than dry goods fulfillment; that the applicant's comparable facilities averaged 780,000 square feet and were not directly scalable; that the study assumed a fifty-fifty directional split on County Road 19 when the regional distribution pattern suggested seventy-thirty eastbound; and that the study analyzed the p.m. peak hour but not the 4:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m. period when he testified food distribution truck volumes peaked. He testified that a corrected projection was in the range of 810 to 900 truck trips per day. He submitted a twelve-page written analysis, marked Exhibit B.

Mr. Kellerman was permitted to respond and stated that Code 155 was the appropriate code, that he would review the directional split assumption, and that he did not dispute that an early-morning analysis period should be added.

**Bertram Achara, 1120 East County Road 19**, testified under oath regarding agricultural drainage, stating that the county ditch system serving his 60 acres discharged through the subject property.

**Reverend Dolores Whitcomb-Ngoy, First Covenant Church, 415 Sixth Street East**, testified under oath in support, citing employment access for congregation members.

**Dale Bergquist, 512 Fifth Avenue South**, testified under oath in support, citing tax base.

**Ingrid Solheim-Batra, 4417 Meadowlark Lane**, testified under oath in opposition, describing noise from refrigeration units and reefer trailers.

**Curtis Molnar-Whitfield, 7719 Highway 61**, testified under oath in support on behalf of the Building Trades Council.

**Dr. Priya Venkataraman-Cole, 2201 Wren Court**, testified under oath in opposition regarding diesel particulate exposure at Bluestem Elementary School, located 4,100 feet from the proposed western property line.

**Harold Haugesund, 15220 County Road 19**, testified under oath as a general partner of the fee owner, stating the family had farmed the land for four generations and could no longer make it pencil.

Of the sixty-three speakers, the Recording Secretary tallied 41 in opposition, 19 in support, and 3 neither in support nor opposition.

## 3. CONTINUED PUBLIC HEARING — CITY FILE No. RZ-2026-001

**Supplemental Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented the supplemental staff report dated March 30, 2026. She reported that the applicant had submitted a revised site plan on March 23, 2026, designated Revision C, containing the following changes from the plan reviewed March 2:

- A continuous earthen berm along the north and west property lines, 1,900 linear feet, at heights of twelve to sixteen feet, planted with a triple row of coniferous and deciduous stock at a density of 220 trees per thousand linear feet.
- Relocation of the truck court and all 142 dock doors to the east and south elevations, away from residential receptors.
- A binding truck routing agreement, negotiated with Baldwin County and the Minnesota Department of Transportation, restricting all site-generated truck traffic to eastbound County Road 19 to Trunk Highway 61, prohibiting westbound movement past Torvald Avenue, with GPS geofencing, quarterly compliance reporting, and liquidated damages of $2,500 per violation.
- Reduction of the building footprint from 1,100,000 to 985,000 square feet, and reduction of the trailer stall count from 340 to 268.
- A commitment to electric yard tractors and to 100 percent electrified reefer plug-ins at all trailer stalls, eliminating diesel auxiliary power units.

Director Bakke reported that the revised traffic study projected **320 fewer daily truck trips** than the original submission, for a revised projection of 320 truck trips per day, attributable to the footprint reduction, the routing agreement, and a revised operating model shifting from three shifts to two.

**Peer Review.** Beth Undseth, P.E., of Trellborg Engineering presented the independent peer review dated March 30, 2026. She testified that she had reviewed both studies and the Lindqvist-Boone analysis. Her conclusions were: that the objection to the directional split was well founded and had been correctly resolved in the revised study; that the addition of a 4:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m. analysis period was appropriate and had been performed; that the revised projection of 320 daily truck trips was reasonable and, in her professional opinion, modestly conservative; that the ITE code selection was defensible; and that the required intersection improvements remained necessary at the reduced volume, with the exception of the southbound right turn lane, which could be reduced from five hundred to three hundred feet.

Assistant County Engineer Delahoyde-Pratt testified that Baldwin County concurred with the peer review and had executed the routing agreement in draft on March 27, 2026, subject to city approval of the rezoning.

**Applicant Presentation.** Ms. Kirchhoff presented Revision C. Attorney Vosberg stated that the applicant did not waive its previously asserted positions but agreed the record now supported approval.

**Continued Public Hearing.** Chair Ocampo-Reyes reopened the continued public hearing at 8:21 p.m. and swore all new speakers. Twenty-nine persons testified.

**Anders Lindqvist-Boone, P.E.**, testified under oath that Revision C substantially addressed his technical objections, that he accepted the revised trip generation figures, and that his remaining concern was enforcement of the routing agreement over a twenty-year lease term. He recommended that any approval require an annual third-party audit at applicant expense.

**Yolanda Prentice-Okafor** testified under oath that Neighbors for the Rural Edge maintained its opposition, stating that the group's objection was to the land use itself and not to its mitigation, and that the appropriate forum was the comprehensive plan update.

**Ingrid Solheim-Batra** testified under oath that the berm and dock relocation addressed her noise concerns and that she no longer opposed the application.

**Dr. Priya Venkataraman-Cole** testified under oath that the electrification commitments materially reduced her concerns regarding particulate exposure but requested monitoring at Bluestem Elementary.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed the public hearing at 9:48 p.m.

**Motion to Recommend Denial.** Commissioner Brandt moved that the commission recommend denial of City File No. RZ-2026-001, on findings that the requested I-2 designation was inconsistent with the adopted comprehensive plan, that the pending plan update was the proper forum for reconsidering the eastern edge, and that approval in advance of the plan would prejudge the update. Commissioner Delacroix-Vang seconded.

**Discussion.** Commissioner Eze stated that the applicant had responded to every objection raised and that denying an application that had met the city's stated concerns would be arbitrary. Commissioner Brandt responded that responsiveness on mitigation did not cure inconsistency with the plan. Vice Chair Wetherbee stated he was persuaded by the peer review and the executed county routing agreement.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Brandt, Delacroix-Vang, Nyquist (3). Nays — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Farooqi (4). **Motion failed 3–4.**

**Findings of Fact.** Commissioner Eze moved adoption of findings of fact prepared by staff and the City Attorney, dated March 30, 2026, comprising thirty-one numbered findings, the substance of which included:

1. The revised application, Revision C, reduced the building footprint to 985,000 square feet and reduced projected truck trips by 320 per day from the original submission.
2. The independent peer review found the revised trip generation reasonable and modestly conservative.
3. The executed county and MnDOT routing agreement, with geofencing and liquidated damages, provided enforceable mitigation of the residential traffic impact.
4. The berm, dock relocation, and electrification commitments mitigated noise and air quality impacts to a degree consistent with the city's performance standards under Zoning Code § 11-8-4.
5. The property abutted an existing I-2 district to the south across the corporate limits of Hollis Township and was served by an arterial county highway.
6. A concurrent comprehensive plan amendment, City File No. CPA-2026-002, was required and was recommended in companion action.
7. The wetland delineation had not yet received Technical Evaluation Panel concurrence, and approval must be conditioned upon it.

Commissioner Farooqi seconded. **Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Farooqi (4). Nays — Brandt, Delacroix-Vang, Nyquist (3). **Findings adopted 4–3.**

**Motion to Recommend Approval.** Vice Chair Wetherbee moved that the commission recommend to the City Council approval of City File No. RZ-2026-001, rezoning 148.3 acres from A-1 Agricultural to I-2 General Industrial, and of the companion comprehensive plan amendment CPA-2026-002, subject to **seventeen conditions**, summarized as follows:

**Members Present:** Chair Beatriz Ocampo-Reyes; Vice Chair Douglas Wetherbee; Commissioner Nnamdi Eze; Commissioner Solveig Brandt; Commissioner Hassan Farooqi (present for Items 1–3 and 6–8; recused from Item 4); Commissioner Ruth Delacroix-Vang; Commissioner Peter Nyquist

## 1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. All seven commissioners were present.

## 2. APPROVAL OF AGENDA AND MINUTES

Vice Chair Wetherbee moved approval of the agenda and of the April 6, 2026 minutes. Commissioner Nyquist seconded. **Motion carried 7–0.**

## 3. REPORT OF COUNCIL ACTION AND REMAND

Director Bakke reported that the City Council took up City Files RZ-2026-001 and CPA-2026-002 at a special meeting held **Thursday, April 30, 2026**, in advance of its regular May 4 meeting, in order to preserve the § 15.99 deadline of May 22, 2026.

She reported that the council, by a vote of five to two, **remanded** both files to the Planning Commission without prejudice, and simultaneously adopted a further sixty-day extension of the review period under § 15.99, subdivision 3(f), extending it to **July 21, 2026**, with the applicant's written consent obtained April 29, 2026.

The stated ground for remand was the wetland delineation. Director Bakke reported that on April 24, 2026 the Technical Evaluation Panel declined to concur with the October 2025 delineation, finding that basin 2 had been delineated during an abnormally dry period, that the panel's field review of April 15, 2026 identified probable wetland extending approximately 1.6 acres beyond the delineated boundary of basin 2, and that the expanded boundary encroached upon the proposed truck court shown on Revision C. The panel directed a re-delineation during the 2026 growing season, not earlier than June 1.

The council's remand order, Resolution No. 2026-41, directed the commission to: (a) receive the re-delineation when complete; (b) determine whether Revision C required modification; (c) reconsider Conditions 5, 6, and 15; and (d) return a supplemental recommendation not later than the commission's regular meeting of **July 6, 2026**.

## 4. REMANDED MATTER — CITY FILE Nos. RZ-2026-001 AND CPA-2026-002

**Recusal.** Before the item was called, Commissioner Farooqi stated for the record that on April 22, 2026 he had learned that his employer, Ostergaard Mechanical Contractors, had executed a subcontract with Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners for mechanical work on a facility in Rochester, Minnesota, unrelated to the Willow Bend site. He stated that although he had no personal financial interest in that contract and had not participated in it, he had consulted the City Attorney and concluded that the appearance of a conflict required his recusal from further participation in the Halcyon Ridge matters.

Attorney Ridder advised that recusal was appropriate and that Commissioner Farooqi's participation in the April 6 votes was not thereby invalidated, the contract having postdated those votes. He further advised that Commissioner Farooqi's recusal did not disturb the April 6 recommendation, which had been transmitted and acted upon.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes accepted the recusal, thanked Commissioner Farooqi, and noted for the record that he left the dais at 7:19 p.m. and did not return until Item 6. The Chair stated that the remaining six members constituted a quorum and that four affirmative votes remained necessary for any recommendation.

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented the remand memorandum dated April 28, 2026, and reviewed the Technical Evaluation Panel's written determination.

**Presentation.** Nora Frydenlund of the Board of Water and Soil Resources explained the panel's reasoning and the required timing of a growing-season delineation. Dr. Adeyemi-Hart stated the applicant did not contest the panel's determination and had scheduled field work for June 8 through June 12, 2026.

Attorney Vosberg stated that the applicant would submit a Revision D site plan responsive to the re-delineation, that it anticipated relocating a portion of the truck court southward without change to the building footprint or dock count, and that it consented to the extended review period.

**Public Comment.** Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened the floor for comment on the remanded matter and swore the speakers. Six persons spoke.

**Yolanda Prentice-Okafor** stated that the panel's finding vindicated her group's position that the record had been rushed and asked that the commission hold a new public hearing on Revision D rather than treat it as a technical amendment.

**Anders Lindqvist-Boone, P.E.** asked whether relocation of the truck court would alter internal circulation in a manner affecting the trip distribution, and requested a memorandum from the peer reviewer on that question.

**Harold Haugesund** asked whether the delay affected his family's purchase option. The Chair advised that the matter was private and not before the commission.

**Motion.** Commissioner Eze moved that the commission accept the council's remand under Resolution No. 2026-41 and direct the following:

1. That upon receipt of the re-delineation and Revision D, staff schedule a **new public hearing**, noticed and published as an original hearing, for the regular meeting of **July 6, 2026**;
2. That Trellborg Engineering be retained to prepare a supplemental memorandum on whether truck court relocation altered internal circulation or trip distribution, at applicant expense, due June 26, 2026;
3. That Conditions 5, 6, and 15 of the April 6 recommendation be held in abeyance pending the re-delineation; and
4. That all other findings and conditions adopted April 6, 2026 remain in effect.

Commissioner Delacroix-Vang seconded.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Delacroix-Vang, Nyquist (6). Nays — none (0). Recused — Farooqi (1). **Motion carried 6–0.**

**Referral to Council.** The Chair directed that a written acknowledgment of the remand and the schedule adopted be transmitted to the City Council for its meeting of May 18, 2026. Director Bakke was assigned this action item, due May 11, 2026.

## 5. ANNUAL WORK PLAN, FISCAL YEAR 2026–2027

*Commissioner Farooqi returned to the dais at 8:14 p.m.*

**Staff Report.** Director Bakke presented the draft annual work plan dated April 27, 2026, organized around five priorities: completion of the 2045 Comprehensive Plan update; a zoning ordinance recodification; the housing overlay study; an industrial performance standards ordinance responsive to issues raised during the Halcyon Ridge proceedings; and a subdivision ordinance update addressing agricultural drainage.

**Discussion.** Commissioner Brandt asked that the industrial performance standards item be advanced ahead of the recodification. Commissioner Nyquist asked whether staff capacity permitted five priorities; Director Bakke responded that the department had filled both vacant positions in March 2026.

**Motion.** Commissioner Brandt moved adoption of the FY 2026–2027 Annual Work Plan as presented, with the industrial performance standards ordinance advanced to second priority and the zoning recodification moved to third. Vice Chair Wetherbee seconded.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Farooqi, Delacroix-Vang, Nyquist (7). Nays — none (0). **Motion carried 7–0.**

**Referral to Council.** Transmitted to the City Council for information at its meeting of May 18, 2026. Action item assigned to Director Bakke, due May 11, 2026.

## 6. HOUSING OVERLAY STUDY — AUTHORIZATION

**Staff Report.** Associate Planner Achterberg presented a scope of work for a housing overlay study examining accessory dwelling units, missing-middle housing types in the R-2 district, and a density bonus for income-qualified units, at an estimated consultant cost of $78,000 from the planning department's professional services account.

**Motion.** Commissioner Delacroix-Vang moved that the commission recommend to the City Council authorization of the housing overlay study as scoped, and that the commission establish a three-member subcommittee to guide the work. Commissioner Eze seconded.

**Roll Call Vote:** Ayes — Ocampo-Reyes, Wetherbee, Eze, Brandt, Farooqi, Delacroix-Vang, Nyquist (7). Nays — none (0). **Motion carried 7–0.**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes appointed Commissioners Delacroix-Vang (chair), Nyquist, and Farooqi to the subcommittee, with a first meeting set for May 26, 2026. Referred to the City Council for its meeting of May 18, 2026; action item assigned to Associate Planner Achterberg, due May 11, 2026.
