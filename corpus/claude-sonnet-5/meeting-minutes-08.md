### Willow Bend Planning Commission

**Date:** Monday, February 2, 2026
**Time:** 6:30 p.m., called to order; adjourned 8:57 p.m.
**Place:** Council Chambers, Willow Bend City Hall, 210 Prairie Avenue, Willow Bend, Minnesota

**Members Present:** Chair Beatriz Ocampo-Reyes; Vice Chair Douglas Wetherbee; Commissioners Nnamdi Eze, Solveig Brandt, Hassan Farooqi, Priya Kapoor, and Grant Ostrowski

**Members Absent:** None

**Others Attending:** Planning Director Erica Bakke; City Attorney Marcus Doyle; Recording Secretary Wendy Alstrom; Marcus Thibodeaux, Vice President of Development, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Colton Vance, Esq., of Vance & Merrick LLP, counsel for the applicant; Carol Anne Whitfield, spokesperson for Neighbors for the Rural Edge; approximately forty members of the public

---

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Recording Secretary Alstrom called the roll; all seven members were present.

**2. Approval of Minutes**

Commissioner Brandt moved to approve the minutes of the January 5, 2026 regular meeting as presented. Commissioner Eze seconded. The motion carried 7–0 on a voice vote.

**3. Staff Report: Comprehensive Plan Update Status**

Planning Director Bakke reported that the comprehensive plan update, originally scheduled for adoption in November 2025, was now approximately nine months behind schedule due to consultant turnover and delays in the housing needs assessment. Bakke stated that the land use chapter, which would govern future industrial siting criteria, remained in draft form and was not expected to be complete before the fourth quarter of 2026. Bakke noted that the delay had direct bearing on the commission's ability to evaluate industrial rezoning requests against updated standards, including the pending application from Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners.

**4. Discussion and Possible Action: Interim Ordinance on Industrial Rezonings**

Bakke presented a staff memorandum recommending that the commission consider recommending to the city council a six-month interim ordinance, pursuant to Minnesota Statutes section 462.355, subdivision 4, temporarily suspending action on new industrial rezoning applications within the city's eastern growth area pending completion of the comprehensive plan update. Bakke stated that the interim ordinance would not apply retroactively to applications already deemed complete, but would govern the standard of review going forward.

City Attorney Doyle advised the commission on the legal authority underlying an interim ordinance and cautioned that any such action must be supported by findings demonstrating a reasonable relationship between the moratorium and the planning study underway. Doyle further stated that he had received a letter dated January 28, 2026, from Attorney Colton Vance on behalf of Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners, asserting that the applicant's rezoning application, deemed complete on December 15, 2025, had vested rights under the zoning ordinance in effect at that time, and that the city's sixty-day review period under Minnesota Statutes section 15.99 would expire February 13, 2026, absent an extension.

Attorney Vance addressed the commission directly, stating for the record that Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners objected to any interim ordinance being applied, directly or indirectly, to delay action on its pending application, and that the applicant reserved all rights, including a claim of automatic approval under section 15.99 should the city fail to act within the statutory period.

Carol Anne Whitfield, spokesperson for Neighbors for the Rural Edge, addressed the commission in support of the interim ordinance. Whitfield stated that her organization had collected 1,300 petition signatures from residents opposed to industrial rezoning of the eastern farmland and requested that the commission halt further processing of large-scale industrial applications until the comprehensive plan update was complete.

Commissioner Wetherbee asked Attorney Doyle whether the sixty-day statutory period could be extended by mutual agreement. Doyle responded that the city had, on January 20, 2026, sent the applicant a written request for a sixty-day extension under section 15.99, subdivision 3(f), which the applicant had not yet accepted or rejected in writing.

Following discussion, the commission made the following findings: (a) the comprehensive plan update remained substantially incomplete; (b) industrial land use policy for the eastern growth area was under active study; (c) processing further industrial rezoning applications without updated standards risked inconsistent land use outcomes; and (d) an interim ordinance of limited duration was a reasonable and proportionate response.

Commissioner Wetherbee moved to recommend that the city council adopt a six-month interim ordinance suspending acceptance and processing of new industrial rezoning applications in the eastern growth area, exclusive of applications already deemed complete as of the date of the ordinance. Commissioner Eze seconded the motion.

Recording Secretary Alstrom called the roll:

- Commissioner Eze — Yes
- Commissioner Brandt — Yes
- Commissioner Farooqi — Yes
- Commissioner Kapoor — No
- Commissioner Ostrowski — No
- Vice Chair Wetherbee — Yes
- Chair Ocampo-Reyes — Yes

The motion carried 5–2. Chair Ocampo-Reyes directed staff to forward the recommendation to the city council for consideration at its February 17, 2026 meeting.

**5. Staff Report: Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners Rezoning Application (Case No. Z-2026-01)**

Bakke reported that the application to rezone approximately 148 acres at the eastern edge of the city from Agricultural to General Industrial (I-2), to accommodate a proposed 1.1 million square foot food distribution warehouse, had been deemed complete on December 15, 2025. Bakke stated that the traffic study submitted by the applicant's consultant projected 640 truck trips per day, and that the applicant had separately requested tax increment financing assistance in the amount of $14.6 million from the city's Economic Development Authority. Bakke advised that the public hearing on the rezoning application had been noticed for March 2, 2026, in accordance with statutory publication and mailing requirements.

**6. Other Business**

There was no other business.

**7. Adjournment**

Commissioner Farooqi moved to adjourn. Commissioner Kapoor seconded. The motion carried 7–0. The meeting adjourned at 8:57 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Wendy Alstrom, Recording Secretary

**Others Attending:** Planning Director Erica Bakke; City Attorney Marcus Doyle; Recording Secretary Wendy Alstrom; Marcus Thibodeaux and Renata Voss, Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners; Colton Vance, Esq., counsel for the applicant; Owen Radcliffe, P.E., resident; Carol Anne Whitfield, Neighbors for the Rural Edge; approximately 140 members of the public

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. Six of seven members were present; Commissioner Kapoor was excused.

**2. Approval of Minutes**

Vice Chair Wetherbee moved to approve the minutes of the February 2, 2026 meeting. Commissioner Brandt seconded. The motion carried 6–0.

**3. Public Hearing: Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners Rezoning Application (Case No. Z-2026-01)**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes opened the public hearing at 6:38 p.m. and stated that the matter concerned a request to rezone approximately 148 acres from Agricultural to General Industrial to allow construction of a 1.1 million square foot food distribution warehouse.

*Staff Report.* Planning Director Bakke presented the staff report, describing the site's location at the eastern municipal boundary, its current agricultural use, adjacent land uses including the Meadowlark Estates residential subdivision approximately 1,400 feet to the south, and the applicable rezoning criteria under Section 11.4 of the zoning ordinance. Bakke summarized the applicant's traffic impact study, which projected 640 truck trips and an additional 280 employee vehicle trips daily, with a proposed primary access from County Road 12. Bakke noted the applicant's pending request for $14.6 million in tax increment financing, which remained under separate review by the Economic Development Authority and was not before the commission for action. Bakke confirmed that notice of the hearing had been published in the Willow Bend Sentinel on February 12 and February 19, 2026, and mailed to all property owners within 1,000 feet of the site.

*Applicant Presentation.* Marcus Thibodeaux, Vice President of Development for Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners, presented the project, describing the proposed facility's footprint, dock configuration, estimated 380 permanent jobs, and construction timeline of approximately eighteen months. Thibodeaux stated the applicant's willingness to work with the city on landscaping and truck routing but did not, at this stage, present modified plans. Attorney Colton Vance addressed the commission on legal criteria for rezoning approval and reiterated the applicant's position that its application, having been deemed complete prior to any interim ordinance, was entitled to review under the zoning standards in effect as of December 15, 2025, and that the statutory sixty-day period under section 15.99 had been extended by written agreement dated February 9, 2026, to April 15, 2026.

*Sworn Testimony.* Chair Ocampo-Reyes administered the oath to all persons wishing to testify. Sixty-three individuals testified over approximately four hours. Testimony included the following:

Carol Anne Whitfield, spokesperson for Neighbors for the Rural Edge, testified in opposition, formally submitting into the record a petition bearing 1,300 signatures of residents opposed to the rezoning, and stated concerns regarding truck traffic, noise, and loss of prime farmland.

Owen Radcliffe, P.E., a retired transportation engineer residing at 1420 Meadowlark Lane, testified under oath challenging the methodology of the applicant's traffic study. Radcliffe stated that the study's trip generation rates were drawn from an outdated edition of the Institute of Transportation Engineers manual and that peak-hour trip estimates for the proposed use class were understated by as much as thirty percent. Radcliffe requested that the commission require an independent peer review of the traffic study before further action.

Elmer Voss, a farmer whose property abuts the site to the north, testified regarding anticipated dust, noise, and drainage impacts on his operation.

Denise Okafor, a parent residing in Meadowlark Estates, testified regarding school bus safety along County Road 12 given the projected truck volumes.

Louis Tran, representing the Willow Bend Area Chamber of Commerce, testified in support of the application, citing anticipated employment and tax base benefits.

Additional speakers, numbering fifty-eight in total beyond those summarized above, offered testimony generally consistent with the positions above, addressing property values, environmental concerns, emergency vehicle access, and economic development benefits.

*Continuance.* At approximately 10:15 p.m., Chair Ocampo-Reyes stated that due to the volume of testimony and the substantive traffic engineering questions raised by Mr. Radcliffe, staff would be directed to commission an independent peer review of the traffic study prior to further commission action. Chair Ocampo-Reyes proposed keeping the written record open for ten days and continuing the public hearing to the commission's next regular meeting.

Commissioner Eze moved to continue the public hearing on Case No. Z-2026-01 to April 6, 2026, and to direct staff to obtain an independent traffic peer review and to keep the written record open through March 12, 2026. Commissioner Farooqi seconded.

Recording Secretary Alstrom called the roll:

- Commissioner Eze — Yes
- Commissioner Brandt — Yes
- Commissioner Farooqi — Yes
- Commissioner Ostrowski — Yes
- Vice Chair Wetherbee — Yes
- Chair Ocampo-Reyes — Yes

The motion carried 6–0. Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed active testimony and continued the item.

**4. Other Business**

Bakke gave a brief verbal update on the housing needs assessment component of the comprehensive plan, with no action taken.

**5. Adjournment**

Vice Chair Wetherbee moved to adjourn. Commissioner Ostrowski seconded. The motion carried 6–0. The meeting adjourned at 10:41 p.m.

Wendy Alstrom, Recording Secretary

**1. Call to Order and Roll Call**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. All seven members were present.

**2. Approval of Minutes**

Commissioner Farooqi moved to approve the minutes of the March 2, 2026 meeting. Commissioner Kapoor seconded. The motion carried 7–0.

**3. Continued Public Hearing: Halcyon Ridge Industrial Partners Rezoning Application (Case No. Z-2026-01)**

Chair Ocampo-Reyes reopened the continued public hearing at 6:35 p.m.

*Staff Report.* Planning Director Bakke reported that the city had retained TrafficWorks Consulting to conduct an independent peer review of the applicant's traffic study, as directed on March 2. Bakke introduced Ken Aldous, P.E., of TrafficWorks, who summarized his findings: that the original trip generation methodology contained a rate error consistent with concerns raised by Mr. Radcliffe, but that the applicant's revised submission, incorporating a reduced building footprint for high-cube storage and an executed truck routing agreement directing all inbound and outbound truck traffic to County Road 12 and away from residential streets, resulted in a revised projection of 320 daily truck trips, a reduction of 320 trips from the original estimate. Bakke stated that staff had prepared seventeen proposed conditions of approval addressing hours of truck operation, berm and landscape screening, lighting, stormwater management, and routing enforcement, and recommended that, should the commission choose to recommend approval, it do so subject to those seventeen conditions.

*Applicant Presentation.* Marcus Thibodeaux presented the revised site plan, which included a thirty-foot vegetated berm along the southern property line adjacent to Meadowlark Estates, the executed routing agreement referenced by staff, and reduced building massing. Attorney Colton Vance stated that the applicant had voluntarily agreed to the seventeen staff-recommended conditions.

*Sworn Testimony.* Chair Ocampo-Reyes reminded those previously sworn that their oath remained in effect and administered the oath to new speakers.

Carol Anne Whitfield testified that Neighbors for the Rural Edge remained opposed notwithstanding the revisions, citing cumulative traffic impacts from other pending development in the corridor and continued loss of agricultural land.

Owen Radcliffe testified that the revised trip generation figures, as validated by the independent peer review, addressed his principal technical objection, though he stated he remained concerned about long-term enforcement of the routing agreement.

Three additional residents testified, generally reiterating concerns regarding property values and noise, and two residents testified in support of the revised plan, citing the berm and reduced truck volume.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes closed the public hearing at 8:20 p.m.

*Commission Deliberation and Findings of Fact.* The commission discussed the application at length. Commissioner Eze stated that, notwithstanding the traffic revisions, he did not believe the rezoning was consistent with the existing comprehensive plan's guidance for the eastern growth area and that approval was premature given the ongoing comprehensive plan update. Commissioner Brandt concurred, stating her view that the cumulative loss of agricultural land had not been adequately studied. Vice Chair Wetherbee stated that the applicant had substantially addressed the traffic concerns raised in March through the independent peer review, the berm, and the routing agreement, and that the seventeen conditions provided adequate protection. Commissioner Farooqi and Commissioner Kapoor concurred with Vice Chair Wetherbee.

The commission made the following findings of fact: (1) the application, as revised, reduced projected daily truck trips from 640 to 320; (2) an independent traffic peer review by TrafficWorks Consulting confirmed the revised trip generation figures were methodologically sound; (3) a thirty-foot berm and executed routing agreement mitigated noise and traffic impacts on adjacent residential property; (4) seventeen conditions of approval, prepared by staff, addressed remaining concerns regarding hours of operation, lighting, stormwater, and enforcement; and (5) the application, as conditioned, was consistent with the general industrial land use policies currently in effect, notwithstanding the ongoing comprehensive plan update.

*Motion to Recommend Denial.* Commissioner Eze moved to recommend that the city council deny the rezoning application, Case No. Z-2026-01. Commissioner Brandt seconded.

Recording Secretary Alstrom called the roll:

Commissioner Kapoor seconded.

Chair Ocampo-Reyes stated that the city council, at its April 21, 2026 meeting, had considered the commission's recommendation of approval and had voted to remand the matter back to the commission for further findings, after the city engineer identified a discrepancy in the wetland delineation report submitted by the applicant's environmental consultant, Heartland Environmental Services, which appeared to undercount jurisdictional wetland acreage on the site by approximately 2.3 acres.
