### 5. Presentation of Counsel: Structural Options and Governing Law
Ms. Sundqvist presented a written memorandum dated January 6, 2026, "Combination Structures Under Iowa Code Chapter 504," distributed in advance. She summarized five structures: (a) statutory merger, with one corporation surviving and the other ceasing to exist; (b) consolidation into a newly formed corporation; (c) member substitution, by which one corporation is admitted as the sole corporate member of the other, which continues in existence with its own board, contracts, and licenses; (d) asset acquisition with dissolution of the transferor; and (e) contractual affiliation, including a management services or administrative services agreement without change of control.

Ms. Sundqvist advised that neither corporation has voting members under its current articles, that board approval alone would therefore suffice to adopt a plan of merger under chapter 504, and that member substitution would require amendment of the Hawkeye Youth Bridge articles to create a sole-member class. She identified consents likely required from the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services, from Seerley Holdings LLC as landlord, and from three private funders holding restricted awards. She advised that a contractual affiliation would not satisfy the Black Hawk Community Foundation's condition of a "signed agreement effecting combination."

Members questioned counsel for thirty-two minutes. Mr. Al-Rashid asked whether member substitution would preserve the Hawkeye Youth Bridge federal employer identification number, Medicaid provider agreement, and name; Ms. Sundqvist answered that it would, at least initially. Ms. Park asked whether either structure would accelerate any liability; counsel deferred to the due diligence findings.

### 5. Due Diligence Reports

**(a) Financial due diligence — Weber, Prasad & Cline LLP.** Mr. Ndiaye presented a twenty-nine-page report dated February 6, 2026, covering both corporations for fiscal years 2022 through 2025 and the interim period through December 31, 2025. He reported clean opinions in all four years for both agencies and no material weaknesses at Cedar Valley. He reported one significant deficiency at Hawkeye Youth Bridge concerning segregation of duties in cash receipts. He then reported the principal adverse finding: **Cedar Valley Family Services participates in the Midwest Human Services Retirement Trust, a multiemployer defined benefit plan, and would incur an estimated withdrawal liability of $310,000 upon complete withdrawal.** The obligation is disclosed nowhere in Cedar Valley's audited statements and was identified only through a February 3, 2026 letter obtained from the plan administrator. Mr. Ndiaye stated the amount was a preliminary administrator estimate, not an actuarial determination, and that a transaction structured as a merger could constitute a withdrawal, while a member substitution might not.

Ms. Park, as Cedar Valley treasurer, stated for the record that neither she nor the Cedar Valley finance committee had been informed of the obligation. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz stated that she had learned of it on February 4 and had notified Ms. Marchetti the same day. Mr. Duraj stated that the plan had not issued an estimate since 2019 and that prior counsel had advised no disclosure was required while participation continued.

**(b) Real property — TransForm Nonprofit Advisors and counsel.** Ms. Trandafir reported that **the Hawkeye Youth Bridge lease at 2210 West Seerley Boulevard expired September 30, 2024 and was never renewed.** The agency has occupied the premises since that date as a holdover tenant paying $8,150 monthly. Ms. Sundqvist advised that the tenancy is terminable by Seerley Holdings LLC on thirty days' notice, that the landlord filed a rezoning application for the parcel on November 12, 2025, and that no combination agreement should be signed without either a written lease of not less than three years or a relocation contingency and reserve.

**(c) Tax — Mr. Oyelaran.** Mr. Oyelaran reported that **Hawkeye Youth Bridge has not filed Form 990-T for fiscal years 2022 through 2025** with respect to the Bridge Print Shop social enterprise, which generated gross receipts averaging $186,000 annually with net income averaging $21,400. He estimated tax, interest, and penalties at $47,000 to $62,000, recommended a voluntary disclosure and delinquent filing under the reasonable cause standard, and stated that the exposure is quantifiable and curable but must be resolved before closing. Ms. Cortez-Bailey stated that the print shop had been treated as program-related by the agency's prior return preparer.

### 6. Motion to Pause the Process
**Motion.** Mr. Buchbinder moved that the committee suspend all due diligence, structural negotiation, and funder engagement for ninety days, until May 13, 2026, to allow each corporation to remediate its own findings independently, and that the co-chairs so notify the Black Hawk Community Foundation. Mr. Al-Rashid seconded.

Debate occupied fifty-one minutes and is summarized. Mr. Buchbinder stated that a $310,000 undisclosed obligation on the Cedar Valley side had changed the premises on which the Hawkeye board had agreed to explore combination, and that no board could responsibly proceed on a June 30 timetable. Mr. Al-Rashid concurred and added that the holdover tenancy and the print shop filings were themselves proof that both organizations needed a year, not five months. Ms. Park stated that she was troubled less by the amount than by the fact that the obligation had been unknown to Cedar Valley's own treasurer, and that a pause would permit an actuarial determination before any structure was chosen. Ms. Marchetti stated that a pause would forfeit the $500,000 challenge grant and would leave both agencies bidding separately in the July reprocurement. Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz stated that every finding reported was of a kind due diligence exists to find, that all three were quantifiable, and that a pause would be read by staff and funders as a collapse. Mr. Kimani stated that Hawkeye Youth Bridge's own position would not improve in ninety days.

**The motion failed on a tie, 3–3, and did not carry.** Voting aye: Mr. Buchbinder, Mr. Al-Rashid, Ms. Park. Voting nay: Ms. Marchetti, Mr. Kimani, Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz. Mr. Kimani, presiding, confirmed that under the charter a tie fails and that he would not cast a second vote.

## MINUTES OF THE MEETING OF WEDNESDAY, APRIL 8, 2026

**Body:** Joint Merger Exploration Committee of the Boards of Directors of Cedar Valley Family Services and Hawkeye Youth Bridge
**Date:** Wednesday, April 8, 2026
**Time:** Called to order 5:00 p.m.; adjourned 8:27 p.m. (Central)
**Place:** Cedar Valley Family Services, Conference Room A, 1418 Sycamore Street, Waterloo, Iowa
**Members present (6):** Obadiah Kimani, co-chair, presiding; Delphine Marchetti, co-chair; Sun-Hee Park; Naomi Feldstein-Ruiz; Roland Buchbinder; Tariq Al-Rashid
**Members absent:** None
**Others attending:** Margarethe Sundqvist and Devin Oyelaran, Sundqvist & Boateng PLC; Hollis Trandafir, TransForm Nonprofit Advisors; Marisol Echeverría, consulting actuary, Gallatin Actuarial Group (item 5); Anselm Duraj; Yvonne Cortez-Bailey; Gretchen Vandermolen, recording secretary
**Presiding:** Mr. Kimani

### 1. Call to Order, Roll, and Quorum
Mr. Kimani called the meeting to order at 5:00 p.m. All six members were present; a quorum was declared.

### 2. Approval of the Agenda
Ms. Marchetti moved, and Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz seconded, approval of the agenda circulated April 2, 2026. Mr. Buchbinder requested that item 9 be taken before item 7; the request was not seconded. **Carried 5–1.** Voting nay: Mr. Buchbinder.

### 3. Approval of Minutes of March 11, 2026
Mr. Al-Rashid moved, and Ms. Park seconded, approval of the March 11 minutes with the correction of the date of his written comments from March 9 to March 8, 2026. **Carried as amended 6–0.**

### 4. Conflict of Interest Disclosures
Ms. Sundqvist noted that item 8 would address the post-closing employment of both chief executives and advised that Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz and Mr. Buchbinder recuse themselves from any vote on their own arrangements. Both agreed.

### 5. Actuarial Determination and Remediation Closeout
Ms. Echeverría presented the Gallatin Actuarial Group determination dated March 27, 2026, fixing the Cedar Valley withdrawal liability at **$327,400** as of December 31, 2025, payable in twenty quarterly installments of approximately $19,200 if triggered. She confirmed that a member substitution not accompanied by a cessation of contributions would not constitute a withdrawal, and that the obligation would not be triggered by the recommended structure.

Ms. Cortez-Bailey reported that Forms 990-T for fiscal years 2022 through 2025 were filed April 2, 2026, with payment of $52,800 and a reasonable cause statement. Mr. Buchbinder reported that Seerley Holdings LLC had executed a five-year lease on March 31, 2026 at $9,400 monthly with a $60,000 relocation payment in the event of redevelopment termination. The committee received all three reports without objection and declared the February 11 findings remediated or provided for.

### 6. Presentation of Counsel and Consultant: Structure and Governance
Ms. Sundqvist and Ms. Trandafir presented a joint recommendation memorandum dated April 2, 2026, recommending a **member substitution** under which Cedar Valley Family Services becomes the sole corporate member of Hawkeye Youth Bridge, effective August 1, 2026, with Hawkeye Youth Bridge continuing in existence as a subsidiary corporation retaining its employer identification number, Medicaid provider agreement, licensure, and subrecipient status under Contract No. 4-2FST-2023; and with a statutory merger of the subsidiary into the parent to be considered by the combined board no earlier than July 1, 2027, following award of the reprocured state contract.

Ms. Trandafir presented the governance model: an eleven-member combined parent board, seven seats designated by the current Cedar Valley board and four by the current Hawkeye Youth Bridge board, with the four Hawkeye designations guaranteed through June 30, 2029; a subsidiary board of five, three of whom must be parent directors; Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz as chief executive of both corporations; and a transition oversight committee reporting quarterly.

Ms. Trandafir presented the **transition budget of $265,000**: legal $78,000; consulting and integration management $52,000; information technology and electronic health record consolidation $46,000; audit, actuarial, and tax $21,000; human resources harmonization including pay-band adjustment $34,000; communications and brand transition $18,000; contingency $16,000. She reported the pay-band costing requested March 11: full upward harmonization would cost $214,000 annually; a phased three-year harmonization would cost $71,000 in year one and was assumed in the $34,000 line.

### 7. Recommendation to the Parent Boards
**Motion.** Ms. Marchetti moved, and Ms. Park seconded, that the committee recommend to both parent boards: (a) combination by member substitution on the terms of the April 2, 2026 memorandum, effective August 1, 2026; (b) a combined board of eleven with a seven-to-four designation split and the guarantee described; (c) a transition budget of $265,000 funded from the Black Hawk Community Foundation challenge grant; (d) a joint response to the July 2026 state reprocurement submitted by Cedar Valley Family Services as applicant with Hawkeye Youth Bridge as named subrecipient; and (e) authority to counsel to prepare a definitive member substitution agreement for board approval on or before May 27, 2026 and execution on or before June 15, 2026.

Debate occupied one hour and six minutes. Mr. Al-Rashid stated that four seats of eleven understated an agency contributing a quarter of the combined program footprint and moved to amend the split to six and five; **the amendment failed 4–2** (aye: Mr. Al-Rashid, Mr. Buchbinder). Mr. Buchbinder stated that the committee had reached, in April, precisely the conclusion his January motion had predicted, and that the structure was a merger in all but name. Ms. Park stated that she had voted to pause in February and would vote in favor now because every finding had been quantified and cured. Mr. Kimani stated that the Hawkeye board's fiduciary position, at 1.2 months of reserves, admitted of no better outcome for the young people the agency served.

**The main motion carried 4–2.** Voting aye: Ms. Marchetti, Mr. Kimani, Ms. Park, Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz. Voting nay: Mr. Buchbinder, Mr. Al-Rashid.

### 8. Executive Leadership
On the motion of Ms. Marchetti, seconded by Mr. Kimani, with Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz and Mr. Buchbinder recused and not voting, the committee recommended that Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz serve as chief executive of the combined organization and that Mr. Buchbinder be offered a twelve-month appointment as founding executive and senior advisor for youth services at his current salary, with the deferred compensation of $41,000 paid at closing. **Carried 4–0**, two recused.

### 9. The Surviving Name
The main motion postponed March 11 was taken up. **Motion to amend** by Ms. Feldstein-Ruiz, seconded by Ms. Marchetti, to add that the Hawkeye Youth Bridge name be retained as the operating brand of the youth division for not less than five years and as the subsidiary's corporate name. **Carried 6–0.** **The main motion, as amended, carried 5–1**, recommending Cedar Valley Family Services as the surviving parent name with the Hawkeye Youth Bridge name retained as described. Voting nay: Mr. Buchbinder.

### 10. Resignation
At 8:04 p.m. Mr. Buchbinder tendered a written resignation from the committee, dated April 8, 2026, effective immediately, and read it into the record. The letter stated that he could not participate further in recommending a structure he had opposed at each stage, that his resignation was from the committee only and not from his position as executive director, and that he would carry out the committee's directives as an employee. Mr. Kimani accepted the resignation on behalf of the committee, entered the letter into the file, and thanked Mr. Buchbinder for thirty years of service to the young people of Cedar Falls. Mr. Buchbinder left the meeting at 8:09 p.m. **Motion** by Ms. Marchetti, seconded by Mr. Al-Rashid, that the resignation be reported to both parent boards with the letter attached and that Mr. Kimani recommend a replacement to the Hawkeye Youth Bridge board by April 20, 2026. **Carried 5–0.**
