## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the process by which Cedar Line Markets receives, classifies, and executes product recalls and market withdrawals across all 34 retail stores, the Airway Heights distribution center (DC), and the commissary. It exists to ensure that affected product is blocked from sale, removed from shelves and storage, reconciled against quantities received, and dispositioned in a controlled and documented manner, and that customers and regulators are notified within defined timeframes.

This version replaces Version 2.1 following the February 2026 Class I Listeria monocytogenes recall event, in which 11 stores retained affected product on shelf 31 hours after notice receipt, resulting in a documented finding by a Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA) inspector, and following a subsequent mock recall that recovered only 71 percent of affected units against a target of 100 percent within four hours. The changes in this version are designed to close the specific failures observed: delayed store acknowledgment, absence of a register block deadline, no lot-level reconciliation requirement, and no defined after-hours pathway.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- All food and non-food product sold in any of the 34 Cedar Line Markets stores in eastern Washington and northern Idaho.
- All product held at the Airway Heights DC.
- All product produced by the Cedar Line commissary, including store-made deli items sold under Cedar Line labels.
- All direct-store-delivery (DSD) product from the 46 DSD vendors whose inventory does not pass through the DC.
- Supplier-initiated recalls, Cedar Line–initiated recalls, regulator-requested recalls, and voluntary market withdrawals.

This procedure does not cover routine damaged-goods returns, ordinary vendor credit disputes, or quality complaints that do not involve a safety, regulatory, or labeling defect. Those are handled under FSQA-SOP-006 (Product Quality Complaint Handling).

## 3. Definitions

**Recall.** Removal of product from commerce because the product violates law or presents a risk of illness, injury, or economic deception. Recalls are classified per Section 6.2.

**Market withdrawal.** Voluntary removal of product for a reason not involving a violation of law or a health hazard (e.g., quality defect, wrong promotional packaging). Withdrawals follow this SOP with the modifications in Section 6.14.

**Class I.** Reasonable probability that use of or exposure to the product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death. Examples: Listeria monocytogenes in ready-to-eat food, undeclared major allergen.

**Class II.** Use of or exposure to the product may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or the probability of serious consequences is remote.

**Class III.** Use of or exposure to the product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences (e.g., minor labeling defect).

**Register block.** An item-file hold pushed to every point-of-sale lane in all 34 stores that prevents a UPC from scanning for sale. A blocked item returns a "DO NOT SELL — RECALL" message at the lane.

**DSD (direct-store-delivery).** Product delivered by a vendor directly to individual stores, bypassing the DC. DSD lot data resides only in store-level receiving records.

**Lot-level receiving record.** The record created at DC or store receiving that captures item, UPC, lot or date code, quantity, receipt date, and delivering vendor.

**Effectiveness check.** A documented verification, at defined intervals, that affected product has been removed from sale, quarantined, and reconciled.

**Reconciliation.** The comparison of quantities received (from lot-level receiving records) against quantities recovered (pulled at stores, quarantined at DC, sold before the block, returned by customers, or otherwise accounted for), with the goal of accounting for 100 percent of units.

**Recall coordinator.** The named individual (primary and backup, listed in Appendix A of the recall contact roster) responsible for executing this procedure end to end.

**Notification platform.** The supplier notification platform through which supplier and trade recall notices arrive and through which Cedar Line issues internal alerts with mandatory acknowledgment tracking.

**Recall event file.** The single electronic folder, opened per Section 6.1, that holds every record generated during a recall event.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Director of Food Safety and Quality Assurance (Priscilla Ndiaye).** Owns this SOP. Classifies each recall (or confirms the supplier's classification). Serves as decision authority for Class I/II actions when the classification is ambiguous. Approves final disposition of Class I product. Signs regulatory reports. Leads the post-event review.

**Senior Vice President of Retail Operations (Grady Sturdivant).** Approves this SOP and any revision. Is notified of every Class I event within one hour of classification. Approves any decision to close a recall with less than 100 percent reconciliation. Authorizes public communications beyond the standard customer notice.

**Recall coordinator (primary and backup).** Executes the procedure: receives and logs notices, opens the recall event file, triggers the register block, issues store and DC instructions, tracks acknowledgments, compiles reconciliation, runs effectiveness checks, drafts regulatory reports, and maintains the recall event file. The backup assumes all duties when the primary is unavailable; both carry after-hours phones per Section 6.13.

**Category managers.** Confirm item identification (UPC, lot codes, pack sizes, private-label crossovers), identify substitute product, suspend affected purchase orders, and process vendor credit claims per Section 6.11.

**Store directors (34).** Acknowledge recall alerts within the clocks in Section 6.3, direct the shelf pull and back-room sweep, verify the register block at the lane, complete and return the store recall response form, secure quarantined product, and manage customer returns at the service desk.

**Receiving leads (store level).** Retrieve store-level lot receiving records for DSD product, refuse delivery of recalled DSD product, and countersign store quarantine counts.

**DC inventory control.** Executes the warehouse management system (WMS) hold, physically quarantines affected product, retrieves DC lot-level receiving records, halts outbound allocation of affected items, and reports quarantined quantities to the recall coordinator.

**Customer care manager.** Executes loyalty-database customer notification per Section 6.8, staffs the recall response line, logs illness or injury complaints, and escalates any reported illness to the FSQA director immediately.

**Marketing director.** Prepares and posts point-of-sale recall notices, website and app postings, and (with SVP approval) press releases.

**Commissary manager.** Maintains commissary batch records, executes commissary-item recalls per Section 6.13.3, and holds affected raw ingredients.

**Legal counsel.** Reviews Class I customer communications and press releases before release; advises on regulatory reporting obligations.

## 5. Prerequisites and Standing Requirements

5.1 The recall contact roster (Appendix A) lists names, titles, office phones, mobile phones, and emails for every role in Section 4, including after-hours numbers. The recall coordinator verifies the roster quarterly and after any personnel change.

5.2 Every store maintains a designated, labeled quarantine area in the back room: a shelf or cage marked "RECALL HOLD — DO NOT SELL — DO NOT RETURN TO VENDOR," physically separated from saleable inventory and, for refrigerated or frozen product, within a designated marked section of the walk-in.

5.3 The DC maintains a fenced or clearly demarcated quarantine zone under WMS location control with hold status.

5.4 The item file team maintains a tested register-block procedure capable of pushing a UPC block to all lanes in all 34 stores within 60 minutes of the request, including outside business hours.

5.5 Mock recalls are conducted at least twice per year: one supplier-item scenario traced through the DC, and one alternating between a DSD scenario and a commissary scenario. The performance standard is 100 percent of affected units accounted for within four hours of alert issuance. Any mock recall below 95 percent triggers a corrective action plan owned by the FSQA director within 10 business days.

5.6 All store directors, receiving leads, and DC inventory control staff complete recall training at hire and annually. Training records are retained per Section 7.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Receipt and Logging of a Recall Notice — Clock Zero

6.1.1 Recall notices may arrive through the supplier notification platform, direct supplier email or phone call, a regulator (FDA, USDA-FSIS, WSDA, Idaho State Department of Agriculture), a trade association alert, or internal discovery (customer complaint, commissary test result, store observation).

6.1.2 Whoever first receives a notice by any channel other than the notification platform forwards it to the recall coordinator immediately by phone (not email alone). All employees are trained that a recall notice is never held for the next business day.

6.1.3 The recall coordinator's receipt of the notice — or, for platform notices, the platform's delivery timestamp — is **Clock Zero**. Every deadline in this SOP is measured from Clock Zero.

6.1.4 Within 30 minutes of Clock Zero, the recall coordinator:

a. Opens a recall event file named `RECALL-[YYYY]-[sequence number]` in the FSQA shared drive.
b. Logs the event in the recall log: event number, date/time of Clock Zero, source of notice, product description, UPC(s), lot code(s)/date codes, reason for recall, supplier or origin.
c. Saves the original notice and any attachments to the event file.
d. Calls the FSQA director. If unreachable within 15 minutes, calls the backup listed in Appendix A.

6.1.5 If information in the notice is incomplete (missing lot codes, unclear UPCs), the recall coordinator contacts the supplier immediately for clarification but **does not delay action**. When lot codes are unknown or unclear, all lots of the affected UPC are treated as affected until the supplier confirms otherwise in writing.

### 6.2 Classification

6.2.1 Within one hour of Clock Zero, the FSQA director (or backup) classifies the event as Class I, Class II, Class III, or market withdrawal. If the supplier or regulator has assigned a classification, Cedar Line adopts it unless the FSQA director documents a reason to treat it more stringently. Cedar Line never downgrades a supplier or regulator classification.

6.2.2 If classification cannot be completed within one hour because information is pending, the event is treated as **Class I by default** until reclassified in writing.

6.2.3 The classification, the name of the person who made it, and the rationale are recorded in the recall event file.

### 6.3 Internal Notification Clocks

The recall coordinator issues the internal recall alert through the notification platform with mandatory acknowledgment. The alert states: product description, UPC(s), lot codes, reason for recall, classification, required actions, and deadlines. Notification and acknowledgment deadlines from Clock Zero:

| Recipient | Class I | Class II | Class III / Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|---|
| FSQA director | 30 min | 30 min | 2 hours |
| SVP Retail Operations | 1 hour | 4 hours | Next business day |
| Item file team (register block request) | 1 hour | 2 hours | 4 hours |
| All 34 store directors | 1 hour | 4 hours | Next business day |
| DC inventory control | 1 hour | 4 hours | Next business day |
| Affected category manager | 1 hour | 4 hours | Next business day |
| Customer care manager | 2 hours | 8 hours | Next business day |
| Marketing director | 2 hours | 8 hours | Next business day |
| Receiving leads (DSD events) | 1 hour | 4 hours | Next business day |

6.3.2 **Acknowledgment requirement.** Each store director must acknowledge the alert within 30 minutes of its issuance for Class I, within 2 hours for Class II. The recall coordinator monitors the platform's acknowledgment dashboard. Any store that has not acknowledged by deadline is called directly by the recall coordinator: store director's mobile first, then the store's main line, then the assistant store director. The recall coordinator logs every call. **No store is considered notified until acknowledgment is captured.** This step directly addresses the February 2026 failure, in which 11 stores did not act on the emailed notice.

### 6.4 Register Block

6.4.1 Within one hour of Clock Zero for Class I (two hours for Class II), the item file team pushes a register block on every affected UPC to all lanes in all 34 stores. The block message reads "DO NOT SELL — RECALL — CALL MANAGER."

6.4.2 The item file team confirms block deployment to the recall coordinator in writing, listing each UPC and the deployment timestamp. The recall coordinator saves this confirmation to the event file.

6.4.3 **Lane verification.** Each store director attempts to scan the affected UPC at one lane within one hour of receiving the alert and records the result on the store recall response form. If the item scans for sale, the store director calls the recall coordinator immediately; the store then pulls the item and posts a manual "do not sell" flag at every lane until the block deploys.

6.4.4 The register block remains in place until the recall coordinator authorizes removal in writing per Section 6.12. Where lot codes distinguish affected from unaffected product, the block still applies to the full UPC; unaffected lots may be re-shelved only after the store director verifies lot codes item by item and documents the verification, and only with the recall coordinator's written approval.

### 6.5 Store-Level Shelf Pull and Quarantine

6.5.1 Immediately upon acknowledgment, and no later than **two hours after alert issuance for Class I** (four hours for Class II), each store director causes a physical sweep of:

a. The primary shelf location and all secondary displays (end caps, cross-merchandising, promotional bins, checkstand racks).
b. Back-room storage, including top stock and pallets.
c. Walk-in coolers and freezers.
d. The service deli, salad bar, and prepared-foods cases if the recalled item is used as an ingredient (see 6.5.4).
e. Any product staged for online-order or curbside fulfillment, and any open online orders containing the item, which are edited to remove it.

6.5.2 All pulled product is moved to the store quarantine area (Section 5.2), tagged with the recall event number, and counted by unit. The count is verified by a second person (receiving lead or assistant store director), and both sign the store recall response form.

6.5.3 The store director completes the **store recall response form** and returns it to the recall coordinator within **four hours of alert issuance for Class I** (eight hours for Class II). The form records: store number; date/time alert received and acknowledged; date/time sweep completed; units found on shelf; units found in back room/other; total units quarantined; lane verification result; lot codes observed; name and signature of counter and verifier. A "zero on hand" report is mandatory — silence is not an acceptable response, and the recall coordinator treats a missing form as a non-compliant store and escalates to the SVP.

6.5.4 **Ingredient check.** If the recalled item could be used as an ingredient in store-prepared foods (e.g., a recalled produce item used on the salad bar), the store director also discards or quarantines any prepared product containing it, records the quantity, and notes it on the response form. When in doubt, discard and document.

6.5.5 Quarantined product is not sold, sampled, donated, consumed by employees, returned to shelf, or returned to the vendor without written disposition instructions per Section 6.10.

### 6.6 DC Quarantine and Reconciliation

6.6.1 Within one hour of the alert for Class I (four hours for Class II), DC inventory control:

a. Places a WMS hold on every affected item/lot, blocking picking, allocation, and outbound shipment.
b. Intercepts any affected product already picked, staged, or loaded but not yet departed; holds any truck at the dock that contains affected product until it is removed.
c. Physically moves affected product to the DC quarantine zone and confirms WMS location transfer.
d. Notifies the recall coordinator of any shipments of the affected lot that departed within the preceding 72 hours, listing destination stores and quantities, so those stores can be flagged for arriving product.

6.6.2 DC inventory control retrieves lot-level receiving records for every receipt of the affected item/lot and reports to the recall coordinator within four hours: total units received, receipt dates, purchase order numbers, units currently on hand, units shipped by store with ship dates, and units quarantined.

6.6.3 **Reconciliation.** The recall coordinator builds the reconciliation worksheet in the event file:

> **Units received (DC + DSD store receipts) = Units quarantined at stores + Units quarantined at DC + Units sold prior to block (POS movement data) + Units returned by customers + Units otherwise accounted for (documented spoilage/shrink/prior vendor return)**

Units sold prior to the block are obtained from POS sales movement by store from the receipt date of the earliest affected lot through the block timestamp. Any unexplained gap is investigated: the recall coordinator directs a re-sweep of the specific stores whose received-versus-recovered figures do not balance. The reconciliation target is **100 percent of units accounted for**. The worksheet is updated at the 24-hour and 72-hour effectiveness checks and at closure.

### 6.7 Category Manager Actions

6.7.1 Within four hours of the alert, the affected category manager:

a. Confirms the full list of affected UPCs, including size variants, multipacks, private-label equivalents produced by the same supplier, and any shipper/display SKUs containing the item, and reports any additions to the recall coordinator (which restarts Sections 6.4–6.6 for the added UPCs).
b. Suspends open purchase orders for the affected item and lots.
c. Notifies the supplier that Cedar Line will not accept further shipments of affected lots.
d. Identifies substitute product for stores if the item is a core assortment item.

### 6.8 Customer Notification

6.8.1 **Loyalty-database direct notice (Class I mandatory; Class II at FSQA director's discretion).** Within 24 hours of Clock Zero for Class I, the customer care manager queries the loyalty database (1.2 million cardholders) for all households that purchased the affected UPC(s) within the affected lot window (or, if the window is unknown, the prior 60 days, or the item's shelf life if longer). Notice is sent by email and, where the cardholder has opted in, SMS. The notice states: product name, UPC, lot codes/dates, reason for recall, health risk in plain language, instruction not to consume, return-for-refund instructions (no receipt required), the recall response line number, and for Class I pathogen recalls, symptom information and advice to contact a physician if symptoms occur.

6.8.2 Legal counsel reviews Class I customer notices before release; review is completed within four hours of the draft so the 24-hour deadline is met.

6.8.3 **Point of sale.** Within 24 hours of Clock Zero for Class I (72 hours Class II), marketing distributes a shelf-edge and service-desk recall notice to all 34 stores; store directors post it at the item's shelf location and the service desk for a minimum of 14 days or as directed by the regulator.

6.8.4 **Website and app.** Marketing posts the recall on the Cedar Line website recall page and app banner within 24 hours for Class I.

6.8.5 **Customer returns.** Service desks refund any returned affected product with or without a receipt, quarantine returned units with the event tag, log the count on a supplemental response form, and report counts to the recall coordinator daily until closure. Returned units feed the reconciliation in 6.6.3.

6.8.6 **Illness reports.** Any customer report of illness or injury is logged verbatim by customer care, escalated to the FSQA director within one hour, and preserved (including the product, if the customer will surrender it) as a potential regulatory and legal record. The FSQA director determines regulator notification per Section 6.9.

### 6.9 Regulatory Reporting

6.9.1 For a Cedar Line–initiated recall of a product Cedar Line manufactured (commissary) or imported, the FSQA director determines within 24 hours whether an FDA Reportable Food Registry submission is required (reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences), and if so submits it within 24 hours of that determination. USDA-FSIS–regulated commissary product follows FSIS notification requirements.

6.9.2 For supplier-initiated recalls, Cedar Line responds fully and promptly to information requests from FDA, USDA-FSIS, WSDA, and the Idaho State Department of Agriculture, including effectiveness-check data. The FSQA director is the sole point of contact with regulators; store directors who receive an inspector visit cooperate on site and notify the FSQA director immediately.

6.9.3 The FSQA director notifies WSDA and/or ISDA proactively of any Class I event affecting Cedar Line stores in their state when illness has been reported, when commissary product is involved, or when a regulator inquiry is anticipated. All regulator communications are logged in the event file.

### 6.10 Effectiveness Checks

6.10.1 **24-hour check.** At 24 hours from Clock Zero (all classes except Class III/withdrawal, which check at 72 hours only), the recall coordinator verifies and documents:

a. All 34 stores acknowledged and returned response forms (including zero reports).
b. Register block confirmed deployed and lane-verified at all stores.
c. DC hold and quarantine confirmed.
d. Reconciliation worksheet current; percentage of units accounted for calculated.
e. For Class I: customer notice sent; POS notices distributed.

Any deficiency triggers immediate corrective action: direct call to the deficient store or department, a re-sweep, and escalation to the SVP if unresolved within two hours.

6.10.2 **72-hour check.** At 72 hours, the recall coordinator conducts **physical spot verification at no fewer than five stores** (selected to include any store with a reconciliation gap, plus a random sample), performed by district managers or FSQA staff using the effectiveness check form: scan the UPC at a lane (must be blocked), inspect the shelf and secondary locations (must be empty of affected product), inspect the quarantine area (product tagged and counted), and verify the POS notice is posted. Results are documented in the event file.

6.10.3 If reconciliation is below 100 percent at 72 hours, the recall coordinator documents the gap, the investigation performed, and the most probable explanation (typically pre-block sales not captured in movement data, or shrink). Closure with less than 100 percent documented accounting requires the SVP's written approval per Section 6.12.

### 6.11 Product Disposition and Credit

6.11.1 No quarantined product moves anywhere until the recall coordinator issues written disposition instructions, which follow the supplier's or regulator's direction. Permitted dispositions:

a. **Return to supplier** via supplier-arranged pickup or supplier-designated carrier, with a signed manifest listing item, lot, and unit count per store/DC.
b. **On-site destruction** (compactor/dumpster rendered unsalvageable — e.g., opened and denatured for Class I product) witnessed by two employees who sign a destruction certificate listing item, lot, quantity, date, method, and witnesses. Class I destruction at the DC may additionally be witnessed by a regulator on request.
c. **Consolidated destruction at the DC**, with store-to-DC transfer documented on a recall transfer manifest.

Recalled product is **never** donated, sold at salvage, or given to employees.

6.11.2 The category manager files the vendor credit claim within 10 business days of disposition, supported by the reconciliation worksheet, response forms, manifests, and destruction certificates. The claim includes product cost, and where the supplier agreement provides, handling and disposal costs. DSD credits are settled through the DSD vendor's route accounting with the store receiving lead's countersignature on picked-up quantities.

### 6.12 Recall Closure

6.12.1 The recall coordinator prepares a closure summary when: all stores and the DC report zero affected product outside quarantine; disposition is complete with documentation; reconciliation is at 100 percent or the gap is documented and explained; customer notification obligations are met; and the supplier or regulator has confirmed no further action.

6.12.2 The FSQA director approves closure for Class II/III and withdrawals. Class I closure, and any closure with less than 100 percent documented accounting, requires the SVP's written approval.

6.12.3 Upon closure, the recall coordinator authorizes the item file team in writing to lift the register block (for lots confirmed unaffected or for the UPC once replacement stock arrives) and notifies all parties that the event is closed.

6.12.4 **Post-event review.** Within 15 business days of closing any Class I event or any event with an effectiveness deficiency, the FSQA director convenes a review with the recall coordinator, a store director representative, DC inventory control, and the category manager. The review produces a written report: timeline against the clocks in this SOP, deficiencies, root causes, and corrective actions with owners and due dates. Corrective actions that require SOP changes trigger an off-cycle revision.

### 6.13 Exceptions and Special Situations

#### 6.13.1 After-Hours Notices

a. The recall coordinator (primary and backup) carries the after-hours recall phone on a published rotation; the notification platform routes alerts to that phone 24/7. Clock Zero applies regardless of the hour.
b. For a **Class I notice received outside store operating hours**, the recall coordinator triggers the register block request immediately (the item file on-call contact in Appendix A supports 24/7 deployment) so the block is live before any store opens. The store alert is issued immediately; store directors' acknowledgment clock starts at the earlier of their acknowledgment or **one hour before their store's opening time**, and the shelf pull must be completed **before the store opens for business**. Opening managers are trained to check the notification platform before unlocking doors.
c. For Class II/III notices received after hours, the coordinator may hold store alerts until 6:00 a.m. local time but issues the register block request immediately.
d. The DC operates extended hours; DC holds are executed on receipt regardless of hour. If the DC is unstaffed, the WMS hold is entered remotely by the coordinator or on-call inventory control, and physical quarantine occurs at the start of the next shift, before any picking begins.

#### 6.13.2 Direct-Store-Delivery (DSD) Vendors

a. Because DSD product from the 46 DSD vendors never touches the DC, Section 6.6 DC steps do not apply. Instead, **store receiving leads** retrieve store-level lot receiving records for the affected item and report quantities received (with dates) to the recall coordinator within four hours of the alert; these figures replace DC receipts in the reconciliation formula in 6.6.3.
b. The register block (6.4), shelf pull (6.5), customer notice (6.8), and effectiveness checks (6.10) apply in full.
c. Receiving leads refuse any delivery of the affected item/lot for the duration of the event and note refusals on the delivery record.
d. Where the DSD vendor performs its own pull, a Cedar Line employee (receiving lead or manager on duty) verifies and countersigns the vendor's pickup count; the vendor does not remove product without Cedar Line documentation. Vendor pickup counts feed reconciliation and the credit claim (6.11.2).
e. If a DSD vendor's lot records are inadequate to identify affected receipts, all on-hand stock of the affected UPC is treated as affected.

#### 6.13.3 Commissary Items Without a Supplier Lot Code

a. Commissary-produced items carry a Cedar Line production date code, not a supplier lot code. When a recall involves a raw ingredient used by the commissary, the commissary manager identifies from batch records every production batch that used the affected ingredient lot and reports, within four hours of the alert, the affected finished-item names, production dates, sell-by dates, and store shipment quantities by store.
b. The store alert identifies affected commissary items by **item name, PLU/UPC, and sell-by date range**. Stores pull all units within the date range. If batch records cannot conclusively tie the affected ingredient lot to specific production dates, **all in-code product of the affected item is pulled**.
c. The commissary quarantines remaining affected raw ingredient and any in-process or finished product on site, halts production of the affected items, and does not resume until the FSQA director approves in writing (which may require sanitation verification and environmental or product testing for a Class I pathogen event).
d. Because commissary items are Cedar Line–manufactured, the FSQA director evaluates Reportable Food Registry and state notification obligations per Section 6.9.1.

#### 6.13.4 Voluntary Market Withdrawals

a. Withdrawals follow this SOP with these modifications: classification is recorded as "Withdrawal"; the register block deadline is 8 hours; store pull deadline is 24 hours; customer direct notice and POS notices are **not** required unless the FSQA director directs otherwise; regulatory reporting under 6.9 does not apply; effectiveness check occurs at 72 hours only.
b. If information emerges during a withdrawal indicating a safety or regulatory issue, the FSQA director reclassifies the event immediately and all recall clocks restart from the reclassification time.
