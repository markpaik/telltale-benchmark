# Inventory of a House That Was Never Built

**Foundation.** Poured in a field that does not exist, in a county that would have been named after a surveyor who drowned. The concrete cured for twenty-eight days in weather that no one recorded because there was no one there. It is still curing. It will be curing at the heat death.

**Load-bearing wall, north.** Intended to hold up the second floor, which was intended to hold up the attic, which was intended to hold up nothing but a slanted ceiling and the particular quality of light that comes through a single square window at four in the afternoon in October. The wall bears this load perfectly. Absence is structurally sound. It is the only material that never fails.

**Doorway between the kitchen and the room off the kitchen.** No door was specified. The architect — there was no architect — left it open so that whoever was cooking could hear whoever was reading. This is the most important dimension in the house and it appears on no plan. Thirty-two inches of air, arranged so that two people can be alone together. Every house that has ever worked has had one of these. Most were accidents.

**Stairs.** Fourteen risers, seven and a quarter inches each. The fourth one from the bottom creaks. This was not designed in; it was simply true from the beginning, the way certain facts about a person are true before they have done anything. Someone would have learned to skip it, coming home late. Someone else would have learned to listen for the skip and known, from the silence where the creak should have been, exactly who was on the stairs and exactly how sorry they were.

**Windows, east-facing, two.** Sized for a morning that would arrive over a ridge. There is no ridge. The windows are sized for it anyway. This is what longing is: a set of measurements taken from a thing you have not seen, which turn out to be correct.

**Hearth.** Fieldstone, gathered from the field, the one that does not exist. There is a shallow depression in the stone at the left corner where someone would have set down a cup ten thousand times. Wear is a form of testimony. This wear happened in advance. The stone remembers a hand that will not arrive, and the remembering is not less real for pointing the wrong direction in time.

**Closet under the stairs.** Full.

**A crack in the plaster above the sink,** running from the corner of the window toward the ceiling, hairline, in the shape of a river seen from very high up. It appeared in the second winter, when the house settled. Houses settle. That is the word for it — as if the house were an animal lying down, as if it were making itself comfortable, as if the process of coming apart and the process of getting at ease were the same motion, which, if you have ever watched anything grow old, you know they are.

**Roof.** Not finished. The rafters go up and meet and there is sky between them. This was going to be fixed in the spring. The spring is a load-bearing element. Everything the house does not have, it is holding up with the spring.

**Contents, miscellaneous.** A chipped bowl. A tool with a wooden handle worn to the shape of a specific grip. Three books that were meant to be returned. A drawer of things too useful to throw away and too particular to use. The smell of a room that has had a wet dog in it in another season. A name called up the stairs, mid-syllable, permanently.

**Occupancy.** Continuous.

**Valuation.** The assessor came, or would have. He stood in the field and turned in a slow circle with his clipboard, and after a while he put the clipboard down in the grass, because a value is a number you can trade for a thing, and here there was nothing to trade and nothing to trade it for, only the shape of a life pressed into an empty place like a fossil pressed into shale — which is not the animal, and is not nothing, and is in fact the only part of the animal that lasted.

**Disposition.** The house is not to be built. Building it would require choosing which four o'clock light, which of the fourteen risers, whose voice on the stairs, and choosing is a kind of demolition. So it stands unbuilt, in the field, in the county, in the weather no one records, complete in every particular, warm, lit, and full of people, and if you want to visit you already know the road.
