# Merrimack Valley Health Partners
## Internal Memorandum Packet: Obstetrics Consolidation, Haverhill to Lowell Campus

---

**TO:** Medical Staff, Haverhill Campus and Lowell Campus
**FROM:** Anjali Ramaswamy, MD, Chief Medical Officer
**DATE:** January 26, 2027
**SUBJECT:** Consolidation of Inpatient Obstetric Services to the Lowell Campus

I am writing to inform you of a decision by the System Leadership Team and the Board of Trustees to consolidate inpatient obstetric services onto the Lowell campus, with a target completion date of October 1, 2027. I recognize this news will be difficult for many of you, particularly colleagues who have built careers delivering babies at Haverhill, and I want to walk through the reasoning as directly as I can.

Births at Haverhill have declined from 1,180 in calendar year 2019 to a projected 690 this year — a drop of roughly 42 percent in seven years. This is not a Haverhill-specific phenomenon; it reflects regional demographic shifts, a falling birth rate across Essex County, and increasing patient preference for higher-volume delivery units. But the operational consequence has been severe. The obstetrics unit at Haverhill lost $4.3 million last fiscal year, a figure that has grown each of the past three years even as we have worked to control costs. At current volumes, we are maintaining round-the-clock labor and delivery capability, anesthesia coverage, and a dedicated OB hospitalist program for an average of fewer than two deliveries a day. This is not sustainable, clinically or financially, and the Board has concluded that continuing to operate two inpatient obstetric units is no longer responsible stewardship of the system's resources.

Consolidating onto Lowell allows us to concentrate nursing and physician expertise where delivery volume supports it, which we believe will improve safety margins for high-acuity deliveries and NICU-adjacent care. It also allows us to redirect capital toward expanding the Lowell unit rather than maintaining duplicate infrastructure at both sites.

I want to be transparent about the regulatory process ahead. Massachusetts requires that we provide the Department of Public Health with 120 days' advance notice of a service discontinuation of this kind, along with an Essential Services Plan describing how patients who would have used the discontinued service will be able to access care, and a public hearing. That hearing has been scheduled for April 9. Devon Okonkwo's office is coordinating the filing, and Latasha Greer's team is leading the community engagement and access components of the Essential Services Plan, including transportation and emergency transfer protocols for patients who present in labor at Haverhill after the unit closes.

This decision affects 62 full-time positions at Haverhill, spanning nursing, obstetric technicians, unit clerks, and support staff. Marisol Vega-Ruiz will be communicating separately and in detail about redeployment options, and I want to be clear that no one should assume their employment with the system is at risk. Our intent is to place as many affected staff as possible into open positions at Lowell, including the expanded obstetric unit there, or into other clinical roles at Haverhill.

I recognize this timeline is aggressive, and I anticipate the Medical Staff Executive Committee will want to review it closely, particularly the arrangements for patients who arrive at Haverhill in active labor or with obstetric emergencies after October 1. I am asking the Committee to convene a special session in the next two weeks so that we can walk through the draft transfer and stabilization protocol before it is finalized, and I welcome that scrutiny. Safe, well-designed transfer protocols for precipitous delivery are the single most important clinical safeguard in this plan, and I would rather have that debate now than discover a gap in April.

I know some of you will disagree with this decision, and I respect that disagreement even where I do not share it. I am available to meet individually with any physician who wants to discuss the clinical implications, and I will hold open office hours at both campuses over the next three weeks.

**TO:** Anjali Ramaswamy, Devon Okonkwo, Marisol Vega-Ruiz, Latasha Greer
**FROM:** Aaron Lindqvist, Chief Financial Officer
**DATE:** February 12, 2027
**SUBJECT:** Revised Capital and Transition Budget — OB Consolidation

This memo updates the executive team on the capital and transition budget for the OB consolidation following receipt of the construction bid for the Lowell unit expansion, and it lays out the funding gap we now need to close.

**Capital Budget.** The Board approved $5.5 million in capital for the Lowell expansion from 12 to 20 beds when this project was first scoped last fall, with an additional $1.0 million reserve, for a total approved line of $6.5 million. Wentworth Construction returned its bid on February 3 at $8.4 million — $1.9 million over the base approval and $2.9 million over the full approved capital line including reserve. The overage is concentrated in three areas: mechanical and electrical upgrades required to support the additional four labor/delivery rooms and four postpartum beds, a code-driven fire suppression retrofit in the adjacent corridor that was not fully scoped in the original design, and a steel price escalation of roughly 11 percent since the estimate was built last August.

I have asked Devon's facilities team to pursue three parallel paths to close this gap. First, value engineering: Wentworth has identified approximately $600,000 in potential savings by substituting finish specifications in non-clinical areas and phasing the corridor fire suppression work into a second contract that does not have to be complete by October 1. Second, I am preparing a supplemental capital request for the March 24 Board Finance Committee meeting seeking authorization for the remaining gap, which after value engineering would be approximately $1.7 million. Third, Latasha's office has identified a potential $400,000 philanthropic commitment from the Merrimack Valley Health Foundation earmarked for maternal-child health capital, which if secured would reduce the Board ask further.

I want to be direct about the sequencing risk. If the Finance Committee does not act by March 24, we will not have a fully funded construction contract in place in time to complete the Lowell buildout by the October 1 target, given Wentworth's stated 26-week construction schedule from notice to proceed. I am treating March 24 as a hard internal deadline and will flag to the full Board if it is at risk.

**Transition Budget.** Separately from capital construction, the Board approved a $2.6 million contingency fund to cover transition costs — severance, relocation assistance, recruitment and sign-on costs for Lowell positions, temporary agency staffing to cover gaps during the changeover, and enhanced transport contracting. As of this week we have committed or spent $1.9 million against that fund, or 73 percent, with roughly seven and a half months remaining before the target close date. The largest driver has been temporary agency nursing at Haverhill, which we activated earlier than planned after two of the four obstetric hospitalists gave notice in February; agency hospitalist coverage alone has consumed $640,000 of the contingency to date.

I do not believe the transition budget is in immediate crisis, but the burn rate is faster than our original model assumed, and I want the team to be conservative about additional discretionary transition spending — signing bonuses beyond the approved schedule, extended relocation packages, and the like — without checking with my office first. I would rather flag this early than come back in July asking for a supplemental appropriation under time pressure.

I will circulate the Finance Committee materials for review by March 10 and would appreciate comments from each of you, particularly Marisol on staffing cost assumptions and Devon on the revised construction schedule, no later than March 5.

**TO:** Nursing Leadership, Lowell and Haverhill Campuses
**FROM:** Marisol Vega-Ruiz, Chief Nursing Officer
**DATE:** March 3, 2027
**SUBJECT:** Redeployment Plan for Affected Haverhill OB Staff and March 15 Bidding Process

This memo lays out the redeployment framework for the 62 full-time positions affected by the Haverhill obstetrics consolidation and describes the bidding process that will run on March 15. I am asking every nurse manager and charge nurse in this distribution to review this carefully and be prepared to answer staff questions over the next two weeks, because the anxiety on the unit right now is high and clear, consistent information matters more than usual.

**The Three Tracks.** Affected staff will have three options, consistent with our contractual obligations under the collective bargaining agreement and our internal redeployment policy.

*Track One — Transfer to the Lowell OB Expansion.* The Lowell unit is expanding from 12 to 20 beds, which will create a meaningful number of new labor and delivery, postpartum, and OB tech positions once construction is complete. I want to be candid that the current nursing vacancy rate on the Lowell OB unit stands at 23 percent, which has been a persistent staffing strain independent of this consolidation. Haverhill OB staff who transfer to Lowell will be filling real, needed positions, not displacing colleagues already there, and I want that message delivered without ambiguity. Staff choosing this track will receive a relocation stipend, a temporary commuting allowance for the first six months, and priority scheduling accommodation during the transition period.

*Track Two — Transfer to Other Haverhill Units.* Med-surg, the emergency department, and post-acute care at Haverhill all have open requisitions. Staff choosing this track will be offered cross-training support, including a paid four-week orientation period, and, where applicable, tuition assistance toward certification in the new specialty area (PCCN, CEN, or equivalent depending on placement).

*Track Three — Voluntary Separation.* Staff who prefer not to transfer may elect a voluntary severance package structured on years of service, or, where eligible, an early retirement incentive. HR will hold individual benefits counseling sessions for anyone considering this option before the bidding deadline.

**Coverage Concerns.** I want to acknowledge directly that this redeployment is happening against a backdrop of real coverage strain. Two of our four obstetric hospitalists at Haverhill gave notice in February, and we are running locum tenens coverage to bridge through closure. I recognize this makes the unit feel less stable in exactly the period we are asking staff to plan their next six months, and I don't want to minimize that. Devon's office has secured locum coverage through September, and Dr. Ramaswamy is actively recruiting a replacement hospitalist team for Lowell rather than Haverhill, given the consolidation timeline.

**The Bidding Process.** Consistent with Article 14 of the nursing contract, open positions created by this consolidation will be filled through a seniority-based bidding process. Affected staff should submit their ranked preferences among available positions — Lowell OB, Haverhill med-surg, Haverhill ED, or voluntary separation — by March 10. Unit walkthroughs at Lowell for interested staff will be held March 6 through March 10 so people can see the physical space and meet the team before committing a preference. Bidding itself will run on March 15, administered jointly by my office and Labor Relations, with seniority as the primary award criterion within each track, subject to minimum qualification requirements for specialty placements. Award notifications will go out by March 19, with target start dates in the new position beginning no later than May 1 for those transferring internally, to allow adequate cross-training runway before the October 1 close.

**Support Resources.** Every affected employee, regardless of track, will have access to the Employee Assistance Program, and I am asking managers to actively encourage staff to use it — this is a stressful transition even for people who end up satisfied with their placement. HR benefits counselors will be on-site at Haverhill every Tuesday and Thursday through March 14 for one-on-one sessions.

I know some of you are also navigating your own uncertainty about how this affects unit culture and workload at Lowell. I will be holding an open forum for Lowell OB staff on March 12 specifically to talk through how we are planning staffing ratios and onboarding support as the unit grows, and I want every incoming transfer to feel like a asset to the team, not a strain on it.

**TO:** Medical Staff Executive Committee
**CC:** Anjali Ramaswamy, MD
**FROM:** Latasha Greer, Director of Community Benefits
**DATE:** March 20, 2027
**SUBJECT:** Response to the Committee's Objection on Transfer Protocols and Timeline

Thank you for the Committee's written objection following the February 4 vote, and for the specificity with which you laid out your concerns regarding precipitous delivery transfer protocols. I want to respond point by point, both because your concerns deserve a substantive answer and because the Essential Services Plan we file with the Department of Public Health must address exactly these questions before the April 9 hearing.

The Committee's central objection — that the current timeline does not yet demonstrate an adequate protocol for patients who present at Haverhill in active or precipitous labor after inpatient obstetrics closes — is fair, and I want to acknowledge that the draft protocol circulated in January was incomplete. Here is where it stands now, following four weeks of work with EMS partners, the Haverhill emergency department, and outside consultants in perinatal transport.

**Emergency Department Stabilization Capability.** Haverhill's emergency department will retain a dedicated perinatal stabilization bay, staffed by ED nurses who will complete a competency-based perinatal emergency training program before October 1, backed by a standing telephone consult line to the Lowell OB attending on call twenty-four hours a day. This is not a substitute for inpatient obstetrics, but it addresses the specific scenario the Committee raised: a patient who cannot safely be transported and delivers, or begins to deliver, before transfer.

**Transport.** Haverhill patients requiring transfer will travel an average of 14 additional miles to reach Lowell. We have finalized a priority-dispatch agreement with Trinity Ambulance and with the Haverhill Fire Department's paramedic unit specifying that any call flagged as an obstetric emergency receives Priority One dispatch with an advanced life support crew, and average transport time under this agreement is projected at approximately 22 minutes door-to-door under normal traffic conditions, with a mutual aid backup arrangement for periods of high call volume. All responding paramedics will receive supplemental training in emergency field delivery and neonatal resuscitation, delivered jointly by our OB and EMS education staff, with the first training cohort completing in April.

**Community Education and Prenatal Support.** We are launching a community education campaign in partnership with the Haverhill community health center network to make sure patients understand, well before their due dates, what to do if labor begins rapidly, including a dedicated OB nurse triage line staffed around the clock. Latasha's team is also arranging transportation vouchers for patients who need to travel to Lowell for routine prenatal visits, recognizing that the added distance is a burden independent of the emergency scenario, and is not something I want this plan to treat as an afterthought.

**On the Timeline Itself.** I recognize the Committee's vote registered opposition to the October 1 date generally, not only to the transfer protocol gap. I don't think it is my place to relitigate the underlying financial and clinical rationale, which Dr. Ramaswamy addressed directly in January — but I do want to say that the protocol work above could not have been meaningfully completed on a faster timeline than the one we are now on, and I would rather ask you to hold your assessment until you have seen the full protocol in writing, which we will circulate to the Committee no later than April 2, one week ahead of the public hearing.

I have asked Dr. Ramaswamy to convene a joint session of the Committee with our EMS and ED leadership before April 9, so that specific clinical questions can be raised directly with the people implementing the protocol rather than filtered through this memo. I believe that session will do more to address the Committee's underlying concern than any further written exchange, and I would like to schedule it for the week of March 30.

I want to close by saying plainly that the Committee's objection made this plan better. The stabilization bay staffing model and the priority-dispatch agreement were both added or substantially strengthened directly in response to concerns raised in your letter, and I am grateful for the scrutiny even where it has made the last six weeks harder.

**TO:** Anjali Ramaswamy, Aaron Lindqvist, Marisol Vega-Ruiz, Latasha Greer
**FROM:** Devon Okonkwo, Vice President of Operations
**DATE:** April 16, 2027
**SUBJECT:** Status Update Following the April 9 DPH Public Hearing

This memo summarizes where the consolidation stands one week after the Department of Public Health public hearing and flags the items I think need executive attention over the next six weeks.

**The Hearing.** Attendance was approximately 140 people, larger than DPH staff told us to expect for a filing of this kind. Public comment ran roughly two hours and split into three broad categories: Haverhill residents and several city councilors raising concerns about travel distance and the loss of a community-based birthing option; a smaller group of current and former patients and two physicians speaking in support of consolidation on safety grounds, citing volume-outcome evidence for higher-acuity deliveries; and a cluster of comments from affected or formerly affected staff, several emotional, about job security and the pace of change. Latasha's team fielded most of the access and transportation questions directly and I thought handled a hostile line of questioning about the 14-mile travel differential well, walking through the transport agreement and stabilization bay staffing in some detail. DPH's presiding officer did not signal a view on the merits but noted the Essential Services Plan appeared "substantially more developed" than the department's initial review draft — I take that as a reasonably positive signal given how much of Latasha's and the EMS work landed in the final filing. DPH indicated a written determination within 30 days, so we should expect a response by approximately May 9.

**Staffing and Redeployment.** Of the 62 affected full-time positions, the March 15 bidding process resulted in 34 employees selecting transfer to the Lowell OB expansion, 19 selecting transfer to other Haverhill units (primarily med-surg and the emergency department), and 9 electing voluntary severance or early retirement. Cross-training for the med-surg and ED transfers is underway on the four-week orientation model Marisol outlined in March. On the Lowell side, the intake of 34 transferring OB staff, combined with ongoing external recruitment, has brought the Lowell OB vacancy rate down from 23 percent to approximately 9 percent — a meaningful improvement, though Marisol's team is still short of full staffing for the 20-bed configuration and continuing to recruit.

On hospitalist coverage, locum tenens arrangements remain in place through September as planned. Dr. Ramaswamy's recruitment for a permanent Lowell-based hospitalist group has produced two signed contracts, with two positions still open; she is optimistic about closing at least one of those before the October transition but has asked me to keep the locum contract flexible through year-end as a hedge.

**Budget.** The Board Finance Committee approved supplemental capital of $1.7 million at its March 24 meeting, and Wentworth subsequently renegotiated the construction contract to $7.6 million after the value-engineering changes Aaron described in February, narrowing but not fully closing the original gap; the Foundation's $400,000 maternal-child health commitment is also confirmed, which effectively closes the remaining difference against the approved line. Construction proceeded to notice-to-proceed on April 1, which keeps us on schedule for substantial completion in early September, ahead of the October 1 target, assuming no further scope changes.

Transition costs, however, are running ahead of the model. We are now at $2.1 million committed against the $2.6 million contingency — 81 percent utilized with just over five months remaining before close. The main new pressure is temporary agency staffing at Haverhill to cover the gap left by the two hospitalist departures and ordinary attrition during the transition period; Aaron and I are reviewing whether to request a modest supplemental contingency now, while there is still runway to do so deliberately, rather than face a shortfall in August.

**Recommendation.** I'd like to propose a formal go/no-go checkpoint at the July executive team meeting, reviewing DPH's determination, construction completion status, hospitalist recruitment, and remaining contingency, so that if anything material has shifted we have time to adjust before the October 1 date rather than discovering a problem in September. I'll circulate a draft checkpoint agenda in early June.

**Communications.** Local media covered the hearing reasonably fairly — the Eagle-Tribune ran a straightforward account of the public comment split, and the Lowell Sun's piece focused mostly on the volume-outcome argument for consolidation, which I'd rather have leading the narrative than the job-loss angle. That said, I expect renewed press interest around the DPH determination in early May, and I'd like Latasha's office to have a short set of prepared statements ready before that date rather than drafting them reactively. I'd also ask that any staff-facing communication about the determination go out before it reaches the press — we've generally been ahead of that internally, and I don't want a leak to change that.

One additional item from the hearing worth flagging: two Haverhill city councilors asked directly whether the system would commit to any minimum retained services at Haverhill beyond the emergency stabilization bay — specifically, whether outpatient prenatal clinic visits would continue to be offered on-site. Latasha responded that outpatient prenatal care is not part of this filing and is not affected, which is accurate, but I think it would be worth a short joint statement from Dr. Ramaswamy's office and Latasha's team reaffirming that commitment explicitly, since the distinction between inpatient and outpatient services was clearly not obvious to everyone in the room. I'd rather close that gap in understanding now than have it resurface as a new objection after the DPH ruling.

I'll plan to send a brief supplemental note to this group once we have the written determination in hand, and will hold the fuller review for the July checkpoint as proposed above.

**TO:** Anjali Ramaswamy, Aaron Lindqvist, Marisol Vega-Ruiz, Latasha Greer
**FROM:** Devon Okonkwo, Vice President of Operations
**DATE:** May 11, 2027
**SUBJECT:** DPH Determination — Essential Services Plan Approved

The Department of Public Health issued its written determination on May 8, one day ahead of the projected window. The department approved the discontinuation of inpatient obstetric services at Haverhill and accepted the Essential Services Plan as filed, with three conditions attached. I want to walk through each, because two of them carry near-term deadlines of their own.

**Condition One — Quarterly Reporting.** DPH is requiring quarterly reports for the first eighteen months following closure documenting transport times for obstetric emergency transfers from Haverhill to Lowell, any adverse outcomes associated with transfer, and utilization of the emergency department stabilization bay. The first report will be due January 15, 2028, covering the fourth quarter of this year. I am asking Latasha's office to take the lead on compiling this, with data support from the ED and EMS partners, since much of the underlying tracking mirrors what her team already built for the hearing filing.

**Condition Two — Independent Protocol Review.** DPH is requiring an independent clinical review of the precipitous delivery transfer protocol within the first 90 days of operation, to be conducted by a reviewer not employed by the system and submitted to the department directly. Dr. Ramaswamy, I'd ask your office to identify candidates for that review by early August so we have someone under contract before October 1 rather than scrambling to find a qualified independent reviewer after the fact.

**Condition Three — Community Notice.** The department is requiring that the system post public notice of the closure at the Haverhill campus and through regional media at least 60 days before the effective date, which given our October 1 target means notice must run no later than August 2. Latasha's team already has draft notice language from the hearing preparation; it should need only minor updating.

None of the three conditions changes the timeline or the underlying plan in any material way, and I consider this a favorable outcome relative to what I understood some regional advocacy groups were pushing for, which was a delay or a requirement to maintain some minimal inpatient presence at Haverhill. DPH's determination explicitly credits the transport agreement and stabilization bay staffing model as adequate to the standard required under the Essential Services statute, which I think validates the additional work that went in after the Medical Staff Executive Committee's February objection.

I'll fold formal responsibility for tracking the three conditions into the July go/no-go checkpoint agenda, but wanted this group to have the determination itself without delay given the deadlines embedded in it, particularly the August 2 notice requirement, which is closer than it may feel from here.
