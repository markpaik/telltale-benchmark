# Saugatuck Hills Regional School District
## Internal Memorandum Packet — Ashford Lane Consolidation & FY28 Budget

---

**TO:** Building Principals; Central Office Administrators; Director of Special Services H. Weissman; Business Manager M. Sanfilippo
**FROM:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools
**DATE:** March 9, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Opening the Enrollment and Facilities Consolidation Study — Charge and Timeline

Following the failure of the February bond referendum and the Board's discussion at its March 3 workshop, I am initiating a formal consolidation study to address the district's structural budget gap for FY28. This memo establishes the study's scope, membership, and timeline, and it asks each of you to identify staff time and building-level data in support of the effort over the next several weeks.

The facts driving this study are not new to most of you, but I want them stated plainly at the outset so that everyone is working from the same baseline. District enrollment has fallen from 4,610 students in the 2020-21 school year to 4,190 this year, a decline of 420 students, or roughly 9 percent, concentrated most heavily at the elementary level. Despite that decline, our fixed costs — buildings, transportation routes, and staffing formulas built for a larger population — have not fallen proportionately. Business Manager Sanfilippo's office projects a $3.2 million structural shortfall for FY28 under a continuation budget. The bond referendum, which would have funded renovations at two of our aging elementary buildings and indirectly relieved some of this pressure through debt restructuring, failed by an 8-point margin with turnout of only 31 percent. We do not have the option of assuming a different outcome on a future ballot question by the time FY28 budget decisions must be finalized.

Given that context, the Board has directed the administration to study building consolidation as one tool — not the only tool, but a necessary one — for closing the gap. I want to be direct with this group: Ashford Lane Elementary, at 218 students and 54 percent of rated capacity, is the facility under closest examination, though the study will formally evaluate capacity, program fit, and cost across all three elementary buildings before any recommendation is finalized. I am not asking any of you to prejudge the outcome, and I am not announcing a decision today. I am announcing a process, and I need it to be credible, evidence-based, and completed on a timeline that allows the Board to act before the FY28 budget must be adopted next spring.

**Study Committee Membership.** The consolidation study will be led by Business Manager Marco Sanfilippo and Director of Special Services Hannah Weissman, working with me directly. I am asking each elementary and middle school principal to designate one staff liaison (a curriculum specialist, lead teacher, or assistant principal) who can speak to programmatic and space-utilization questions at your building. Ashford Lane Principal Odalys Marte will serve as a standing member given the direct relevance of her building to the study; I am also asking her to begin tracking community questions and concerns as they arise so we are not caught flat-footed later.

**Scope of Work.** The study will examine, at minimum: (1) building capacity and utilization across all elementary schools; (2) transportation time and cost implications of redistricting scenarios; (3) staffing and collective bargaining implications of any consolidation, including seniority and placement obligations under our agreements with the SHEA; (4) the fiscal impact of closure versus non-closure scenarios, to be developed by Mr. Sanfilippo; and (5) the impact on our special education continuum, to be developed by Ms. Weissman, particularly as it intersects with the corrective action plan now due to the State Department of Education by June 30. I want to be explicit that these two workstreams — facilities consolidation and special education compliance — are related but separate obligations, and neither should be allowed to distort the other. We are not consolidating special education programs because of the state review, and we are not delaying compliance work because of the budget study. Both are proceeding on their own merits and their own clocks.

**Timeline.** Data collection and building capacity analysis will run from mid-March through mid-April. I will hold two informational sessions for staff — one at Ashford Lane and one districtwide — in late March so that any building-level closure discussion does not arrive as a surprise to those most affected. Community forums, coordinated by Ms. Marte in partnership with central office, will be scheduled for May and June; details will follow under separate cover. Mr. Sanfilippo will deliver a revised FY28 budget memo, with and without a closure scenario, in early May. Ms. Weissman's memo on the corrective action plan and any related program consolidation will follow in June, ahead of the state's June 30 deadline. I intend to bring a final administrative recommendation to the Board by late June, with a Board vote targeted for August, so that any approved changes can be implemented for the start of the 2027-28 school year with adequate lead time for staffing, transportation routing, and family notification.

I recognize this timeline is compressed given the stakes involved, and I recognize that for staff at Ashford Lane in particular, this process will feel personal long before it feels administrative. I am asking every principal and central office administrator to treat this study with the seriousness and transparency it requires. Please have your liaison names to my office by March 16.

**TO:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools; Board of Education
**FROM:** Marco Sanfilippo, Business Manager
**DATE:** May 4, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Revised FY28 Budget Projections — With and Without Ashford Lane Closure

This memo updates the FY28 budget projection I presented in outline form at the March workshop and responds to the Superintendent's March 9 charge to model the fiscal impact of the Ashford Lane consolidation scenario against a continuation budget. The bottom line has not changed in direction, only in precision: closure materially narrows the gap but does not, on its own, eliminate it.

**Baseline Position.** The current FY27 operating budget is $103.0 million. Enrollment has declined from 4,610 to 4,190 students over five years, a trend our demographer projects will continue at a slower rate, with a further decline to roughly 4,050 students by FY30. Fixed and semi-fixed costs — debt service on prior building projects, transportation contracts, utilities, and administrative overhead — have not declined proportionately with enrollment, which is the core structural problem underlying the shortfall. Under a continuation budget that maintains all current facilities and staffing formulas, and incorporating contractual salary and benefit increases, projected state Education Cost Sharing aid, and the town's anticipated appropriation under its spending cap, I project a $3.2 million gap for FY28.

**Scenario One: No Closure.** Absent any facility consolidation, closing the $3.2 million gap would require some combination of the following, none of which I recommend in isolation: an override request to the town above the spending cap (politically difficult in the wake of the February referendum result); across-the-board program and staffing reductions in the range of 28-30 FTE positions districtwide, which would affect class sizes and program offerings at every level, not only elementary; or a further draw on the district's non-lapsing reserve fund, which currently stands at $4.1 million and was not built to absorb recurring structural deficits. Relying on reserves to close a structural gap simply postpones the reckoning to FY29 with a smaller cushion.

**Scenario Two: Ashford Lane Closure.** Closing Ashford Lane Elementary and redistributing its 218 students — 118 through formal redistricting to Bridgemill and Hollow Road Elementary, with the remainder accounted for through normal attrition, magnet and choice placements, and the special education program moves described in Ms. Weissman's memo — generates estimated annual savings of $1.9 million. This figure reflects reduced staffing (net of the redistricting-driven need for some additional sections at receiving schools), elimination of a building-level administrative team, reduced utilities and custodial costs, and deferral of an estimated $640,000 in near-term facility maintenance that would otherwise be required to keep Ashford Lane's mechanical systems functional through FY30. I want to flag for the Board that this savings estimate assumes an orderly closure with adequate lead time for staff placement and transportation rebidding; a compressed timeline would erode some of this savings in year one through severance, unemployment compensation exposure for non-renewed staff, and one-time moving and reconfiguration costs at the receiving buildings, which I estimate at approximately $210,000 non-recurring.

**Remaining Gap.** Even with the full $1.9 million in closure savings applied, the district would still face an estimated $1.3 million shortfall for FY28. This is not a failure of the closure scenario; it reflects the reality that enrollment decline and cost growth are running faster than any single facility action can offset in one budget cycle. I am recommending the Board plan to close this remaining gap through a combination of: a modest reduction in discretionary supply and equipment lines (approximately $400,000 districtwide); renegotiation of our transportation contract, which is up for rebid in FY28 regardless of the closure decision and which our consultant believes can be reduced by $300,000 to $450,000 through route consolidation; and a targeted staffing reduction through attrition of approximately 6 to 8 FTE positions districtwide, achievable without involuntary layoffs beyond those already contemplated in the closure scenario, given normal retirement and resignation patterns.

**Debt and Facilities Note.** I want to flag one complication for the Board's awareness. The failed bond referendum would have funded renovations at two elementary buildings, not including Ashford Lane. Closure of Ashford Lane does not eliminate the deferred maintenance need at those other buildings; it simply means the district is carrying that need forward without the additional capacity pressure that keeping Ashford Lane open would otherwise create. I recommend the Board begin discussing, separately from this consolidation decision, whether and when to return to voters with a scaled-down bond proposal, informed by the lower turnout and the margin of defeat in February.

I am available to walk through the underlying spreadsheets with any Board member individually before the June meeting, and I will provide an updated one-page summary table for the packet closer to the vote.

|---|---|---|

| Closure Savings Applied | — | $1.9M |

**TO:** Special Education Staff — Certified and Paraprofessional
**FROM:** Hannah Weissman, Director of Special Services
**DATE:** June 12, 2026
**SUBJECT:** State Corrective Action Plan and Consolidation of Self-Contained Programs

I am writing to update you on two related but distinct pieces of work: our response to the January state monitoring findings, and the planned consolidation of our two self-contained programs into hub sites at Bridgemill and Hollow Road Elementary. I know both pieces of news are landing at once, and I know that is not ideal for a staff group already carrying heavy caseloads. I want to lay out what we know, what is required of us, and what support you can expect from my office over the summer.

**The State Monitoring Findings.** In January, the Department of Education's Bureau of Special Education completed its periodic monitoring review of our district. Reviewers sampled a cross-section of IEPs across all levels and found 19 percent out of compliance, concentrated in two areas: transition planning documentation for students age 16 and older, and evidence that service minutes specified in the IEP were actually delivered and documented as delivered. Neither finding suggests students were denied services outright in most cases; rather, the review found gaps in our documentation practices — missing transition assessments, transition goals not clearly tied to postsecondary outcomes, and service logs that did not reliably capture make-up sessions when a related service provider was absent or a student was pulled for testing. That distinction matters, but it does not reduce our obligation to fix it. A corrective action plan is due to the state by June 30, and the state has scheduled a follow-up monitoring visit for this fall to verify implementation.

The corrective action plan I am submitting includes four elements, each with an owner and a deadline: first, a full record review of every IEP for students age 14 and older, completed by our transition coordinator and case managers by August 15, with any gaps remediated before the first day of school; second, mandatory training for all case managers and related service providers on transition planning requirements and service-minute documentation, to be delivered during August in-service days; third, a revised service log format, moving from paper tracking to a shared electronic log that flags missed sessions automatically for make-up scheduling, effective with the start of the school year; and fourth, a designated compliance coordinator position, which I am recommending be created within existing staffing rather than added headcount, to conduct a rolling quarterly audit of a sample of IEPs going forward so we catch drift before it becomes a pattern. I will be scheduling individual meetings with case managers over the summer to review your specific caseloads against these requirements. Please expect that outreach in the coming weeks.

**Program Consolidation.** Separately from the corrective action plan, and as part of the district's broader consolidation study tied to the FY28 budget, the administration has determined that our two self-contained special education programs — currently housed at Ashford Lane and at a second site — will be consolidated into two hub locations at Bridgemill and Hollow Road Elementary beginning in the 2027-28 school year. I want to be candid about why this is happening in the same window as the corrective action plan, because I know it will raise questions about whether one is driving the other. It is not. The hub consolidation is being planned because our self-contained programs currently operate at partial enrollment across two sites, which limits our ability to appropriately group students by need and makes efficient staffing of related services more difficult. Concentrating the programs at two well-resourced hub sites will allow us to build stronger peer groupings, deploy speech, OT, and behavioral staff more efficiently across a larger student base at each site, and — not incidentally — support more consistent service-minute delivery, which directly supports our compliance goals even though that is not the primary driver of the decision.

I recognize this consolidation affects staff assignments directly. For paraprofessionals and case managers currently assigned to the program housed at Ashford Lane, your position will move with the program to whichever hub site is determined to be the best programmatic fit for your current caseload; this will be communicated individually, not by blanket assignment, and I am committed to weighing continuity of relationships with students wherever possible. I know this is one more layer of uncertainty layered onto the broader Ashford Lane closure conversation, and I do not want to minimize that. My office will hold a dedicated informational meeting for special education staff, separate from the districtwide sessions, in early July to walk through both the compliance work and the hub transition in detail and to take your questions directly.

Thank you for your continued work with students during what I recognize is an unsettled stretch. I will follow up individually with each case manager before the end of the month.

**TO:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools
**FROM:** Odalys Marte, Principal, Ashford Lane Elementary
**DATE:** July 14, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Status Report — Community Forums and Staff Placement

This memo summarizes where things stand with the three community forums held this spring and early summer, and provides an update on staff placement for the 31 positions affected by the proposed closure. I am also flagging several open items where I need direction from your office before we move further into the summer.

**Community Forums.** We held three forums as planned: April 22 at Ashford Lane, May 13 at Bridgemill (to reach families who would be receiving redistricted students), and June 10 as a combined evening session at the high school auditorium to accommodate working parents who could not attend the earlier daytime and early-evening sessions. Attendance was 94, 61, and 142 respectively, the last figure reflecting, I think, both the growing awareness of the proposal and the fact that the June session followed shortly after the Board's public discussion of the redistricting boundary lines. Across all three sessions, the concerns raised were consistent and fall into four categories.

First, walkability and transportation: a significant number of Ashford Lane families currently walk their children to school, and the proposed redistricting would place roughly 40 of our students into bus zones for the first time, some with ride times parents estimate at 25 to 35 minutes depending on the route. Second, sibling and continuity concerns: several families with children at both Ashford Lane and the middle school raised concerns about split pickup logistics and about younger siblings losing the same peer group their older children had. Third, class size: this was the most frequently raised concern at the June session specifically, with parents citing the Board's own class-size guidelines and asking pointed questions about projected section sizes at Bridgemill and Hollow Road after absorbing 118 additional students. I was not in a position at these forums to give firm numbers, since I understand that modeling is still being finalized, and I told families as much. Fourth, and least tractable, a number of longtime residents raised concerns about the fate of the Ashford Lane building itself, which opened in 1962 and has some sentimental and historic standing in the neighborhood; several asked whether the district has a disposition plan (sale, lease, alternative district use) and I deferred those questions to central office, as I do not have information to share.

I want to note for the record that the tone at all three forums was largely respectful, if clearly anxious, and I did not observe the kind of hostility that sometimes accompanies these processes. That said, the two Board members who have stated publicly they intend to vote against the closure were both present at the June session and were vocal in raising the class-size and redistricting-line questions from the floor, which I expect will surface again in whatever written objection reaches your office.

**Staff Placement.** Of the 31 staff positions affected by the proposed closure, I can report the following status as of this week. Of the 17 tenured staff, 11 have been placed or have accepted placement offers at receiving buildings through the seniority-based transfer process outlined in the SHEA agreement, 4 are in active interviews for open positions at Bridgemill, Hollow Road, or the middle school, and 2 have indicated they are considering retirement rather than transfer, which would need to be finalized by their notification deadline. Of the 14 non-tenured staff, whose positions are not protected under the same transfer rights, 5 have been offered interviews for open positions elsewhere in the district for which they are qualified, 3 have accepted offers, and I have not yet been able to identify openings appropriate for the remaining 6, several of whom hold specialized certifications (art, instrumental music) where we simply have fewer open sections districtwide. I would appreciate guidance from your office on whether central office intends to prioritize placement for non-tenured staff with strong evaluation histories over posting those positions externally, since several of these teachers have asked me directly and I do not want to promise something that is not district policy.

**Open Items.** I need three things from your office before the end of the summer: final confirmation of the redistricting boundary lines so I can stop fielding conflicting information from families comparing notes with Bridgemill and Hollow Road families; a decision on whether Ashford Lane will hold a formal closing observance in the spring, which several longtime staff and families have asked about and which I think is worth planning for regardless of the emotional weight involved; and clarity on the timeline for notifying the 6 unplaced non-tenured staff, since state and contractual notice requirements will start to constrain our options if this drags into August.

I remain available to meet whenever useful and appreciate the chance to raise these directly rather than let them sit.

**TO:** Board of Education
**FROM:** Dr. Chidinma Eze, Superintendent of Schools
**DATE:** July 27, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Response to Written Objection — Redistricting and Class Size

I am writing in response to the written objection submitted by two Board members following the June 10 community forum, raising concerns about projected class sizes at Bridgemill and Hollow Road Elementary under the proposed redistricting plan, and asking the administration to address those concerns in writing before the Board's scheduled vote. I appreciate the specificity of the questions raised, and I want to respond to each directly rather than in general terms, since I know both members intend to bring this response back to the full Board for discussion.

**The Core Objection.** As I understand it, the objection rests on two related claims: first, that absorbing 118 redistricted students into two receiving schools risks pushing class sizes above the Board's own adopted guideline of 22 students per section in grades K-2 and 24 in grades 3-5; and second, that the administration has not provided sufficient section-by-section detail to allow the Board to evaluate that risk before voting on the closure itself. I want to take the second point first, because it is fair. Earlier projections shared with the Board were presented at a grade-level and building-level aggregate, which was appropriate for early planning but is not sufficient for a vote of this consequence. Attached to the Board's packet for the August meeting will be a section-by-section projection for every grade at both Bridgemill and Hollow Road, current as of enrollment registrations through July 15, along with the assumptions used to build it.

**What the Data Shows.** Based on that more granular modeling, redistricting would bring Bridgemill from its current 402 students to approximately 468, and Hollow Road from 389 to approximately 441. At the section level, the sharpest pressure point is second grade at Bridgemill, where redistricting would create three sections averaging 23.3 students absent any adjustment — within but close to the ceiling of the Board's guideline. Kindergarten at Hollow Road shows similar pressure, with two sections projected at 22 students each before accounting for any incoming students who register late in August, which our registration data shows happens in a meaningful number of cases every year. No grade level at either building is currently projected to exceed the adopted guideline, but I want to be honest with the Board that "does not exceed" is not the same as "comfortable margin," and a handful of late registrations in the wrong grade and building could push us over the line in at least one section.

**Administrative Response.** To address this directly, I am recommending the Board approve, as part of the closure resolution rather than as a separate later action, authorization for the administration to add one additional section at either receiving building if enrollment as of the September 1 count exceeds the projections above by more than 4 students at any single grade level. Mr. Sanfilippo has confirmed that the cost of one additional section — should it prove necessary — is approximately $92,000 and can be absorbed within the closure savings without reopening the FY28 budget, since it would still leave the district well ahead of the continuation-budget scenario. I believe building this contingency into the resolution itself, rather than leaving it to be negotiated after the fact if problems emerge in September, is the more responsible course and directly answers the concern that the Board is being asked to approve a plan without a safety valve.

**On the Redistricting Lines Themselves.** The objection also raised whether the specific boundary lines were drawn to minimize transportation cost at the expense of walkability, referencing the roughly 40 students who would move from walk-zone to bus-zone status. I want to address this directly: the lines were drawn using our transportation consultant's standard walk-zone radius (1.0 mile for grades K-5, consistent with current district policy at every other elementary school), applied consistently to the redistricted area. They were not drawn to minimize cost, though cost was one input among several, alongside sibling continuity and minimizing the number of students who would cross a state highway on their walk route, which was a specific concern raised by the transportation consultant for two of the affected streets. I recognize that does not fully resolve the underlying concern for the 40 families affected, and I am not able to change geography, but I want the Board to understand that the lines reflect an application of existing policy rather than a departure from it.

**Recommendation.** I am asking the Board to consider the section-by-section data and the contingency-section authorization described above as sufficient to address the specific concerns raised, and I would ask both members who submitted the written objection to let me know before the August meeting if there are questions this response does not adequately answer, so that I can prepare further material rather than have new questions surface for the first time during the vote itself. I recognize this remains a difficult decision for the full Board regardless of how thoroughly the administration answers these particular questions, and I do not expect this memo to change anyone's underlying judgment about the merits of the closure. I do hope it demonstrates that the specific technical concerns raised have been taken seriously and addressed with real numbers rather than reassurance alone.

I am available to meet with either member individually before the meeting, and I will have Mr. Sanfilippo and Ms. Marte available as well to answer budget and building-level questions respectively.

I recognize, too, that timing matters here. The August meeting is the last regularly scheduled Board session before staff notification deadlines under the collective bargaining agreement begin to constrain our options for the non-tenured staff Ms. Marte referenced in her July 14 memo. A delay of the vote past August would not preserve flexibility; it would simply compress the placement window for those six staff members into a period when fewer open positions remain available districtwide. I say this not to pressure the Board's judgment on the merits, but because I think it is relevant context for how the timing question and the substantive objection interact, and I did not want that connection to go unstated.

**Attachments for the August Packet.** In addition to the section-by-section enrollment projections described above, the Board will receive: the updated transportation route maps showing walk-zone and bus-zone boundaries for all affected addresses; a written summary from our transportation consultant explaining the state-highway crossing consideration referenced above; and a one-page comparison of the contingency-section proposal against the FY28 budget figures in Mr. Sanfilippo's May memo, confirming that the $92,000 contingency cost does not require the Board to reopen any other line item.

Finally, I want to note for the record that Ms. Weissman's corrective action plan work and the special education hub consolidation described in her June 12 memo proceed on their own track and are not affected by this particular objection, since the two dissenting members' letter was directed specifically at the general-education redistricting lines rather than the special education program moves. I did not want that distinction to become blurred in Board discussion simply because both items appear in the same resolution.
