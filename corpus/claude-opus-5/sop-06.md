## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines the required method for reprocessing flexible gastrointestinal endoscopes at Klamath Valley Regional Medical Center so that every endoscope used on a patient has been precleaned, leak tested, manually cleaned, verified clean, high-level disinfected, dried, and stored in a manner that renders it safe for the next patient.

This procedure was revised in full following a cluster of three patients who cultured an indistinguishable strain of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* over eleven weeks in the spring of 2026, a borescope survey that identified channel defects, retained debris, or distal end damage in 6 of 22 endoscopes in inventory, Joint Commission Requirements for Improvement issued in May 2026 related to high-level disinfection, and a state health authority request for a plan of correction. Version 4.0 adds mandatory borescope inspection, expanded cleaning verification sampling, forced-air drying requirements, per-scope traceability to technician, and defined exception paths.

## 2. SCOPE

**2.1 Applies to** all flexible gastroscopes and colonoscopes owned by, leased by, or loaned to Klamath Valley Regional Medical Center, and to all reprocessing performed in the Gastroenterology Lab decontamination and clean rooms and in the Sterile Processing Department.

**2.2 Applies to all personnel** who preclean, transport, reprocess, inspect, store, or issue flexible GI endoscopes, including reprocessing technicians, procedure room registered nurses, gastroenterology technicians, transport staff, biomedical engineering staff, the infection preventionist, and the department educator.

**2.3 Does not apply to** rigid endoscopes, bronchoscopes, transesophageal echocardiography probes, urologic endoscopes, or duodenoscopes and linear echoendoscopes. Duodenoscopes and echoendoscopes are governed by SPD-END-002, which requires double high-level disinfection and per-patient culture surveillance; Klamath Valley Regional Medical Center does not currently hold either instrument in inventory.

**2.4 Precedence.** Where this procedure and a manufacturer's instructions for use (IFU) conflict, the IFU governs the specific step in question, and the discrepancy must be reported to the Owner within one business day so this document can be corrected. No employee may resolve a conflict independently at the point of use.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**3.1 AER** — Automated endoscope reprocessor. The washer-disinfector that performs the automated high-level disinfection cycle.

**3.2 ATP** — Adenosine triphosphate. An organic marker present in all cells; measured by luminometer in relative light units (RLU) as a surrogate for residual organic soil.

**3.3 Bedside precleaning** — Wiping the insertion tube and flushing all channels at the point of use immediately upon scope removal from the patient, before the scope leaves the procedure room.

**3.4 Borescope** — A slender illuminated camera used to inspect the internal lumen of an endoscope channel for retained debris, fluid, scratches, delamination, and other defects not visible externally.

**3.5 Cleaning verification** — A quantitative or qualitative test performed after manual cleaning and before high-level disinfection to confirm that organic soil has been reduced below a defined threshold. At this facility, ATP and protein residue tests.

**3.6 Delayed reprocessing** — Any endoscope for which more than 60 minutes elapses between the completion of bedside precleaning and the start of leak testing.

**3.7 HLD** — High-level disinfection. Destruction of all vegetative microorganisms, mycobacteria, viruses, and fungi, with partial destruction of bacterial spores.

**3.8 Hang time** — The maximum interval an endoscope may remain in a drying cabinet after HLD and be used on a patient without reprocessing. At this facility, 7 days.

**3.9 IFU** — Instructions for use. The manufacturer's written reprocessing instructions specific to a model, including required brush diameters, detergent concentration, soak times, and channel flush volumes.

**3.10 Loaner scope** — Any endoscope temporarily in the facility's possession that the facility does not own, whether from a manufacturer, a vendor, or another hospital.

**3.11 Patient-ready** — An endoscope that has completed this procedure in full, passed final inspection, and is documented as available for patient use.

**3.12 Point of use** — The procedure room, or any other location where a patient procedure is performed.

**3.13 Scope tracking system** — The barcode-based electronic record (Censitrac) in which every reprocessing event, technician, AER, cycle, and patient association is recorded.

## 4. RESPONSIBILITIES

**4.1 Manager, Sterile Processing and Endoscope Reprocessing (Owner)**
Maintains this procedure and the current IFU library; assigns and documents competency; reviews the cleaning verification log and AER cycle records weekly; approves release of any endoscope removed from service; schedules quarterly water testing and annual borescope surveys; reports metrics monthly to the Infection Control Committee; authorizes exception paths in Section 6.

**4.2 Vice President, Quality and Patient Safety (Approver)**
Approves this procedure and revisions; adjudicates conflicts between departments; authorizes patient notification and lookback in coordination with Infection Prevention; reports to the Quality Committee of the Board.

**4.3 Reprocessing Technicians**
Perform leak testing, manual cleaning, cleaning verification, AER loading and unloading, drying, inspection, and storage exactly as written; scan the barcode at every required step; complete documentation contemporaneously, not retrospectively; stop and escalate any deviation rather than proceeding.

**4.4 Procedure Room Registered Nurses and Gastroenterology Technicians**
Perform bedside precleaning within 60 minutes of scope removal from the patient; scan the endoscope barcode against the patient's medical record number before insertion and after removal; place the scope in a transport container; notify Sterile Processing of any suspected damage or any procedure involving a known transmissible pathogen.

**4.5 Transport Staff**
Move closed, labeled transport containers from the procedure room to the decontamination room within 30 minutes of pickup; never open a container; never transport a soiled and a clean endoscope in the same container or on the same cart.

**4.6 Infection Preventionist**
Reviews quarterly AER and rinse water test results within 5 business days of receipt; investigates any positive high-concern organism; leads any infection investigation involving an endoscope; co-signs sequestration of an implicated scope; reviews this procedure at each revision.

**4.7 Biomedical Engineering**
Performs and documents scheduled preventive maintenance on AERs, drying cabinets, and borescopes; verifies AER cycle parameters and alarm function semiannually; responds to AER faults within 4 hours during business hours and 12 hours after hours; maintains calibration records for luminometers.

**4.8 Department Educator**
Delivers initial didactic and hands-on training; administers initial and annual competency assessments; maintains the training file; delivers reeducation within 5 business days of any documented deviation.

## 5. PROCEDURE

> **Standing requirements applicable to every step below.** Work proceeds in one direction only, from the decontamination room to the clean room; no item and no person carries soiled material into the clean room. Doors between rooms remain closed. Personal protective equipment (PPE) in the decontamination room consists of a fluid-resistant gown with sleeves, a full-face shield or goggles plus mask, a bouffant cap, nitrile gloves with cuffs over the gown sleeve, and fluid-resistant shoe covers. Personal electronic devices are prohibited in both rooms. Every endoscope carries a unique barcode on the control body; every scan is performed with the handheld reader, not typed in manually. Where this procedure states a number, the number is a minimum unless the IFU specifies otherwise.

### 5.1 Bedside Precleaning (Procedure Room, performed by RN or GI technician)

**5.1.1** Before insertion into the patient, scan the endoscope barcode and the patient's wristband to bind the scope to the patient encounter in the scope tracking system. If the tracking system is unavailable, record the scope serial number, patient medical record number, physician, procedure, date, and time on the downtime form (SPD-F-07) and enter it into the system within 4 hours of system restoration.

**5.1.2** Immediately upon removal of the endoscope from the patient, and before the scope is disconnected from the light source, wipe the entire insertion tube from the control body toward the distal end with a fresh lint-free cloth saturated in freshly prepared enzymatic detergent solution. Use a single pass; do not wipe back and forth. Discard the cloth.

**5.1.3** Place the distal end in the detergent solution and suction 200 mL of solution through the suction/biopsy channel until the returning fluid runs clear, then suction 50 mL of air.

**5.1.4** Depress the air/water valve to flush the air and water channels for 30 seconds per the model IFU. On models requiring a dedicated cleaning adapter, attach the adapter now.

**5.1.5** For colonoscopes with auxiliary water or forward-water jet channels, attach the flushing adapter and flush 90 mL of detergent solution through each auxiliary channel.

**5.1.6** Detach the endoscope from the light source and suction pump. Remove and discard all single-use valves, caps, and biopsy port covers. Reusable valves are placed in a perforated basket that stays with the scope through reprocessing so that valve and scope remain matched.

**5.1.7** Attach the leak test cap or soaking cap required by the IFU before transport if the model requires one for immersion protection.

**5.1.8** Coil the insertion tube in loops no smaller than 12 inches in diameter and place the endoscope in a rigid, lidded, puncture-resistant transport container lined with a moisture-absorbent pad dampened with detergent solution. Do not fully immerse the scope in liquid for transport. Close the lid and affix a biohazard label.

**5.1.9** Scan the scope barcode to record "precleaned" with the time and the performing employee's identity. Enter the time bedside precleaning was completed on the container tag.

**5.1.10** Notify Sterile Processing by phone (extension 4412) if the scope was used on a patient with a known or suspected transmissible pathogen requiring enhanced handling, if the scope is suspected of damage, or if the procedure ended after 1800 hours.

### 5.2 Transport (performed by transport staff or GI staff)

**5.2.1** Transport the closed container to the decontamination room within 30 minutes of the completion of bedside precleaning. Use the designated soiled utility route; do not pass through the clean room, waiting areas, or the main corridor of the procedure suite.

**5.2.2** Hand the container to a reprocessing technician or place it on the designated soiled receiving counter. Do not open the container. Do not leave a container unattended in a corridor.

**5.2.3** Return the transport cart to the decontamination room for cleaning with a hospital-approved disinfectant after each use, observing the wet contact time on the product label.

### 5.3 Receipt and Leak Testing (Decontamination Room)

**5.3.1** Don full PPE per the standing requirements. Scan the scope barcode to record "received in decontamination" with the time. The tracking system calculates the interval since precleaning. If the interval exceeds 60 minutes, follow the delayed reprocessing path in Section 6.3.

**5.3.2** Retrieve the model-specific IFU reprocessing card from the wall rack above the sink. Confirm the card version matches the version listed in the IFU register. Keep the card visible throughout manual cleaning.

**5.3.3** Perform a leak test before any immersion. Attach the leak tester specified in the IFU, pressurize the scope to the pressure stated on the reprocessing card, and confirm the gauge holds pressure for 30 seconds in air.

**5.3.4** With pressure maintained, immerse the entire scope in clean water. Angulate the distal bending section through its full range in all four directions and hold for 30 seconds in each position. Observe the bending rubber, insertion tube, control body, universal cord, and connector for a continuous stream of bubbles. Depress each valve cylinder and observe.

**5.3.5** Interpret the result. A continuous stream of bubbles from any location, or a gauge pressure drop, is a failed leak test. Isolated bubbles released on initial immersion that do not recur are not a failure. Any uncertainty is treated as a failure.

**5.3.6** If the leak test fails, stop and follow Section 6.1. Do not proceed to manual cleaning. Do not immerse further.

**5.3.7** If the leak test passes, release the pressure per IFU, disconnect the tester, and scan the barcode to record "leak test pass," the pressure held, and the technician's identity.

### 5.4 Manual Cleaning (Decontamination Room)

**5.4.1** Prepare a fresh detergent bath in the designated sink for every single endoscope. Use the enzymatic detergent named on the reprocessing card at the dilution and water temperature stated on the card, measured with the calibrated wall thermometer and the dosing pump or graduated pitcher. Record the temperature on the manual cleaning log (SPD-F-02). Never reuse a detergent bath for a second scope.

**5.4.2** Fully immerse the endoscope in the detergent bath. Confirm the scope is submerged with no coils exposed above the waterline.

**5.4.3** Wash the exterior. With a soft lint-free cloth or the soft-bristled brush specified on the card, wash the entire external surface, working from control body to distal end. Pay particular attention to the bending section grooves, the distal end face, the air/water and suction valve cylinders, and the connector electrical contacts, which are cleaned only as the IFU permits.

**5.4.4** Brush the distal end. Using the small conical brush specified on the card, brush the air/water nozzle opening, the lens surface, the elevator-free distal face, and the biopsy channel opening under the water surface. Inspect the nozzle under magnification with the illuminated magnifier at the sink.

**5.4.5** Brush the channels. Select brushes strictly by the diameters printed on the reprocessing card for that model — this facility stocks 2.8 mm, 3.7 mm, and 4.8 mm channel brushes and a 5 mm valve cylinder brush; using an undersized brush is a deviation requiring escalation. Pass the correct brush through each channel and out the opposite port. Repeat until no visible debris appears on the bristles, and in every case a minimum of three passes per channel. Channels to be brushed on gastroscopes and colonoscopes: suction/biopsy channel through the control body port, suction channel through the connector, the biopsy port and its cylinder, and the suction valve cylinder. Inspect and rinse the brush in the bath between every pass and discard any brush with bent, splayed, or missing bristles.

**5.4.6** Flush all channels. Using the model-specific cleaning adapters and a 60 mL syringe or the channel flushing pump set to the card pressure, flush each channel with a minimum of 100 mL of detergent solution, including the air channel, water channel, suction/biopsy channel, and any auxiliary or forward-water channel. Confirm effluent exits each channel.

**5.4.7** Soak. Leave the scope fully immersed with all channels filled with detergent for the exact soak time stated on the card — currently 5 minutes for the Olympus 190-series and 3 minutes for the Fujifilm 700-series. Set the sink timer. Do not shorten the soak because of case pressure; if a scope is needed sooner, use a different scope.

**5.4.8** Rinse. Transfer the scope to the dedicated rinse sink. Flush every channel with a minimum of 200 mL of clean utility water to remove all detergent, and rinse the exterior thoroughly. Detergent carryover into the AER is a documented cause of high-level disinfectant dilution.

**5.4.9** Purge. Flush each channel with air until no water exits, then wipe the exterior with a clean lint-free cloth to remove surface water so that residual fluid does not dilute the disinfectant.

**5.4.10** Clean reusable valves and adapters in the same detergent bath as their matched scope: brush every cylinder, lumen, and spring, flush all lumens, soak for the card time, rinse, and keep them in the matched basket.

**5.4.11** Scan to record "manual cleaning complete" with the technician's identity, the detergent lot number, the water temperature, and the soak time.

### 5.5 Cleaning Verification Testing

**5.5.1 Sampling frequency.** Cleaning verification is performed after manual cleaning and before HLD according to the following schedule, which supersedes the sampling frequency in Version 3.2:

- Every endoscope, every reprocessing cycle, for the first 90 days after the effective date of this version, then reassessed by the Owner and the Infection Preventionist with a written determination filed as an addendum.
- After the 90-day period and only upon written determination: one in every five reprocessing cycles per endoscope, with every endoscope in inventory tested at least once per week.
- Every reprocessing cycle regardless of schedule for: any endoscope returning to service after repair, any loaner scope on first use and each of its first three cycles, any endoscope reprocessed under the delayed reprocessing path, any endoscope reprocessed by a technician within 30 days of initial competency, and any endoscope implicated in an infection investigation.

**5.5.2 ATP testing.** Flush 20 mL of sterile water through the suction/biopsy channel and collect the effluent in the collection cup. Activate the swab, sample the effluent per the kit instructions, and read in the luminometer within the kit's stated hold time. Also swab the distal end face and the biopsy port. Record all RLU values in the cleaning verification log (SPD-F-03).

**5.5.3 ATP interpretation.** Pass is less than 100 RLU for a channel flush sample and less than 100 RLU for a surface swab. A value of 100 RLU or greater is a failure. Failures follow Section 6.2.

**5.5.4 Protein residue testing.** Perform protein residue testing on the channel flush effluent using the qualitative kit. Interpret the color change against the kit reference card at the stated development time. Any color change indicating protein at or above the kit's detection threshold is a failure. Record the result in SPD-F-03.

**5.5.5** Store test kits at the temperature stated on the package, check the expiration date before every use, and never use an expired kit. Record kit lot numbers weekly in SPD-F-03. Biomedical Engineering verifies the luminometer with the manufacturer's calibration check monthly and documents it.

**5.5.6** Scan to record "cleaning verification" with the result. A scope may not enter the AER with an unresolved failed verification result.

### 5.6 Automated High-Level Disinfection

**5.6.1** Before the first cycle of each day, confirm the AER has completed its self-disinfection cycle, the water filters are within their service interval per the log on the unit, and Biomedical Engineering's preventive maintenance sticker is current.

**5.6.2** Test the high-level disinfectant with the manufacturer's minimum recommended concentration (MRC) test strip before every cycle. Dip for the stated time, read against the reference, and record the result, strip lot, and technician in the AER log (SPD-F-04). A strip reading below MRC requires that the disinfectant be discarded and replaced; the solution may not be used regardless of its reuse-life date. Record the solution's in-use date and the number of days in use; never exceed the labeled reuse life even if the strip passes.

**5.6.3** Load the endoscope in the AER basin per the AER manufacturer's diagram, without kinking or crossing the insertion tube. Connect every channel connector required for that scope model, verifying each connection by gentle traction. An unconnected channel does not receive disinfectant and is the most common cause of an inadequately processed scope.

**5.6.4** Place the matched valves and adapters in the AER accessory tray in the same load as their scope. Never load two endoscopes in a single-scope basin. Never add a second scope to a cycle in progress.

**5.6.5** Scan the scope barcode, the AER barcode, and the technician badge to open the cycle record. Select the cycle program named on the reprocessing card for that model. Close and latch the lid and start the cycle.

**5.6.6** Do not open the AER during the cycle. If the cycle is interrupted for any reason — power loss, alarm, door opening, aborted program — the endoscope is not disinfected. Return it to the decontamination room and repeat Sections 5.3 through 5.6 in full from leak testing forward. Document the interruption in SPD-F-04 and notify the Owner.

**5.6.7** On cycle completion, review the printout or electronic record and confirm every parameter passed: disinfectant contact time, temperature, alcohol flush if programmed, and air purge. Sign or initial the record and attach it to the daily log. Any parameter outside range means the scope is not patient-ready; repeat from Section 5.3.

**5.6.8** Scan to record "HLD complete" with the AER identifier, cycle number, start and end times, disinfectant lot, MRC result, and technician identity.

### 5.7 Drying, Inspection, and Storage (Clean Room)

**5.7.1** Don clean gloves and remove the endoscope from the AER, handling it only with clean hands and transferring it directly to the clean room. Do not lay a disinfected scope on an unprotected surface.

**5.7.2** Flush each channel with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol if the AER did not perform an alcohol flush, then purge every channel with filtered instrument air at the pressure stated on the reprocessing card until no moisture exits. Wipe the exterior dry with a clean lint-free cloth.

**5.7.3** Hang the endoscope vertically, fully extended, in the drying cabinet with the distal end hanging free and not touching the cabinet floor or another scope. Connect each channel to the cabinet's forced-air ports. Confirm the cabinet display shows airflow and filtered-air status within specification. Minimum drying time in the cabinet before patient use is 10 minutes with all channel connections made; scopes stored longer than 10 minutes satisfy this requirement inherently.

**5.7.4** Store valves and adapters in the labeled matched bag or tray beside the scope, not attached to the scope, so that channels remain open to airflow.

**5.7.5** Perform documented visual inspection with the illuminated magnifier at each reprocessing: examine the bending rubber for creases, bubbling, or wrinkles; the distal end face for scratches, adhesive loss, or debris; the insertion tube for dents, buckling, or delamination; and the connector for bent pins or fluid ingress.

**5.7.6 Borescope inspection schedule.** Inspect the suction/biopsy channel and the distal end of every endoscope with the borescope on the following schedule, recording images to the scope's file in the tracking system:

- Every endoscope, monthly, on a rotating calendar maintained by the Owner such that all 22 scopes are inspected within each calendar month.
- Every endoscope after any repair, after any failed leak test, and on receipt and return of any loaner scope.
- Every endoscope in inventory during the annual full-inventory survey each March.
- Any endoscope implicated in an infection investigation, immediately upon sequestration.

Findings of retained debris, retained fluid, scratches deeper than the surface sheen, channel delamination, or distal end damage remove the scope from service under Section 6.1.

**5.7.7 Hang time.** Maximum hang time is 7 calendar days from the recorded HLD completion time, provided the scope is stored in a closed drying cabinet with documented forced filtered airflow and has not been handled or unpacked. The tracking system flags an expiring scope at day 6. At day 7, the scope is reprocessed from Section 5.3 forward; a leak test is required, and manual cleaning is performed in full.

**5.7.8** Scan to record "patient-ready," the storage cabinet and position, and the hang time expiration. The scope may now be issued for a case.

### 5.8 Documentation and Traceability

**5.8.1** Every reprocessing cycle produces a record in the scope tracking system containing: scope serial number and barcode; patient medical record number and encounter for the preceding use; performing physician; each technician who performed precleaning, leak testing, manual cleaning, verification, and AER loading, identified individually; time stamps for each step; detergent and disinfectant lot numbers; MRC test result; cleaning verification results; AER identifier and cycle number; cycle parameter pass or fail; drying cabinet location; and hang time expiration.

**5.8.2** Records are retained for 10 years. AER printouts and downtime forms are scanned into the record within 5 business days.

**5.8.3** The Owner audits 20 records per week for completeness and reports the results monthly to the Infection Control Committee with the following metrics: percentage of cases with complete scope-to-patient linkage, cleaning verification failure rate, leak test failure rate, borescope findings, delayed reprocessing events, hang time expirations, and AER cycle aborts.

**5.8.4** Documentation is contemporaneous. Recording a step before performing it, or after leaving the room, is falsification and is managed under the human resources corrective action policy.

### 5.9 Water Quality Testing

**5.9.1** Each quarter, the Owner arranges sampling of final rinse water from every AER for heterotrophic plate count and for *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Legionella*, and nontuberculous mycobacteria, plus endotoxin. Samples are collected by the reference laboratory's stated method and chain of custody.

**5.9.2** Action limits: heterotrophic plate count greater than 10 CFU/100 mL, or any detection of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Legionella*, or nontuberculous mycobacteria, or endotoxin above 10 EU/mL. Any result exceeding an action limit is reported to the Owner and Infection Preventionist within 1 business day of receipt.

**5.9.3** On an exceeded limit, the affected AER is removed from service, water filters are replaced, the unit and its water line are disinfected per the AER manufacturer's protocol, and the AER is resampled with two consecutive passing results before return to service. The Owner and Infection Preventionist jointly determine whether a scope lookback is required and document the determination.

**5.9.4** Water filters, water lines, and the AER self-disinfection cycle are maintained on the schedule in the AER manual, documented by Biomedical Engineering in the equipment file. Following the spring 2026 cluster, monthly rather than quarterly sampling is in effect through 2027-03-31.

### 5.10 Competency

**5.10.1 Initial competency.** No employee reprocesses an endoscope independently until all of the following are documented in the training file: completion of didactic instruction on this procedure, the model-specific IFUs, PPE, and one-way workflow; observation of a full reprocessing cycle by a qualified technician; a minimum of ten supervised reprocessing cycles across both gastroscopes and colonoscopes; a written examination with a score of 90 percent or higher; and direct observation checklists (SPD-F-05) completed by the Educator for leak testing, manual cleaning, cleaning verification, and AER loading, each performed correctly without prompting.

**5.10.2 Annual competency.** Every technician repeats direct observation of all four checklists annually within the anniversary month of hire, plus a written examination and a review of any IFU changes. Procedure room nurses and GI technicians complete annual direct observation of bedside precleaning. Transport staff complete annual observation of container handling.

**5.10.3 Event-based competency.** Reeducation and repeat observation within 5 business days follow any documented deviation, any cleaning verification failure attributable to technique, the introduction of a new scope model or AER, or any change to this procedure.

**5.10.4** The Owner maintains a competency roster. An employee whose competency has lapsed is removed from the reprocessing assignment until it is restored. Given the resignation of two of eight technicians in May 2026, the Owner maintains a minimum of five currently competent technicians and escalates to the Approver in writing when the roster falls below five.

## 6. EXCEPTION PATHS

### 6.1 Failed Leak Test, Borescope Finding, or Visible Damage

**6.1.1** Stop immediately. Do not immerse the scope further and do not place it in the AER; fluid invasion converts a minor repair into a major one and leaves an uncleanable interior.

**6.1.2** Manually clean the exterior only, following Section 5.4.3 without immersion, using a cloth damp with detergent, then wipe with water and dry.

**6.1.3** Tag the scope "OUT OF SERVICE — DO NOT USE" with the date, finding, and technician name. Place it in the quarantine cabinet in the clean room. Scan to record "removed from service" with the reason.

**6.1.4** Notify the Owner the same shift. The Owner notifies the GI Lab charge nurse so the case schedule can be adjusted, and initiates a repair requisition.

**6.1.5** Determine whether the finding indicates the scope may have been used in a damaged condition on prior patients. The Owner and Infection Preventionist review the last 30 days of use, or since the last passing borescope inspection, whichever is longer, and document a written determination on whether patient notification or culture surveillance is warranted. The Approver authorizes any patient notification.

**6.1.6** On return from repair: the vendor's service report is filed; the scope receives a leak test, full manual cleaning, cleaning verification, borescope inspection of all channels and the distal end, and a complete HLD cycle before release. The Owner documents release to service by signature.

### 6.2 Failed Cleaning Verification

**6.2.1** Do not proceed to HLD. Return the scope to Section 5.4 and repeat manual cleaning in full, using a fresh detergent bath, fresh brushes, and full brush counts and soak time.

**6.2.2** Repeat cleaning verification. If the second test passes, the scope proceeds to HLD; both results, the repeat, and the technician are recorded in SPD-F-03.

**6.2.3** If the second test fails, remove the scope from service under Section 6.1 and perform borescope inspection of all channels before any further action. Notify the Owner the same shift.

**6.2.4** The Owner reviews every failure within 5 business days to determine whether the cause was technique, brush condition, detergent preparation, elapsed time, or device damage, and documents the determination and the corrective action. Three failures on the same scope within 30 days require vendor evaluation. Three failures by the same technician within 90 days require reeducation under Section 5.10.3.

### 6.3 Delayed Reprocessing

**6.3.1** A scope for which more than 60 minutes has elapsed since bedside precleaning is reprocessed under this path. The tracking system flags the interval automatically.

**6.3.2** Perform the leak test per Section 5.3, then extend manual cleaning as follows: soak in fresh enzymatic detergent for the maximum time permitted by the IFU rather than the minimum; double the brush passes in every channel to a minimum of six; and double the channel flush volumes.

**6.3.3** Cleaning verification is mandatory regardless of the sampling schedule. A failure follows Section 6.2.

**6.3.4** If more than 12 hours have elapsed since precleaning, or if bedside precleaning was not performed at all, the scope may not be returned to service on the strength of this procedure alone. The Owner is notified, the manufacturer's technical support line is contacted for a model-specific delayed-reprocessing instruction, and the instruction is documented and followed. Absent a manufacturer instruction, the scope goes to the vendor for evaluation.

**6.3.5** Every delayed reprocessing event is logged on SPD-F-06 with the cause. The Owner reports counts and causes monthly; recurring causes are addressed as process failures, not individual ones.

### 6.4 Loaner Scopes

**6.4.1** No loaner scope is used on a patient until it has been reprocessed at this facility under this procedure. A vendor's statement that a scope was reprocessed elsewhere is not accepted.

**6.4.2** On receipt, the Owner or designee documents on SPD-F-08: model, serial number, arrival date, vendor, and the presence of the model-specific IFU. If the IFU is absent, or if the model requires brushes, adapters, or an AER connector set the facility does not stock, the scope is refused and returned. This determination is made before the case is scheduled, not on the morning of the case.

**6.4.3** Assign a facility barcode and create a tracking system record so that every use and cycle is traceable.

**6.4.4** Perform leak testing, full manual cleaning, cleaning verification, borescope inspection of all channels and the distal end, and a full HLD cycle before first use. Document all results with images.

**6.4.5** Cleaning verification is performed on the first three reprocessing cycles of any loaner scope regardless of the standard sampling schedule.

**6.4.6** Before return to the vendor, reprocess the scope fully, perform a final borescope inspection, and document the condition on SPD-F-08 with the return date and courier. Retain the record for 10 years.

**6.4.7** Any loaner scope failing any step is quarantined under Section 6.1, the vendor is notified in writing, and the scope is not used.

### 6.5 After-Hours Emergency Cases

**6.5.1** For an emergency case outside staffed reprocessing hours, the charge nurse first checks the drying cabinet for a patient-ready scope within hang time. A scope within hang time is used; there is no justification for abbreviating reprocessing when a compliant scope exists.

**6.5.2** If no patient-ready scope is available, the charge nurse calls the on-call reprocessing technician from the call roster. The technician reports within 45 minutes and performs the full procedure. There is no abbreviated after-hours cycle. Manual cleaning steps, soak times, brush counts, cleaning verification, and the full AER cycle are performed exactly as written.

**6.5.3** If no reprocessing technician is reachable, the charge nurse escalates to the Administrator on Call and the attending physician. The decision to delay, transfer, or proceed is a clinical decision made by the attending physician and the Administrator on Call, documented in the medical record. Reprocessing steps are never omitted or shortened to enable a case. Section 5.4.7 applies without exception.

**6.5.4** Drying: the full 10-minute forced-air drying period in the cabinet with channel connections is required before use, in addition to the alcohol flush and air purge. This period is not waived.

**6.5.5** All after-hours reprocessing is documented in the tracking system in real time. Cleaning verification is mandatory for any after-hours cycle. The Owner reviews every after-hours event on the next business day.

### 6.6 Scope Implicated in an Infection Investigation

**6.6.1** On notification from Infection Prevention, the laboratory, a physician, or Quality that an endoscope may be associated with a patient infection or a cluster, the scope is located immediately and sequestered before any further reprocessing. If the scope is in an AER cycle, the cycle is allowed to complete and the scope is removed to quarantine without further handling; if the scope is soiled, it is placed in a labeled container and quarantined without cleaning, and Infection Prevention is consulted on culture sampling before any cleaning.

**6.6.2** Tag the scope "SEQUESTERED — INFECTION INVESTIGATION — DO NOT REPROCESS OR USE." Store it in the locked quarantine cabinet. The Owner and Infection Preventionist co-sign the sequestration log (SPD-F-09).

**6.6.3** Notify within 1 hour of learning of the implication: the Owner, the Infection Preventionist, the GI Lab medical director, and the Approver.

**6.6.4** Infection Prevention arranges microbiologic sampling of the scope channels and distal end, and of the AER used, its rinse water, and the drying cabinet, coordinating with the reference laboratory. Isolates are retained for strain typing.

**6.6.5** Retrieve from the tracking system the full use and reprocessing history of the scope for a minimum of 6 months: every patient, every technician, every AER and cycle, every verification result, every borescope image, and all repair records. Provide it to the Infection Preventionist within 1 business day.

**6.6.6** The Infection Preventionist and Approver determine the lookback population and whether patient notification, testing, or reporting to the state health authority and the manufacturer under medical device reporting requirements is required, and document the determination and its rationale.

**6.6.7** The scope remains sequestered until the Approver documents release in writing. Release requires, at minimum: identification and correction of the root cause; borescope inspection of all channels; two consecutive negative cultures after full reprocessing; and, if a device defect was found, vendor evaluation or replacement.

**6.6.8** The Owner convenes a multidisciplinary review within 10 business days including Infection Prevention, the GI Lab medical director, Biomedical Engineering, the Educator, and nursing leadership, with a written root cause analysis and corrective action plan submitted to the Approver and the Infection Control Committee. Corrective actions are tracked to closure with named owners and due dates.
