**DATE:** January 15, 2026

**SUBJECT:** NJDOL Audit Closure — $2.4 Million Assessment and 45-Day Response Deadline; Second Audit Period Signaled

The New Jersey Department of Labor and Workforce Development closed its examination of our owner-operator arrangements for the period January 2023 through December 2025 on January 9. The Department found that our contracted owner-operators are misclassified as independent contractors under the "ABC" test codified at N.J.S.A. 43:21-19(i)(6), and assessed $2.4 million in back unemployment and temporary disability contributions, statutory interest, and penalties. We have 45 days from the date of the determination letter — a response deadline of February 23, 2026 — to pay, negotiate a settlement, or file for an administrative hearing before the Division.

The finding turns on Prongs B and C of the ABC test. The Department concluded that our owner-operators perform services within the usual course of our business (Prong B, essentially unwinnable for a carrier whose business is moving freight) and that they are not customarily engaged in an independently established trade of the same nature (Prong C), given dispatch control, exclusive-use requirements, company placards, and settlement structures that mirror payroll. I want to be direct with this group: I do not believe an appeal reverses Prong B, and our Prong C evidence, while better, is not strong enough to justify betting the company on full reversal. My honest assessment is a 15 to 25 percent chance of a material reduction on appeal, achieved mostly through negotiated abatement of penalties rather than reversal of the underlying contribution liability.

More consequential than the dollar figure is what the examiner said informally at the exit conference and confirmed in a follow-up email on January 13: the Division considers 2026 forward to be a new, open audit period, and it intends to monitor our classification practices going forward rather than treating this as closed business. That means an appeal of the $2.4 million — even a successful one — resolves only historical liability. It does nothing to protect us against continuing exposure for every settlement check we cut to an owner-operator after today, at a rate the Department has now told us, in writing, that it considers unlawful.

I recommend a two-track approach, and I want the Executive Committee to authorize it this week. First, we file a timely request for an administrative hearing to preserve our appellate rights on the $2.4 million and to negotiate penalty abatement; this costs us little and forecloses nothing. Second, and independent of the appeal's outcome, we need to eliminate the owner-operator classification model prospectively. I recognize this is an operational and financial decision that belongs to Mr. Mbaye and Mr. Bhattacharya, but from a legal-risk standpoint, every pay period we continue the current arrangement after receiving this determination increases both our exposure in the newly opened audit period and the argument that any violation was willful — which matters for penalty multipliers and, under N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.10, potential personal liability for corporate officers who control payroll practices. That exposure reaches this table specifically.

I have asked our outside employment counsel, Fein & Ostrander, to prepare the hearing request for filing no later than February 16 to leave a buffer before the deadline. I will circulate a draft position statement to this group by January 30. In the meantime, I ask that no one discuss the audit findings with drivers, customers, or terminal staff beyond what Mr. Mbaye authorizes, and that all owner-operator settlement records from 2023 forward be preserved and not altered in form or substance. I am available to walk through the hearing request and the classification analysis with anyone on this distribution before we finalize our filing.

**TO:** All Terminal Managers

**FROM:** Cheikh Mbaye, Chief Operating Officer

**DATE:** February 20, 2026

**SUBJECT:** Owner-Operator Conversion to Employee Status Effective January 1, 2027; Albany Cross-Dock Opening November 15, 2026

I am writing to announce two decisions that will shape our operations for the remainder of this year and into next. Both were approved by the Executive Committee this week, and I want every terminal manager to hear them directly from me before details reach the field secondhand.

First, effective January 1, 2027, all 310 owner-operators currently under contract with Meadowlands Freight Systems will convert to employee driver status. This follows the Department of Labor's closure of its audit into our classification practices and its assessment in January of $2.4 million in back contributions and penalties. General Counsel Camille Beauregard has advised the Committee that the Department considers our classification approach an ongoing violation, not a closed matter, and that continuing the owner-operator model exposes the company to compounding liability regardless of how our appeal of the existing assessment proceeds. This is a legal and financial necessity, not a preference, and I want to be candid that it is also a retention opportunity. Our driver attrition is running at 31 percent against an industry average near 24, and exit interviews consistently cite income volatility and lack of benefits among owner-operators as a factor pushing drivers to competitors who offer W-2 stability. Converting these 310 drivers to employee status with predictable pay and benefits should help close that gap.

Sanjay Bhattacharya's office is finalizing the budget and financing plan for the associated cost increase and for the 218 tractors we will need to purchase or lease to equip converted drivers who do not retain suitable equipment of their own. Lorraine Petrucci's office is building the pay, benefits, and seniority framework that will govern the transition. You will receive detailed guidance from both of them in the coming months. Your job between now and then is preparatory: by March 15, each terminal manager must submit a current roster of owner-operators based at your terminal, including equipment ownership status (owned outright, financed, leased), average weekly miles, and any known intent to leave the company rather than convert. Please route these rosters to Lorraine's office with a copy to me.

Second, we are opening a cross-dock terminal in Albany, New York, no later than November 15, 2026. This facility is required to secure a new contract with a national retail customer worth $31 million annually, contingent on that opening date and on a 98.5 percent on-time service commitment. Tomasz Wisniewski has been named Albany Terminal Director and is already engaged with the landlord, the build-out contractor, and our operations planning group. I recognize that the timing of this opening alongside the owner-operator conversion places real strain on planning, capital, and staffing simultaneously. That is deliberate on our part in one respect: having employee drivers under direct dispatch control, rather than independent contractors who can decline loads, gives us the operational reliability the Albany contract's 98.5 percent commitment demands. The two initiatives support each other even though they compete for resources.

I will hold a call with all terminal managers on March 3 to take questions on both initiatives. Please come prepared with operational concerns specific to your terminal, particularly regarding lane coverage during the conversion period and any equipment or staffing implications you foresee for Albany-bound freight. I know this is a lot to absorb at once. It is the right set of decisions for the company, and I need this management team aligned in explaining it to drivers and customers.

**FROM:** Cheikh Mbaye, Chief Operating Officer

**SUBJECT:** Response to Concerns Regarding Timing of the Owner-Operator Conversion

Following the March 3 call and the written comments several of you submitted afterward, I want to respond directly to the position that we should delay the owner-operator conversion until the appeal of the Department of Labor's $2.4 million assessment is resolved. I understand the argument, and I want to explain why the Executive Committee is not adopting it.

The core of the concern, as I understand it from Denise Alvarez at Newark and Ray Costanzo at Elizabeth in particular, is that committing to a 19-cent-per-mile cost increase and to buying or leasing 218 tractors before we know the outcome of the appeal is fiscally premature. If we prevail on appeal, the argument goes, we will have spent money we did not need to spend and disrupted 310 driver relationships unnecessarily.

Here is the problem with waiting: the appeal addresses only the $2.4 million assessment for conduct in 2023 through 2025. It does not address, and cannot address, our exposure going forward. Camille Beauregard's office has confirmed that the Department of Labor has already signaled it is treating 2026 forward as a new, separate audit period under active review. That means every settlement check we issue to an owner-operator today is potentially a new violation, independent of how the historical appeal comes out. Even in the best-case scenario — full reversal of the $2.4 million — the arrangement that produced that assessment would still be unlawful going forward, because the Department's finding rests on how the work is structured and controlled, not on any defect specific to 2023 through 2025. Winning the appeal would tell us the state waived or miscalculated a past liability. It would not tell us our current classification model is sound. Waiting for the appeal to resolve, which our counsel estimates could take twelve to eighteen months through the administrative hearing process, means twelve to eighteen more months of the exposure that created this problem in the first place, at increasing dollar exposure the longer the current model runs.

I also want to address the cost concern on its own terms rather than dismiss it. Sanjay Bhattacharya's office has structured the tractor financing so that acquisition is staged against the conversion schedule, not front-loaded, which limits our capital exposure if any conversions slip. I recognize that does not eliminate the 19-cent-per-mile operating cost increase, which is real and which the revised budget memo you will receive from Sanjay's office addresses directly. But I want to note that this cost increase buys us something beyond compliance: it buys predictable driver availability at a moment when our attrition rate of 31 percent, well above the industry average, is itself a cost we are already absorbing in recruiting, training, and service failures. Lorraine Petrucci's forthcoming memo on pay and benefits is designed in part to make conversion a retention tool, not purely a compliance expense.

Finally, I want to acknowledge that some of you raised legitimate operational sequencing concerns — which terminals convert first, how we avoid a capacity gap during transition, and how conversion interacts with the Albany opening's demands on tractor availability. Those are the right questions, and I am asking each of you to submit terminal-specific transition sequencing input to my office by March 24 so we can build a schedule that does not strand freight. The decision to proceed with conversion on the current timeline is final. How we execute it well is not yet finalized, and that is where I want your input concentrated.

**FROM:** Sanjay Bhattacharya, Chief Financial Officer

**DATE:** April 15, 2026

**SUBJECT:** Revised FY2026–2027 Operating Budget — Owner-Operator Conversion Cost Impact and Tractor Financing Plan

This memo presents the revised operating budget incorporating the owner-operator conversion, the Albany cross-dock opening, and the liability insurance renewal, all of which land in the same fiscal window and together require us to reforecast margin for the remainder of 2026 and all of 2027.

### Conversion Operating Cost

The 310 converting drivers currently average approximately 100,000 miles annually, for a combined base of roughly 31 million miles. The finance and HR joint costing model — which loads employer payroll tax, workers' compensation premium, health and retirement benefit cost, and paid time off against the wage rate, net of the settlement rates we currently pay owner-operators — produces a net cost-per-mile increase of 19 cents. Applied to 31 million miles, that is approximately $5.9 million in incremental annual operating cost once conversion is complete. Because conversion will roll out by terminal from July through December rather than all at once, we expect roughly $1.5 million of this impact to hit FY2026 results and the full $5.9 million annualized run rate to begin in FY2027.

### Tractor Acquisition and Financing

Of the 310 converting drivers, we estimate 92 will retain and continue operating equipment they currently own or lease independently, subject to DOT and company safety inspection standards. The remaining 218 require company-provided tractors. We have structured a blended acquisition:

- **150 units on operating lease** through our existing relationship with Meridian Fleet Capital, at an estimated average of $2,900 per unit per month, for an annualized lease expense of approximately $5.2 million once fully deployed. Leasing this portion preserves balance sheet flexibility and matches monthly expense to the phased conversion schedule.
- **68 units purchased outright** through a term loan at an estimated blended rate of 7.25 percent over 84 months, with an average unit cost of $145,000, for total financed principal of approximately $9.9 million and estimated first-year debt service near $1.7 million.

Delivery is staged against the terminal-by-terminal conversion sequence Mr. Mbaye's office is finalizing, with the first 40 units scheduled for delivery in August to support early-converting terminals and the balance following through year-end.

### Insurance

Our liability renewal, effective June 1, came in 24 percent above expiring premium following two nuclear-verdict settlements involving carriers in our operating region, which affected the entire regional risk pool independent of our own claims history. This moves annual premium from approximately $8.3 million to approximately $10.3 million, an incremental cost of roughly $2.0 million. We are evaluating a higher self-insured retention layer for FY2027 to partially offset this, and will report back by August.

### Net Impact and Offsetting Revenue

Combining the conversion operating cost, lease and debt service on the tractor fleet, and the insurance increase, incremental annual cost once fully phased in is approximately $10.8 million against FY2026 revenue of $284 million — roughly 3.8 percent of revenue. Against this, the Albany cross-dock contract contributes $31 million in new annual revenue at an estimated 11 percent operating margin once the facility reaches steady-state utilization, and we expect meaningful though harder-to-quantify savings from reduced attrition-driven recruiting and training cost, currently running approximately $3,400 per driver hire against a base of roughly 290 driver separations annually. I will bring a formal recruiting-cost offset estimate to the Committee once Lorraine's office has finalized retention assumptions under the new pay structure. On a consolidated basis, I project operating margin compression of approximately 60 basis points for FY2026, narrowing to roughly 25 basis points by the second half of FY2027 as Albany volume ramps and conversion-driven retention savings materialize. I recommend the Committee approve the capital budget for the 218-tractor acquisition at the April 22 meeting so procurement can proceed against the delivery schedule above.

**FROM:** Lorraine Petrucci, Vice President of Human Resources

**DATE:** June 1, 2026

**SUBJECT:** Pay, Benefits, and Seniority Policy for Converting Owner-Operator Drivers

This memo establishes the policy governing the conversion of our 310 owner-operators to employee driver status, effective by terminal on a rolling schedule from July 2026 through completion by January 1, 2027. Please review this in full before meeting individually with affected drivers, and direct any questions to your assigned HR business partner rather than improvising answers, since these terms are legally significant and must be applied consistently.

### Pay Structure

Converting drivers will move to a mileage-based hourly-equivalent pay structure rather than a percentage-of-load settlement. Base pay is set at $0.71 per mile, benchmarked so that a driver at our current average annual mileage of 100,000 miles earns gross wages of approximately $71,000, layered with overtime eligibility for non-driving duties (loading, waiting time, terminal work) consistent with applicable wage and hour law. This rate was set deliberately above our prior average owner-operator net settlement, after equipment costs, to ensure conversion is not perceived as a pay cut, and it is the primary driver of the 19-cent-per-mile cost increase reflected in the revised operating budget.

### Benefits

Converting drivers become eligible for company group health, dental, and vision coverage on the first of the month following 60 days of employment, consistent with our standard new-hire waiting period. They are eligible to enroll in the 401(k) plan immediately, with company match beginning after 90 days, and accrue paid time off at the standard rate for their assigned seniority tier as described below. Drivers electing to sell or trade in personally owned equipment to the company as part of conversion will be referred to Fleet Services for an independent appraisal; no driver is required to sell equipment to the company, but those retaining personal equipment remain responsible for maintenance and insurance on units not covered by our fleet policies.

### Seniority

Years of active service as a Meadowlands owner-operator will count toward seniority for purposes of route and schedule bidding, vacation selection, and shift preference, on a one-for-one basis with employee driver years of service. This credit does not apply to pension plan vesting or 401(k) matching schedules, which run from the employee's conversion date under plan documents already on file with the Department of Labor. Drivers with disputes about their credited seniority years should raise them with their terminal manager within 30 days of their conversion date; after that window, credited seniority becomes final absent documented error.

### Equipment and Onboarding

Drivers converting without suitable personal equipment will be assigned company tractors from the 218-unit acquisition Finance has authorized, allocated by terminal according to the conversion sequence your terminal manager will confirm with you. All converting drivers, regardless of equipment source, must complete a two-day onboarding covering company safety policy, electronic logging device procedures, and dispatch protocols before their conversion date takes effect; onboarding may be completed in the two weeks preceding conversion and does not require the driver to be off the road for longer than that window.

### Timeline and Sequencing

Conversion will proceed terminal by terminal according to the sequencing schedule Mr. Mbaye's office is finalizing with fleet managers, beginning with terminals feeding the Albany startup in July and August, and concluding with the remaining terminals by December 15, 2026, to give a two-week buffer before the January 1, 2027 deadline. Terminal managers will receive individual driver conversion dates by June 20. Please schedule one-on-one meetings with each converting driver at least three weeks before their conversion date to review this policy and complete required paperwork.

I believe we open on schedule November 15. I do not believe we open with margin to spare, and I wanted this Committee to hear that plainly six weeks out rather than read it in a post-opening incident report. I will send a two-week update on October 29 and would welcome anyone on this distribution visiting the site before opening.

**SUBJECT:** Administrative Hearing Scheduled — NJDOL Assessment Appeal

The Division of Workforce Development has scheduled our hearing on the $2.4 million assessment for March 3, 2027, before Administrative Law Judge Renata Fiore in Trenton. This is later than we initially hoped, but it is consistent with current Division caseload delays that Fein & Ostrander report affecting most carrier classification appeals filed since late 2025.

Our position statement, filed in February, argued primarily for penalty and interest abatement rather than reversal of the underlying contribution finding, consistent with the assessment I gave this group in January. I want to update that assessment now that conversion is substantially complete: our hearing posture is materially stronger than it would have been had we continued the owner-operator model through this process. Judge Fiore's office has, in prior rulings Fein & Ostrander have reviewed, given weight to a carrier's demonstrated good-faith remediation when assessing penalty abatement, even where the underlying contribution liability stands. Completing conversion before the hearing date, rather than waiting on its outcome, gives us an affirmative equitable argument we would not otherwise have.

I do not want to overstate this. The $2.4 million in back contributions for the 2023–2025 period is very unlikely to be eliminated outright; our realistic target remains abatement of the penalty and interest components, which together make up approximately $650,000 of the assessment. I will circulate a hearing preparation memo to Sanjay's office in January so Finance can plan for the contribution liability regardless of the penalty outcome, and I will keep this group updated as the March date approaches. Separately, I have had no further written communication from the Division regarding the second, currently open audit period since the informal notice referenced in my January memo, which I read as a modestly favorable sign given the completed conversion, though I would caution against reading too much into silence at this stage.

**SUBJECT:** Preliminary Attrition Data — Converted Driver Population

You asked for early data on retention among converted drivers ahead of your January retrospective, and I have enough now to give you a preliminary read, with the caveat that our sample is still young and skewed toward the earliest-converting terminals.

Among the 214 drivers converted as of mid-November, we have had 11 separations in the period following their conversion date, an annualized rate of approximately 19 percent, measured from each driver's individual conversion date rather than calendar year. That compares favorably to the 31 percent fleet-wide attrition figure that was part of the original case for conversion, though I want to flag two reasons for caution before we treat this as a settled result. First, several of these drivers are still within what amounts to a honeymoon period on new pay and benefits, and second, our sample excludes the Bayonne, Perth Amboy, Linden, and Elizabeth cohorts converting this month and next, who include some of our longest-tenured owner-operators and whose reaction to conversion may differ from the earlier group.

Of the 11 separations, exit interview data shows 6 left for reasons unrelated to conversion terms — two retirements, one relocation, three for what interviewers coded as "better opportunity" without further detail — and 5 cited dissatisfaction specifically with the mileage-based pay structure relative to their prior settlement earnings, concentrated among drivers who ran unusually high weekly mileage as owner-operators and are now capped by hours-of-service and overtime rules in a way that caps their effective take-home relative to before. I flagged this dynamic to Sanjay's office informally in October; it affects a small subgroup, but it is the one substantive pattern in an otherwise encouraging early dataset, and I think it is worth the Committee's attention before we finalize 2027 pay bands. I will have a fuller quarter's data, including the four terminals converting this month, in time for your January retrospective.
