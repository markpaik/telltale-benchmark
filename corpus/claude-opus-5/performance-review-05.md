## Section 2 — Goals Set for FY2026 and Outcomes

**Goal 1: Place 180 households in permanent or permanent supportive housing.**

Outcome: 214 households placed. Exceeded.

The composition matters as much as the count. Ninety-one placements were households with at least one member with a documented disability, which is the population where our navigators historically stall. Thirty-eight were households that had cycled through our intake more than once in the prior three years. Alonzo made a deliberate decision in September to have navigators carry the hardest cases themselves rather than distribute them evenly, and to protect two navigators for high-acuity work only. The board of directors saw the aggregate number in the Q3 dashboard. They did not see that choice, which is the reason the number exists.

**Goal 2: Reduce average time to placement from 71 days to 60 days.**

Outcome: 49 days. Exceeded by a wide margin.

The reduction came from three changes Alonzo introduced without being asked. He built a standing Tuesday landlord call block that navigators are required to hold. He negotiated conditional pre-approval with four property management companies covering roughly 300 units, which removed an average of nine days from the application cycle. And he moved document collection to intake rather than post-match, which is the single largest contributor. I have asked him to write this up for the state housing coalition. He has not had time.

**Goal 3: Bring the navigator team to full staffing and reduce turnover.**

Outcome: Met. Two vacancies filled in October and January. Zero voluntary departures from the navigator team during the review period, against an agency-wide program staff turnover rate of 22 percent.

This is the goal I weight most heavily and the one least visible in reporting. In a year when he was doing two jobs, he lost nobody.

**Goal 4: Assume interim coverage of the Program Director, Housing vacancy (added August 2025, mid-year).**

Outcome: Met, five months, August 11 through January 9.

During this period Alonzo carried the housing portfolio in the executive program meeting, held the relationship with the Continuum of Care lead agency, signed off on the HMIS data quality submissions, and represented Puente at the city's homelessness response table. He did this in addition to supervising eight direct reports and maintaining his own caseload of complex placements. He was not given a stipend. I asked for one in September and was told to wait for the fiscal picture to clarify. It clarified in the wrong direction.

I want the record to show that this was an organizational failure, not a budget event. A stipend of $4,000 was available in August and was not authorized because I did not push hard enough for it. That is mine.
