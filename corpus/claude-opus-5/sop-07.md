## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure establishes the minimum requirements for isolating machines and equipment from all sources of hazardous energy, and for keeping them isolated, before any employee performs servicing or maintenance where the unexpected energization, start-up, or release of stored energy could cause injury.

This procedure exists because those controls failed. In March 2026 an operator performing a die change on the 200-ton press brake in Bay 3 lost part of two fingers when a second employee restored power at the wall disconnect, believing the job was complete. OSHA issued three citations, including a repeat item under 29 CFR 1910.147, with $94,500 in proposed penalties. The informal settlement agreement obligates Teton Fabrication Works to maintain machine-specific written energy control procedures for all 148 pieces of covered equipment and to document annual periodic inspections. This document, and the equipment-specific procedures it governs, satisfy that obligation.

The requirement is simple and absolute: **the person exposed to the hazard controls the isolation, and only that person removes their own lock.**

## 2. SCOPE

### 2.1 Covered Activities

This procedure applies to all servicing and maintenance of machines and equipment at the Idaho Falls facility, including but not limited to: die changes and tooling changes, lubrication, cleaning, unjamming, adjustment, alignment, inspection requiring entry into a point of operation, repair, component replacement, calibration, installation, and decommissioning.

It applies whenever an employee must:

- Remove or bypass a guard or other safety device; or
- Place any part of their body into a point of operation or into an area where an associated danger zone exists during a machine operating cycle.

### 2.2 Covered Personnel

- 74 authorized employees
- Approximately 200 affected employees
- 9 maintenance technicians (all of whom are also authorized employees)
- 6 shift supervisors
- The training coordinator
- All outside contractors, vendors, and service representatives performing work on covered equipment, including the conveyor vendor servicing the powder coat line

### 2.3 Covered Equipment and Energy Sources

All 148 pieces of equipment on the Master Equipment Energy Control Register (TFW-EHS-014-R1), including press brakes, fiber laser cutting cells, robotic weld cells, the powder coat line and its overhead conveyor, shears, saws, material handling equipment, air compressors, and dust collection systems.

Energy sources present at this facility include:

| Energy Type | Typical Magnitude | Facility Locations |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical | 480 VAC three-phase; 240 VAC; 120 VAC control | All production equipment |
| Pneumatic | 110 psi shop air | Press brakes, weld cells, conveyor actuators, blow-off stations |
| Hydraulic | Up to 3,000 psi; **accumulators retain pressure after shutdown** | Press brakes, shears, powder coat line lifts |
| Gravity / potential | Suspended loads, elevated rams, conveyor sections | Press brake rams, overhead powder coat conveyor, hoists |
| Thermal | Cure oven, weld cell fixtures, laser cutting beds | Powder coat oven, weld cells |
| Chemical | Powder coat pretreatment chemistry, shielding gas | Powder coat line, weld cells |
| Stored mechanical | Springs, flywheels, capacitors in laser drives | Presses, lasers, conveyors |

### 2.4 Exclusions

This procedure does not apply to:

- **Normal production operations**, except where servicing activity occurs during production as described in Section 9.
- **Cord-and-plug connected equipment** where the conditions in Section 9.2 are fully met.
- **Hot tap operations** on pressurized piping — not performed at this facility. Any proposal to perform such work requires written EHS approval and a separate procedure.
- **Work on energized electrical conductors** performed under NFPA 70E energized work permit — see TFW-EHS-021, Electrical Safe Work Practices. Such work is prohibited at this facility except for diagnostics and testing where de-energization introduces greater hazard.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Affected Employee.** An employee whose job requires them to operate or use a machine on which servicing is being performed under lockout, or whose job requires them to work in an area where such servicing is performed. Affected employees do not apply locks and do not perform the servicing.

**Authorized Employee.** An employee who locks out or tags out machines or equipment in order to perform servicing or maintenance. An affected employee becomes an authorized employee when their duties include performing servicing under this procedure and they have completed authorized-employee training.

**Capable of Being Locked Out.** An energy isolating device is capable of being locked out if it has a hasp or other attachment to which a lock can be affixed, has a built-in locking mechanism, or can be locked without dismantling, rebuilding, or replacing the device or permanently altering its energy control capability.

**Energy Isolating Device.** A mechanical device that physically prevents the transmission or release of energy. Examples: a manually operated circuit breaker, a disconnect switch, a line valve, a block. **Push buttons, selector switches, E-stops, and control circuit devices are not energy isolating devices.**

**Energy Source.** Any source of electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal, gravitational, or other energy.

**Group Lockout.** A lockout involving more than one authorized employee, administered so that each employee's personal protection is equivalent to that provided by a personal lockout.

**Lockout.** The placement of a lockout device on an energy isolating device, in accordance with an established procedure, ensuring that the energy isolating device and the equipment being controlled cannot be operated until the lockout device is removed.

**Lockout Device.** A device that uses a positive means such as a keyed lock to hold an energy isolating device in the safe position. At Teton Fabrication Works, all personal locks are red, keyed differently, stamped with the employee's badge number, and issued only by EHS.

**Periodic Inspection.** The annual audit, required by 29 CFR 1910.147(c)(6), in which an authorized employee not involved in the energy control procedure being inspected reviews the procedure and each authorized employee's responsibilities under it.

**Servicing and Maintenance.** Workplace activities such as constructing, installing, setting up, adjusting, inspecting, modifying, and maintaining or servicing machines or equipment, including lubrication, cleaning or unjamming, and making adjustments or tool changes.

**Tagout Device.** A prominent warning device with a means of attachment that can be securely fastened to an energy isolating device to indicate that the equipment may not be operated. Tags are warning devices only; they provide no physical restraint.

**Zero Energy State.** The verified condition in which all energy sources to a machine have been isolated, all stored and residual energy has been relieved, dissipated, or restrained, and verification testing confirms the machine cannot operate.

## 5. WRITING AND REVISING EQUIPMENT-SPECIFIC PROCEDURES

### 5.1 Requirement

Every piece of equipment on the Master Equipment Energy Control Register shall have a written, machine-specific energy control procedure. As of the effective date of this version, 61 of 148 procedures are missing or outdated; Section 5.6 governs the remediation schedule.

### 5.2 Required Content

Each equipment-specific procedure (form TFW-EHS-014-F1) shall state:

1. **Equipment identification** — asset number, description, manufacturer, model, serial number, physical location by bay and column grid.
2. **Scope of the procedure** — the specific servicing tasks it covers.
3. **A complete energy source inventory** — every energy type, its magnitude, and the location of every energy isolating device, identified by the permanent label affixed to that device.
4. **Sequenced shutdown steps** in the order they must be performed.
5. **Sequenced isolation steps**, naming each device and the position it must be placed in.
6. **Stored energy relief steps** — bleed valves, accumulator dump valves, ram blocking, conveyor section restraint, capacitor discharge wait times.
7. **Verification method** — the specific test to be performed, the instrument required, and the expected result.
8. **A photograph or labeled diagram** showing the location of each energy isolating device.
9. **Release and restart steps.**
10. **Special conditions** — accumulator dwell times, thermal cool-down periods, required blocking devices, minimum number of authorized employees.
11. **Required PPE and test instruments.**
12. **Author, EHS reviewer, approval date, revision number.**

### 5.3 Writing a New Procedure

**Step 5.3.1** — The requesting supervisor or maintenance technician opens a work order in the maintenance system, type code `LOTO-PROC`, and attaches the equipment asset number.

**Step 5.3.2** — A maintenance technician performs a physical walkdown of the equipment with the manufacturer's manual and electrical schematic in hand. The technician traces every energy source to its isolating device and records the device label text exactly as it appears on the device.

**Step 5.3.3** — If any energy isolating device lacks a permanent label, the technician tags it, records the deficiency in the work order, and requests a label from the maintenance planner. **Procedures shall not reference unlabeled devices.**

**Step 5.3.4** — If any energy isolating device is not capable of being locked out, the technician stops and escalates to EHS. EHS shall arrange replacement or modification of the device. Tagout alone is permitted only under Section 9.5.

**Step 5.3.5** — The technician drafts the procedure on form TFW-EHS-014-F1 and attaches photographs of each isolating device.

**Step 5.3.6** — The technician performs a dry run: following the draft exactly, without improvising, isolating the equipment and verifying zero energy. Any step that requires knowledge not written on the page is a defect. The technician corrects the draft.

**Step 5.3.7** — The draft is submitted through the work order to EHS.

**Step 5.3.8** — EHS reviews for completeness against Section 5.2, performs an independent field verification with a second authorized employee, and either approves or returns the draft with comments.

**Step 5.3.9** — Upon approval, EHS assigns the document number in the format `TFW-LOTO-[asset number]`, sets revision 1.0, and publishes the controlled copy to the maintenance system document library, linked to the equipment asset record.

**Step 5.3.10** — EHS prints a laminated field copy and mounts it in the procedure holder at the equipment. The field copy carries a printed "verify current revision in maintenance system" watermark.

**Step 5.3.11** — EHS updates the Master Equipment Energy Control Register and notifies affected authorized employees through the shift supervisors.

### 5.4 Revising an Existing Procedure

Revision is required whenever equipment is modified, an energy isolating device is added, removed, relocated, or replaced, a new servicing task falls outside the current scope, a periodic inspection identifies a deficiency, or an incident or near miss reveals a gap.

Steps 5.3.2 through 5.3.11 apply, with these differences: the revision number increments by whole numbers for changes to isolation steps or energy sources, and by tenths for editorial corrections. The superseded revision is archived in the maintenance system, never deleted. The obsolete laminated field copy is destroyed by EHS, not simply discarded at the machine.

### 5.5 Interim Control When No Procedure Exists

If servicing is required on equipment with no current approved procedure, work does not proceed under improvisation. The supervisor contacts EHS. EHS or a designated authorized employee performs a walkdown and issues a **Single-Use Energy Control Plan** (form TFW-EHS-014-F2), valid for that one job only, signed by EHS and the supervisor. A `LOTO-PROC` work order is opened simultaneously to produce the permanent procedure.

### 5.6 Remediation Schedule

The 61 deficient procedures identified in the internal audit shall be completed on the following schedule, tracked weekly by EHS and reported to the VP of Manufacturing Operations:

| Priority | Equipment Class | Count | Completion Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Press brakes, shears, presses | 14 | 28 August 2026 |
| 2 | Robotic weld cells, fiber lasers | 19 | 25 September 2026 |
| 3 | Powder coat line and conveyor | 11 | 23 October 2026 |
| 4 | Balance of register | 17 | 20 November 2026 |

Equipment in Priority 1 through 4 without a current procedure shall be serviced only under a Single-Use Energy Control Plan until its permanent procedure is issued.

## 6. THE LOCKOUT SEQUENCE

This sequence is mandatory and is performed in order. Do not skip steps. Do not perform steps out of order.

### 6.1 Step 1 — Preparation and Review

**6.1.1** Confirm the servicing task is within scope of this procedure (Section 2.1).

**6.1.2** Retrieve the equipment-specific procedure. Open it in the maintenance system, or use the laminated field copy after confirming its revision number matches the system copy.

**6.1.3** Read the procedure. Identify every energy source and every isolating device listed.

**6.1.4** Gather required items: personal lock stamped with your badge number, danger tag, hasps, required blocking devices, and the test instrument named in the procedure.

**6.1.5** Confirm your test instrument is within calibration. Check the calibration sticker date. An out-of-calibration meter is not acceptable for verification.

**6.1.6** If two or more authorized employees will work on the equipment, proceed under Section 8, Group Lockout.

### 6.2 Step 2 — Notification of Affected Employees

**6.2.1** Notify the shift supervisor of the intended lockout, the equipment, and the estimated duration.

**6.2.2** The supervisor notifies all affected employees in the area — operators of the equipment, operators of adjacent equipment whose work is affected, material handlers, and anyone downstream of the equipment in the process flow.

**6.2.3** The supervisor records the lockout in the shift lockout log: date, time, equipment asset number, authorized employee name and badge, task description, expected duration.

**6.2.4** For the powder coat line and overhead conveyor, notification extends to both the load and unload stations and to the oven area, because the conveyor spans multiple work zones.

### 6.3 Step 3 — Orderly Shutdown

**6.3.1** Shut down the equipment using its normal stopping procedure — the operator control panel, the cycle stop, the program halt. Follow the sequence in the equipment-specific procedure.

**6.3.2** Allow the machine to complete its cycle and come to a full stop. Do not use an emergency stop to shut down for lockout unless the equipment-specific procedure directs it; E-stops may leave the machine in an unpredictable state.

**6.3.3** For robotic weld cells, return the robot to its home position before shutdown if the procedure so requires.

**6.3.4** For the powder coat line, index the conveyor to a position where no part is suspended over a work area, and where the section to be serviced is at its documented safe stopping position.

**6.3.5** Confirm the machine has stopped. Do not proceed while any component is coasting, rotating, or in motion.

### 6.4 Step 4 — Isolation of Every Energy Source

**6.4.1** Working through the equipment-specific procedure in the order listed, operate each energy isolating device to its isolating position.

**6.4.2** **Electrical.** Move the disconnect handle to OFF. For 480 V equipment, confirm the handle is fully in the OFF detent, not resting between positions. Where the procedure identifies a separate control transformer disconnect, a servo drive disconnect, or a welder power feed, operate each one.

**6.4.3** **Pneumatic.** Close the air isolation valve at the equipment drop. Confirm the valve handle is perpendicular to the line for a ball valve, or in the position marked on the valve tag.

**6.4.4** **Hydraulic.** Close the hydraulic supply and return isolation valves as directed. Note that closing valves does not relieve accumulator pressure — that occurs in Step 5.

**6.4.5** **Gravity.** Lower the ram, slide, platen, or suspended load to its lowest position, or install the blocking device specified in the procedure. On the overhead powder coat conveyor, install the conveyor section restraint pin at the location shown in the procedure diagram.

**6.4.6** **Thermal.** Isolate the heat source and observe the cool-down period stated in the procedure before proceeding. The powder coat cure oven requires a documented cool-down before entry.

**6.4.7** If any listed isolating device cannot be operated, is damaged, or is not where the procedure says it is, **stop work.** Notify the supervisor and EHS. Do not substitute an alternative device without a revised procedure.

### 6.5 Step 5 — Application of Locks and Tags

**6.5.1** Apply your personal lock directly to each energy isolating device, or to a hasp installed on the device.

**6.5.2** Attach a danger tag to each lock. Complete the tag legibly: your name, badge number, department, date, time, and the reason for the lockout. An incomplete tag is a violation.

**6.5.3** Each authorized employee applies their **own** lock. Locks are not shared, not applied on behalf of another person, and not left with a supervisor to apply.

**6.5.4** Where a single isolating device must accept multiple locks, install a hasp. Where more than six locks are needed, use additional hasps in series or proceed under group lockout with a lock box.

**6.5.5** Keep your key on your person. Keys are never left at the machine, in a toolbox, on a hook, or in a desk drawer.

**6.5.6** Locks are issued by EHS, keyed differently, and stamped with a badge number. Personal locks are used for lockout only — never for lockers, gang boxes, or gates.

### 6.6 Step 6 — Relief of Stored and Residual Energy

**6.6.1** **Hydraulic accumulators.** Open the accumulator dump valve identified in the procedure. Observe the pressure gauge and confirm it reads zero. Hold the dump valve open for the dwell time specified in the procedure — typically 60 seconds — because accumulator bladders release pressure gradually. **A closed system that reads zero on one gauge may hold pressure elsewhere; check every gauge listed.**

**6.6.2** **Pneumatic.** Open the bleed valve or actuate the exhaust port downstream of the isolation valve. Listen for the air to exhaust fully. Confirm the equipment pressure gauge reads zero. Cycle any air-actuated component manually if the procedure directs it, to purge trapped air in cylinders.

**6.6.3** **Electrical capacitance.** For fiber laser drives, variable frequency drives, and servo systems, observe the discharge wait time posted on the equipment and stated in the procedure. Do not open enclosures before the wait time elapses.

**6.6.4** **Mechanical springs and flywheels.** Release spring tension or install the restraint specified. Allow flywheels to stop completely; do not brake them by hand or with a tool.

**6.6.5** **Gravity.** Confirm the blocking device or restraint installed in Step 4 is fully seated and load-bearing. Blocks must be rated for the load and must be the blocks specified in the procedure — not scrap, not tooling, not wood.

**6.6.6** If stored energy can re-accumulate — for example, a hydraulic system with a check valve, or a conveyor section that can drift — the procedure shall specify continuous monitoring or a positive restraint. Verify the restraint is in place.

### 6.7 Step 7 — Verification of Zero Energy

**This is the step that would have prevented the March injury. It is not optional and it is not delegable.**

**6.7.1** Confirm no personnel are exposed. Look into the point of operation, the die area, the cell interior, and under the conveyor section. Announce your intent to test.

**6.7.2** **Electrical verification.** Using the calibrated meter specified in the procedure, perform a live-dead-live test: verify the meter on a known live source, test the equipment conductors phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground at the point specified in the procedure, then verify the meter again on the known live source. Record zero volts. Personnel performing electrical verification inside enclosures shall wear PPE per TFW-EHS-021.

**6.7.3** **Mechanical verification.** Attempt to start the equipment using the normal operating controls. Press the start button, the cycle initiate, the foot pedal, the two-hand control — whichever apply. Confirm nothing moves and nothing energizes.

**6.7.4** **Return all controls to the neutral or OFF position after testing.**

**6.7.5** **Pressure verification.** Read each gauge listed in the procedure and confirm zero.

**6.7.6** **Physical verification.** Where the procedure directs, attempt to move the component by hand or with the specified tool to confirm it is restrained.

**6.7.7** If verification does not produce the expected result, the machine is not isolated. **Stop.** Do not enter the danger zone. Notify the supervisor and EHS, and treat the equipment as energized until the discrepancy is resolved and the procedure corrected.

**6.7.8** Verification is performed by the authorized employee who will be exposed. Under group lockout, verification is performed by the principal authorized employee, and each additional authorized employee confirms zero energy state before entering the danger zone.

### 6.8 Step 8 — Perform the Servicing

**6.8.1** Perform the work. If the scope changes such that additional energy sources become involved, stop, exit the danger zone, and return to Step 1 with a procedure that covers the new scope.

**6.8.2** If you leave the equipment for a break, a meeting, or any reason other than end of shift, your lock stays on. Locks are not removed for convenience.

**6.8.3** If work extends past your shift, follow Section 7.

## 7. RELEASE FROM LOCKOUT AND RESTART

### 7.1 Inspection Before Release

**7.1.1** Confirm the servicing is complete and the work order is ready to close.

**7.1.2** Inspect the work area. Remove all tools, parts, rags, test leads, and debris from the machine and the point of operation.

**7.1.3** Reinstall every guard, cover, interlock, light curtain, and safety device removed or bypassed. Confirm interlocks and light curtains function before restart if the procedure directs a functional test.

**7.1.4** Remove blocking devices, restraint pins, and chocks. Account for every device against the procedure list.

### 7.2 Personnel Check

**7.2.1** Visually confirm all employees are clear of the machine, the cell interior, the die area, the conveyor path, and any pinch point.

**7.2.2** Announce, audibly and clearly, that the machine is about to be re-energized. For the powder coat line, the announcement is made at both the load and unload stations.

**7.2.3** Walk the perimeter of large equipment — weld cells, the conveyor run — before releasing.

### 7.3 Lock Removal

**7.3.1** Each authorized employee removes their own lock and tag. No exceptions except Section 9.1.

**7.3.2** Remove locks in the reverse order of application where the procedure specifies a sequence.

**7.3.3** Retain the tag for the shift log if the supervisor requires it; otherwise discard the used tag. Tags are single-use.

**7.3.4** Return hasps and shared devices to the lockout station.

### 7.4 Re-energization and Restart

**7.4.1** Restore energy isolating devices to their operating positions in the sequence given in the equipment-specific procedure. Sequence matters — restoring air before electrical, or the reverse, can cause unexpected motion on some equipment.

**7.4.2** The authorized employee, or the operator under the authorized employee's direction, performs the start-up.

**7.4.3** Observe the first cycle from outside the danger zone. Confirm normal operation.

**7.4.4** Notify the supervisor that energy has been restored. The supervisor closes the entry in the shift lockout log and notifies affected employees that the equipment is back in service.

**7.4.5** Close the work order in the maintenance system, recording work performed, parts used, and any procedure discrepancies observed.

### 7.5 Shift Change with Work in Progress

Lockout protection is continuous. It does not lapse at shift change.

**7.5.1** The outgoing authorized employee and the incoming authorized employee meet at the equipment before the outgoing employee removes any lock.

**7.5.2** The outgoing employee briefs the incoming employee at the machine, covering: work completed, work remaining, the isolation points locked, stored energy relieved, blocking devices installed, any deviation or anomaly.

**7.5.3** The incoming employee applies their personal lock and tag to every isolating device.

**7.5.4** The incoming employee independently verifies zero energy per Section 6.7. **The incoming employee does not accept the outgoing employee's verification.**

**7.5.5** Only after the incoming employee's lock is applied and verification is complete does the outgoing employee remove their lock.

**7.5.6** Both employees sign the Shift Change Lockout Transfer entry in the shift lockout log. Both shift supervisors initial the transfer.

**7.5.7** If no incoming authorized employee is available to accept transfer, the equipment remains locked out. The outgoing employee's lock is replaced by a supervisor-applied continuity lock from the department lock box, documented in the log, and the equipment stays out of service until an authorized employee resumes work under a fresh lockout.

## 8. GROUP LOCKOUT

### 8.1 When Required

Group lockout applies whenever two or more authorized employees service the same equipment, or when a job involves multiple crafts, multiple shifts, or a number of isolation points that makes individual locks on every device impractical.

### 8.2 Principal Authorized Employee

**8.2.1** The supervisor designates one Principal Authorized Employee (PAE) in writing on the Group Lockout Permit (form TFW-EHS-014-F3).

**8.2.2** The PAE is accountable for isolation, verification, the lock box, and the roster. The PAE does not assume responsibility for any individual's personal protection; each employee retains that.

### 8.3 Lock Box Procedure

**8.3.1** The PAE performs Steps 1 through 7 of Section 6, applying an operations lock to every energy isolating device.

**8.3.2** The PAE places the keys to all operations locks into the group lock box and closes it.

**8.3.3** The PAE applies their personal lock to the lock box hasp first.

**8.3.4** Each additional authorized employee applies their personal lock to the lock box hasp and signs the Group Lockout Permit roster with name, badge number, craft, and time in.

**8.3.5** Before entering the danger zone, each employee personally confirms zero energy state — at minimum, an attempted start using the normal controls, plus any test the PAE directs.

**8.3.6** As each employee completes their portion, they sign out on the roster and remove their personal lock from the box.

**8.3.7** The PAE removes their personal lock last, after confirming the roster shows all employees signed out and Section 7.1 and 7.2 checks are complete.

### 8.4 Lock Box Control

**8.4.1** All lock boxes are numbered and registered to EHS. The three lock boxes found during the internal audit with unaccounted keys have been retired and destroyed; replacement boxes 07 through 09 are in service.

**8.4.2** Each lock box carries a printed key inventory card listing every operations lock keyed to that box.

**8.4.3** At the close of each group lockout, the PAE reconciles the keys in the box against the inventory card and initials the card.

**8.4.4** Any discrepancy between keys present and keys listed is reported to EHS **immediately**, before the equipment is released. EHS treats an unaccounted key as a loss of isolation integrity: the affected locks are cut and replaced under EHS supervision, and the incident is investigated.

**8.4.5** EHS audits all lock boxes monthly and records the audit in the maintenance system.

## 9. EXCEPTIONS AND SPECIAL SITUATIONS

### 9.1 Removal of an Absent Employee's Lock

Removing another employee's lock is the single most dangerous act permitted under this procedure. It is permitted only when every step below is completed and documented on the Lock Removal Authorization (form TFW-EHS-014-F4). No step may be skipped, and no step may be performed retroactively.

**9.1.1** The shift supervisor verifies the authorized employee is not on site. Check the badge access log, call the department, and check the parking area.

**9.1.2** The supervisor makes documented attempts to contact the employee — a phone call to the number on file and a text message, at minimum. Record the times of each attempt.

**9.1.3** The supervisor waits a minimum of 30 minutes between the first contact attempt and lock removal, unless the equipment presents an immediate hazard in its locked state.

**9.1.4** The supervisor inspects the equipment and confirms the servicing is complete or can be safely abandoned in its current state.

**9.1.5** The supervisor confirms no person is in or near the danger zone, including inside cells, under conveyor sections, and behind guards.

**9.1.6** The supervisor obtains verbal concurrence from EHS, or from the VP of Manufacturing Operations if EHS is unavailable. The name and time of the concurring manager is recorded.

**9.1.7** A second person — another supervisor or an authorized employee — independently confirms items 9.1.4 and 9.1.5 and signs the form as witness.

**9.1.8** The supervisor cuts the lock. The cut lock and its tag are retained and attached to the completed form.

**9.1.9** The supervisor documents the removal on form TFW-EHS-014-F4 and submits it to EHS the same shift.

**9.1.10** **The supervisor ensures the employee is informed of the removal before that employee resumes work at the facility.** This is done in person or by phone at the start of the employee's next shift, before the employee approaches any equipment. The notification is recorded on the form.

**9.1.11** EHS reviews every lock removal. A pattern of removals for a single employee, area, or shift triggers a formal investigation.

### 9.2 Cord-and-Plug Connected Equipment

Lockout is not required for servicing cord-and-plug connected equipment when **all** of the following are true:

- The equipment is connected by a single cord and plug.
- Unplugging the cord eliminates all hazardous energy — there is no stored energy, no capacitance, no spring, no gravity load.
- **The plug is under the exclusive control of the employee performing the servicing** — physically in their hand, in their pocket, or within their line of sight and arm's reach at all times.

If the plug leaves the employee's exclusive control for any reason, or if the employee cannot see it, a plug lockout device shall be applied with a personal lock. Plug lockout devices are stocked at every lockout station.

Examples at this facility: hand grinders, portable lighting, bench-top drill presses, some inspection equipment. **Not applicable to** any equipment hard-wired, on a disconnect, or supplied by more than one circuit.

### 9.3 Minor Servicing Exception

Minor tool changes, adjustments, and other minor servicing activities that take place during normal production operations are exempt from full lockout **only if** the work is routine, repetitive, integral to production, and performed using alternative measures that provide effective protection.

**9.3.1 Approved minor servicing tasks.** Only tasks listed on the Minor Servicing Register (TFW-EHS-014-R2) qualify. EHS approves additions after a documented hazard analysis. Clearing a jam in a fiber laser cutting bed is one such approved task, subject to 9.3.2.

**9.3.2 Alternative measures for clearing a laser jam.** The operator shall:

1. Press cycle stop and allow the machine to come to a complete halt. Do not use the E-stop as the primary means unless the laser is actively cutting.
2. Confirm the laser beam-on indicator is extinguished and the shutter status shows CLOSED on the HMI.
3. Engage the maintenance mode key switch and remove the key, retaining it on their person. The key switch disables beam emission and axis motion.
4. Confirm the enclosure interlock has tripped and the interlock status shows OPEN.
5. Clear the jam using the reach tool provided. Do not place hands into the cutting envelope where a tool will serve.
6. Exit the envelope, close the enclosure, reinsert the key, and reset.

**9.3.3** If clearing the jam requires bypassing an interlock, defeating a guard, entering the cutting envelope with the key switch unavailable, or reaching into the machine with any part of the body beyond the wrist, **the minor servicing exception does not apply.** Perform full lockout per Section 6.

**9.3.4** Die changes on press brakes are **not** minor servicing and require full lockout, without exception, regardless of duration. This includes changes that appear to take less than one minute.

### 9.4 Contractor Coordination

**9.4.1** Before any contractor performs covered work, EHS conducts a coordination briefing with the contractor's site supervisor. The briefing covers: this procedure, the equipment-specific procedure for the affected equipment, energy sources present, lock and tag color conventions, the shift lockout log, emergency contacts, and the process for reporting discrepancies.

**9.4.2** The contractor provides a copy of their own written energy control program. EHS compares it against this procedure. Where the two differ, the more stringent requirement governs, and the governing requirement is recorded on the Contractor Energy Control Coordination Form (TFW-EHS-014-F5).

**9.4.3** Contractor employees apply their own personal locks and tags to the same energy isolating devices used by Teton employees. Contractor locks shall be a color other than red, and shall identify the contractor company and the individual.

**9.4.4** Where Teton employees and contractor employees work on the same equipment simultaneously, group lockout under Section 8 applies, with the PAE designated from Teton personnel.

**9.4.5** The Teton project owner — the person who engaged the contractor — notifies affected Teton employees of contractor lockout activity and enters it in the shift lockout log.

**9.4.6 Powder coat conveyor vendor.** The overhead conveyor presents gravity hazards along its full run, crossing multiple work zones. Before conveyor work, the vendor and the Teton project owner shall walk the entire affected run, identify every zone where a suspended section or carrier could move, install restraint pins at each documented location, and barricade the floor beneath the work area. Both parties sign the walkdown record on form TFW-EHS-014-F5.

**9.4.7** EHS countersigns every Contractor Energy Control Coordination Form and retains it with the contractor file.

### 9.5 Tagout-Only Situations

Tagout without lockout is permitted only where an energy isolating device is not capable of being locked out and cannot practicably be replaced. Where tagout alone is used:

- EHS shall document why lockout is not feasible.
- At least one additional safety measure shall be applied — removing an isolating circuit element, blocking a control switch, opening an extra disconnecting device, or removing a valve handle.
- The tag shall be attached at the same location the lock would occupy.
- The authorized employee shall be trained in the limitations of tags: a tag is a warning, not a restraint, and it does not stop anyone.
- Any device replaced or modified after the effective date of this version shall be capable of being locked out. Tagout-only is a transitional state, not a permanent condition.

### 9.6 Testing or Positioning Requiring Temporary Re-energization

Where the servicing task requires the machine to be energized to test or position a component:

**9.6.1** Clear the machine of tools and materials.

**9.6.2** Remove all employees from the machine area and the danger zone.

**9.6.3** Remove lockout devices per Section 7.3.

**9.6.4** Energize and proceed with the test or positioning. Only the authorized employee performing the test operates the controls. Duration is limited to what the test requires.

**9.6.5** De-energize and reapply energy control measures per Section 6 — including full verification of zero energy — before resuming servicing. **Verification is repeated in full every time.** A prior verification does not carry over.

**9.6.6** Where a task requires repeated test cycles, the equipment-specific procedure shall document the cycle and the authorized employee shall follow it each time.

## 10. TRAINING AND AUTHORIZATION

### 10.1 Authorized Employee Training

Initial training, minimum four hours, covering: recognition of hazardous energy sources, type and magnitude of energy at this facility, methods and means of isolation and control, this procedure in full, the equipment-specific procedures for equipment they will service, and a hands-on practical demonstration on at least one machine in their work area.

The practical demonstration requires the employee to perform a complete lockout, including verification, while the training coordinator observes against a checklist. The employee must pass every checklist item. A failed item requires re-demonstration.

### 10.2 Affected Employee Training

Minimum one hour, covering: the purpose and use of energy control procedures, how to recognize lockout in effect, lock and tag appearance and meaning, and the absolute prohibition on attempting to start, energize, or use locked or tagged equipment. This training explicitly addresses the March 2026 incident and the mechanism by which it occurred.

### 10.3 Other Employees

All employees who work in areas where energy control procedures may be used receive orientation on the prohibition against tampering with locks and tags.

### 10.4 Retraining

Retraining is required annually for authorized employees, every two years for affected employees, and immediately upon: a change in job assignment, a change in machines or processes presenting a new hazard, a change to this procedure, a periodic inspection revealing deviations or inadequacies, or any observed failure to follow the procedure.

### 10.5 Authorization Records

EHS maintains the Authorized Employee Register listing each authorized employee, badge number, department, shift, equipment authorized, initial training date, most recent retraining date, next due date, and practical demonstration score. An employee whose retraining lapses is suspended from authorized status until retrained; EHS notifies the supervisor and the employee.

## 11. PERIODIC INSPECTION

### 11.1 Frequency and Coverage

A periodic inspection of energy control procedures shall be performed **at least annually**, covering every equipment-specific procedure and every authorized employee, **on both shifts**. No record of periodic inspection exists for either shift for the preceding two years; the first inspection cycle under this version begins 3 August 2026 and shall be complete by 31 October 2026.

Inspections shall be distributed across the year rather than compressed, so that no authorized employee goes more than twelve months without being observed.

### 11.2 Inspector Qualification

The inspection is performed by an authorized employee **other than** those using the energy control procedure being inspected. EHS maintains a roster of qualified inspectors. The inspector shall not inspect their own department's procedures where they perform servicing.

### 11.3 Inspection Method

**11.3.1** The inspector reviews the equipment-specific procedure against field conditions: are all energy sources listed, are all isolating devices where the procedure says, are labels legible and matching.

**11.3.2** The inspector observes an authorized employee performing an actual or simulated lockout on the equipment, following the procedure step by step.

**11.3.3** The inspector reviews each observed employee's responsibilities with them individually, confirming understanding of: their duties, isolation points, verification method, the shift change requirement, and the prohibition on removing another's lock. This is a conversation, not a signature line.

**11.3.4** Where tagout is used, the inspector additionally reviews the limitations of tags with each affected employee.

**11.3.5** The inspector records findings on the Periodic Inspection Record (form TFW-EHS-014-F6): date, equipment, procedure number and revision, employees included, inspector name, each deviation or inadequacy identified, and corrective action assigned with owner and due date.

### 11.4 Certification

EHS certifies each inspection, recording the equipment identified, the date, the employees included, and the inspector. Certifications are retained in the maintenance system, linked to the equipment asset record, for a minimum of three years.

### 11.5 Corrective Action

Every deviation or inadequacy generates a corrective action work order with an owner and a due date not exceeding 30 days. EHS tracks open actions weekly and reports status monthly to the VP of Manufacturing Operations. Findings that indicate a procedure is inadequate trigger revision under Section 5.4. Findings that indicate an employee is not following the procedure trigger retraining under Section 10.4.

---

## 12. ENFORCEMENT

Failure to follow this procedure is a serious safety violation. Consequences range from retraining and suspension of authorized status to termination, depending on severity and intent. The following are treated as gross violations subject to immediate suspension pending investigation:

- Operating, energizing, or attempting to start equipment bearing a lock or tag.
- Removing another employee's lock outside Section 9.1.
- Working in a danger zone without applying a personal lock.
- Skipping verification of zero energy.
- Falsifying a tag, log entry, permit, or inspection record.

Employees who stop work over a safety concern, report a procedural gap, or refuse to service equipment lacking a current procedure are protected from retaliation without exception.
