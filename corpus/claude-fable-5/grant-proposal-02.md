# Ansel Brack Trust — Rural Maternal Health Access Program

## Full Application Narrative

**Applicant:** Platte Meridian Community Health
**Project Title:** Every Mother, Every Mile: Rural Maternal Health Access in Central Nebraska
**Amount Requested:** $3,750,000 over five years
**Submitted:** November 14, 2026

---

## Statement of Need

Platte Meridian Community Health is a federally qualified health center serving 28,400 patients across six sites in central Nebraska with 214 staff and a $41 million annual budget. Our service area covers a stretch of the Platte River valley where agriculture, meatpacking, and food processing anchor the economy and where the population our health center serves looks very different from the state as a whole. Sixty-two percent of our patients are Hispanic or Latino. Eleven percent are Somali or Sudanese, drawn to the region by employment in processing plants over the past fifteen years. Thirty-eight percent of our patients are best served in a language other than English, principally Spanish, Somali, and Arabic. Forty-four percent live at or below 138 percent of the federal poverty level, and 27 percent are uninsured.

Within that population, the maternal health picture has changed rapidly and for the worse along the dimension that matters most: access. Since 2021, the number of prenatal patients in our care has grown from 610 to 1,180 — a 93 percent increase in five years, driven by in-migration of young working families, by the closure of private obstetric practices in the region, and by our reputation among immigrant communities as the place where a pregnant woman can be seen regardless of insurance status or language. We now expect roughly 950 births each year among our prenatal panel.

Our capacity has not kept pace, and the consequences show up exactly where the Ansel Brack Trust's outcome measures direct attention:

**First-trimester entry to care.** Only 51 percent of our prenatal patients enter care in the first trimester, against a Nebraska state average of 74 percent. Nearly half of our patients begin prenatal care after the window in which early risk screening, dating, chronic disease management, and interventions for hypertension and diabetes are most effective. The reasons are documented in our own patient interviews: patients working shift schedules at processing plants cannot attend appointments offered only between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m.; patients without documentation or insurance delay care out of fear of cost; and patients new to the country do not know that pregnancy care is available to them at all until a friend, relative, or church contact tells them.

**Postpartum follow-up.** In 2025, 54 percent of our delivered patients completed a postpartum visit within twelve weeks. The remainder — disproportionately uninsured patients and those whose Medicaid coverage lapses in the postpartum period — are lost to follow-up during the interval when hypertension, hemorrhage complications, depression, and interpregnancy chronic disease are most likely to surface untreated.

**Distance to delivery.** In 2023, Meadowlark Regional Hospital closed its obstetrics unit, the third labor and delivery closure in this part of Nebraska since 2019. The nearest delivery room is now 38 miles from our main site, and for patients at our westernmost sites the drive can exceed 55 miles. Our patients deliver at a hospital where, until this year, few of our clinicians held privileges and where interpreter capacity is thin. The distance is not an abstraction. Since the closure we have documented twelve unplanned out-of-hospital or en-route deliveries among our patients, and our clinicians report a rise in patients who arrive at the delivering hospital with no prenatal records available because care fragmentation across the 38 miles remains unsolved.

**Birth outcomes.** Our low birth weight rate in 2025 was 8.9 percent, against a Nebraska rate of 7.4 percent. We recorded seven cases meeting CDC severe maternal morbidity criteria among our patients' 2025 deliveries, identified through hospital transfer records and post-delivery chart review — a rate we believe is undercounted precisely because our visibility into delivery-hospital records is incomplete.

**Cost.** Our internal cost accounting places the average cost per prenatal episode at $2,410, inflated by late entry (which concentrates care into fewer, higher-acuity visits), by missed appointments (our prenatal no-show rate is 19 percent), and by avoidable emergency department use among patients without a reliable connection to our care team between visits.

The need, stated plainly: a prenatal population that has nearly doubled, entering care late, delivering far away, following up inconsistently, and bearing worse outcomes at higher cost than the state around it. Every one of the Trust's five scored measures identifies a dimension on which our patients are currently underserved. This application describes what we intend to do about it.

## Project Description

Every Mother, Every Mile is a five-year redesign of maternal health services at Platte Meridian Community Health built on four components: expanded midwifery capacity, a community health worker corps recruited from and trained in our own communities, contracted doula support through delivery and postpartum, and evening access at three sites. Together these components attack the specific failure points identified above — late entry, missed follow-up, fragmentation across the 38 miles to delivery, and cost per episode.

### Component 1: Two Certified Nurse-Midwives

We will recruit and employ two full-time certified nurse-midwives, expanding our prenatal clinical capacity by approximately 3,400 visits annually. The midwives will carry prenatal and postpartum panels at our three highest-volume sites, provide group prenatal care using the CenteringPregnancy model adapted for Spanish- and Somali-language cohorts, and — critically — seek privileges at Loup Valley Regional Medical Center, the delivery hospital 38 miles away, so that our patients encounter a known clinician at delivery and their records travel with a person, not only a fax. One of our current family physicians already obtained courtesy privileges at Loup Valley in early 2026; the midwives will follow that pathway. Midwifery-led care is the single most evidence-supported model for the outcomes the Trust scores: lower preterm birth and low birth weight rates, higher postpartum visit completion, and lower cost per episode relative to physician-only models for low- and moderate-risk pregnancies.

### Component 2: Four Community Health Workers

We will hire four full-time community health workers — two Spanish-speaking, one Somali-speaking, one Nuer/Arabic-speaking — recruited from the communities we serve and trained through the community health worker certificate program at Central Community College, with which we have an executed training agreement. Each CHW will carry a caseload of approximately 90 pregnant and postpartum patients at a time. Their responsibilities are concrete and measurable:

- **Early identification and enrollment.** CHWs will conduct outreach through churches, mosques, employers, the local WIC office, our own walk-in and dental clinics, and community events, with a specific mandate to find pregnant women before the end of the first trimester. Every positive pregnancy test at any Platte Meridian site triggers same-week CHW contact and an appointment within seven days.
- **Navigation across the 38 miles.** CHWs will arrange transportation to delivery-hospital appointments, coordinate the transfer of records, accompany patients to key visits, and manage the delivery plan each patient carries in her own language.
- **Postpartum retention.** CHWs will contact every delivered patient within 72 hours of discharge, schedule the postpartum visit before the patient leaves that phone call, and conduct home visits for patients who miss appointments, screening for hypertension warning signs and postpartum depression using validated tools in the patient's language.
- **Coverage enrollment.** CHWs will be trained as certified application counselors, enrolling eligible patients in Medicaid — including Nebraska's twelve-month postpartum Medicaid extension — and documenting sliding fee eligibility for the remainder.

### Component 3: Doula Contract

We will contract with Prairie Doula Collective, a Nebraska-based network of trained community doulas, to provide labor support and two postpartum home visits for up to 250 patients per year, prioritized by risk score and social need. Doula support directly addresses severe maternal morbidity risk — continuous labor support is associated with fewer cesarean deliveries and better recognition of complications — and it puts a trusted, language-concordant advocate in the delivery room 38 miles from home, where our patients currently labor without one.

### Component 4: Evening Hours at Three Sites

Beginning in month four of year one, our three highest-volume sites will offer prenatal and postpartum appointments until 8:00 p.m. two evenings per week, staffed by the project midwives on a rotating schedule with existing clinical support staff on differential pay. This component responds directly to what our patients have told us: shift workers cannot come at 2:00 p.m. on a Tuesday. Evening access is our principal operational lever for moving first-trimester entry, because the barrier for a working patient is not willingness — it is the choice between a prenatal visit and a shift's wages.

### The Delivery Partnership: Where It Stands

The Trust deserves a candid account of a significant change since our letter of intent. Our original design named Prairie Women's Health, an obstetric group practicing at Loup Valley Regional Medical Center, as our formal delivery partner, with co-management protocols and shared privileging. In June 2026, Prairie Women's Health was sold to an investor group and withdrew its commitment to this project. We will not present that partnership as intact, because it is not.

Here is the arrangement as it stands today. First, we hold an executed patient transfer and records-sharing agreement directly with Loup Valley Regional Medical Center, signed August 2026, which governs prenatal record transmission, delivery notification back to our care team within 24 hours, and interpreter coordination. Second, one Platte Meridian physician holds courtesy privileges at Loup Valley, and the hospital's medical staff office has confirmed in writing that certified nurse-midwives employed by Platte Meridian are eligible to apply for privileges under its existing bylaws; privileging of the project midwives is a year-one milestone with hospital cooperation documented in the attached letter of support from Loup Valley's chief executive. Third, we remain in discussion with the successor entity to Prairie Women's Health regarding a consultative agreement for high-risk co-management; those discussions are unresolved, and our project design does not depend on their success. The redesigned model routes continuity through our own privileged clinicians, our transfer agreement with the hospital itself, and our doulas and CHWs at the bedside — an architecture that is more durable than one resting on a single private practice, as this year taught us.

### Implementation Timeline

**Year 1:** Recruit midwives (months 1–6); enroll CHW cohort at Central Community College (months 1–4) with employment beginning month 3 and full certification by month 9; execute doula contract (month 2); launch evening hours (month 4); midwife privileging applications at Loup Valley (months 6–12); baseline data systems live (month 3). **Year 2:** Full-model operation; CenteringPregnancy cohorts launched in Spanish and Somali; first annual evaluation cycle. **Years 3–4:** Steady-state operation, mid-course corrections from evaluation findings, and phased transition of CHW positions to billed services as described under Sustainability. **Year 5:** Full sustainability transition; final evaluation; dissemination to other Nebraska FQHCs facing obstetric unit closures.

## Goals and Measurable Objectives

The project's goals are organized around the five measures the Ansel Brack Trust scores. All baselines are from Platte Meridian's 2025 UDS and internal registry data; all targets are for the project's fifth year unless noted, with annual interim targets specified in our evaluation plan.

**Goal 1: Pregnant patients enter care early.**
- *Objective 1.1:* Increase the percentage of prenatal patients entering care in the first trimester from 51 percent to 74 percent — parity with the state average — by year five, with interim targets of 57 percent (year 1), 62 percent (year 2), 66 percent (year 3), and 70 percent (year 4).
- *Objective 1.2:* By the end of year one, 90 percent of patients with a positive pregnancy test at any Platte Meridian site receive a first prenatal appointment scheduled within seven calendar days.
- *Objective 1.3:* By year two, at least 30 percent of new prenatal patients report first contact through CHW community outreach, documented at intake.

**Goal 2: Postpartum patients complete follow-up.**
- *Objective 2.1:* Increase completion of a postpartum visit within twelve weeks of delivery from 54 percent to 85 percent by year five (interim: 62, 70, 76, 81 percent).
- *Objective 2.2:* By year two, 95 percent of delivered patients receive CHW contact within 72 hours of hospital discharge.
- *Objective 2.3:* By year three, 90 percent of postpartum patients are screened for depression with a validated, language-appropriate instrument, with documented follow-up for positive screens.

**Goal 3: Severe maternal morbidity declines.**
- *Objective 3.1:* Reduce the severe maternal morbidity rate among Platte Meridian prenatal patients by 25 percent from the corrected baseline established in year one. Because we believe our current count of seven 2025 cases is an undercount, year one will establish a complete baseline using delivery-hospital data flowing through our new transfer agreement; reduction targets apply from that corrected figure.
- *Objective 3.2:* By year two, 100 percent of patients with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are enrolled in a home blood pressure monitoring protocol with CHW support.
- *Objective 3.3:* By year three, at least 250 patients per year receive continuous doula labor support, prioritized by clinical and social risk.

**Goal 4: Low birth weight declines.**
- *Objective 4.1:* Reduce the low birth weight rate among our patients' deliveries from 8.9 percent to 7.4 percent — the current state rate — by year five (interim: 8.6, 8.3, 8.0, 7.7 percent).
- *Objective 4.2:* By year three, at least 200 patients per year participate in group prenatal care, which carries documented effects on preterm birth in comparable populations.

**Goal 5: Cost per prenatal episode declines.**
- *Objective 5.1:* Reduce average cost per prenatal episode from $2,410 to $2,150 or below by year five, in constant dollars, through earlier entry, reduced no-shows, and reduced avoidable emergency department use.
- *Objective 5.2:* Reduce the prenatal no-show rate from 19 percent to 10 percent by year four.
- *Objective 5.3:* Reduce pregnancy-related emergency department visits for ambulatory-care-sensitive conditions by 30 percent by year five, measured through claims and hospital data.

## Evaluation Plan

The evaluation is designed to answer three questions: Are the Trust's five outcomes moving? Which project components are driving movement? And is the model reproducible for other rural health centers losing obstetric capacity?

**Design.** We will use a pre/post design with annual measurement against the 2025 baseline, supplemented by comparison to statewide Nebraska rates (which serve as a contemporaneous benchmark controlling for secular trends) and, where our external evaluator judges it feasible, comparison to a matched panel of Nebraska FQHCs drawn from public UDS data. Interrupted time-series analysis of monthly registry data will let us associate outcome inflections with component launch dates — evening hours in month four, full CHW certification in month nine, doula ramp-up in year one — giving the Trust component-level attribution rather than a single aggregate claim.

**Data sources and measurement.** First-trimester entry, postpartum visit completion, no-show rates, and screening rates come from our Epic-based electronic health record and prenatal registry, which our informatics team will configure in the project's first ninety days with automated monthly dashboards. Low birth weight and severe maternal morbidity come from delivery records transmitted under our transfer agreement with Loup Valley Regional Medical Center, cross-checked annually against Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services vital statistics files — a redundancy that specifically corrects the undercounting problem in our current SMM figures. Cost per prenatal episode is calculated by CFO Bao Tran's team using our existing cost-accounting methodology, held constant across the project period and inflation-adjusted, incorporating visit costs, CHW and doula service costs, and attributable emergency department utilization from claims data.

**External evaluator.** We will contract with the University of Nebraska Medical Center College of Public Health's Center for Reducing Health Disparities as external evaluator, at $40,000 per year. The evaluator will validate our measurement methodology in year one, conduct the annual outcome analyses independently of program staff, lead qualitative work — annual focus groups with patients in Spanish and Somali, and structured interviews with CHWs, doulas, midwives, and Loup Valley staff — and author the final evaluation report.

**Process measures.** Alongside outcomes, we will track monthly: prenatal visits delivered in evening hours; CHW caseloads, contacts, and outreach-attributed enrollments; doula-supported deliveries; days from positive pregnancy test to first visit; interpreter-supported delivery-hospital encounters; and midwife privileging status. Process data feed a quarterly management review chaired by Director of Women's Health Yesenia Padilla, so that underperforming components are corrected within the quarter rather than discovered in an annual report.

**Reporting.** The Trust will receive annual reports presenting all five scored measures against baseline and interim targets, component-level analysis, patient-voice findings, and a candid account of what has not worked. Chief Medical Officer Karin Lindholm will present findings annually to our patient-majority board, and de-identified results will be shared with the Nebraska Perinatal Quality Improvement Collaborative so that other centers facing obstetric closures can use what we learn.

## Organizational Capacity

Platte Meridian Community Health has operated as a federally qualified health center for over two decades and today runs six sites, employs 214 staff, and manages a $41 million budget with an operating margin that has been positive in each of the past five fiscal years. We provide primary medical, dental, behavioral health, and pharmacy services; we have grown our prenatal program from 610 to 1,180 patients since 2021 without external maternal-health grant support, which is both the source of the strain this proposal addresses and evidence that this organization absorbs growth and keeps functioning. Our workforce reflects our patients: 58 percent of clinical support staff are bilingual, and our board — a patient majority, as FQHC governance requires — includes members of the Hispanic, Somali, and Sudanese communities this project serves.

**Project leadership.** Chief Executive Officer Oluwaseun Adebayo, who has led the organization for nine years, and Director of Women's Health Yesenia Padilla are the project's co-designers; Ms. Padilla, a bilingual family nurse practitioner who built our current prenatal program, will direct daily operations at 0.5 FTE. Chief Medical Officer Karin Lindholm, MD, oversees clinical quality, credentialing, and the hospital relationship. Chief Financial Officer Bao Tran manages the cost-accounting and sustainability modeling described below. Grants Director Elias Mwangi, who administers our current portfolio of federal and philanthropic awards — including Section 330 grant funds under HRSA's compliance framework — will manage reporting, subcontracts, and Trust communications. This team has jointly managed awards of comparable scale, and our finance office produces clean single audits; our most recent audit contained no findings.

**The 2025 HRSA site visit.** The Trust should hear about our compliance record directly from us. HRSA's 2025 operational site visit resulted in two conditions on our Health Center Program award. The first concerned credentialing and privileging: files for a subset of licensed independent practitioners lacked complete primary-source verification documentation at the time of the visit. Dr. Lindholm led the corrective action — a full re-verification of all provider files, a new credentialing tracking system with automated expiration alerts, and delegation of primary-source verification to a credentialing verification organization. HRSA reviewed the corrective evidence and closed that condition in March 2026. The second condition, concerning sliding fee discount program documentation, remains open. The finding was that income verification documents supporting sliding fee eligibility determinations were inconsistently retained across sites; patients were charged correctly, but our files did not uniformly prove it. Our corrective action plan — a standardized eligibility documentation workflow, retraining of all front-desk and eligibility staff, and a monthly ten-file-per-site internal audit that has run since April 2026 with results reported to the board's finance committee — has been submitted to HRSA, and our monthly audits since June have shown documentation completeness above 95 percent. We expect closure at HRSA's follow-up review this winter, and we will notify the Trust when the condition closes. We raise this unprompted for two reasons: because the Trust's due diligence will find it, and because the sliding fee correction is directly relevant to this project — the same disciplined eligibility documentation now in place is what our CHWs will use when enrolling uninsured prenatal patients, and it strengthens rather than threatens the project's operations.

**Clinical and operational infrastructure.** Our Epic electronic health record, shared instance with Loup Valley Regional Medical Center's health information exchange participation, supports the registry and data flows the evaluation requires. Our facilities at the three evening-hours sites require no capital modification. We hold current 340B pharmacy participation, which lowers medication costs for hypertensive and diabetic prenatal patients. Our human resources team has recruited successfully in a difficult rural clinical labor market, most recently filling two family medicine vacancies in under seven months using National Health Service Corps eligibility, which extends to certified nurse-midwives at our sites and materially strengthens our midwife recruitment position.

**Partnerships as they stand.** Executed agreements: patient transfer and records-sharing agreement with Loup Valley Regional Medical Center; CHW training agreement with Central Community College; letters of support from the county WIC program, two community churches, the Islamic center serving our Somali patients, and Loup Valley's chief executive. In negotiation: the doula services contract with Prairie Doula Collective (terms agreed, execution contingent on award) and a consultative high-risk co-management arrangement with the successor entity to Prairie Women's Health, which we pursue but do not depend on, for the reasons stated in the project description.

## Sustainability

The question the Trust is right to ask is what happens in year six, and the honest answer differs by component, so we address each on its own terms. CFO Bao Tran and Grants Director Elias Mwangi built the sustainability model below, which is attached as a five-year pro forma.

**The midwives sustain themselves.** As an FQHC, Platte Meridian is reimbursed for billable encounters at our Medicaid prospective payment system rate. Each midwife at mature productivity — approximately 1,700 billable encounters annually — generates patient service revenue that covers her fully loaded cost from a payer mix matching our prenatal population, in which Medicaid coverage of pregnant women is broad even where our general uninsured rate is 27 percent. Our model shows the two midwife positions reaching revenue neutrality in year three and contributing positive margin thereafter; Trust funds carry the ramp-up years and the unbillable time midwives spend at the delivery hospital, in group-care facilitation, and in evening-access administration.

**The community health workers: a phased path to billing.** The CHW positions are the component most often orphaned when grants end, and our plan for them is specific. Nebraska Medicaid began reimbursing community health worker services in 2024, covering health education, navigation, and self-management support delivered by certified CHWs under a supervising billing provider. Our four CHWs will complete the Central Community College certificate program in year one and obtain the certification Nebraska Medicaid requires. Beginning in year two, we will bill Medicaid for eligible CHW services delivered to covered patients, and Mr. Tran's team will track collections against cost quarterly. Our model — using conservative assumptions of 60 percent of CHW patient-facing time spent with Medicaid-covered patients and current Nebraska CHW reimbursement rates — projects CHW billing revenue covering 35 percent of CHW costs in year two, 55 percent in year four, and 70 percent by year five. The remaining 30 percent in year six and beyond is committed in two parts: first, the cost-per-episode savings this project is designed to produce — our target reduction of $260 per episode across roughly 1,180 episodes represents approximately $300,000 in annual avoided cost, a portion of which accrues to our own bottom line through reduced no-shows and uncompensated acute care; second, a board-approved commitment, documented in the attached resolution of intent, to absorb the residual CHW cost into the operating budget as a permanent program, contingent on the positions demonstrating the outcome and revenue performance in this plan. The Trust's five years buy the certification, the billing infrastructure, and the evidence; Medicaid and our own operations carry the positions afterward.

**The doula contract and evening hours.** Nebraska Medicaid does not currently reimburse doula services. We will participate in the state advocacy effort seeking coverage, as a growing number of states have adopted it, but our sustainability plan does not assume it: in year five we will right-size the doula contract to a risk-prioritized cohort fundable through a combination of any Medicaid coverage then available, targeted philanthropy for which Mr. Mwangi has identified three Nebraska funders with maternal-health interests, and operating margin from the midwifery service. Evening hours, once established, cost only staffing differentials against fully billable visits and sustain themselves; the model shows evening sessions revenue-positive from year two.

**Institutional sustainability.** Beyond financing, the project leaves permanent assets: privileged clinicians at the delivery hospital, an executed transfer agreement, a certified bilingual CHW workforce with a local training pipeline at Central Community College, a prenatal registry and dashboard infrastructure, and an evaluated model documented for replication. Those do not expire with the grant.

## Budget Narrative

Platte Meridian requests $3,750,000 over five years, an average of $750,000 annually, allocated as follows. Figures are year-one amounts; out-year adjustments reflect three percent annual cost inflation offset by the phase-down of Trust support for CHW positions as Medicaid billing revenue phases in, per the sustainability plan.

**Personnel — $556,700 in year one.** Two certified nurse-midwives at $118,000 salary each plus 28 percent fringe ($302,080 total): the full cost is Trust-funded in year one, declining to 60 percent in year three and 25 percent in year five as encounter revenue matures, with the difference reallocated within the annual budget to the transition reserve described below. Four community health workers at $46,000 salary each plus fringe ($235,520 total): Trust-funded at 100 percent in year one, stepping down to 65 percent in year two, 55 percent in year three, 45 percent in year four, and 30 percent in year five as Nebraska Medicaid CHW billing revenue replaces grant support on the schedule in our pro forma. Project direction: 0.5 FTE of Director of Women's Health Yesenia Padilla is provided as organizational match and is not charged to the Trust, as are the time of the CEO, CMO, CFO, and Grants Director — a combined in-kind contribution valued at approximately $145,000 annually. A 0.5 FTE bilingual project coordinator handling scheduling, data entry, and doula logistics is charged at $27,500 plus fringe.

**Contractual — $128,000 in year one.** The doula services contract with Prairie Doula Collective is budgeted at $85,000 in year one (ramp-up to approximately 150 supported deliveries) and $102,000 annually in years two through five (250 deliveries at a blended rate of approximately $400 per supported birth including two postpartum home visits). The external evaluation contract with the University of Nebraska Medical Center is $40,000 annually. Interpretation services for delivery-hospital encounters beyond our staff capacity: $3,000.

**Training and certification — $24,500 in year one.** Central Community College CHW certificate tuition and materials for four staff ($14,000, year one only); CenteringPregnancy facilitator training and site licensing for the midwifery team ($8,500, year one only); ongoing continuing education for CHWs and doula coordination in subsequent years at $6,000 annually.

**Evening hours operations — $58,000 annually.** Shift differentials and extended-hours support staffing (nursing and front desk) at three sites, two evenings per week, at $52,000; incremental facility operating costs at $6,000. Provider time during evening sessions is not charged here — it is within the midwife positions above — so this line funds only the marginal cost of keeping doors open, against which evening visits generate billable revenue from year one.

**Supplies, equipment, and patient support — $37,000 in year one.** Home blood pressure monitoring cuffs and validated devices for the hypertension protocol ($16,000 in year one, $8,000 in replacement years); patient transportation assistance — gas cards and rideshare vouchers for the 38-mile corridor, administered by CHWs under a documented policy ($15,000 annually); outreach and patient education materials in Spanish, Somali, and Arabic ($6,000).

**Data systems — $21,000 in year one.** Epic registry configuration and dashboard build (internal informatics staff time charged at cost, $15,000, year one; $5,000 maintenance thereafter); Nebraska vital statistics data linkage fees ($6,000 annually).

**Indirect costs.** We apply a 10 percent de minimis-style rate on direct costs, approximately $75,000 in year one, well below our federally negotiated rate of 18.4 percent; the difference is a further organizational contribution to the project.

**Five-year shape.** Year one totals approximately $900,200, front-loaded for recruitment, training, and systems; years two and three approximately $780,000 and $755,000; years four and five approximately $690,000 and $625,000 as CHW billing revenue and midwife encounter revenue replace grant support. Underspending generated by the personnel phase-down schedule is directed, with Trust approval, to a sustainability transition reserve capped at $100,000 to cushion the year-six absorption of residual CHW costs into operations. Total request: $3,750,000. Total organizational contribution across five years — leadership time, indirect rate differential, and midwife revenue reinvestment — exceeds $1.1 million, which we believe is the right signal of what this project is: not a grant-funded program that ends, but a five-year transition, underwritten by the Ansel Brack Trust, to a maternal health service our patients keep.
