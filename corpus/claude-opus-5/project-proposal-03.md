## 1. Executive Summary

Prairie Gate's service performance has fallen from 96.1 percent on-time to 87.4 percent in eight quarters. The decline is not evenly distributed. Our Kansas, Nebraska, and Missouri lanes run 93.1 percent. Our Oklahoma lanes run 79.3 percent. Substantially all of the network-level erosion originates in a southern operating footprint that was designed in 1994 for a freight base that no longer exists and that now sorts Oklahoma City metropolitan freight in two small terminals located 84 and 96 miles from the customers who receive it.

Kesterson Ag Supply, which represents 16 percent of consolidated revenue — $49.6 million — placed Prairie Gate on a 90-day service watch on December 8. Their contract expires June 30. Their measured on-time performance on our network is 81.2 percent against a 95 percent contractual standard. Three additional accounts representing $18.3 million have issued formal service notices in the last two quarters.

We propose a $38.5 million rebuild of the southern network:

- Construction of a 112-door cross-dock and yard on a 34-acre site in the I-40/Douglas Boulevard corridor in Oklahoma City, replacing our capacity-constrained 44-door leased facility;
- Closure of the Ponca City and Enid terminals;
- A re-cut of 31 linehaul lanes, removing an estimated 1.86 million annual miles and 18 daily departures from the southern operating plan;
- Execution over 22 months, from board approval in February 2026 to full run rate in the first quarter of 2028.

Financing is a $30 million equipment-backed term loan arranged by the CFO plus $8.5 million of balance-sheet cash. At full run rate the plan produces $9.4 million of annual operating improvement against a simple payback of 4.1 years, an unlevered IRR of 17.8 percent over ten years, and an NPV of $18.4 million at our 9.5 percent weighted average cost of capital. It also protects a revenue base we do not believe we can defend inside the current footprint.

We want to be direct with the committee about two things.

**First, this project does not save the Kesterson contract by June 30.** No capital project can. A parallel operating bridge — described in Section 6.1 and funded at $1.4 million from the FY2026 operating budget, not from this request — is what has to hold service between now and midyear. What this proposal does is give Prairie Gate something credible to put in front of Kesterson's supply chain committee in March: a funded, board-approved, dated plan that ends the structural cause of the failures rather than another apology and another expedite allowance.

**Second, we are aware that this committee killed a smaller version of this plan in 2023, and why.** The Springfield dock expansion closed 41 percent over its $8.9 million authorization. That overrun was a failure of project governance, not of engineering judgment, and Section 9 sets out the specific controls — staged capital release, guaranteed maximum price at 90 percent design rather than 40 percent, an independent owner's representative, and a contingency the committee itself releases — that we have built into this request to prevent a repetition. We are asking the committee to approve a project management regime as much as a project.

## 2. The Problem

### 2.1 Service

Prairie Gate operates 11 terminals, 640 tractors, and 1,850 employees against $310 million of revenue. Network on-time performance by quarter:

| Quarter | Network | Northern lanes | Southern lanes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q4 FY2023 | 96.1% | 96.8% | 94.4% |
| Q4 FY2024 | 92.0% | 95.1% | 86.2% |
| Q2 FY2025 | 89.7% | 94.0% | 82.1% |
| Q4 FY2025 | 87.4% | 93.1% | 79.3% |

The southern region — Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Ponca City, and Enid, plus the southbound half of Wichita and Salina — carries 33.5 percent of network tonnage and generated $104 million of revenue in FY2025 at a 103.6 operating ratio. The rest of the network ran 96.8.

Three operating measures explain most of the gap.

**Handles per shipment.** Network standard is 1.9. Southern actual is 2.7. A shipment tendered in Wichita for delivery in Norman is cross-docked at Wichita, moved to Enid or Ponca City for regional sort, then trucked to Oklahoma City for final dock assignment before it reaches a delivery route. Every handle adds four to nine hours of dwell and a claims exposure.

**Empty miles.** Southern empty mileage is 17.4 percent against a network figure of 11.2 percent. The Ponca City and Enid terminals do not generate enough outbound tonnage to balance the inbound linehaul that must reach them, and the lane structure that connects them to Wichita, Tulsa, and Oklahoma City is a triangle in which one leg is nearly always light.

**Purchased transportation.** Outside cartage and expedite spend in the southern region grew from $2.1 million in FY2023 to $6.8 million in FY2025. This is the cost of buying our way out of service failures one shipment at a time, and it has become the single fastest-growing line in the operating statement.

The Oklahoma City terminal itself is the binding constraint. It was leased in 2011 with 44 doors and a design capacity of approximately 620 shipments per day. It handled an average of 1,140 shipments per day in FY2025. Trailers stage on the street. The yard holds 84 trailer positions against a peak requirement of 210. Average trailer dwell at Ponca City, which absorbs the overflow, is 11.4 hours.

### 2.2 Customers

Kesterson Ag Supply moves seed, crop protection chemicals, fertilizer components, irrigation equipment, and replacement parts through 14 of our terminals, with the heaviest density on Wichita–Oklahoma City–Tulsa and the Enid agricultural corridor. Their business is intensely seasonal, and our worst service months are their most important ones.

The December 8 service watch letter specifies 95 percent on-time measured monthly, a claims ratio below 0.60 percent, and an electronic delivery confirmation compliance rate above 99 percent, with a review on March 9 and a contract decision by April 30 ahead of the June 30 expiration. In Q4 FY2025 we recorded 96 late-delivery incidents on Kesterson freight, of which 71 originated in the southern region.

We estimate the direct contribution margin at risk on the Kesterson account at $6.9 million annually. The indirect exposure is larger: Kesterson's regional distribution footprint anchors density on nine of our southern lanes, and losing it would raise unit cost on freight we keep.

### 2.3 Drivers

Network driver turnover is 94 percent. Southern region turnover is 118 percent. Enid, our worst domicile, ran 141 percent in FY2025. At a fully loaded recruiting, onboarding, and orientation cost of $6,300 per driver, FY2025 driver acquisition spend was $8.1 million.

Turnover in the southern region is not primarily a pay problem. Exit interviews conducted between March and November 2025 (n=143) cite unpredictable dispatch and forced layover as the leading and second-leading causes, together accounting for 61 percent of stated reasons. Twenty-two of our 47 southern linehaul lanes currently require an overnight layover because the terminal spacing does not permit a legal same-day turn. Nine of those lanes become same-day turns under the proposed lane structure. This is the most direct retention lever available to us, and it is available only through geography.

### 3.3 The lane re-cut

The current southern operating plan runs 47 lanes and 84 daily linehaul departures with Wichita as the sole breakbulk. Under the proposal, Oklahoma City becomes the second breakbulk, and 31 lanes are re-cut:

- **11 lanes eliminated.** Principally the Wichita–Enid, Wichita–Ponca City, Enid–Oklahoma City, Ponca City–Tulsa, and Enid–Tulsa relays, which exist only to feed the closing terminals.
- **14 lanes redirected.** Freight now moving Wichita → Enid → Oklahoma City or Wichita → Ponca City → Tulsa moves Wichita → Oklahoma City direct, with Oklahoma City sorting for Norman, Lawton, Ardmore, Woodward, Enid, Ponca City, Stillwater, and the Tulsa relay.
- **6 lanes created.** Direct loading Oklahoma City → Tulsa (two departures), Oklahoma City → Wichita (two departures), Oklahoma City → Springfield (one), and an Oklahoma City → Amarillo partner interchange (one).

Modeled effect at full run rate: 66 daily southern departures (down 18), 1.86 million annual miles removed, empty mileage down from 17.4 to 11.9 percent, handles per shipment down from 2.7 to 2.0, and average southern transit time reduced by 0.8 days on 41 percent of shipments.

Nine layover lanes convert to same-day turns, returning 14 drivers to daily home time.

## 4. Why Now

Four reasons, in order of force.

**The Kesterson contract expires June 30.** We will be in front of their supply chain committee on March 9 and again in April. We have made and missed service commitments to this account in each of the last five quarters. What we have never done is show them a funded structural fix with a board authorization behind it. A February 12 approval lets us do that in March. A deferral to the April or June board meeting does not.

**The Oklahoma City lease expires in October 2027.** The landlord has signaled a renewal at $8.95 per square foot NNN, up from $5.40, on a five-year minimum term. Renewal would commit us to the constrained building through 2032 at $1.1 million of incremental annual rent for a facility that is already 84 percent over design capacity.

**Construction cost and schedule.** Our design-build partner's non-binding estimate carries a 90-day validity and assumes an October 2026 construction start. Oklahoma City industrial construction cost escalation ran 6.8 percent in 2025. A six-month deferral is worth approximately $1.4 million in escalation alone, before considering a second winter in the schedule.

**Compounding service damage.** Our southern account retention rate fell from 94.2 percent to 88.1 percent in FY2025. Freight that leaves an LTL network for service reasons does not return quickly, and the density loss makes the remaining freight more expensive to move, which degrades service further. We are in the early phase of that spiral and can still arrest it.

## 6. Phasing, and How Freight Keeps Moving

### 6.1 The bridge — now through Month 20

Nothing in the capital plan improves service before the fourth quarter of 2027. The bridge program, approved by the CEO on January 14 and funded at $1.4 million within existing FY2026 operating authority, is what carries us:

- **Interim overflow space.** A 35,000 sq ft, 18-door short-term lease on SE 29th Street in Oklahoma City, executed on a 24-month term with a six-month exit, relieving the yard and door constraint at the current terminal. Occupancy targeted March 15.
- **Kesterson service cell.** A four-person dedicated desk in Wichita with proactive tender confirmation, exception escalation within 45 minutes, and a weekly scorecard delivered to Kesterson's logistics director. Live February 2.
- **Second night sort at Wichita.** Adds 11 dock positions Tuesday through Friday, pulling the southern sort forward by 2.5 hours. Live February 16.
- **Trailer lease.** Forty additional 53-foot dry vans on a 24-month operating lease to break the equipment shortage that is forcing partial loads.
- **Southern driver pay adjustment.** A $0.04 per mile increase on the 22 layover lanes and a $75 per-night layover premium, effective February 1, held until the lanes convert.

Bridge performance targets: southern on-time to 86 percent by March 31 and 90 percent by June 30; Kesterson-specific on-time to 91 percent by March 31. We will report against these to the committee monthly. If we do not hit the March numbers, the Kesterson conversation changes materially, and we will say so.

### 6.3 Cutover mechanics

The cutover is the highest-risk 60 days in this plan, and freight moves through it under four rules.

**Rule 1 — Sequence, never simultaneity.** Oklahoma City relocates first, into a building with excess capacity and no volume change. Ponca City closes two weeks later. Enid closes four weeks after that. At no point are two sorts changing in the same week.

**Rule 2 — Overlap, don't switch.** The current Oklahoma City terminal is held for 21 days past the relocation date as a live overflow dock. Ponca City and Enid each run 10 days of parallel operation with reduced volume, with freight diverted progressively — 25 percent, 50 percent, 100 percent — over the closure window, not at a single cut.

**Rule 3 — Waves, not a big bang, on the lane re-cut.** The 31 lanes convert in three waves of 11, 12, and 8 lanes, one week apart, sequenced from lowest to highest customer sensitivity. Kesterson-density lanes are in Wave C and convert last, after two weeks of demonstrated stability on the earlier waves.

**Rule 4 — A defined stop.** Each wave has a published rollback procedure, a designated rollback decision-maker (the VP of Network Planning), and a service floor. If southern weekly on-time drops below 84 percent, the next wave is suspended and the prior wave is evaluated for rollback. Rollback capability is preserved through Month 24 by holding the Enid lease space (which we are paying for regardless) and deferring the equipment disposition at Ponca City.

Cutover occurs in October and November deliberately. It is the lowest-volume window in the agricultural freight year, after fall application and before spring pre-buy. Cutting over in March or April would put us into Kesterson's peak.

WARN Act notice will be issued at Month 18 for both sites. Both closures independently trigger notice; there is no aggregation question.

### 7.3 Hiring in Oklahoma City

Fifty-seven external hires — approximately 34 dock, 15 city drivers, 5 clerical, 3 supervisory — against a metropolitan labor market with 3.7 percent unemployment and known transportation-sector competition from three national LTL carriers and two parcel operations. We treat this as a real risk, not a formality. The hiring plan begins at Month 15, five months before opening, with a $2,500 sign-on bonus, a wage band set at the 65th percentile of the Oklahoma City market survey (approximately 6 percent above our current Oklahoma City rates), and two 20-person training classes run at the interim facility before the new building opens. Training cost is carried in operating expense, not this request.

### 7.4 Labor relations

**The facts.** Teamsters organizers have been present at the Salina and Enid docks since March 2025. As of this writing no representation petition has been filed at either location, no authorization card showing has been demonstrated to us, and Prairie Gate has no collective bargaining relationship at any facility.

**The exposure.** Closing a facility during an active organizing campaign creates a real, foreseeable risk of an unfair labor practice charge under Section 8(a)(3), and the *Darlington* line of authority governing partial closings. The legal test turns on motive: a closing motivated by a purpose to chill unionism elsewhere in the enterprise is unlawful, even where an employer may lawfully close entirely.

**Why we believe the record supports us, and how we intend to keep it that way.**

The Southern Network Study that produced this proposal was commissioned by the VP of Network Planning in June 2024 and presented to the executive team on November 19, 2024 — four months before any organizing activity was observed at Enid. The prior version of this plan was brought to and rejected by this committee in 2023. The business case rests on documented service, mileage, and cost data that has nothing to do with union activity, and the same consolidation logic applies at Ponca City, where there has been no organizing activity at all. That contemporaneous record is our principal asset, and it is why counsel has advised that the study documentation, the 2023 board materials, and this proposal be preserved and indexed now.

**Controls.** Outside labor counsel (Barrow Hendriks LLP) has been engaged and has reviewed this document. Supervisor guidance and a communication protocol have been issued for both Salina and Enid; no manager will discuss the campaign in connection with the closure or the reverse. All closure communications will be reviewed by counsel before release. Salina is not affected by this plan in any respect, and we will say so plainly and in writing at the time of announcement, because the inference the law is concerned with is exactly the one an ambiguous announcement would invite. Budget carries $150,000 for counsel; the risk register in Section 10 carries a contingent exposure estimate.

We note for the committee that deferring or abandoning a soundly-supported business decision because a union campaign is underway does not eliminate this risk and may create others. Counsel's advice is to proceed on the documented business case, on the documented timeline, with disciplined communications.

## 8. Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Capital / cost | Service outcome | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Repair service inside current footprint** | $3.8M opex/yr | Southern on-time ceiling ~91% | Rejected |
| **B. Lease existing OKC building (Council Road)** | $9.4M capital + $47.6M lease NPV | Adequate to ~FY2029 | Rejected |
| **C. Build 84 doors instead of 112** | $34.4M | Adequate to ~FY2029 | Rejected |
| **D. Third-party cross-dock partner in OKC** | Variable, ~$4.1M/yr | Unpredictable | Rejected |
| **E. Exit the Oklahoma market** | Net proceeds ~$6M | N/A | Rejected |
| **F. Proposed rebuild** | $38.5M | 95%+ sustained | **Recommended** |

**A — Repair inside the current footprint.** We modeled a package of additional dock labor, a third shift at Enid, 60 additional trailers, expanded dispatch staffing, and a permanent southern pay premium: $3.8 million of annual operating cost, in perpetuity, for a modeled southern on-time ceiling of approximately 91 percent. The ceiling exists because the constraint is geometric. No amount of labor removes the extra handle imposed by sorting Oklahoma City freight in Enid, and no amount of labor creates dock doors at a 44-door building running 1,140 shipments a day. Ninety-one percent does not satisfy Kesterson's 95 percent standard. This alternative spends $38 million over ten years and does not solve the problem.

**B — Lease an existing Oklahoma City building.** We evaluated two candidates. The stronger is a 78-door former grocery distribution facility on Council Road, available Q3 2026 on a 15-year term at $6.10 per square foot NNN with a $4.1 million tenant improvement allowance. Total capital requirement including racking removal, dock conversion, yard improvements, and equipment is $9.4 million — $29 million less than the proposal, which is a serious advantage and the reason this alternative received the most analysis.

It fails on three counts. First, doors: 78 against a modeled FY2030 peak requirement of 96, with no expansion right. We would be capacity-constrained again by FY2029, in a building we could not leave until 2041. Second, yard: 7.2 usable acres against an 18-acre requirement; the parcel is landlocked by a rail spur and a retention pond, and the trailer position count of 132 is below our current peak need of 210. Third, cost: the 15-year lease has an NPV of $47.6 million at our discount rate, against $26 million of owned facility with a residual. On a total-cost-of-occupancy basis over 15 years the lease is $13.8 million more expensive, and it delivers a facility that does not fit.

A second candidate on SE 29th Street has 64 doors and worse yard geometry; we have taken 35,000 square feet of it as the bridge overflow space described in Section 6.1, which is the right use for it.

**C — Build smaller.** An 84-door design saves $4.1 million and reaches modeled capacity in FY2029. Adding doors to an operating cross-dock costs roughly 2.3 times the marginal cost of building them initially and requires taking dock capacity out of service to do it. Rejected on the same reasoning as B: we would be solving this problem twice.

**D — Third-party cross-dock partner.** Variable cost, no capital, and fast. We attempted this structure at Tulsa in 2022 with Meridian Handling Group. Service was 83 percent on-time, we had no visibility into freight in their building, claims ran 2.1 percent, and we exited after 14 months. The failure mode was not the vendor's competence; it was that service accountability cannot be outsourced when the customer holds us to a 95 percent standard.

**E — Exit Oklahoma.** Modeled for completeness. Oklahoma is 22 percent of revenue and the anchor of the Kesterson relationship, which extends into Kansas. Exit forfeits the account and strands cost across the remaining network, raising network operating ratio by an estimated 2.4 points. Rejected.
