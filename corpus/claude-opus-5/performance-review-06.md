## 1.1 Position Summary and Review Context

Ms. Manzanares supervises the utility billing section: nine authorized positions (seven filled), 34,000 active accounts, four billing cycles per month, and approximately $61 million in annual billed revenue. During this review period the section absorbed the single largest operational change in its history — the September 2025 cutover from the legacy mainframe customer information system to the Cascade CIS platform — while operating two positions short under Budget Directive 25-04 (hiring freeze, now in its fourteenth month).

Any fair reading of this year has to hold two facts at once. The conversion's first billing cycle was a public failure. The recovery from it was, in my judgment, the strongest sustained supervisory work performed anywhere in this utility during the review period.

## 1.2 Goals Established for FY 2026 and Results

**Goal 1 — Complete CIS conversion with a first-cycle exception rate at or below 2 percent.**
*Not met.* The October 2, 2025 cycle misbilled 4,100 of 34,000 accounts — a 12.1 percent exception rate. Root causes documented in the November 14, 2025 post-implementation report: (a) rate-table mapping errors on 2,880 irrigation-only and mixed-use meters, (b) failure of the vendor's proration routine on the short cutover cycle, affecting 890 accounts, and (c) sub-account consolidation errors on 330 multi-family premises. Responsibility for items (a) and (b) rests substantially with the vendor and with the project's testing scope, which was set by a steering committee Ms. Manzanares sat on but did not chair. Responsibility for item (c) is hers; the multi-family mapping was validated by her staff and signed off on September 12.

**Goal 2 — Stabilize billing accuracy within two cycles of go-live.**
*Exceeded.* Exception rates fell to 0.9 percent (November), 0.4 percent (December), and have held at or below 0.15 percent since January, against a pre-conversion baseline of 0.31 percent. All 4,100 affected accounts were re-billed or adjusted by November 21, 2025. She personally designed the tiered remediation: automatic credit-hold, no penalties or disconnections on affected accounts through December 31, and interest-free repayment terms of up to twelve months on under-billed balances. Of $412,000 in net under-billings identified, $377,000 has been recovered or is current on a payment plan; $22,400 was written off under the adjustment authority in Ordinance 2019-77; $12,600 remains in active collection.

**Goal 3 — Reduce call abandonment to below 15 percent.**
*Exceeded.* Abandonment fell from 31 percent (October 2025 peak) to 9 percent (June 2026); the twelve-month average is 16 percent, weighted heavily by the crisis months. Average speed of answer improved from 6:12 to 1:48. She achieved this with fewer people, by redesigning the queue, publishing a self-service adjustment form, and taking calls herself during the December and January cycles — payroll records show 96 hours of her own time on the phones.

**Goal 4 — Increase e-billing/portal enrollment to 55 percent of accounts.**
*Not met.* Enrollment is 47 percent, up from 39 percent. The campaign was suspended from October through February so that outbound messaging would not compound customer confusion. That was the right call and I approved it; the goal is carried forward.

**Goal 5 — Absorb Billing Clerk II vacancies (positions 4412 and 4419) without service degradation.**
*Met.* Both positions have been held vacant under the freeze since May 2025 and September 2025 respectively. She restructured cycle work into a four-team rotation, moved delinquency processing to a batch model, and eliminated two redundant reconciliation reports. Section productivity is 4,857 accounts per filled FTE, against a benchmarked norm of roughly 3,600.

**Goal 6 — Complete written desk procedures for all section functions.**
*Partially met.* Eleven of sixteen procedures are complete and current. Five — bankruptcy handling, commercial deposits, backflow billing, lien processing, and the annual write-off packet — remain in draft. This is the second consecutive year this goal has slipped.
