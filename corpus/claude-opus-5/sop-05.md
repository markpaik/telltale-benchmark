## 1. Purpose

1.1 This procedure establishes one uniform, district-wide method for receiving, dating, tracking, and completing initial special education referrals and evaluations in Northline Public Schools, from the moment a referral arrives through the development of an individualized education program (IEP) for a student found eligible.

1.2 This procedure exists because an April 2026 OSPI citizen complaint decision found that Northline exceeded the 35 school day initial evaluation timeline in 38 of 112 initial evaluations, with an average of 71 school days; that two middle schools maintained no referral log; and that prior written notice was absent from 22 student files. This procedure is the written procedure required by Corrective Action Item 1 and is a district directive, not guidance.

1.3 The procedure is written so that a staff member new to the district can execute each step without asking a question. Where a step requires judgment, the procedure states who exercises it, what the standard is, and what documentation results.

---

## 2. Scope

2.1 **In scope.** All initial evaluations for special education eligibility of students enrolled in, attending, or residing within Northline Public Schools boundaries, ages 3 through 21, including:

- students enrolled in Northline schools;
- students parentally placed in private schools located within district boundaries;
- students residing in the district but attending school elsewhere, or not attending school (homeschooled, unenrolled, expelled);
- students in transitional housing, foster care, or juvenile detention within district boundaries;
- students referred while a disciplinary proceeding is pending;
- students who transfer into Northline with an evaluation in progress.

2.2 **Out of scope.** Reevaluations (see SE-PRO-002), IEP revision for already-eligible students (SE-PRO-004), Section 504 evaluations (SE-PRO-011, owned by the Director of Student Services), Part C to Part B transitions at age 3 (SE-PRO-003, which incorporates this procedure by reference for the evaluation steps but has its own referral timeline), and independent educational evaluations (SE-PRO-009).

2.3 **Relationship to MTSS.** Northline's multi-tiered system of supports is a general education intervention framework. **Participation in, or the absence of, any tier of intervention is never a reason to delay or decline a referral.** A referral may be made at any time by any person listed in Section 7.1.2, regardless of intervention history.

## 3. Definitions

**Assessor.** A school psychologist, speech-language pathologist (SLP), occupational therapist, physical therapist, teacher of the deaf/hard of hearing, teacher of the visually impaired, special education teacher, school nurse, or contracted licensed professional assigned to administer one or more components of an evaluation.

**Business day.** Any day the district administrative office is open, excluding weekends and district-observed holidays. Used only where expressly stated.

**Calendar day.** Every day, including weekends, holidays, and breaks. Used for the 30-day IEP development window.

**Consent.** Written parental permission given after the parent has been fully informed, in the parent's native language or other mode of communication, of all information relevant to the activity for which consent is sought; the parent understands and agrees in writing; the consent describes the activity and lists the records to be released and to whom; and the parent understands the consent is voluntary and may be revoked at any time.

**Day 0.** The date a referral is received by any district employee at any Northline location or through any district-sponsored channel. Day 0 is a stamp, not a counted day; counting begins the next school day.

**Eligibility determination date.** The date the evaluation group meets and signs the eligibility determination (form SE-160). This date starts the 30 calendar day IEP clock.

**Evaluation group.** The group of qualified professionals and the parent who review evaluation data and determine eligibility.

**IEP system.** Northline's electronic special education document system of record for evaluation plans, evaluation reports, eligibility determinations, prior written notices, consents, and IEPs.

**Parent.** As defined in WAC 392-172A-01125: a biological or adoptive parent, foster parent, guardian, person acting in the place of a parent (grandparent, stepparent, or other relative with whom the child lives), a surrogate parent appointed under WAC 392-172A-05130, or the student once educational rights have transferred at age 18.

**Prior written notice (PWN).** Written notice, on form SE-115 (refusal) or SE-170 (proposal), given to the parent a reasonable time before the district proposes or refuses to initiate or change the identification, evaluation, educational placement, or provision of FAPE to the student, containing all seven content elements listed in Section 7.5.4.

**Referral.** A written or verbal request that the district evaluate a student for eligibility for special education, made by a parent, a district or agency employee, or another person with knowledge of the student. A request need not use the word "evaluation," "special education," "IEP," or form SE-100 to be a referral.

**Registrar.** The building-level classified employee responsible for enrollment records, including date-stamping and routing of referral documents; at buildings without a registrar title, the principal's designated office manager.

**School day.** Any day, including a partial day, that students are in attendance at school for instructional purposes, per the Board-adopted district calendar. Non-student days, teacher work days without student attendance, holidays, breaks, and emergency closure days are **not** school days. Summer school days are school days only for students actually enrolled in a summer program; for all other students, summer recess days are not school days.

**Skyward.** The district's student information system of record for demographics, enrollment, attendance, home language, discipline, and the referral log fields defined in Section 7.2.

**Timeline dashboard.** The single district-wide tracking instrument maintained by the records clerk under Section 7.12, showing every open referral and evaluation, its dates, its assigned staff, and its status color.

## 5. General Requirements

### 5.1 The two governing timelines

| Clock | Length | Starts | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral decision | **25 school days** | The school day after Day 0 (receipt of referral) | The date the district provides PWN proposing evaluation **and** requests consent, or PWN refusing to evaluate |
| Initial evaluation | **35 school days** | The school day after the date the registrar or Special Services date-stamps the parent's signed consent | The date the evaluation group meets, determines eligibility, and signs SE-160 |
| IEP development | **30 calendar days** | The eligibility determination date | The date the IEP meeting is held and the IEP is finalized |

### 5.2 Internal targets

Legal maximums are not operating targets. Northline staff work to these internal targets, and the dashboard flags them:

| Milestone | Internal target | Legal maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Referral logged in Skyward and dashboard | 1 school day | — |
| Referral review meeting held | Day 15 | — |
| PWN and consent packet delivered to parent | Day 20 | Day 25 |
| All assessments administered | Consent Day 25 | — |
| Draft evaluation report circulated to group | Consent Day 28 | — |
| Eligibility meeting held | Consent Day 32 | Consent Day 35 |
| IEP meeting held | Eligibility + 21 calendar days | Eligibility + 30 calendar days |

### 5.3 Counting rules

5.3.1 Day 0 is not counted. Counting begins on the next school day the student's school is in session.

5.3.2 If the receiving building is closed but the district is in session (e.g., a building-specific closure), counting follows the district calendar.

5.3.3 If a referral or consent is received after 4:00 p.m., on a non-school day, or during a break, Day 0 is the date of actual receipt; counting begins the next school day. **Receipt during a break does not move the receipt date forward.**

5.3.4 The records clerk computes all due dates in the dashboard from the district calendar. No staff member computes due dates by hand. If a staff member believes a computed date is wrong, they notify the records clerk the same day; the records clerk resolves it within one business day and documents the correction.

5.3.5 Emergency closures (snow, air quality, utility failure) are removed from the count by the records clerk within one business day of the closure, and the dashboard recalculates all affected due dates automatically. The records clerk notifies affected case managers of new due dates.

### 5.4 Language access

5.4.1 The student's home language and the parent's preferred spoken and written language are read from the Skyward Home Language Survey fields at referral logging and recorded on the dashboard. Approximately 9% of Northline referrals require interpretation across 33 home languages.

5.4.2 Any parent-facing document in Sections 7.4 through 7.10 — PWN, consent, meeting invitation, evaluation summary, eligibility determination, procedural safeguards notice — is provided in the parent's native language in writing where a written translation exists or can be produced. If a written translation cannot be produced within the timeline, the document is translated orally or by other mode of communication by a qualified interpreter, and the interpreter and the staff member sign form SE-118 (Documentation of Oral Translation) recording the date, language, document translated, and confirmation the parent understood.

5.4.3 Requests to Language Access Services are made at the moment a meeting is scheduled — never later than 5 business days before the meeting.

5.4.4 **Students, siblings, other family members, and untrained staff may not interpret.** If no qualified interpreter is available for a scheduled meeting, the meeting is rescheduled; the reason and the new date are documented in a contact log entry, and the case manager notifies the Director the same day so timeline impact can be assessed.

5.4.5 Language need never justifies exceeding a timeline. It justifies earlier scheduling.

### 5.5 Assessor capacity (2026–27)

5.5.1 Northline entered 2026–27 with 7 of 11 school psychologist positions filled. Until staffing is restored, the Director maintains an **assessment capacity plan** reviewed every Monday, consisting of: (a) a contracted-evaluator pool of licensed school psychologists under master services agreements, (b) cross-building assignment authority (Section 7.6.4), and (c) role reallocation, under which non-evaluation duties of school psychologists are reassigned or suspended by the Director in writing.

5.5.2 **Capacity is not a permitted reason to exceed the 35 school day timeline and may never be given to a parent as an explanation for delay.** A capacity shortfall is a district escalation event under Section 7.12.5, not a student-level delay.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Step 1 — Receiving a referral (Day 0)

7.1.1 **Any employee who receives a request that the district look into whether a student needs special education has received a referral.** The employee does not evaluate the merit of the request, does not ask whether interventions have been tried, and does not redirect the parent to a counselor, a team meeting, or a later date.

7.1.2 A referral may be made by: a parent; any district employee; a student age 18 or older; a physician, therapist, or other professional; an agency (DCYF, Head Start, ESD 112, juvenile court); a private school administrator or teacher; or any other person with knowledge of the student.

7.1.3 **Recognizing a referral.** Treat as a referral any communication in which a person asks for an evaluation, testing, an IEP, "special ed," a "child study," or "an assessment"; states that a student may have a disability affecting learning; or provides a diagnosis and asks what the school will do. When in doubt, log it as a referral. **Over-logging carries no penalty. Under-logging is a compliance failure.**

7.1.4 **Actions on receipt, same school day:**

a. Date-stamp the document with the physical stamp or, for email, print or export with the received timestamp visible. For verbal requests, complete SE-100 sections 1–4 in the requester's presence or by phone, read it back, and record the date and time of the request as Day 0.
b. Write the receiving employee's name and building on the document.
c. Hand or forward the document to the building registrar. If the registrar is absent, deliver it to the building administrator.
d. Tell the parent, in plain language: "I've recorded your request today. Someone from our team will contact you within five school days, and we will let you know our decision about testing no later than [date]." Do not promise or predict an outcome.

7.1.5 **Referrals received at the district office**, at a program site, or by a staff member from a building other than the student's are forwarded to the student's building registrar by end of the next business day, with the original date stamp intact. The date stamp is never changed on transfer.

7.1.6 **A referral is never returned, discarded, or held pending more information.** If required fields on SE-100 are blank, the registrar logs the referral and the building administrator obtains missing information within 5 school days without pausing the clock.

### 7.2 Step 2 — Logging (within 1 school day)

7.2.1 The registrar creates the Skyward referral record within one school day of Day 0, completing all of the following fields. Every field is mandatory; the record cannot be saved incomplete:

`Student ID` · `Legal name` · `DOB` · `Grade` · `Building` · `Referral received date (Day 0)` · `Receipt channel` · `Referral source name and relationship` · `Home language` · `Parent preferred language` · `Interpretation required (Y/N)` · `504 plan in place (Y/N)` · `Currently enrolled (Y/N)` · `Private/out-of-district (Y/N)` · `Disciplinary proceeding pending (Y/N)` · `Assigned building administrator` · `Assigned case manager` · `Referral decision due date (auto)` .

7.2.2 The registrar uploads the scanned source document to the student's IEP system record under "Referral — Source Document" the same school day.

7.2.3 The registrar emails the automatic SE-110 log entry to: the building administrator, the assigned school psychologist, the district records clerk, and the Special Education Program Supervisor for that level.

7.2.4 **Every building maintains a referral log. There are no exceptions and there is no alternative local method.** The log is the Skyward-generated report "SE Referral Log — Building," run and reviewed by the building administrator every Friday and initialed. Buildings do not keep paper logs, spreadsheets, or counselor-maintained lists as substitutes.

7.2.5 The records clerk verifies within one school day that each Skyward referral has a dashboard entry. Any mismatch is reported to the Director the same day.

### 7.3 Step 3 — Gathering information (Days 1–14)

7.3.1 Within **5 school days** of Day 0, the assigned case manager contacts the parent by phone (with interpreter if needed), documents the contact in the IEP system contact log, and: confirms receipt of the referral; explains the 25 school day decision and the meeting date; asks about vision, hearing, health, developmental and educational history, languages spoken in the home, and parent concerns; and mails or emails the procedural safeguards notice in the parent's language.

7.3.2 The building administrator requests, and the following staff supply within **5 school days**:

| Data | Supplied by |
|---|---|
| SE-105 Teacher Input form, work samples, curriculum-based measures | Each current general education teacher |
| Intervention records: what, how long, how often, by whom, with progress data | Interventionist / MTSS lead / general education teacher |
| Attendance and discipline history (3 years or length of enrollment) | Registrar |
| Vision and hearing screening results within the last 12 months; if none, screening scheduled within 10 school days | School nurse |
| Cumulative file review, prior school records, prior evaluations, 504 plan | Registrar |
| English language proficiency assessment results and instructional history | Multilingual Learner program |

7.3.3 Late data does not delay the referral review meeting. The meeting proceeds with what is available; missing data is noted in the minutes with the name of the person responsible and a date certain.

### 7.4 Step 4 — Referral review meeting and the 25 school day decision (by Day 15; hard deadline Day 25)

7.4.1 The building administrator schedules and chairs the referral review meeting no later than **Day 15**. Required participants: building administrator (chair), school psychologist or designated case manager, at least one current general education teacher, a special education teacher, and, for suspected communication needs, the SLP. The parent is invited in writing and by phone and is encouraged but not required to attend; the meeting is not postponed for parent non-attendance beyond one reschedule and never past Day 20.

7.4.2 The group reviews all information from Section 7.3 and answers two questions:

a. Is there reason to suspect the student has a disability under one or more of the eligibility categories?
b. Is there reason to suspect the student may, by reason of that disability, need special education and related services?

7.4.3 If the answer to either question is "possibly," the group recommends **evaluation**. The threshold is *suspicion*, not proof. The group does not require: a specific number of interventions, a specific duration of intervention, a medical diagnosis, a minimum attendance rate, or a completed 504 process.

7.4.4 The group also determines, and records on SE-125, the areas of suspected disability and the assessment areas required. Northline evaluates in all areas of suspected disability, and at minimum considers: health, vision, hearing, social and emotional status, general intelligence, academic performance, communicative status, motor abilities, and adaptive behavior. Any area considered and not assessed is listed with the reason.

7.4.5 The chair enters the decision, participants, and rationale in the IEP system meeting record the same school day, and updates the dashboard field `Referral decision date` and `Decision (Evaluate / Do not evaluate)`.

7.4.6 **The decision must be documented and communicated to the parent, with PWN, no later than school day 25.** The records clerk sends automated reminders at Day 15, Day 20, and Day 23 to the case manager, building administrator, and Program Supervisor. Any referral without a recorded decision at Day 23 is escalated to the Director that day.

### 7.5 Step 5 — Prior written notice and consent (by Day 25)

7.5.1 **Every decision produces a prior written notice.** A decision to evaluate produces SE-170. A decision not to evaluate produces SE-115. There is no referral outcome that does not generate a PWN. The absence of PWN was a finding in the April 2026 decision; the IEP system will not close a referral record without an uploaded, signed PWN.

7.5.2 **If evaluating:** the case manager prepares and delivers to the parent, within **2 school days** of the meeting and never later than Day 25:

a. SE-170 PWN describing the proposed evaluation;
b. SE-120 Consent for Initial Evaluation, listing every assessment area and instrument type;
c. SE-125 Evaluation Plan summary;
d. Procedural safeguards notice;
e. A cover letter stating that the evaluation will be completed within 35 school days of the district's receipt of signed consent, with a phone number and named contact.

Delivery method: hand delivery with signature, or mail plus email, or electronic signature through the IEP system parent portal. The delivery date and method are logged.

7.5.3 **If not evaluating:** only the Director of Special Education may approve and issue a refusal. The case manager submits the draft SE-115 to the Director within 2 school days of the meeting; the Director reviews and issues it no later than Day 25. The refusal describes what the parent may do if they disagree, including dispute resolution options, and offers a meeting to discuss the decision.

7.5.4 **Every PWN contains all seven elements**, and the IEP system validates their presence:

1. A description of the action proposed or refused;
2. An explanation of why the district proposes or refuses to take the action;
3. A description of each evaluation procedure, assessment, record, or report used as a basis for the action;
4. A statement that the parent has protection under procedural safeguards and how to obtain a copy;
5. Sources the parent may contact for help understanding the notice;
6. A description of other options the group considered and why they were rejected;
7. A description of other factors relevant to the district's proposal or refusal.

Written in language understandable to the general public, and in the parent's native language per Section 5.4.

7.5.5 **Obtaining consent.** If signed consent is not returned within 5 school days of delivery, the case manager makes documented contact attempts on SE-145 at days 5, 10, and 15 after delivery, using at least two different methods (phone, email, mail, home visit, in-person at pickup), each in the parent's language. All attempts are logged with date, time, method, and result. The evaluation clock does not start until consent is received.

7.5.6 **Date-stamping consent is the single most important act in this procedure.** Whoever receives the signed SE-120 — registrar, case manager, front office, Special Services clerk — date-stamps it the day it arrives, uploads it to the IEP system under "Consent — Initial Evaluation," and emails the records clerk. The records clerk enters `Consent received date` in the dashboard within one school day, which auto-generates the **35 school day evaluation due date**.

### 7.6 Step 6 — Evaluation planning and assessor assignment (within 3 school days of consent)

7.6.1 Within 3 school days of the consent date, the Director (or Program Supervisor as designee) issues form SE-130 naming, for each assessment area on SE-125: the assigned assessor, the assessor's building or contract, the assessment window, and the date by which results must be entered in the IEP system (consent Day 25).

7.6.2 Assignment priority order, applied in sequence:

1. The building's assigned school psychologist or SLP, if their open caseload is at or below 12 active evaluations;
2. A school psychologist or SLP at another building within the same level, under cross-assignment;
3. A contracted evaluator from the master services agreement pool;
4. An ESD 112 contracted evaluator.

7.6.3 The Director confirms the assignment in writing to the assessor and the building administrator. The assessor confirms acceptance and calendar availability within **1 school day**. If an assessor cannot meet the assessment window, they say so immediately; silence is treated as acceptance.

7.6.4 **Cross-building assignment.** Building administrators may not decline a cross-assigned assessor or refuse space. Buildings provide a quiet, private, appropriately lit testing space and access to the student. Assessment is not scheduled against a student's preferred activities where avoidable, and never against the same core class repeatedly.

7.6.5 **Contracted evaluators** receive: the SE-125 plan, the consent, relevant records, the report template, this procedure, and the due date. They enter results directly in the IEP system or submit to the case manager no later than consent Day 22 to allow integration. Contracted evaluator invoices are not processed until the report is received and accepted.

7.6.6 The case manager schedules the eligibility meeting **at the time of assignment**, not after assessments are complete, targeting consent Day 32. The meeting invitation goes to the parent at least 10 calendar days in advance, in the parent's language, listing participants by name and role. Interpreter requested at the same moment.

### 7.7 Step 7 — Conducting the evaluation (consent Days 1–25) and tracking (continuous)

7.7.1 Evaluations use a variety of assessment tools and strategies, are administered in the student's native language or mode of communication, are technically sound, are administered by trained and knowledgeable personnel in conformance with instructions, are not racially or culturally discriminatory, and are not based on a single measure.

7.7.2 Assessment components include, as planned: record and file review, parent interview, teacher interview, structured classroom observation (at least one, in the setting of concern), standardized and criterion-referenced measures, curriculum-based data, rating scales, health and developmental history, and vision and hearing screening results.

7.7.3 **Each assessor enters the completion date of each component in the IEP system within 1 school day of completing it.** The dashboard reads these entries. An assessment is not "complete" until the date is entered.

7.7.4 **Tracking and reminder schedule** (automated by the records clerk's dashboard):

| Point | Trigger | Recipients |
|---|---|---|
| Consent Day 1 | Evaluation opened; due date published | Case manager, assessors, building administrator |
| Consent Day 15 | Mid-point check: components complete vs. planned | Case manager, Program Supervisor |
| Consent Day 20 | Yellow flag if any component incomplete | Case manager, building administrator, Program Supervisor |
| Consent Day 25 | All assessment must be complete; red flag if not | Above plus Director |
| Consent Day 28 | Draft report due to group | Case manager, Director |
| Consent Day 30 | Red flag; Director contacts case manager same day | Director |
| Consent Day 33 | Escalation to Deputy Superintendent | Director, Deputy Superintendent |

7.7.5 **Status colors.** Green: on or ahead of internal target. Yellow: at internal target with an incomplete component. Red: past internal target, or fewer than 5 school days remain with incomplete components, or any missed reminder acknowledgment. Red items appear on the Director's daily report and require a written recovery plan from the case manager within one school day naming what will be done, by whom, and by when.

7.7.6 **Missed student sessions.** If a student is absent, refuses, or is unavailable, the assessor reschedules within 2 school days and logs the attempt. Three consecutive missed sessions trigger notification to the building administrator, who secures attendance, and to the parent.

### 7.8 Step 8 — Evaluation report (draft by consent Day 28)

8.1 The case manager compiles form SE-150, which contains: identifying information; reason for referral and referral source; consent date and evaluation due date; language and interpretation used; all assessment procedures, dates, and results; classroom observation; educational history and intervention data; health, vision, hearing; parent input; discussion of whether lack of appropriate instruction in reading or math or limited English proficiency is the determinant factor; each eligibility criterion addressed with data; a statement of whether the student has a disability and needs special education; recommendations for specially designed instruction and related services; and the signatures and roles of all participants, including any dissent.

7.8.2 The draft is circulated to the evaluation group and, on request, to the parent at least 2 school days before the eligibility meeting. Northline's practice is to offer the draft to every parent in advance.

7.8.3 The Program Supervisor reviews every initial evaluation report for completeness against a checklist (SE-150C) before the meeting and returns it with comments within 1 school day.

### 7.9 Step 9 — Eligibility meeting (by consent Day 35)

7.9.1 Participants: the parent; a district representative qualified to commit resources; at least one general education teacher of the student; at least one special education teacher; each assessor or a person who can interpret the instructional implications of their results; the student where appropriate; others with knowledge or special expertise invited by either party.

7.9.2 The group reviews all evaluation data, determines whether the student meets the criteria for one or more eligibility categories and needs specially designed instruction, and documents the determination on SE-160. The eligibility determination date is entered in the dashboard the same day and **closes the 35 school day clock**.

7.9.3 The parent receives a copy of the evaluation report and the eligibility determination at no cost at the meeting.

7.9.4 PWN (SE-170) documenting the eligibility determination — whether eligible or not eligible — is provided within 2 school days of the meeting.

7.9.5 If the student is found **not eligible**, the group documents recommendations for general education supports and a 504 referral where appropriate, and the case manager notifies the building administrator to initiate them within 5 school days.

### 7.10 Step 10 — The 30 calendar day IEP

7.10.1 If the student is eligible, the IEP meeting is held within **30 calendar days of the eligibility determination date**, targeting 21 calendar days. Wherever the parent agrees and participants are present, Northline holds the eligibility and IEP meetings on the same day, in sequence, with the eligibility determination made first.

7.10.2 The case manager (special education teacher assigned at eligibility) schedules the IEP meeting before leaving the eligibility meeting, issues the written invitation, and arranges interpretation.

7.10.3 The IEP is developed, PWN issued proposing initial placement, and **written parental consent for initial provision of special education services (SE-175)** obtained before any special education service is delivered. Services begin no later than **5 school days** after consent, and the start date is recorded in the IEP system.

7.10.4 The records clerk tracks the IEP due date on the dashboard with reminders at calendar days 14, 21, and 27.

### 7.11 Exception paths

**7.11.1 Student transfers into Northline mid-evaluation.**
On enrollment, the registrar asks whether an evaluation is in progress and records the answer in Skyward. If yes, the registrar notifies the case manager and records clerk the same day and requests records from the sending district within 2 school days. The dashboard is opened with the **original consent date from the sending district**; the 35 school day clock does not restart. The case manager contacts the sending district within 3 school days to identify completed components, obtains raw data and reports, and revises SE-125 to cover only what remains. Northline may adopt the original timeline or, with the parent's written agreement on a specific completion date (SE-140), extend it — but only where the district is making sufficient progress and the parent agrees in writing to the new date. Time-only extensions "because the student is new" are not permitted.

**7.11.2 Student transfers out of Northline mid-evaluation.**
The case manager completes and forwards all evaluation data to the receiving district within 5 school days of request, notifies the parent, and closes the dashboard entry with status "Transferred — data forwarded [date]." The referral is not deleted; the record is retained.

**7.11.3 Parent repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the student.**
The 35 school day timeline does not apply where the parent repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the student for evaluation. This exception applies only where the district documents on SE-145 at least **three** attempts to schedule, spanning at least 15 school days, using at least two contact methods, in the parent's language, including at least one written notice stating the consequence of non-attendance. The Director must review and approve invocation of this exception in writing before the dashboard status changes; the status becomes "Tolled — production," and the case manager continues attempts at least every 10 school days. The clock resumes the day the student is produced. Student absence, illness, or scheduling difficulty that the district has not documented under this paragraph is **not** an exception.

**7.11.4 Parent refuses or revokes consent for initial evaluation.**
If the parent refuses consent, or does not respond after documented attempts, the district may but is not required to pursue mediation or a due process hearing. Only the Director decides whether to pursue, within 10 school days, after consultation with the Deputy Superintendent. If the district does not pursue, the case manager issues PWN documenting the decision and closes the record as "Closed — consent not obtained," and the district does not violate child find by declining to override. If a parent revokes consent in writing before the evaluation is complete, all assessment stops immediately, the Director is notified the same day, PWN is issued within 5 school days acknowledging revocation, and the record is closed as "Closed — consent revoked." A new referral at any later date starts a new 25 school day clock.

**7.11.5 Agreed written extension.**
An extension of the 35 school day evaluation timeline is permitted only when: (a) the parent and district agree **in writing** on form SE-140; (b) the agreement states a **specific new completion date**, not a duration or a range; (c) the reason is stated; and (d) the Director signs before the agreement is sent to the parent. Extensions are limited to circumstances such as the need for a specialized assessment not available within the district, a medical condition preventing assessment, or additional data required by the group. Extensions are **not** available for staff vacancy, caseload, scheduling convenience, or forgotten deadlines. A second extension for the same evaluation requires Deputy Superintendent approval. Every executed SE-140 is reported by name in the monthly report.

**7.11.6 Referrals received in June, or near the end of the school year.**
Referrals do not wait for September. The following rules apply and are stricter than the legal minimum:

- **Received on or before June 1:** the referral review meeting is held, and PWN and consent are issued, before the last student day.
- **Received after June 1 and before the last student day:** the building administrator convenes the referral review meeting within 5 school days, or, if that is not possible, the district office team convenes it; PWN and consent are issued before the last student day whenever possible, and in all cases no later than the **fifth school day of the following school year**, with the school-day count suspended over summer recess.
- **Received during summer recess** (no school days in session): Day 0 remains the actual date of receipt. The Special Services office acknowledges receipt to the parent in writing within 5 business days, states the date the count begins, and offers a voluntary summer meeting. The referral review meeting is held no later than the **fifth school day** of the new school year.
- **Consent signed in June:** the 35 school day count suspends during summer recess and resumes on the first student day. The case manager offers to conduct assessment over the summer where assessors and the family are available; the Director authorizes summer assessment pay for this purpose.
- All June and summer referrals are listed individually in the monthly report with their computed due dates.

**7.11.7 Parentally placed private school and out-of-district referrals.**
Northline conducts child find for students parentally placed in private schools located within district boundaries, regardless of the student's district of residence, and for resident students who are homeschooled or not enrolled. Procedure: the Special Services office is the receiving point; a district office clerk performs the registrar functions in Section 7.2; the same 25 and 35 school day timelines apply. The Director assigns a district assessor and coordinates access with the private school administrator, in writing, within 5 school days. Assessment may occur at the private school, at a Northline building, or at the district office. If found eligible, the district issues PWN offering a free appropriate public education through enrollment in Northline; if the parent declines, the student's services, if any, are addressed through the proportionate-share services plan process (SE-PRO-007), not an IEP. For a **resident student attending school in another district** (e.g., interdistrict transfer, running start, institutional placement), the Director determines the responsible district in writing within 5 school days, notifies the parent and the other district, and — where Northline is not responsible — forwards the referral with the original date stamp within 2 school days and documents the transfer. **Northline never closes a referral on the assumption that another entity will act; closure requires written confirmation that the other district received it.**

**7.11.8 Suspected disability raised during a disciplinary proceeding.**
If, at any point in a disciplinary proceeding — investigation, hearing, appeal, or emergency expulsion review — a parent, teacher, administrator, or other person requests an evaluation or asserts the student may have a disability, the receiving employee logs it as a referral under Section 7.1 **that day** and notifies the Director and the building administrator immediately by phone and email.

- The evaluation is conducted in an **expedited manner**; the case manager assembles the evaluation plan within 2 school days and the Director assigns assessors within 1 school day, drawing on the contracted pool if necessary.
- The disciplinary proceeding continues; the student remains in the placement determined by school authorities pending the evaluation, including suspension or expulsion if applicable.
- Northline additionally requires the discipline officer to determine whether the district **had knowledge** the student was a student with a disability before the behavior occurred — that is, whether the parent had expressed concern in writing to supervisory or administrative personnel or a teacher, whether the parent had requested an evaluation, or whether a teacher or other staff member had expressed specific concerns about a pattern of behavior directly to the Director of Special Education or other supervisory personnel. This determination is documented on form SE-135 and filed with the discipline record.
- If the district had such knowledge, the student receives the protections available to students already eligible, and the Director is notified immediately.
- If the student is determined eligible, the IEP team meets before the disciplinary placement is finalized, and educational services are provided as required.
- All discipline-related referrals are flagged in the dashboard field `Disciplinary proceeding pending = Y` and are reviewed individually by the Director each week.

**7.11.9 Re-referral after a "not eligible" determination.** A new referral for the same student is logged and processed under this procedure with a new Day 0. The prior determination is not a reason to decline. The referral review group considers what has changed and documents it.

### 7.12 Monitoring, escalation, and monthly reporting

7.12.1 **Daily.** The records clerk runs the exception report at 8:00 a.m.: referrals logged but not on the dashboard; referrals with no decision at Day 20; consents received but no assignment; evaluations at yellow or red; eligibility determinations with no IEP scheduled. Distributed to the Director and Program Supervisors by 9:00 a.m.

7.12.2 **Weekly.** The Director holds a 45-minute Timeline Review each Monday with Program Supervisors and the records clerk, reviewing every yellow and red item by name and recording actions and owners. Building administrators verify and initial their building log each Friday.

7.12.3 **Monthly.** The records clerk assembles, and the Director delivers to the Deputy Superintendent by the **5th of each month**, the Monthly Special Education Timeline Report covering the prior month:

a. Number of referrals received, by building and channel;
b. Number and percent of referral decisions made within 25 school days, with each exception listed by student ID, date, and reason;
c. Number and percent of initial evaluations completed within 35 school days, with each exception listed by student ID, consent date, completion date, days elapsed, and root cause;
d. Number and percent of IEPs developed within 30 calendar days;
e. All executed SE-140 extensions and all invoked exceptions under 7.11.3;
f. Referral log audit results for all 19 buildings (compliant / not compliant);
g. PWN presence audit: percent of closed records containing all required PWNs;
h. Open evaluations by assessor and by building, with capacity analysis;
i. Corrective action progress: compensatory services determinations completed and pending;
j. Actions taken since the prior report.

7.12.4 The Deputy Superintendent reviews and signs the report by the **10th** and submits it to OSPI as required by the corrective action order. Reports are retained for the corrective action period plus six years.

7.12.5 **Escalation ladder.**

| Level | Trigger | Action | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yellow status | Case manager written recovery plan to Program Supervisor | 1 school day |
| 2 | Red status | Director contacts case manager and building administrator; reassignment or contracted assessor authorized | Same day |
| 3 | Timeline at risk within 3 school days | Director notifies Deputy Superintendent; Deputy authorizes emergency contracting and staff release | Same day |
| 4 | Timeline exceeded | Director notifies parent in writing within 2 school days with a specific completion date; compensatory services review opened under 7.13; root cause analysis filed within 5 school days | 2–5 school days |
| 5 | Any building with two exceeded timelines in a rolling 90 days | Deputy Superintendent conducts an on-site review with the principal; corrective plan filed | 10 school days |

### 7.13 Compensatory services determinations

7.13.1 For each of the 38 students identified in the April 2026 decision, and for any student whose evaluation exceeds the timeline going forward, the Director convenes a compensatory services determination.

7.13.2 The group — Director or Program Supervisor, the student's case manager, a general education teacher, an assessor familiar with the student, and the parent — reviews the number of school days of delay, the services the student would likely have received, the student's present performance, and the parent's input, and determines the type, amount, frequency, provider, and schedule of compensatory services on form SE-190.

7.13.3 PWN documenting the determination is issued within 5 school days. Services begin within 15 school days of the determination. Delivery is logged monthly and reported under 7.12.3(i) until complete.

7.13.4 The determination is individualized. Formulaic hour-for-hour awards are permitted only if the group finds them appropriate for that student and documents why.
