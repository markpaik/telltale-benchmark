## Overall Rating: 4 — Exceeds Expectations

Dev delivered the strongest store result in District 3 and one of the strongest in the chain during a year when the company comped down 1.8 percent. Rio Rancho finished at **+7.2 percent comparable sales** on $12.1 million in volume, against a district comp of **-2.4 percent**. He built the contractor sales desk pilot from a whiteboard concept to **$1.1 million in first-year revenue** against a $750,000 plan, and he did it while holding shrink to 1.28 percent in a district that finished at 2.4 percent. He is the reason District 3 was not a full-district failure this year.

The rating is a 4 rather than a 5 because two things kept the year from being unqualified. The contractor desk's gross margin ran 24.6 percent against a 31.0 percent store average, and roughly $190,000 of that gap traces to bids Dev approved above his discount authority without a pricing exception. And his bench is thinner than a store of this performance should have — he lost his strongest assistant manager to a promotion in October and has not built a replacement behind her. Both are the same underlying issue: Dev moves faster than the structure around him, and he has not yet made the structure keep up.

## Performance Against FY26 Goals

**Goal 1 — Deliver comparable sales at or above chain plan (weight 30%).**
Target: chain plan of +1.0 percent. Result: **+7.2 percent**, best in district, second best among the 22 stores. Growth was broad rather than one-category — lumber and building materials +14.1 percent, paint +6.8 percent, hardware +3.2 percent. Transaction count rose 4.4 percent, which matters more than the dollar figure; he grew traffic in a market where the rest of the district lost it. **Rating: 5.**

**Goal 2 — Launch and prove the contractor sales desk pilot (weight 25%).**
Target: $750,000 in year-one revenue, 120 active accounts, break-even contribution by Q4. Result: **$1.1 million revenue, 214 active accounts, contribution positive from Q3 forward.** The pilot is being written into the FY27 chain operating plan for four additional stores, and the will-call and jobsite delivery process Dev wrote is going into the chain standard operating procedure with his name on it. This is the single best piece of original operating work anyone in this district produced this year. The margin shortfall described above is real and is addressed in Goal 5 and in the FY27 goals, but it does not change the verdict on the pilot. **Rating: 5.**

**Goal 3 — Hold shrink at or below 1.6 percent (weight 20%).**
Result: **1.28 percent**, down from 1.51 percent the prior year, best in district. Cycle count completion 97 percent against a 95 percent standard. Return-to-vendor backlog cleared to zero at every month-end. Two internal theft cases identified by Dev's own team and referred to loss prevention before the January count. Owen Trujillo's post-inventory note called Rio Rancho "the cleanest count in the region." **Rating: 5.**

**Goal 4 — Staffing, retention, and bench (weight 15%).**
Time to fill averaged **17 days** against the 21-day standard. Voluntary turnover **38 percent** against a district average of 54 percent. Two associates were promoted to department supervisor and one assistant manager, Keisha Amankwaa, was promoted to Store Manager at Farmington in October — a genuine credit to Dev's development work, and the second manager he has produced.

The gap is what came after. The assistant manager position vacated in October was filled externally in January, and Dev has not identified a successor for himself or for his remaining assistant manager. When I asked in April for two names ready for a store manager role within 18 months, he gave me one, and that person is not yet on the succession slate. A store performing at this level should be a talent exporter continuously, not once. **Rating: 3.**

**Goal 5 — Safety, compliance, and controls (weight 10%).**
All four quarterly safety and compliance walks passed — scores of 94, 96, 97, and 95. Zero lost-time injuries. Forklift certification current at 100 percent all year.

Controls were the weak point. A March internal audit flagged **three contractor bids totaling $412,000 approved at discounts of 18 to 22 percent** against Dev's 12 percent authority, with no pricing exception filed. Dev's position was that the bids would have been lost during the approval wait. That may be true. It is still an unauthorized commitment of company margin, and it is the kind of thing that ends careers when the number gets large enough. He filed retroactive exceptions in April and has been clean since. **Rating: 3.**

## Overall Rating: 2 — Partially Meets Expectations

This is the hardest review I have written this year, and Pauline deserves to know why before she reads the detail.

Santa Fe holds the best customer survey score in District 3 at **78**, four points above the next store and eight above district average, for the fifth consecutive year. In a year when the chain comped down 1.8 percent and the district comped down 2.4 percent, Santa Fe finished **flat**, which is real relative outperformance on $13.9 million — the largest volume in the district. Nine years of consistency at the highest-expectation store in the chain is not a small thing and no part of this review should be read as forgetting it.

But shrink finished at **2.9 percent against a 1.6 percent target**, or $403,000 in lost inventory, up from 2.1 percent the prior year. Time to fill ran **46 days against a 21-day standard**. Two assistant managers transferred out of the store and both named Pauline's decision-making in their exit interviews. These are not three unrelated problems. They are one problem with three symptoms: every decision in Store #6 runs through one person, and one person cannot count inventory, hire a workforce, and serve customers at 78 all at the same time. The customer score is what she protects when there is only enough of her for one thing. The rating reflects the other two.

## Performance Against FY26 Goals

**Goal 1 — Comparable sales (weight 25%).**
Target: +1.0 percent. Result: **+0.1 percent** — flat, and 2.5 points better than the district. Santa Fe held its seasonal business, held paint against a competitive opening in October, and lost ground in lumber and appliances. In the context of the year, this is a defensible result and it is the strongest part of her performance record. **Rating: 3.**

**Goal 2 — Shrink at or below 1.6 percent (weight 25%).**
Result: **2.90 percent, $403,000.** Prior year 2.1 percent. Second-worst in the district and in the bottom three chainwide.

The causes are documented and none of them are ambiguous. **Cycle count completion finished at 61 percent against the 95 percent standard**, the lowest in the district; counts were scheduled and then not run because the associates assigned were pulled to the front end. **Return-to-vendor backlog reached $88,000 aged past 90 days** at the January count, of which $51,000 was written off as unrecoverable. Loss prevention audits in September and February both found the **receiving door propped and unattended**, the second time after a corrective action plan had been signed. Owen Trujillo's post-inventory memo states: "Store 6's variance is not a theft profile. It is a process profile. The store does not know what it has."

Santa Fe alone accounts for approximately 0.4 points of the 0.8-point district miss that cost every manager in District 3 their bonus. **Rating: 1.**

**Goal 3 — Staffing and time to fill (weight 20%).**
Result: **46 days average time to fill against a 21-day standard**, the worst in the district by 14 days. The mechanism is documented in the requisition audit I ran in April: **requisitions sat an average of 11 days waiting for Pauline's personal interview availability**, and a further 9 days between her interview and her offer decision. She retains sole hiring approval for all positions, including part-time seasonal associates at Grade 2. No assistant manager, department supervisor, or human resources coordinator in Store #6 holds any hiring authority.

Voluntary turnover finished at **51 percent**. The store carried an average of 9 open positions against 104 budgeted through the fall season, and the assistant manager position vacated in November remained open for 94 days. **Rating: 1.**

**Goal 4 — Talent development and bench (weight 15%).**
Two assistant managers transferred out during the year: **Renata Ortiz** to Store #9 in November and **Bryce Kaltenbach** to Store #21 in February. Both are still with the company, which is the good news. Both named Pauline in their exit interviews, which is the rest of it.

From Ortiz's exit interview: "I was an assistant manager for two years and I never once approved a schedule, a markdown, or a hire. I asked for the receiving department and was told it was too important to hand off." From Kaltenbach's: "Pauline is not unkind and she is not unfair. She just does not believe anyone else will do it right, and after a while you believe her."

No candidate from Store #6 has entered the district succession slate in four years. In the same period, Store #14 has produced two store managers. **Rating: 1.**

**Goal 5 — Customer experience (weight 15%).**
Result: **78**, first in District 3 for the fifth straight year, ninth chainwide out of 22. Service recovery closure at 100 percent within 48 hours. Special-order accuracy 96 percent. This is genuinely excellent and it is the reason a rating of 2 rather than 1 is the right call. **Rating: 5.**

## Overall Rating: 3 — Meets Expectations

Keisha took Store #19 on October 6, 2025, eleven days after the previous store manager resigned without notice in the middle of the fall season, leaving **37 of 92 budgeted positions open** — 40 percent of the store. She had never run a store. She had no assistant manager for her first six weeks. The store had not completed a full cycle count in four months.

She reached **full staffing by February**. She rebuilt the leadership team, closed the comp gap from -6.8 percent in the quarter before her arrival to **-1.4 percent in her final quarter**, and moved the customer survey score from 64 to 70.

She also **failed two of three quarterly safety walks** and finished the January inventory **$84,000 short**, at 3.61 percent shrink. Those are serious, and under normal conditions a safety record of two failures in three walks caps a store manager at a 2. The rating is a 3 because the conditions were not normal, the trajectory across all three quarters is unambiguous, and the third walk passed at 92. She should understand that this rating is generous by exactly the width of the circumstances she inherited, and that the circumstances expire now.

## Performance Against FY26 Goals

Keisha's goals were set on November 3, 2025, four weeks after she took the store, and were written as stabilization goals rather than standard store manager goals. They are assessed as written.

**Goal 1 — Return the store to full staffing (weight 30%).**
Target: 95 percent of budgeted positions filled by April 30, 2026. Result: **full staffing reached February 21, 2026**, ten weeks ahead of target. She hired 41 people in 19 weeks, ran three hiring events including one at San Juan College, and filled both assistant manager positions internally by promoting from her own department supervisor ranks. Time to fill, which was untracked at her arrival, finished the fourth quarter at **24 days**. Voluntary turnover among her post-October hires is 22 percent, which is the strongest new-hire retention figure in the district. **Rating: 5.**

**Goal 2 — Stabilize sales (weight 20%).**
Target: hold comp at or above -5.0 percent for the balance of the year. Full-year store comp: **-4.1 percent**. By quarter: Q1 (before her arrival) -6.8 percent, Q2 -5.2 percent, Q3 -3.9 percent, **Q4 -1.4 percent**. Volume $8.6 million. Every quarter she owned improved on the one before it, in a district that comped -2.4 percent and a chain that comped -1.8 percent, and her final quarter closed most of the gap to chain. **Rating: 4.**

**Goal 3 — Inventory accuracy and shrink (weight 20%).**
Result: **3.61 percent shrink, $84,000 short at the January count**, against a 1.6 percent target and a 2.8 percent prior year. Worst in the district.

The contributing causes, from Owen Trujillo's February review: receiving operated **without a supervisor from September through mid-December**, with no receiving log for eleven weeks; lumber yard cycle counts were not run at all between July and January; and three internal theft cases were identified during her tenure, resulting in two terminations and one police referral in Kirtland. Trujillo's memo concludes: "A meaningful share of the Store 19 variance predates the current manager and was baked in before October. That does not make the number smaller."

That is the correct framing and I apply it. Approximately $50,000 of the variance is attributable to the unlogged receiving period before Keisha had a receiving supervisor in place, which she hired in December — as fast as the requisition process allowed. The remainder is hers, particularly the lumber yard, which she did not begin counting until February despite my direction in November. **Rating: 2.**

**Goal 4 — Safety and compliance (weight 20%).**
Result: **two of three quarterly walks failed.** The store's standard is a score of 90 or better.

- **Q2 walk, November 12, 2025: 81 — FAIL.** Findings: electrical panel blocked by seasonal freight, four associates operating powered equipment with lapsed forklift certification, safety data sheet binder incomplete in the paint department, no documented rack inspection in the prior six months.
- **Q3 walk, February 18, 2026: 84 — FAIL.** Findings: forklift certification corrected, panel access corrected; SDS binder still incomplete, two blocked emergency exits in the lumber aisle, ladder inspection tags missing on 6 of 14 ladders.
- **Q4 walk, May 20, 2026: 92 — PASS.** All prior findings closed. Two minor findings, both corrected same day.

The store recorded **three OSHA-recordable injuries** against one the prior year, including **one lost-time injury** on January 8 when an associate fell from an unsecured ladder — an incident directly connected to the ladder findings from the November walk that had not been closed. That is the most serious single item in this review and it is not mitigated by inherited conditions. The November findings were hers to close and she had eight weeks. **Rating: 2.**

**Goal 5 — Customer experience (weight 10%).**
Score moved from **64 at her arrival to 70 by Q4**, matching district average. Complaint volume down 41 percent. Service recovery closure within 48 hours moved from 62 percent to 94 percent. **Rating: 4.**

**Reviewer response.** This is the most honest self-assessment I received this year from anyone at any level, and Keisha should know that the willingness to write the second paragraph is itself a qualification for the job.

On safety, her account is correct and her accounting is correct. The November failure is largely explicable by inexperience. The February failure is not, and she names the reason accurately: she prioritized staffing and the season over closing known findings, and an associate was injured on one of them. She does not need me to add weight to that. She needs the structure below so that it cannot recur, because good intentions and genuine remorse have never closed a rack inspection log.

On the $84,000: her instinct to distrust her own exculpation is a good instinct, and it happens to be wrong in this case. Loss prevention's independent finding is that a meaningful share predates her. The lumber yard is hers and she has claimed it without being asked to.

On what she needs: she is right, and it is the identical development gap I am working on with Pauline Villanueva-Estrada at nine years in the role. Keisha has identified it at nine months. That is a substantial advantage and she should use it.

On where she stands, my answer is in the conversion section below and I gave it to her verbally before this document was written.

## Compensation and Bonus Outcome

Current base: $84,000, set at promotion in October 2025. **FY27 merit increase: 2.0 percent** — $1,680, new base **$85,680**, effective September 1, 2026. This is a full-pool award at a rating of 3 and, given the compression created by funding a 3.5 percent award elsewhere in the district, it is more than the guideline strictly indicated. I have noted separately to Talia Chacón that Keisha's base sits **$12,400 below the District 3 store manager median** and that a market equity adjustment should be reviewed at the January 2027 cycle if the FY27 trajectory holds.

**FY26 bonus: not paid.** Her prorated target for nine months in role was $12,600. The district bonus is gated on district shrink of 1.6 percent or better; District 3 finished at 2.4 percent, and Store #19's 3.61 percent contributed materially to the miss. Keisha therefore worked the hardest nine months of anyone's year in this district and received nothing beyond salary for it. I have told her, and repeat here, that this is a fair application of a plan she did not design, and that it does not reflect what she accomplished. My written objection to the district-gate structure, submitted to Marisol Baca, cites her situation alongside Dev Ramchandani's.

## Impact of the March Format Conversion

Farmington is **not** one of the two converting stores. Store #19 remains a full-format location at Grade 12, and Keisha's position is not affected directly.

The indirect effect is significant and she asked about it directly, so here is the direct answer. The conversion removes one store manager position from District 3 in March 2027, and the **placement review during the week of November 16, 2026** will place an experienced, tenured store manager into the district's available roles. Separately, Store #14 Rio Rancho is the likeliest opening in this district in FY27.

Two things follow. First, Keisha's hold on Farmington is not in question on the strength of her FY26 record — a rating of 3 in a first partial year in those conditions is a defensible result and I will defend it. But the district now has one more experienced manager than it has stores, and that means the standard by which every Store #19 result is judged in FY27 is higher than it would otherwise have been. A second consecutive year of failed safety walks would put her position genuinely at risk. She should hear that as information, not as a threat.

Second, the same crowding means that if she delivers FY27 — specifically a clean safety record and shrink under 2.2 percent — she moves from "the manager who saved Farmington" to a candidate for a larger store, and Rio Rancho is a larger store. She was an assistant manager there fourteen months ago. That is not a promise and I have not made her one. It is the shape of the opportunity.

## FY27 Goals

1. **Safety and compliance (weight 30%).** Pass all four quarterly safety walks at 90 or better, with **zero repeat findings between consecutive walks** — the repeat-finding condition is the actual goal. All prior-quarter findings closed and documented within 21 days. Recordable injuries reduced to one or fewer. Complete the chain's Store Safety Leadership certification by October 31, 2026.
2. **Shrink (weight 25%).** Deliver shrink of 2.2 percent or better at the January 2027 inventory. Lumber yard cycle counts running weekly from August 1, 2026 with no exceptions. Cycle count completion at 90 percent or better every month from September forward. Receiving log audited by the assistant manager weekly and reviewed by Keisha monthly — reviewed, not performed.
3. **Comparable sales (weight 20%).** Deliver full-year comp at or above chain plan. Given her Q4 exit rate of -1.4 percent against a chain of -1.6 percent, this is achievable and it is the first goal she has been given that is not a stabilization goal.
4. **Delegation and leadership development (weight 15%).** Assign full end-to-end ownership of receiving, scheduling, and safety-walk preparation to named leaders by September 30, 2026, with Keisha in a weekly review role only. Place one Store #19 leader on the district succession slate by June 30, 2027. Measured by the Voice of Zia item "I have the authority I need to do my job," target 75 percent favorable or better.
5. **Retention (weight 10%).** Hold voluntary turnover at 45 percent or better and open positions under 5 percent of budgeted headcount through the spring season.

**Development plan.** Assigned mentor: Dev Ramchandani through December 2026, or his successor thereafter, with a monthly session focused specifically on delegation — she has asked for this and he has agreed. Enrollment in the New Store Manager Foundations program, September 2026 cohort, which she missed at promotion because of the circumstances of her appointment; this was a company failure and is being corrected. Biweekly one-on-one with me through October 2026, monthly thereafter. She will lead the district safety standards session in January 2027, which is deliberate: the fastest way to make her expert in the thing she failed at is to make her teach it.

**Manager statement.** Keisha was handed the worst situation in this district in a decade and did not fail. Two safety walks and $84,000 are the price of the education, and the price was too high, and she knows it better than I could tell her. What I saw between November and June is a manager becoming one in real time. I want her second year to be the one where she stops carrying the store and starts running it.
