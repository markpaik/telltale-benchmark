# Performance Review: Emmanuel Owusu-Bempah

**Title:** Dispatch Supervisor
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Hala Nasrallah, Terminal Manager, San Antonio Hub
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

## Performance Against Goals

Emmanuel's goals for the period, set last August, were to hold on-time service at or above 95 percent through an anticipated wave of driver attrition, to reduce empty-mile percentage on city routes by rebuilding the route plan around the new zone boundaries, and to cross-train at least one dispatcher to cover his role during absences.

He exceeded the first two goals and made partial progress on the third. On-time performance for the twenty-four city routes under his dispatch held at 96.4 percent for the twelve-month period, achieved while the driver seat turned over at 68 percent — the highest attrition rate this terminal has recorded in at least five years. He rebuilt the city route plan in the fall, reassigning delivery windows around the two new retail accounts and consolidating stops in the medical-center corridor, and empty miles fell 11 percent against the prior year's baseline. Both results came without an increase in overtime hours in the dispatch office itself, though city driver overtime rose modestly as a function of turnover coverage.

Cross-training lagged. Emmanuel identified a candidate, but the wage pool did not allow a stipend for the added responsibility, and the candidate declined the additional workload without compensation. One dispatcher has partial familiarity with the routing software, but no one besides Emmanuel can currently run the full planning cycle. This is a single point of failure I am flagging as a terminal risk, not a mark against Emmanuel, who raised it with me in October and again in March.

## Compliance Findings and Customer Escalations

The September federal compliance review centered on hours-of-service records and driver qualification files, both of which sit with the safety office rather than dispatch. Dispatch's schedules were reviewed as supporting documentation and were not cited as a source of the conditional rating. Where dispatch touches the finding is downstream: with the qualification-file backlog slowing new-hire road tests to a nine-day wait, Emmanuel absorbed the resulting seat gaps into the same route plan that was already compensating for 68 percent turnover, without additional dispatch staff.

The retail account escalation — a 90-store, $3.8 million customer that placed the terminal on notice over 12 percent late deliveries — falls partly within Emmanuel's area. Of the deliveries flagged late on that account, dispatch records attribute roughly a third to route and dispatch decisions, with the remainder tied to dock cutoff misses and open driver seats. Emmanuel rebuilt the delivery windows on that account in November as part of the broader route redesign, and late deliveries on the account specifically have fallen from 12 percent to under 6 percent since the rework took effect. Corporate has not yet closed the notice.

## Incidents and Feedback

No safety or conduct incidents are recorded for Emmanuel this period. Driver feedback, gathered informally through ride-alongs and exit interviews, was consistently favorable: several departing drivers cited fair route assignment and responsiveness to schedule complaints as reasons they stayed as long as they did despite pay concerns elsewhere in the market. One exit interview specifically credited Emmanuel with talking a driver out of resigning in January by restructuring his route to cut a 45-minute deadhead.

Peer feedback from the safety office was positive on cooperation, though Marisela Quintanilla noted in a joint meeting that dispatch has, at times, pushed to get drivers on the road before file review was complete — a friction point that reflects the nine-day road-test backlog more than any resistance from Emmanuel personally.

The retail account's regional logistics manager, in the same call where the formal notice was issued, singled out dispatch's communication during the transition to new delivery windows as "the one part of this relationship that's gotten better, not worse."

**Employee Self-Assessment:**

> "I held the line on service this year with fewer drivers than I've ever had to work with, and I don't think that shows up clearly enough in a percentage. The route rebuild is the piece of work I'm proudest of — it wasn't asked for, I built it because the old zone map hadn't been touched since before I started. I applied for the ops manager role in July because I think I've been doing pieces of that job for two years already, and I understand the freeze, but I need to be honest that I have an offer in New Braunfels and I have not said no to it. I'd rather stay here. I want to know what this year looks like before I decide."

## Rating

**Exceeds Expectations.**

This is the strongest dispatch performance I have evaluated at this terminal. The on-time and empty-mile results were achieved against headwinds — turnover, a compliance-driven road-test bottleneck, and a major account under notice — that would have degraded weaker planning. The cross-training gap is a resourcing problem, not a performance deficiency, and I am recording it as such.

## Promotions and Wage Pool

Corporate's freeze on supervisor promotions means the operations manager posting Emmanuel applied for in July remains closed regardless of this rating. I want to be direct in this review, as I was with him directly: this is not a reflection of his qualification for that role, which I believe is strong, and I have said so to corporate in writing. The 2.5 percent wage pool this cycle will apply to his base per the standard formula; it does not create room for a title change, a retention bonus, or an equity adjustment against the competing offer he has disclosed. I am escalating the retention risk to corporate separately from this review, because losing him during a compliance-sensitive, tonnage-down year would be a serious operational loss, not a routine backfill.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Maintain on-time performance at or above 95 percent through the current staffing environment, with specific attention to the retail account's delivery windows until the customer notice is formally closed.
2. Document the full route-planning process in writing, including the software workflow, so that dispatch is no longer a single-person dependency regardless of whether a paid cross-training slot becomes available.
3. Partner with the safety office to shorten the new-hire-to-road gap on the dispatch side — specifically, sequencing route assignments so that qualification-file delays do not cascade into service commitments on named accounts.
4. Revisit the empty-mile initiative for a second phase targeting the outlying rural routes, which were out of scope for this year's rebuild.

# Performance Review: Marisela Quintanilla

**Title:** Safety Supervisor
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Hala Nasrallah, Terminal Manager, San Antonio Hub
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

## Performance Against Goals

Marisela's goals for the period were to continue the multi-year reduction in preventable accidents per million miles, to bring driver qualification files current ahead of an anticipated compliance audit, and to reduce the new-hire road-test wait, which had already been identified as a bottleneck the prior year.

The accident result is unambiguous and strong: preventable accidents fell from 3.1 to 1.9 per million miles, continuing an improvement trend she has sustained across multiple years and driver populations, including this year's unusually high 68 percent turnover in the city fleet. This is the best accident rate this terminal has recorded.

The qualification-file goal was not met, and the shortfall is serious. The September federal compliance review sampled 31 driver qualification files and found 22 incomplete — missing certifications, lapsed medical cards, or absent annual reviews of driving record. This is the direct cause of the conditional safety rating the terminal now carries. I want to be precise about where responsibility sits: the qualification files are Marisela's functional ownership, and the finding is a performance failure in that part of her role, whatever the surrounding constraints. At the same time, the constraints are real and material to how I am weighing the rating below. Marisela is the only employee at this terminal trained on the compliance system that manages these files; there is no backup, and there has not been one at any point in her eleven years in the role. She raised the lack of a backup in writing in each of the last three annual reviews, including this one prior to the September finding. The 68 percent driver turnover in the city fleet directly multiplied her file volume: every departure and every new hire is a new file to build, verify, and maintain, and the volume this year was roughly double a typical year against unchanged staffing in her office.

The road-test wait, at nine days, did not improve and by some measures worsened, which is directly downstream of the same file backlog: a road test cannot be scheduled until the qualification file supporting it is complete, and incomplete files were, this year, the norm rather than the exception.

## Compliance Findings and Customer Escalations

The conditional safety rating is the single most consequential event of the year for this terminal, and it sits substantially in Marisela's area. Corporate has required a corrective action plan, which Marisela authored and which I approved and submitted in October; as of this writing, 26 of the 31 sampled files have been brought current, and a full-file audit beyond the sample is roughly 60 percent complete. I regard the corrective action work itself as competent and appropriately urgent once the finding landed. My concern is that the underlying condition — incomplete files at this volume — existed before the audit found it, and the absence of a proactive audit or a backup system meant the terminal learned of it from a federal reviewer rather than from its own safety function.

The retail account's late-delivery notice does not implicate the safety office directly, but the same driver-availability strain that slowed deliveries — new hires waiting on road tests — traces back to the file backlog. Marisela's function and Emmanuel's dispatch function are more entangled than an org chart suggests, and I have noted in Emmanuel's review that this friction is best solved jointly rather than assigning it entirely to either supervisor.

## Incidents and Feedback

No preventable-accident-attributable incidents involving safety-office judgment are on record this year; the improved rate reflects sustained training and coaching effort, evidenced by ride-along logs and driver coaching records reviewed for this cycle. Dispatch feedback, referenced above, was that scheduling pressure has at times pushed against file-review timing — a structural point, not a complaint about Marisela's diligence. No driver has filed a complaint against the safety office this year. Two new hires, in exit or transfer interviews, cited the nine-day wait as a factor in early dissatisfaction with the job, though neither left the company over it.

**Employee Self-Assessment:**

> "I have flagged the staffing gap in this office in writing for three years running, and this year it caught up with us in the worst possible way — a federal finding instead of an internal one. I own the files, and I own that 22 of 31 were wrong when someone from outside this building looked at them. What I want on the record is that I have been the only person who can touch the compliance system since before Hala took over this terminal, and that this year's driver turnover roughly doubled what one person can keep current by hand. The accident number is real and I'm proud of it, but I understand it doesn't offset a conditional safety rating, and I'm not asking that it should. I need a second trained person in this office. I have asked for that every year. I am asking again."

## Rating

**Partially Meets Expectations.**

I am not rating Marisela on a single axis, because the two halves of her performance genuinely point in different directions and I do not think a blended middle rating communicates either honestly. The accident-rate result, taken alone, would merit Exceeds Expectations; it represents sustained, effective coaching under the hardest staffing conditions this terminal has faced. The qualification-file failure, taken alone, would merit Does Not Meet Expectations, given its severity and its role in producing a federal conditional rating — regardless of the staffing context, the files were her direct responsibility and were found deficient by an outside reviewer rather than by internal review. The overall rating reflects both facts held together, and reflects that the corrective action taken since September has been prompt and is on a credible path to closing the finding.

## Promotions and Wage Pool

The promotion freeze does not directly affect Marisela, who has not sought a title change this cycle, but it forecloses any near-term path to formalizing a second, trained safety role through a promotion from within — which is the most obvious internal fix to the single-person dependency she has raised for three years. The 2.5 percent wage pool likewise does not fund a new compliance-system seat or a stipend for cross-training a backup. I am escalating the need for a funded second compliance-trained position to corporate as a distinct request tied to the corrective action plan and the conditional rating, separate from the standard merit cycle, because I do not believe this terminal can close the underlying risk through this year's ordinary compensation tools.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Complete the full driver qualification file audit, not only the federally sampled files, and close all findings under the corrective action plan ahead of any follow-up review.
2. Train a second employee — dispatch or dock administrative staff, pending role definition — on the compliance system sufficiently to serve as a functional backup for file review and system access.
3. Reduce the new-hire road-test wait from nine days toward a five-day target by sequencing file completion ahead of, rather than behind, driver onboarding.
4. Maintain the preventable accident rate at or below 2.0 per million miles through the coming year's turnover, whatever that turnover turns out to be.

# Performance Review: Jamal Whitfield

**Title:** Dock Supervisor
**Review Period:** October 6, 2025 – July 31, 2026 (first review since appointment)
**Reviewer:** Hala Nasrallah, Terminal Manager, San Antonio Hub
**Date of Review:** July 30, 2026

## Performance Against Goals

Jamal was appointed dock supervisor in October after his predecessor was terminated for falsifying a manifest, a circumstance that shaped this year's goals: stabilize the dock, restore accurate manifest and load-count practices, and improve throughput without repeating the shortcuts that led to the prior supervisor's termination.

Throughput improved substantially, from 14 to 19 bills per hour on his shift, a result he achieved within his first two quarters and has sustained since. This is a meaningful contribution against a year of declining tonnage and an account under notice for late deliveries, since dock speed directly affects whether freight makes its outbound cutoff.

The throughput gain came with a cost that needs to be stated plainly: damage claims on his shift rose 40 percent in the first quarter of his tenure. I reviewed this closely given the circumstances of his appointment, and found no evidence of manifest falsification or concealment — the claims were logged accurately and reported through normal channels, which is itself a meaningful contrast with the prior supervisor's conduct. The increase appears attributable to faster handling outpacing initial training on the new pace, particularly among newer dock hands. Jamal adjusted stacking and staging procedures in February and added a short pre-shift safety-handling briefing; damage claims returned to baseline by June and have remained there since. I consider this a genuine correction, not a temporary dip, though it is only five months of sustained data and I want another full quarter before calling it settled.

## Compliance Findings and Customer Escalations

The dock is not named directly in the September compliance findings, which centered on hours-of-service and driver qualification files. The dock does, however, bear on the retail account's late-delivery notice: dock cutoff misses account for a meaningful share of the late deliveries flagged on that account, alongside the dispatch and driver-availability factors addressed in Emmanuel's review. The throughput improvement under Jamal's supervision has measurably reduced dock-attributable lateness since the fourth quarter, though the account remains formally on notice and the terminal has not yet received word that the notice is lifted.

## Incidents and Feedback

Two senior dock workers filed complaints, one in December and one in April, regarding Jamal's overtime assignment practices, specifically alleging that overtime shifts were offered inconsistently and, in their view, favored newer hires willing to work at the faster pace over senior staff. I investigated both complaints. I did not find evidence of favoritism rising to a policy violation, but I did find that Jamal had not been documenting the basis for overtime offers, which made the process difficult to audit and easy to perceive as arbitrary even where it may not have been. I directed him in April to begin logging overtime offers and the stated reason for each assignment; he has done so since, and no further complaints have been filed. I regard this as resolved but recently resolved, and worth naming clearly in this review given that it involves two senior employees on a dock that is still stabilizing after a termination for dishonesty.

No customer has raised a complaint naming Jamal or the dock specifically; the retail account's escalation names the terminal's overall on-time performance rather than the dock in particular. Peer feedback from dispatch was positive regarding cutoff coordination, and Emmanuel specifically credited improved dock timing with easing pressure on the route rebuild described in his own review.

**Employee Self-Assessment:**

> "I took this job knowing what happened to the person before me, and my first goal for myself was that nobody would ever have a reason to question whether my numbers were real. I pushed the pace hard in the first few months and the damage claims went up because of that, and I own that — I should have slowed the ramp and trained the new hands better before I asked for more bills an hour. I fixed the process once I saw the claims data, and I think June through now shows it. On the overtime complaints, I wasn't trying to favor anyone, but I can see now why it looked that way without anything written down, and I've kept a log since April. I'd ask that this review weigh the fact that I inherited a broken dock and I've been building the plane while flying it for nine months."

## Rating

**Meets Expectations.**

Jamal took over a dock in crisis, following a termination for falsified records, and produced a genuine throughput gain without any indication of the dishonesty that ended his predecessor's tenure — that baseline matters and I am weighing it. The first-quarter damage spike and the overtime complaints are real performance issues, not incidental ones, and both were corrected only after they surfaced rather than anticipated. Nine months is a short track record for a full endorsement, and I want to see a full year of sustained throughput, controlled damage claims, and clean overtime administration together before considering a higher rating.

## Promotions and Wage Pool

Jamal was promoted into this role in October under the prior supervisor's termination and is not seeking a further title change this cycle, so the corporate freeze on supervisor promotions does not directly block an outcome he applied for. It does mean that any formal recognition of this year's stabilization work — a title adjustment or lead-supervisor designation some peers have informally suggested — is not available under current policy. The 2.5 percent wage pool applies to his base under the standard formula.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Sustain throughput at or above 19 bills per hour for a full four quarters without a recurrence of the damage-claim pattern seen in the first quarter of his tenure.
2. Maintain overtime assignment logs consistently and make the criteria for assignment visible to dock staff, closing out the concern raised by senior workers in December and April.
3. Formalize the pre-shift safety-handling briefing introduced in February as a standing procedure with written documentation, so it survives beyond his own presence on the floor.
4. Continue coordination with dispatch on cutoff timing specifically in support of the retail account, until the account's late-delivery notice is formally resolved.

I have tried in each review to be specific about where a given supervisor's work contributed to a shared problem and where it did not, rather than letting one finding blur across three people's ratings.

## Memorandum: Staffing and Retention Risks Accompanying FY Annual Reviews

**To:** Regional Vice President of Operations; Human Resources, Corporate
**From:** Hala Nasrallah, Terminal Manager, San Antonio Hub
**Date:** July 30, 2026
**Re:** Retention and structural risks identified through FY2025–2026 supervisor reviews

This memorandum accompanies the annual performance reviews of three San Antonio Hub supervisors — Emmanuel Owusu-Bempah, Dispatch; Marisela Quintanilla, Safety; and Jamal Whitfield, Dock — submitted under separate cover. I am writing separately because each review surfaced a structural risk that I do not believe the standard review and compensation cycle is positioned to address, and I want these risks in front of corporate as decisions rather than as footnotes to individual ratings.

### 1. Dispatch Retention — Emmanuel Owusu-Bempah

Emmanuel has an active competing offer from a carrier in New Braunfels and applied in July for the operations manager position that corporate froze the same month. His performance this year — 96.4 percent on-time service through 68 percent driver turnover, an 11 percent reduction in empty miles — is, in my assessment, the strongest supervisory performance at this terminal this cycle. He has told me directly that he would prefer to stay and that he has not declined the outside offer. The frozen posting and the 2.5 percent wage pool leave me with no compensation or title tool to respond to that offer. I am requesting that corporate consider either an exception to the promotion freeze for this specific role, given the operational risk of losing dispatch leadership during a year when tonnage is down 9 percent and a $3.8 million account is on notice, or, failing that, a retention adjustment outside the standard wage pool. I do not believe this can wait until the next scheduled compensation cycle.

### 2. Safety Office Single Point of Failure — Marisela Quintanilla

The September compliance finding — 22 of 31 sampled driver qualification files incomplete, producing a conditional safety rating — occurred in an office staffed by one person trained on the compliance system, a condition Marisela has flagged in writing for three consecutive annual reviews prior to this one. Her preventable accident rate this year, 1.9 per million miles, is the best this terminal has recorded, achieved during the same period the file backlog developed, which tells me the failure is a capacity problem rather than a competence problem. The corrective action plan is progressing — 26 of 31 sampled files now current, a broader audit roughly 60 percent complete — but the underlying single-person dependency remains unaddressed and will recreate the same risk the next time driver turnover spikes or Marisela is unavailable for an extended period. I am requesting a funded second compliance-trained position, distinct from this year's wage pool, tied directly to closing out the corrective action plan corporate approved in October.

### 3. Dock Leadership Recognition — Jamal Whitfield

Jamal took over the dock in October following his predecessor's termination for manifest falsification and has produced a genuine throughput gain, from 14 to 19 bills per hour, without any indication of the dishonesty that ended the prior supervisor's tenure. He corrected a first-quarter damage-claim spike on his own initiative once the data showed it, and resolved an overtime-documentation complaint from two senior dock workers once I directed him to log assignment decisions. This is a shorter track record than the other two supervisors' — nine months against a twelve-month review cycle — and I am not requesting an exception to the promotion freeze on his behalf this year. I am flagging it now so that if his throughput and damage-claim performance hold through a full year, as I expect, corporate has advance notice that a lead-supervisor or title conversation is likely to come forward next cycle, and that dock staff have already begun raising it informally.

### Summary

None of these three requests is routine, and I recognize corporate froze promotions and held the wage pool to 2.5 percent for reasons that extend well beyond this terminal. I am bringing them forward together because I believe the compounding risk — losing dispatch leadership, carrying an unresolved single-person compliance dependency, and deferring dock recognition indefinitely — is larger than any one of the three problems taken alone, particularly in a year when this terminal is already operating under a conditional safety rating and a major customer's notice of termination risk.

I am available to discuss any of the above by phone or in person before the next regional operations meeting.
