## 1.1 Goals Carried Forward from 2024–25

Three goals were set in the September 2024 planning conference and reaffirmed at the October 2025 checkpoint after the school's designation for targeted state support.

**Goal 1 — Student growth.** Raise the median student growth percentile (MGP) in Grade 8 mathematics from 61 to 65 or better, with particular attention to the lowest-performing quartile, which is the subgroup driving the school's designation.

*Result: exceeded.* The Grade 8 mathematics MGP finished at 68, the highest of any tested grade-level cohort in Canyon Vista Unified. Lowest-quartile MGP was 64, up from 52. This is the outcome the goal was written to produce, and it was produced in the year the school was least equipped to produce it.

**Goal 2 — Departmental coherence.** As chair, establish a common formative assessment cycle across Grades 6–8 mathematics and lead the department's response to the targeted support designation.

*Result: met.* A six-week common assessment cycle was in place by the second week of October, with item analysis protocols run in department meetings on October 14, November 25, January 20, March 3, and April 21. Mr. Castellanos wrote the mathematics section of the school's targeted support plan and presented it to the district accountability committee on November 6. Grade 6 and Grade 7 MGPs both moved (54 to 59, and 57 to 60), which I attribute in part to the assessment cycle and to the coaching Mr. Castellanos provided to two teachers new to the grade level.

**Goal 3 — Intervention structure.** Design and staff a supplemental mathematics program for students two or more years below grade level.

*Result: exceeded.* The Saturday academy ran twenty-two sessions and served 96 unduplicated students. Mr. Castellanos recruited four colleagues, wrote the curriculum, secured transportation through a Title I amendment, and taught nineteen of the twenty-two sessions himself. Attendance across the year averaged 71 percent, which is high for a voluntary weekend program in a school where 62 percent of families qualify for free or reduced-price meals and many students have weekend caregiving responsibilities. Academy participants posted an MGP of 66 against a matched non-participant comparison group of 55.
