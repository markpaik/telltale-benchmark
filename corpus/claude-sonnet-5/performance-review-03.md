# Performance Review: Rosa Elena Trujillo

**Title:** Charge Nurse, Progressive Care Unit
**Employee Tenure:** 14 years
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Adaeze Obi, MSN, RN, Nurse Manager, Progressive Care Unit
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

## Goals Set for the Review Period

1. Maintain charge-nurse coverage and staffing safety during periods of traveler turnover and vacancy above 15 percent.
2. Precept a minimum of two new graduate nurses to competency sign-off.
3. Sustain unit patient experience scores at or above the 75th percentile.
4. Pursue Clinical Ladder Level IV application.
5. Support the medication reconciliation audit process following the October 2025 accreditation finding.

## Manager's Narrative Assessment

Rosa Elena Trujillo continues to be the single most reliable clinical and operational anchor of this unit. Against a backdrop of an 18 percent RN vacancy rate and eleven of our 62 budgeted RN positions covered by travelers, Rosa has functioned for much of this period as de facto flow coordinator, not merely charge nurse. The most visible demonstration of this came over the Fourth of July weekend, when the emergency department boarded as many as 40 patients awaiting inpatient beds. Rosa organized hourly huddles across three shifts, reassigned acuity in real time, and worked directly with the house supervisor to open surge capacity without a single reported safety event. I received unsolicited praise from the ED charge nurse and from the hospital's chief nursing officer regarding her composure and clinical judgment during that weekend.

Rosa precepted five new graduate nurses this year, exceeding her goal of two, at a time when the unit had no dedicated educator to support onboarding — a direct consequence of the educator line being eliminated in the January budget reduction. She absorbed this responsibility without complaint and without a corresponding adjustment to her own patient assignment on most shifts, which I recognize as an inequity I am escalating for the coming budget cycle.

Her patient experience scores remain the highest on the unit, at the 84th percentile, reflecting consistent, compassionate bedside communication even during high-acuity, high-boarding conditions. Colleague feedback gathered during peer review cited her as "the person new nurses go to when they're scared," and "someone who tells you the truth about your patient without making you feel small."

Rosa submitted a completed application for Clinical Ladder Level IV this spring, supported by her CCRN certification, precepting log, and unit outcomes data. I have submitted a strong letter of support to the ladder committee; the decision rests with that body and not with this unit's budget. Separately, Rosa applied for the unit educator position when it was posted last fall. That position was eliminated in the January reduction-in-force planning before the posting could be filled, and I want to be direct in this document that the position's elimination was a budget decision made above the unit level, not a reflection of Rosa's qualification for the role, which was strong.

### Employee Self-Assessment (as submitted by Rosa Elena Trujillo)

> "This year tested the unit more than any year I can remember, and I'm proud of how we held together as a team over the Fourth of July, when we had almost no beds and no time to think. I precepted five new nurses this year because someone had to, and I don't regret it, but I want to be honest that doing that job without the educator title or the protected time that used to come with it has been hard to sustain. I still believe I'm ready for Level IV, and I was disappointed the educator role was cut before I had a chance to interview for it. I'd like to talk about what a realistic path looks like from here, because I don't want to be doing two jobs indefinitely without either being recognized."

## Rating

**Overall Rating: Exceeds Expectations**

This rating reflects sustained performance well above the standard expected of a charge nurse: crisis leadership during the July boarding event, precepting volume nearly triple the stated goal, and the unit's top patient experience scores. Under the current labor contract, a rating above satisfactory qualifies Rosa for consideration under the 2.4 percent merit pool this cycle, and I am recommending her for the maximum allocation available to this unit under that pool. This is separate from and does not resolve the Clinical Ladder IV application, which follows its own committee timeline, or the loss of the educator posting, which I have flagged to administration as a structural gap this rating cannot fix.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Continue as primary preceptor of record for incoming residency cohorts, with a request for two hours per week of protected non-clinical precepting time, formally built into the staffing grid rather than absorbed informally.
2. Serve as the unit's designated point person for the ongoing monthly medication reconciliation audit following the October accreditation finding, including monthly variance review with pharmacy.
3. Complete the Clinical Ladder IV interview process; manager will continue to advocate at the committee level.
4. Mentor Sandeep Chaudhary and other second-year residency nurses through independent high-acuity assignments, with particular attention to titration competencies.
5. Partner with manager on a revised staffing proposal to formally recognize precepting and coordination duties in workload calculation, to be presented at the fall budget cycle.

# Performance Review: Denise Kowalczyk

**Title:** Registered Nurse, Progressive Care Unit
**Employee Tenure:** 22 years
**Review Period:** August 1, 2025 – July 31, 2026
**Reviewer:** Adaeze Obi, MSN, RN, Nurse Manager, Progressive Care Unit
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

## Goals Set for the Review Period

1. Complete corrective action plan following medication event review and demonstrate sustained improvement in documentation compliance.
2. Zero repeat medication safety events for the remainder of the review period.
3. Successful return to full clinical duty following medical leave, with a graduated ramp-up plan.
4. Participate fully in high-acuity patient assignments consistent with unit rotation.

## Manager's Narrative Assessment

Denise Kowalczyk's review period must be read in two parts, before and after her twelve-week medical leave, which began in the prior period and concluded with her return to the unit in March.

Prior to her leave, Denise was the subject of two documented medication events, the more serious of which involved a heparin drip dosing error that triggered a rapid response call. No patient harm resulted, but the event was serious enough to warrant a formal corrective action plan, which was opened under the terms of the collective bargaining agreement and completed with Denise's full cooperation in June of this year. I want to note for the record that Denise never contested the findings, engaged constructively in every step of the plan, and did not treat the process adversarially.

The results of that plan are measurable and real. Denise's medication documentation compliance rose from 71 percent to 94 percent over the period under review, verified through the unit's monthly audit process that has been in place since the October accreditation finding on medication reconciliation. There have been no further medication events of any classification since the corrective action plan closed. This is a significant and sustained improvement, and I do not want it understated by what follows.

What follows is this: peer feedback gathered independently from two colleagues, consistent with observations I have made directly, indicates that Denise continues to avoid the sickest patient assignments on the unit when she has discretion to do so, more than a year removed from the original events and four months removed from closing her corrective action plan. This is not insubordination — Denise has not refused an assignment when directly ordered by the charge nurse — but it does represent a pattern of self-selecting toward lower-acuity patients in ways that shift acuity burden onto colleagues, particularly during periods when the unit is already short-staffed at an 18 percent vacancy rate. I raised this pattern with Denise directly in April and again in this review.

Denise's return from leave in March was managed with a graduated ramp-up over three weeks per her physician's release and hospital policy, and by all accounts that transition went smoothly, with no attendance concerns and no lapses in her improved documentation practice.

### Employee Self-Assessment (as submitted by Denise Kowalczyk)

> "Coming back from leave this year on top of everything from the heparin event was the hardest stretch of my career, and I appreciate that Adaeze and the unit gave me room to complete the corrective action plan the right way instead of rushing it. I know my documentation numbers show the work I put in, and I'm proud of that 94 percent. I'll be honest that I still feel my confidence isn't fully back on the sickest patients, and I'd rather say that directly than pretend it isn't true. I'm not avoiding assignments to avoid work — after 22 years I don't need to prove I can work hard — I think I need a structured way to rebuild trust with myself on high-acuity patients, maybe pairing with someone for a stretch, rather than just being told to take the assignment and figure it out."

## Rating

**Overall Rating: Satisfactory (Meets Expectations)**

Denise met the core requirements of her corrective action plan and has had no repeat safety events, which are the threshold findings for this rating. The unresolved pattern of assignment avoidance keeps this rating at satisfactory rather than above, and under the labor contract a satisfactory rating entitles Denise to her scheduled step increase; it does not qualify her for consideration under this year's 2.4 percent merit pool, which is reserved for ratings above satisfactory. I want to be transparent with Denise that closing the corrective action plan restored her standing but did not by itself constitute an above-satisfactory year.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Accept high-acuity assignments in normal rotation without requiring direct charge-nurse assignment override; charge nurses will track assignment distribution monthly to document this objectively.
2. Maintain medication documentation compliance at or above 90 percent, verified through the continuing monthly reconciliation audit.
3. Participate in a structured six-week acuity confidence-building rotation, paired with Rosa Elena Trujillo or another senior preceptor, focused specifically on high-acuity titration and drip management, at Denise's request and with manager support.
4. Zero medication safety events, consistent with the closed corrective action plan's terms.

# Performance Review: Sandeep Chaudhary

**Title:** Registered Nurse, New Graduate Residency Program, Progressive Care Unit
**Employee Tenure:** 7 months (started January 2026)
**Review Period:** January 2026 – July 31, 2026 (partial-year review, residency program cycle)
**Reviewer:** Adaeze Obi, MSN, RN, Nurse Manager, Progressive Care Unit
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

## Goals Set at Residency Start

1. Progress to an independent patient assignment by week 16 of the residency program.
2. Meet attendance expectations consistent with residency program policy.
3. Achieve competency sign-off on all core skills, including high-risk medication titration, on the standard residency timeline.
4. Deliver patient-centered care consistent with unit service standards.

## Manager's Narrative Assessment

Sandeep Chaudhary's first year presents a genuinely mixed picture, and this review is written to be direct about both halves of it, because a residency-year review that only tells one story does not serve him.

On the strength side: Sandeep progressed to a fully independent patient assignment in week fourteen of the residency, two weeks ahead of the sixteen-week program target. That is a real accomplishment, particularly on a unit operating at 18 percent RN vacancy with heavy traveler reliance, where new graduates often face a harder, less consistent precepting environment than the program assumes. He was named specifically, by name, in two separate patient letters this year — one describing his patience with a family member struggling to understand a discharge plan, and another describing him staying past shift end to sit with a dying patient's spouse. Both letters were forwarded to me by the patient experience office unprompted. This is not typical for a new graduate seven months into practice, and it reflects real clinical presence and empathy that I want on the record clearly.

On the concern side: Sandeep called out three shifts during the February patient surge, at a time when the unit was already managing high acuity with limited staff. Residency program policy treats attendance during high-demand periods as a specific competency area precisely because new graduates learn early whether they are part of the team's coverage plan or an exception to it; three call-outs in a single surge period is a pattern I need him to hear as a pattern, not three unrelated events. Separately, and more seriously from a clinical standpoint, Sandeep required a second remediation on high-risk drip titration after an initial competency sign-off did not hold up under direct observation. He completed that second remediation successfully and has since passed unobserved competency checks, but two remediations in one skill area within a single residency year is above what we expect and is the reason his independent assignment, while early by the calendar, has been more closely shadowed on high-acuity patients than the week-fourteen milestone alone would suggest.

I want to be honest that these two threads — genuine clinical promise and real reliability gaps — sit uncomfortably next to each other in the same nurse, and my job this year is to make sure the promise doesn't get lost while the gaps get closed.

### Employee Self-Assessment (as submitted by Sandeep Chaudhary)

> "This year was a lot faster and a lot harder than I expected coming out of school. I'm proud that I got to my own assignment ahead of schedule, and the letters from patients meant more to me than almost anything else that's happened in my career so far — that's the reason I wanted to do this job. I know the February call-outs were bad timing and I'm not going to make excuses about it; I had some things going on outside work that I let affect my attendance and I should have talked to Adaeze about it instead of just calling out. The titration remediation is the thing I think about the most. I passed it the second time and I feel much more solid now, but I know that having to redo it once already means I need to keep proving I'm consistent, not just capable, and I want to be someone people trust with the sickest patients without a second thought."

## Rating

**Overall Rating: Satisfactory (Meets Expectations), with a Formal Development Plan**

Sandeep meets the threshold for a satisfactory rating: he reached independent practice ahead of the program's standard timeline and has demonstrated genuine, verified patient-centered care recognized externally by patients themselves. Under the labor contract, this satisfactory rating entitles him to his scheduled residency-year step progression. It does not rise to an above-satisfactory rating this cycle because of the February attendance pattern and the second titration remediation, both of which are documented competency and reliability concerns rather than one-time events, and both of which keep him outside consideration for the 2.4 percent merit pool this period.

## Goals for the Coming Year

1. Zero unscheduled absences during any declared surge or high-census period, with a standing expectation that any personal circumstance affecting attendance be raised proactively with the charge nurse or manager rather than surfacing as a call-out.
2. Maintain independent, unobserved competency on high-risk drip titration through at least two additional quarterly spot-checks without need for further remediation.
3. Continue paired assignments with a senior preceptor (Rosa Elena Trujillo) on the highest-acuity patients through the end of the calendar year as a confidence and consistency measure, transitioning to fully independent high-acuity assignment on manager's sign-off.
4. Sustain the patient-centered communication practice reflected in this year's patient letters, including continued participation in family and end-of-life communication scenarios as a growth area to build on, not just a strength already proven.

## Appendix C: Definitions of Rating Categories Used in This Review Cycle, Per Labor Contract Article 9

**Exceeds Expectations:** Performance consistently and substantially above the standard expected for the role across the review period, warranting consideration for the discretionary merit pool in addition to the contractual step increase.

**Satisfactory (Meets Expectations):** Performance consistent with the standard expected for the role across the review period, warranting the contractual step increase. Satisfactory performance does not, by itself, qualify an employee for consideration under the discretionary merit pool.

**Needs Improvement:** Performance below the standard expected for the role in one or more material respects, requiring a formal improvement plan and subject to review of contractual step increase eligibility per Article 9, Section 4.

**Unsatisfactory:** Performance substantially below the standard expected for the role, subject to the disciplinary and step-increase provisions of Article 9, Section 5.

None of the three employees reviewed in this document received a rating of Needs Improvement or Unsatisfactory during this cycle. Where individual performance concerns are noted in the narrative sections above — specifically for Denise Kowalczyk and Sandeep Chaudhary — those concerns are addressed through the coming-year goals and, in Sandeep Chaudhary's case, a formal development plan, rather than through a rating reduction, consistent with the documented improvement each employee has already shown during this review period.
