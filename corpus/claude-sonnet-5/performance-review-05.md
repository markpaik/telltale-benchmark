# Review One: Alonzo Ferrer

**Title:** Supervisor, Housing Navigation Program
**Review Period:** July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026
**Reviewer:** Renata Osei, Chief Program Officer
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

### Goals and Outcomes

Alonzo's goals for FY2026 were set at three levels: hit a placement target of 180 households across the eight-person navigation team, continue driving down average time-to-placement from the prior year's baseline of 71 days, and maintain the team's caseload and file quality during a year in which the program director position sat vacant. He exceeded the first two on every measure available to me. The team placed 214 households, 34 above target and a 19 percent overshoot, and cut average time-to-placement to 49 days, a 31 percent reduction achieved primarily through the intake-to-inspection workflow changes he introduced in September and a landlord-relationship tracking system he built without being asked to.

The third goal is harder to score because the job itself changed underneath him. When the program director role went vacant in October, Alonzo absorbed director-level responsibilities — cross-program reporting to funders, participation in the leadership team, sign-off on housing budget lines — for five months, from November through March, without a change in title or compensation. He did this while continuing to carry his own supervisory caseload of eight navigators. I want to be direct in this review that the placement numbers above were produced under that condition, not a normal one, and that matters for how I weigh them.

> **Self-assessment (Ferrer):** *"I said yes to covering the director work because the program needed it, and honestly because I thought it would lead somewhere. I'm proud of what the team did this year — 214 placements is a number we earned, not one we got lucky into. But I've been doing two jobs since November and I need Puente to tell me, plainly, whether one of them is going to become mine. I've asked twice. I'm asking again in this review because I'd rather have the conversation here than have it be a surprise in September."*

I'm noting that quote in full because it is the correct summary of where things stand, and I do not think it should sit only in his self-assessment.

### Audit and Funder Findings

The housing navigation program was not named in the single audit's material weakness finding on subrecipient monitoring; that finding centers on Grants and Compliance's oversight structure. Alonzo's area does intersect with it at one point: two landlord-partner data-sharing agreements under his program were among the file gaps auditors flagged, and the corrective action plan now assigns him joint responsibility, alongside Sunita Kapoor, for getting those agreements executed and filed by the end of Q1 FY2027. This is a modest item relative to the finding as a whole, and I do not read it as a mark against his performance this year, but it is now a compliance obligation attached to his role and I am flagging it here so it is not lost between reviews.

### Incidents and Feedback

I have no incident reports for Alonzo's team this year. Staff feedback gathered during my check-ins describes him as accessible and fair, including from navigators who disagreed with caseload decisions he made. The clearest external signal is the one he raised himself: a peer agency has interviewed him for a role at or above what a formal program director title here would pay, and he has now told me twice, in writing, that he needs clarity by September. I take that at face value. It is not leverage-seeking; it is someone giving fair warning.

### Overall Rating

| Category | Rating |
|---|---|
| Goal achievement (placements, time-to-placement) | Exceeds Expectations |
| Compliance and documentation | Meets Expectations |
| Team leadership and coverage under vacancy | Exceeds Expectations |
| **Overall** | **Exceeds Expectations** |

### What the Salary Freeze Means

Alonzo's current salary is above the board's $55,000 threshold, which means this year's compensation freeze applies to him regardless of rating. The 4 percent adjustment approved for staff below that line is not available to him. I want to be plain in his file about what that means in practice: the strongest performance review I am writing this cycle carries no compensation change, and it lands three weeks before the deadline he has given me. I do not have authority to change board policy in this document, but I am recommending to the Executive Director, as part of this review, that Puente resolve the program director question — through either a formal title and pay change, an interim stipend for director-level duties already performed, or an explicit decision not to fill the role from within — before September 1, and that this recommendation be treated as urgent rather than routine.

### Goals for the Coming Year

1. Resolve the program director role question, in either direction, by September 1, 2026, with the decision documented and communicated directly to Alonzo before any external deadline he faces.
2. Complete the two outstanding landlord data-sharing agreements identified in the audit corrective action plan by the end of Q1 FY2027.
3. Maintain the 49-day average time-to-placement standard, with a stretch target of 45 days, without relying on unsustainable director-level overtime.
4. Formalize the intake-to-inspection workflow he built informally this year into a documented program procedure so it survives any staffing transition.
5. Identify and begin developing a lead navigator to serve as backup coverage, reducing single-person dependency on Alonzo during any future leadership gap.

# Review Two: Sunita Kapoor

**Title:** Manager, Grants and Compliance
**Review Period:** July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026
**Reviewer:** Renata Osei, Chief Program Officer
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

### Goals and Outcomes

Sunita's goals for the year were to submit all nine quarterly federal reports on time, maintain subrecipient monitoring in line with federal award requirements, keep internal budget question turnaround to a defined standard, and continue building Puente's funding portfolio. The results are mixed in a way that requires separating two different stories rather than averaging them into one number.

On reporting timeliness, four of nine quarterly federal reports went in late this year, with delays ranging from eight to twenty-two days past deadline. On subrecipient monitoring, the single audit returned a material weakness, and it names her area directly: auditors found no documented risk assessments for two of Puente's subrecipients, no evidence of the required annual monitoring visits for one of them, and no compensating review process to catch either gap before the audit did. This is the most serious compliance finding Puente has received in the years I have been here, and it sits with the person who holds sole signature authority on the reports it concerns.

Against that, Sunita is the reason Puente is holding an unsolicited $600,000 state award that was not built into any FY2026 plan. She identified the opportunity, wrote the application largely on her own, and secured it in a competitive round with a short turnaround window. That is real and I am not going to let the audit finding erase it from the record.

> **Self-assessment (Kapoor):** *"I'm not going to pretend the late reports and the audit finding aren't mine — they are, and I own the corrective action plan. But I want to name what's true: I am the only person with signature authority on federal reports, I review every subrecipient file myself because there is no one else to do it, and budget questions from four program areas come through me with no support staff underneath. I closed the $600,000 award working nights and weekends because the daytime hours were already spoken for. I'm not asking for the finding to be excused. I'm asking for a second signer and a monitoring calendar to be part of what comes out of this review, not just a warning."*

### Audit and Funder Findings

The material weakness finding requires a formal corrective action plan submitted to the cognizant federal agency, and it increases the likelihood of closer scrutiny — potentially a higher-risk designation — on Puente's next single audit. The corrective action plan Sunita has already drafted commits to documented risk assessments for all subrecipients by September 30, a monitoring visit calendar posted quarterly, and the two outstanding landlord agreements shared with Alonzo Ferrer's team. I have reviewed and support this plan. Separately, the $340,000 federal grant that closed on June 30 was fully closed out with a clean final report, which is worth noting since it is easy, in a year like this one, to let that get lost.

### Incidents and Feedback

Multiple program staff, across at least two departments, have told me independently that budget questions routed to Sunita's office commonly sit unanswered for roughly two weeks. I do not believe this reflects indifference; I believe it reflects one person carrying a compliance, reporting, and finance-liaison workload that was sized for a team. It is, nonetheless, a real problem for program supervisors trying to manage their own budgets in real time, and it needs to be named as such in this review rather than only explained away.

|---|---|
| Regulatory reporting and subrecipient monitoring | Does Not Meet Expectations |
| Resource development | Exceeds Expectations |
| Internal responsiveness | Needs Improvement |
| **Overall** | **Needs Improvement** |

### What the Salary Freeze Means

Sunita's salary is also above the $55,000 threshold, so the freeze applies to her as well, independent of this rating. I want to be explicit that this is a coincidence of the policy line, not a compensation consequence tied to the audit finding — the freeze would apply at her salary level this year regardless of performance, and it would have applied at Alonzo's level too had his rating gone the other direction. The corrective action plan and the reporting improvements required of her this year are mandatory regardless of any compensation question, and should not be read as either punishment or a pass.

### Goals for the Coming Year

1. Submit all quarterly federal reports on time, with a written escalation protocol if a delay becomes unavoidable.
2. Execute the audit corrective action plan in full by its committed dates, including subrecipient risk assessments and the monitoring visit calendar, with quarterly written status updates to me.
3. Identify and train a backup signatory for federal reports so the organization is not structurally dependent on one person for this function.
4. Reduce internal budget question turnaround to five business days, which will likely require a staffing or workflow change I will help her make the case for.
5. Continue building the funding portfolio, with the $600,000 state award as a model, but not at the expense of the compliance timeline above.

# Review Three: Devon Pritchard

**Title:** Supervisor, Family Case Management
**Review Period:** July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2026
**Reviewer:** Renata Osei, Chief Program Officer
**Date Prepared:** July 30, 2026

### Goals and Outcomes

Devon's goals for the year were a placement target of 90, a funder-required file documentation rate of 85 percent, and stabilization of a five-person case management team following two years of relatively low turnover. He was out on approved leave for eight weeks and returned in November, which means roughly a fifth of the review year occurred with him absent and his team functioning under interim coverage. The team closed 41 of the 90 targeted placements, 46 percent of goal, and the file documentation rate finished the year at 62 percent against the funder's required 85 percent.

I am not going to attribute the full shortfall to the leave, because two of his five case managers resigned in the spring — well after his return — citing supervision as the reason, and the team ran below capacity for the balance of the year as a result. Both factors are real, and they are not the same factor. The leave explains a gap early in the year that a strong team could partly absorb. The spring resignations point to something in how the team was being managed after he was back.

> **Self-assessment (Pritchard):** *"I came back from leave in November behind on everything, and I told my team that directly instead of pretending otherwise. I focused what time I had on keeping cases moving and staying present with clients, because that's the part of this job I think actually changes outcomes, and I'd rather be honest that documentation slipped than pretend I split my attention evenly. I know two people left this spring and said it was about supervision, and I've thought about that more than I've talked about it. I don't think I gave either of them enough structure when I was still catching up myself. I'm not asking this review to erase that. I am asking for real support rebuilding the team, because right now three people are covering what five people used to cover, and that is not sustainable going into another year."*

### Audit and Funder Findings

Family case management was not named in the single audit's material weakness finding. The documentation shortfall is a separate matter: the funder's monitoring review this spring flagged the 62 percent file documentation rate as below the 85 percent contractual requirement and issued a corrective notice requiring a written improvement plan, due within 60 days of that notice. This is a funder compliance issue in its own right and needs to be tracked separately from the audit, with its own deadline and its own reporting line to me.

### Incidents and Feedback

The two spring resignations are the most significant staffing event in Devon's area this year, and both departing case managers named supervision — described in exit conversations as inconsistent availability and unclear expectations — as a primary reason for leaving. Against that, the annual participant survey rated Devon the highest of any supervisor at Puente on measures of respect, responsiveness, and being treated as a whole person rather than a case file. Both of these are legitimate data points about the same person, and I am not going to resolve the tension between them by ignoring either one. It is possible to be exceptional with clients and inconsistent with staff, and the record this year says that is what happened.

### What the Salary Freeze Means

I want to note for the file that this rating is not being used as cover for a pay reduction — Puente does not have a mechanism for that, and I would not recommend one here. The rating exists to set direction for the coming year, not to justify a compensation outcome that the freeze already determines.

### Goals for the Coming Year

2. Bring file documentation to the funder's required 85 percent by the corrective notice deadline, with monthly file audits in the interim rather than a single check at year-end.

4. Set a placement target for the coming year that accounts for a full staffing year, to be finalized once the team is rebuilt, rather than carrying the prior target forward unchanged.
5. Use his strength with participants deliberately — through a defined mentoring role for newer case managers on client engagement — so that what clients experience as his strongest quality also becomes a strength of the team he supervises.

## Appendix A: Rating Scale Definitions

Ratings on the preceding reviews are drawn from Puente's standard four-point performance scale, applied at both the category and overall level:

**Exceeds Expectations** — Performance consistently surpassed the goal or standard set for the period, with results attributable to the employee's own initiative or judgment rather than favorable circumstance alone.

**Meets Expectations** — Performance was consistent with the goal or standard set for the period. Minor gaps may exist but did not affect program, compliance, or funder outcomes in a material way.

**Needs Improvement** — Performance fell short of the goal or standard in one or more areas in a way that had a real effect on program, compliance, staff, or participant outcomes, but the shortfall is addressable through a defined plan within the coming review period.

**Does Not Meet Expectations** — Performance fell substantially short of the goal or standard, produced a compliance, funder, or program consequence of significant weight, or repeated a prior year's unresolved gap.

An overall rating is not a mathematical average of category ratings. It reflects the reviewer's judgment of which category or categories carried the greatest weight for the organization during the period, which is why, in more than one review above, an overall rating does not split the difference between a strong category and a weak one.
