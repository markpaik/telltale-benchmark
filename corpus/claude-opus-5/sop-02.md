## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure establishes the method by which Cedar Line Markets removes unsafe, misbranded, or otherwise defective product from commerce, notifies affected customers, and demonstrates to regulators that the removal was complete.

The February 2026 recall of supplier ready-to-eat chicken salad for *Listeria monocytogenes* revealed material failures in the prior version of this procedure. Eleven of thirty-four stores still had recalled product available for sale thirty-one hours after the supplier notice was received. A Washington State Department of Agriculture inspector documented product on the shelf at the Moses Lake store. The mock recall conducted the following month recovered 71 percent of affected units against a standard of 100 percent within four hours. Version 4.0 rewrites the notification cascade, adds a mandatory register block as the first executed step, assigns individual accountability at each of the thirty-four stores, and creates hard reconciliation between quantities received and quantities recovered.

The standard this procedure is written to meet is simple: **product is unsaleable at every register within sixty minutes of classification, physically off the sales floor within four hours, and reconciled to unit level within twenty-four hours.**

## 2. SCOPE

### 2.1 In Scope

This procedure applies to:

- All 34 Cedar Line Markets retail stores in eastern Washington and northern Idaho
- The Airway Heights distribution center
- The Cedar Line commissary
- All product sold, distributed, or donated by Cedar Line Markets, including national brand, private label, direct-store-delivery (DSD), commissary-produced, and in-store-produced items
- Supplier-initiated recalls, FDA- or USDA-initiated recalls, state-initiated actions, and Cedar Line-initiated voluntary withdrawals
- Non-food product recalls affecting general merchandise, health and beauty care, pet products, and consumer electronics carried in the assortment

### 2.2 Out of Scope

- Routine quality complaints not involving a health hazard or regulatory violation. These follow FSQA-SOP-009, Consumer Complaint Handling.
- Routine damaged or short-coded product removal. See OPS-SOP-022, Code Date Management.
- Supplier quality holds on product that has not left the DC and has not shipped to any store. These follow FSQA-SOP-011, Receiving Hold and Rejection, unless the hold is escalated to a recall by the supplier or FSQA.

### 2.3 Regulatory Basis

- 21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C — Recalls
- 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart J — Establishment, Maintenance, and Availability of Records (FSMA 204 traceability where applicable)
- 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart H — Reportable Food Registry
- 9 CFR Part 418 — USDA FSIS Recall procedures for meat and poultry
- WAC 16-160 — Washington State Department of Agriculture Food Processing
- Idaho Food Code, IDAPA 16.02.19

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Class I Recall** — A situation in which there is a reasonable probability that use of or exposure to the product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death. Examples: undeclared major allergen, *Listeria monocytogenes* in ready-to-eat food, *E. coli* O157:H7, botulism risk, foreign material capable of causing injury.

**Class II Recall** — A situation in which use of or exposure to the product may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or where the probability of serious consequences is remote.

**Class III Recall** — A situation in which use of or exposure to the product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences. Examples: minor labeling defects, net weight shortfalls.

**Market Withdrawal** — Removal or correction of a distributed product that involves a minor violation not subject to legal action, or that involves no violation at all. Quality defects, spoilage complaints, and supplier-elected removals with no health hazard are market withdrawals.

**Stock Recovery** — Removal or correction of product that has not left the direct control of Cedar Line Markets, meaning product still located at the DC or commissary that has not shipped to any store.

**Recall Coordinator** — The FSQA staff member designated to run day-to-day recall execution. Primary is the FSQA Manager. Secondary is the FSQA Specialist. Tertiary is the Director of FSQA.

**Recall Notice** — Any written or verbal communication alleging that product in Cedar Line's possession or previously sold by Cedar Line is subject to removal.

**Register Block** — An item file flag that causes the point-of-sale system to refuse the sale of a UPC at every lane in every store, displaying "DO NOT SELL — RECALL — CALL FSQA."

**Effectiveness Check** — A verification that a party who received recalled product has received the recall notice and has taken the specified action.

**DSD (Direct Store Delivery)** — Product delivered by a vendor directly to a store without passing through the Airway Heights DC. Cedar Line carries DSD from 46 vendors.

**Affected Units** — The total quantity of recalled product that Cedar Line received, calculated from DC receiving records, DSD invoices, or commissary production records.

**Recovered Units** — Product physically located, segregated, and counted during the recall.

**Sold Units** — Affected units minus recovered units minus documented shrink. Sold units drive the customer notification decision.

**Lot Code** — Any supplier-applied identifier permitting identification of a production run: lot number, batch code, Julian date, best-by date, establishment number plus date, or case code.

**Health Hazard Evaluation (HHE)** — The written assessment supporting a recall classification.

- Execute or personally supervise the shelf pull

- Apply recall hold tags

- Place a system hold on the affected item within 30 minutes
- Physically locate and move product to the quarantine cage
- Pull lot-level receiving records and shipment history for all 34 stores
- Provide the recall coordinator with quantities received, quantities shipped by store, and quantities on hand
- Stop all outbound movement of the affected item
- Intercept in-transit loads where possible

### 4.8 Customer Care

- Staff the recall phone line
- Process customer refunds without receipt for recalled product
- Log all customer contacts, including any illness reports, on the recall contact log
- Route any illness or injury allegation to the Director of FSQA within 15 minutes

### 4.9 Marketing

- Execute the loyalty database notification
- Post recall notices to the website and app
- Draft press releases for approval
- Produce store-level recall signage
- Remove the affected item from any live or scheduled advertising

### 4.10 Commissary Manager

- Identify all production lots using the affected ingredient
- Provide production and distribution records by lot
- Halt production using the affected ingredient
- Segregate finished goods and work in process

## 5. PROCEDURE

### 5.1 Step 1 — Receipt and Logging of Notice

**5.1.1** Recall notices arrive by one of the following channels:

| Channel | Monitored By | Monitoring Window |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier notification platform | Recall coordinator | Continuous, with mobile push alerts |
| FSQA shared inbox (fsqa-recalls@cedarline.com) | Recall coordinator | Continuous, with mobile push alerts |
| FSQA recall hotline (509-555-0147) | Recall coordinator, then on-call FSQA | 24 hours |
| Category manager forwarding a supplier call | Category manager | Business hours |
| Store director report of vendor notice | Store director | Store hours |
| Regulatory agency contact | Director of FSQA | Continuous |

**5.1.2** Any Cedar Line employee who receives information suggesting a recall shall forward it to the FSQA recall hotline immediately and shall not wait for confirmation. There is no penalty for forwarding a notice that turns out not to be a recall.

**5.1.3** Within **15 minutes** of receipt, the recall coordinator opens a record in the recall log and assigns a recall number in the format `CLM-YYYY-NNN` (example: CLM-2026-014).

**5.1.4** The recall coordinator captures the following minimum data set. Missing fields are marked "PENDING" and pursued in parallel with execution. **Execution does not wait for a complete data set.**

- Product name and brand
- UPC(s) — all sizes and variants
- Cedar Line item number(s)
- Supplier name and contact
- Lot codes, best-by dates, establishment numbers
- Reason for recall
- Recalling firm's classification, if stated
- Whether FDA or USDA has been notified
- Date of first shipment and last shipment
- Whether any illness has been reported

**5.1.5** If the notice arrives verbally, the recall coordinator requests written confirmation but proceeds on the verbal notice.

---

### 5.2 Step 2 — Classification

**5.2.1** The Director of FSQA classifies the event within **30 minutes** of logging. If the Director is unreachable within 30 minutes, the recall coordinator classifies at the more severe of the supplier's stated class or the coordinator's own assessment, and the Director confirms or amends when reached. **Under no circumstance does execution wait for classification confirmation.**

**5.2.2** Classification uses this decision table:

| Condition | Class |
|---|---|
| Undeclared milk, egg, fish, shellfish, tree nut, peanut, wheat, soy, or sesame | I |
| Pathogen in ready-to-eat food (*Listeria*, *Salmonella*, *E. coli* O157:H7, *Cronobacter*) | I |
| Foreign material capable of laceration, choking, or dental injury | I |
| Undeclared sulfites above 10 ppm | I |
| Botulism risk, including underprocessed canned or vacuum-packed goods | I |
| Pathogen in product intended to be cooked, with cooking instructions present | II |
| Undeclared minor allergen or non-allergenic ingredient | II |
| Nutrient content deviation with potential health effect | II |
| Product below labeled net weight | III |
| Minor label graphics or ingredient order error, no health effect | III |
| Product quality, spoilage, or appearance defect, no health hazard | Market Withdrawal |

**5.2.3** When in doubt, classify higher. A Class II event executed as a Class I costs labor. A Class I executed as a Class II costs a public health failure.

**5.2.4** The Director of FSQA documents the classification and its rationale on the Health Hazard Evaluation form (FSQA-FRM-014-A) within four hours.

---

### 5.3 Step 3 — Notification Cascade

**5.3.1** Notification clocks start at **classification** or at **T+30 minutes from logging**, whichever comes first.

| Recipient | Class I | Class II | Class III / Withdrawal | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Register block requested | 15 min | 30 min | 2 hours | Item file team, phone + ticket |
| Director of FSQA | Immediate | 30 min | 2 hours | Phone |
| SVP Retail Operations | 30 min | 2 hours | Next business day | Phone |
| All 34 store directors | 30 min | 1 hour | 4 hours | Recall alert (SMS + email + app push) |
| DC inventory control | 30 min | 1 hour | 4 hours | Phone + email |
| Category manager | 30 min | 1 hour | 4 hours | Phone + email |
| Commissary manager | 30 min | 1 hour | 4 hours | Phone + email |
| Customer care supervisor | 1 hour | 2 hours | 4 hours | Phone + email |
| Marketing | 1 hour | 2 hours | 4 hours | |
| Legal counsel | 2 hours | 4 hours | As needed | Email |
| Regulators | See 5.11 | See 5.11 | See 5.11 | Per 5.11 |

**5.3.2 The recall alert.** Store directors receive a single message containing: recall number, class, product name, brand, all UPCs, all lot codes, all locations to check, the required actions, the response deadline, and the recall coordinator's direct line. The message ends with the sentence: **"Reply ACK plus your store number to this message now. Do not begin the pull before you acknowledge."**

**5.3.3 Acknowledgment tracking.** The recall coordinator tracks acknowledgments against a roster of all 34 stores on the recall tracking workbook. This is the single control that failed in February.

- At **T+30 minutes**, the coordinator calls the store phone of every unacknowledged store.
- At **T+45 minutes**, the coordinator calls the store director's mobile.
- At **T+60 minutes**, the coordinator calls the assistant store director, then the district manager.
- At **T+90 minutes**, the coordinator escalates the unacknowledged store to the SVP of Retail Operations by phone, and the SVP contacts the store personally.

**5.3.4** Acknowledgment by any means other than the tracked reply — a verbal "got it" in passing, a forwarded email, a third party's assurance — does not count. Only a logged ACK or a coordinator-documented phone confirmation closes the store on the roster.

---

### 5.4 Step 4 — Register Block

**5.4.1** The register block is **the first executed action of any recall**, ahead of the shelf pull, because it stops sale at every lane in every store simultaneously and does not depend on any store's response time.

**5.4.2** The recall coordinator contacts the item file team by phone and submits a written block request containing every affected UPC. For Class I, the phone call precedes the written request.

**5.4.3** The item file team applies the block and confirms completion to the recall coordinator in writing, with a timestamp. Target: **within 15 minutes of request for Class I.**

**5.4.4** The recall coordinator verifies the block by directing three geographically separated stores — one Spokane metro, one rural Washington, one Idaho — to scan the affected UPC at a register and photograph the screen. The photographs are attached to the recall file. Verification is complete within **30 minutes of block confirmation**.

**5.4.5** Where lot-specific product is recalled but the UPC covers both affected and unaffected lots, the block is applied to the **entire UPC**. Cashiers cannot verify lot codes at the lane. Unaffected lots are released only after the pull is complete and store-level segregation is verified.

**5.4.6** The block is removed only by written authorization of the Director of FSQA, after reconciliation is complete.

---

### 5.5 Step 5 — Store-Level Shelf Pull

**5.5.1** On acknowledgment, the store director immediately assigns the pull. The store director personally verifies completion; the store director does not delegate verification.

**5.5.2 Locations to check.** Every store checks all of the following and records each on the response form. A location that does not exist at that store is marked N/A.

1. Primary shelf location
2. Secondary and promotional displays, endcaps, and ad stacks
3. Cooler and freezer sales floor cases
4. Backstock in the backroom
5. Receiving dock and staged pallets
6. Cooler and freezer backstock
7. Salvage and damaged goods cage
8. Employee purchase or markdown rack
9. Deli, bakery, meat, and produce departments where the item may be an ingredient
10. Foodservice and hot bar preparation areas
11. Store-level donation staging (food bank pickup)
12. Sampling and demo station stock
13. Online order staging and pickup lockers
14. Any offsite storage trailer

**5.5.3** Recovered product is placed in the store's designated recall quarantine location — a marked area in the backroom, physically separated, or a locked cage where one exists. Each case or unit receives a red RECALL HOLD tag (FSQA-FRM-014-C) marked with the recall number, date, time, and the name of the person who pulled it.

**5.5.4** Product held for recall is **not** to be donated, discarded, marked down, sold to employees, given to staff, fed to animals, or moved to any location other than quarantine until disposition is authorized under 5.9.

**5.5.5 Store recall response form (FSQA-FRM-014-B).** The store director submits within the deadline:

- Store number and store director name
- Recall number
- Time pull started, time pull completed
- Units recovered by UPC and lot code
- Each of the 14 locations checked, with initials
- Whether the item was on ad in the last 30 days
- Whether the item was used as an ingredient in any store-produced item, and if so, which items and what quantity
- Signature attesting the pull is complete

**5.5.6 Deadlines for form submission from acknowledgment:**

| Class | Deadline |
|---|---|
| I | 4 hours |
| II | 8 hours |
| III | 24 hours |
| Market withdrawal | 48 hours |

**5.5.7 Zero-count stores.** A store that finds no product still submits the form with "0" recorded and all locations initialed. A store that does not submit a form is treated as non-compliant, not as a zero-count store. In February, six of the eleven late stores had assumed no form was required because they believed they had no product.

**5.5.8 Signage.** For Class I and Class II recalls, marketing pushes a store notice to every store within two hours. Store directors print and post it at the customer service desk and at the shelf location where the product was displayed. The notice stays posted for 30 days.

---

### 5.6 Step 6 — DC Quarantine

**5.6.1** Within **30 minutes** of notification, DC inventory control places a system hold in the warehouse management system on all affected item numbers, blocking picking, replenishment, and shipping.

**5.6.2** DC inventory control physically locates all affected product across pick slots, reserve racking, cold storage, freezer, repack, and returns staging. Product is moved to the recall quarantine cage, tagged, and counted by lot.

**5.6.3 Records to be pulled within 4 hours:**

- All purchase orders for the affected item covering the recall lot window plus 30 days on either side
- All receiving records with lot codes and quantities received by date
- All outbound shipment records by store, with date, quantity, and lot where lot-level capture exists
- Current on-hand quantity by lot
- Any transfers to other stores, to the commissary, or to donation

**5.6.4 In-transit intercept.** DC inventory control identifies any load carrying the affected item that has departed. The transportation supervisor contacts the driver and directs return to the DC, or directs the receiving store to refuse the item at the door. Refused product is segregated at the store as recall quarantine, not returned to the shelf.

**5.6.5** The DC provides the recall coordinator a distribution table showing, for each of the 34 stores, the quantity of affected lots shipped. This is the denominator for reconciliation.

**5.6.6** Where lot-level receiving records do not permit exact attribution — for example, a store received cases from a mixed pallet spanning two lots — the DC reports the **maximum possible quantity** attributable to that store. Reconciliation is conservative.

---

### 5.7 Step 7 — Reconciliation

**5.7.1** Reconciliation is the arithmetic that proves the recall worked. The recall coordinator maintains it in the recall tracking workbook and updates it as store forms arrive.

**5.7.2 The reconciliation formula:**

```
Units Received (DC + DSD + commissary production)
  − Units Recovered at Store
  − Units Recovered at DC
  − Units Recovered at Commissary
  − Units Documented as Shrink or Prior Disposal
  = Units Presumed Sold
```

**5.7.3** For each of the 34 stores, the workbook shows: units shipped, units recovered, variance, and variance as a percentage. A store with recovery below 60 percent of units shipped, where the item is not a fast mover, is queried by the coordinator by phone and re-checks its locations.

**5.7.4** "Documented as shrink" requires a shrink record with date. An assertion that "it must have sold" is not documentation; that quantity is counted as presumed sold.

**5.7.5** Reconciliation for Class I is complete within **24 hours** of classification. Class II within 48 hours. Class III and withdrawals within five business days.

**5.7.6** The reconciliation table is the core exhibit in the closure report and the document a regulator will ask for first.

---

### 5.8 Step 8 — Customer Notification

**5.8.1** Customer notification through the loyalty database is **mandatory** for all Class I recalls in which any unit is presumed sold. It is at the discretion of the Director of FSQA for Class II, and is not performed for Class III or market withdrawals unless directed by a regulator or supplier.

**5.8.2** Marketing queries the loyalty database (1.2 million cardholders) for all transactions containing the affected UPC(s) within the recall date window, plus a three-day buffer on each end.

**5.8.3 Contact hierarchy** — each identified household is contacted by all available channels:

| Priority | Channel | Timing (Class I) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | SMS to mobile on file | Within 4 hours of classification |
| 2 | Email | Within 4 hours |
| 3 | App push notification | Within 4 hours |
| 4 | Automated outbound voice call | Within 12 hours |
| 5 | Postal mail | Within 3 business days, if no other channel exists |

**5.8.4 Message content.** Every customer message states: the product name and brand, the UPC, the affected lot codes and dates, the hazard in plain language, the instruction to discard the product or return it to any Cedar Line store, the statement that a full refund is available without a receipt, the recall phone number, and a link to the website recall page. The message does not minimize, does not use the phrase "out of an abundance of caution" for Class I, and does not describe the event as anything other than a recall.

**5.8.5** For Class I, marketing posts the notice to the Cedar Line website and app within **two hours** of classification, and to the company's social channels within four hours.

**5.8.6** A press release is issued for any Class I recall involving product sold to customers who cannot be identified through the loyalty database in a proportion exceeding 25 percent of presumed sold units, or where directed by a regulator or the recalling firm. The SVP of Retail Operations and Marketing jointly approve; legal counsel reviews.

**5.8.7** Customer care logs every inbound contact with name, phone, product purchased, whether product was consumed, and whether illness is alleged. Any illness allegation is routed to the Director of FSQA within **15 minutes** and is reported to the recalling firm and to WSDA or the appropriate health jurisdiction the same day.

**5.8.8** Refunds for recalled product are issued without receipt at any store, at the customer service desk, to a maximum of the current retail price times the quantity claimed. No manager approval is required.

### 5.9 Step 9 — Effectiveness Checks

**5.9.1 The 24-hour check.** At 24 hours after classification, the recall coordinator verifies for each of the 34 stores:

- The response form was received and is complete
- The register block is verified active by test scan at that store
- Photographic evidence of the empty shelf location, submitted by the store director
- Photographic evidence of the quarantine area with tagged product
- Verbal confirmation from the store director by phone call, not text

**5.9.2** For Class I recalls, the 24-hour check includes an **unannounced physical audit at a minimum of six stores** — at least 18 percent of the fleet — selected to include the two stores with the highest presumed-sold variance, two randomly selected, and two that were late in the most recent prior recall. The audit is conducted by FSQA staff or by a district manager who is not the direct supervisor of the audited store. The auditor walks all 14 locations from 5.5.2 independently.

**5.9.3 The 72-hour check.** At 72 hours, the recall coordinator verifies:

- Reconciliation is complete and variances are explained
- All quarantined product is accounted for and disposition is underway
- Customer notification metrics: messages sent, delivery rate, open rate, inbound contacts, refunds issued
- No affected product has been received since the recall began
- Signage remains posted

**5.9.4 Failure standard.** A store fails an effectiveness check if product is found on the sales floor, if the response form was falsified, or if the quarantine is not physically segregated. Any failure is reported to the SVP of Retail Operations within one hour of discovery, triggers a full re-check of that store, and is documented in the store director's performance record.

**5.9.5** A recall is not closed while any effectiveness check is outstanding.

---

### 5.10 Step 10 — Disposition and Credit

**5.10.1** Disposition options, in order of preference:

1. **Return to supplier** — where the supplier has provided a return authorization and pays freight
2. **Destruction at store** — for small quantities of low value, witnessed and documented
3. **Consolidation at DC and destruction** — for larger quantities, in a single documented event
4. **Denaturing and landfill** — where required by the hazard or regulator
5. **Diversion to non-food use** — only with written FSQA and regulator approval

**5.10.2** Class I product is **never** donated, sold for animal feed, sold for salvage, or reworked. Class I product is destroyed or returned to the supplier for destruction.

**5.10.3** Store-level destruction requires two employees: one to destroy, one to witness. Both sign the disposition record (FSQA-FRM-014-D) recording the recall number, product, quantity, method, date, time, and location. A photograph of the destroyed product is attached.

**5.10.4** Consolidated destruction at the DC is documented with a certificate of destruction from the disposal vendor, retained in the recall file.

**5.10.5** No product leaves quarantine without a disposition authorization signed by the Director of FSQA.

**5.10.6 Credit.** The category manager files a claim with the supplier within five business days of reconciliation, covering: product cost at landed cost, store and DC labor at the standard recall labor rate, freight, destruction fees, customer refunds issued, and customer notification costs. The claim is supported by the reconciliation table and disposition records. The category manager reports claim status to the recall coordinator at 30, 60, and 90 days.

**5.10.7** Store shrink for recalled product is coded to the recall shrink account with the recall number in the reference field, so that store P&L is not charged for supplier-caused loss.

---

### 5.11 Step 11 — Regulatory Reporting

**5.11.1** The Director of FSQA is the sole point of contact for regulators. No other employee speaks to a regulator about a recall. Store directors receiving a regulatory visit call the Director of FSQA immediately, extend full cooperation, provide requested records, and refer all substantive questions to FSQA.

**5.11.2 Reporting obligations:**

| Trigger | Agency | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Class I recall of any product | WSDA Food Safety Program | 24 hours of classification |
| Class I recall affecting Idaho stores | Idaho Dept. of Health and Welfare | 24 hours |
| Reportable food, Cedar Line as responsible party | FDA Reportable Food Registry | 24 hours of determination |
| Recall of USDA-regulated meat or poultry we manufactured | USDA FSIS District Office | 24 hours |
| Any illness allegation linked to recalled product | WSDA and local health jurisdiction | Same day |
| Commissary-produced item recall | WSDA Food Processing | 24 hours |

**5.11.3** Where the recall is initiated by a supplier and Cedar Line is a downstream retailer, the supplier holds the primary reporting obligation. Cedar Line still notifies WSDA for Class I events affecting Washington stores, and confirms in writing that the supplier has notified FDA or FSIS.

**5.11.4** Regulator communications are in writing where possible. Verbal communications are memorialized in a memo to file the same day, recording date, time, agency, individual, and content.

**5.11.5** The Director of FSQA provides the closure report to any regulator who requests it.

---

### 5.12 Step 12 — Closure

**5.12.1** A recall closes when: all effectiveness checks pass, reconciliation is complete with all variances explained, disposition is documented for all recovered product, customer notification metrics are recorded, regulator reporting is complete, and the register block has been removed or the item permanently discontinued.

**5.12.2** The recall coordinator drafts the **closure report** containing: summary, timeline with timestamps for every milestone, reconciliation table by store, effectiveness check results, customer notification metrics, disposition records, regulatory correspondence, root cause where known, corrective actions, and cost summary.

**5.12.3** The Director of FSQA signs the closure report. The SVP of Retail Operations countersigns for Class I.

**5.12.4** A post-recall review is held within 10 business days of closure for every Class I recall and every recall in which any store missed a deadline. Attendance: Director of FSQA, recall coordinator, SVP of Retail Operations, affected category manager, DC manager, and two store directors. Output is a corrective action log with owners and dates, reviewed at the next quarterly FSQA meeting.

## 6. EXCEPTIONS AND SPECIAL CASES

### 6.1 After-Hours Notices

**6.1.1** Recall notices arriving between store close and 5:00 a.m. are handled by the FSQA on-call phone, which rotates weekly among the recall coordinator, FSQA specialist, and Director of FSQA. The on-call number is published in the recall binder and on the intranet.

**6.1.2** For a **Class I** notice received after hours:

- The register block is requested immediately. The item file on-call technician is reachable 24 hours at the number in the recall binder. This action does not wait for morning.
- Store directors are alerted immediately by SMS, with the notation "PULL AT OPEN — ACKNOWLEDGE NOW."
- Store directors acknowledge on receipt regardless of hour. Acknowledgment is not optional overnight.
- Stores operating 24 hours (currently Spokane Valley, Coeur d'Alene North, and Kennewick) execute the pull immediately using overnight crew, with the overnight lead completing the response form.
- All other stores execute the pull before the doors open, and the response form is due within two hours of open.
- The SVP of Retail Operations is called, not texted, for any after-hours Class I.

**6.1.3** For a **Class II** notice after hours, the register block is requested immediately and store alerts are sent at 5:00 a.m. with a four-hour response window.

**6.1.4** For **Class III and market withdrawals**, notice is queued to the next business morning.

**6.1.5** The recall coordinator documents the time of receipt and the time of each after-hours action. Overnight delay in the register block is a reportable procedural failure.

---

### 6.2 Direct-Store-Delivery Vendors

**6.2.1** DSD product from 46 vendors never passes through the DC. There is no warehouse receiving record and no distribution table. This is the largest blind spot in the recall system and requires the compensating controls below.

**6.2.2 Determining affected units.** In the absence of DC records, the recall coordinator establishes affected quantity from, in order of preference:

1. Vendor-supplied delivery records by store, requested from the vendor within one hour of notification
2. Store-level DSD receiving logs, which every store maintains at the receiving door
3. Store scan sales data for the affected UPC over the recall window
4. Where none of the above resolves quantity, the store's physical count of recovered product plus scan-sold units is used as the affected quantity, and the reconciliation is annotated as estimated

**6.2.3 Vendor obligation.** Every DSD vendor agreement requires the vendor to notify Cedar Line FSQA of any recall within two hours of the vendor's own notification, and to provide store-level delivery quantities within four hours of request. The category manager enforces this. Failure to provide records within 24 hours is a documented vendor performance failure reported to the category manager and the Director of FSQA.

**6.2.4 Vendor-executed pulls.** DSD vendors frequently offer to perform the shelf pull themselves. **A vendor-performed pull does not satisfy this procedure.** Cedar Line store personnel execute and verify the pull independently, complete the response form, and count recovered units, regardless of whether the vendor also visits. Vendor-recovered product is counted and documented by the store before the vendor removes it, on a signed vendor pickup receipt (FSQA-FRM-014-E).

**6.2.5** DSD product is subject to the same register block, the same four-hour Class I pull deadline, and the same effectiveness checks as warehouse product.

**6.2.6** The receiving lead notifies the DSD vendor's route driver in writing at the door to stop delivery of the affected item, and records the notification on the DSD receiving log.

---

### 6.3 Commissary Items Without Supplier Lot Codes

**6.3.1** Commissary-produced deli items and store-produced items frequently carry only a pack date or a use-by date rather than a supplier lot code. Where the recall concerns an **ingredient** used in commissary production, the following applies.

**6.3.2** The commissary manager, within **two hours** of notification, identifies:

- Every production batch that used the affected ingredient lot
- Where ingredient lot attribution to a specific batch is not possible, **every batch produced from the date the affected ingredient lot was first received through the date it was fully consumed**, expanded by one production day on each end
- All finished goods produced from those batches, by item and by pack date
- Distribution of those finished goods to each store
- Any remaining work in process and raw ingredient

**6.3.3** In the absence of ingredient-level traceability, the recall scope defaults to the **full date range**, not to a narrower estimate. A narrower scope requires written justification approved by the Director of FSQA and supported by production records.

**6.3.4** Commissary production using the affected ingredient halts immediately. Production resumes on written FSQA authorization after the ingredient is confirmed clear or replaced.

**6.3.5** Commissary items are recalled by **item number and pack date range**, and the register block is applied to the commissary UPC for the full pack date range. Because the register cannot distinguish pack dates, the block covers the whole item until the pull is verified complete at all 34 stores.

**6.3.6** Store-produced items made from a recalled commissary item — for example, a sandwich assembled in-store from commissary salad — are identified by the store director on the response form under section 5.5.5 and are pulled and destroyed as part of the same recall.

**6.3.7** Because Cedar Line is the manufacturer of commissary product, Cedar Line holds the primary regulatory reporting obligation for commissary recalls. The Director of FSQA notifies WSDA within 24 hours and evaluates Reportable Food Registry obligations within 24 hours of determination.

### 6.4 Voluntary Market Withdrawals

**6.4.1** A market withdrawal is used where a defect exists but presents no health hazard: quality complaints, off-flavor, poor appearance, packaging defects without contamination risk, supplier-elected removals, or short-shelf-life concerns.

**6.4.2** A market withdrawal is initiated by the Director of FSQA, or by a category manager with written FSQA concurrence. The concurrence is recorded on the recall log.

**6.4.3** Withdrawals use the same numbering, logging, and tracking as recalls, with the prefix `CLM-YYYY-NNN-W`.

**6.4.4 Modified requirements:**

| Requirement | Recall | Market Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Register block | Yes | Yes |
| Store notification clock | 30 min – 4 hr | 4 hours |
| Pull deadline | 4 – 24 hr | 48 hours |
| Response form | Required | Required |
| 24-hour effectiveness check | Required | Not required |
| 72-hour effectiveness check | Required | Required, simplified |
| Reconciliation | Required | Required |
| Customer notification | Class I mandatory | Not performed |
| Regulator notification | Per 5.11 | Not required |
| Press release | Per 5.8.6 | Never |
| Disposition | Per 5.10 | May include donation or salvage where wholesome |
| Closure report | Full | Abbreviated |

**6.4.5** If at any point during a withdrawal a health hazard is identified, the event is immediately reclassified as a recall, all recall clocks restart at the moment of reclassification, and the Director of FSQA is notified without delay.

**6.4.6** Withdrawn product that remains wholesome may be donated with written FSQA approval and the receiving organization's written acknowledgment of the defect.

## 7. MOCK RECALLS

**7.1** The recall coordinator conducts a mock recall **quarterly**, at least one of which annually is unannounced and initiated outside normal business hours.

**7.2** Over any rolling 12 months, mock recalls cover: one DC-distributed item, one DSD item, one commissary item, and one after-hours scenario.

**7.3 Performance standards:**

| Metric | Standard |
|---|---|
| Register block active | 100% of lanes within 15 minutes |
| Store acknowledgment | 34 of 34 within 30 minutes |
| Response forms received | 34 of 34 within 4 hours |
| Units reconciled | 100% within 24 hours |
| Traceability from store back to supplier lot | 100% within 4 hours |

**7.4** Any mock recall falling short of standard triggers a written corrective action plan within five business days, approved by the Director of FSQA, and a repeat mock recall within 30 days.

**7.5** Store directors whose stores fail two consecutive mock recalls complete retraining on this procedure, documented in the training record, before the next quarterly exercise.

---

## 8. TRAINING

**8.1** All store directors, assistant store directors, receiving leads, DC inventory control staff, category managers, customer care representatives, and commissary supervisors complete training on this procedure within 30 days of the effective date and annually thereafter.

**8.2** New hires in these roles complete training within 14 days of assuming the role.

**8.3** Training records are retained by Human Resources and are auditable by FSQA and by regulators.

**8.4** Training includes a practical exercise: locating an item's lot code, completing the response form, and identifying all 14 check locations in the trainee's own store.
