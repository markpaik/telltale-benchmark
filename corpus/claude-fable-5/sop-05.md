## 2. Purpose

This procedure establishes a single, districtwide process for receiving, dating, logging, deciding upon, and completing initial special education evaluation referrals, and for convening the eligibility meeting and developing the initial Individualized Education Program (IEP) for eligible students. It exists to ensure that:

1. Every referral, regardless of the form in which it arrives, is date-stamped, logged, and tracked from the day of receipt.
2. The district's decision whether to evaluate is made and communicated within 25 school days of the referral, as required by WAC 392-172A-03005.
3. Initial evaluations are completed within 35 school days of receipt of parental consent (or of the decision to evaluate where consent is not required to begin), as required by WAC 392-172A-03005 and 392-172A-03015.
4. Prior written notice (PWN) is issued at every decision point required by WAC 392-172A-05010, in the parent's native language or other mode of communication.
5. An IEP meeting is held and an initial IEP is in effect within 30 calendar days of the eligibility determination, as required by WAC 392-172A-03105.
6. Exception situations — mid-evaluation transfers, unavailable students, revoked consent, agreed extensions, end-of-year referrals, private school and out-of-district students, and disabilities suspected during disciplinary proceedings — are handled consistently and documented.
7. The district satisfies the April 2026 OSPI Corrective Action Order, including the requirement for monthly timeline reporting for twelve months.

This procedure is written so that a staff member new to the district can execute each step without needing to ask a question. Where judgment is required, the procedure states who exercises it and what evidence must document it.

---

## 3. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- All 19 Northline Public Schools buildings and all district programs.
- All initial special education evaluation referrals for students ages 3 through 21, including students enrolled in district schools, students placed by their parents in private schools located within district boundaries, students experiencing homelessness, students in foster care, and students who are the subject of child find activities.
- All staff who touch any part of the referral-to-IEP process: front office staff, registrars, building administrators, school counselors, general education teachers, school psychologists, speech-language pathologists (SLPs), occupational and physical therapists, special education teachers, school nurses, the special education records clerk, and Special Education Department administrators.

This procedure does **not** apply to:

- Reevaluations of students already eligible for special education (see SPED-SOP-002, when issued).
- Section 504 referrals, except that a Section 504 referral in which a disability requiring specially designed instruction is suspected must be converted to a special education referral under Section 6.1 of this procedure.
- Part C (birth-to-three) referrals, except transition referrals under Section 6.9.5.

## 4. Definitions

**Business day.** Monday through Friday, excluding district-observed holidays.

**Calendar day.** Every day, including weekends, holidays, and school breaks. The 30-day IEP timeline runs in calendar days.

**School day.** Any day, including a partial day, on which students are in attendance at school for instructional purposes. School days do **not** include weekends, holidays, breaks, or summer recess unless the student is enrolled in a district summer program in which students district-wide are in attendance. The 25-day referral decision timeline and the 35-day evaluation timeline run in school days. Weather closure days that are not made up as student days do not count.

**Referral.** Any written or oral request that a student be evaluated for special education, from any source, in any format. A referral exists the moment it is received, whether or not it is on form SE-100.

**Referral date (Day 0).** The date the referral is first received by any district employee. The 25-school-day clock starts on the first school day on or after the referral date.

**Consent date.** The date the district receives the parent's signed consent for initial evaluation. The 35-school-day clock starts on the first school day on or after the consent date.

**Parent.** As defined in WAC 392-172A-01125: biological or adoptive parent, foster parent, guardian, individual acting in place of a parent, or surrogate parent.

**Prior written notice (PWN).** The written notice required by WAC 392-172A-05010 whenever the district proposes or refuses to initiate or change the identification, evaluation, educational placement, or provision of FAPE.

**Evaluation group.** The group of qualified professionals and the parent who review existing data, plan the evaluation, and determine eligibility (WAC 392-172A-03020).

**Case manager (evaluation).** The school psychologist (or, for communication-only referrals, the SLP) assigned to coordinate a specific evaluation and personally accountable for its timeline.

**Timeline dashboard.** The district's tracking spreadsheet/database maintained by the special education records clerk, containing one row per referral with all dates defined in Section 7.

**Skyward.** The district student information system; source of enrollment, attendance, and demographic data, including home language.

**IEP system.** The district's electronic special education document platform; the system of record for referrals, consent forms, PWNs, evaluation reports, and IEPs.

**SE-100.** The district's standard referral form, available in paper at every front office, on the district website, and in the top nine home languages.

## 5. Responsibilities

**Director of Special Education (Yvonne Castellanos).** Owns this procedure. Assigns evaluation case managers districtwide during the psychologist staffing shortage. Reviews the timeline dashboard weekly. Approves or denies all extension agreements. Submits the monthly timeline report to OSPI by the 10th of each month through August 2027. Ensures compensatory services determinations ordered by OSPI are completed and documented. Initiates annual review of this procedure.

**Deputy Superintendent (Dr. Malia Fonoti).** Approves this procedure and all revisions. Receives the monthly timeline report before OSPI submission. Resolves cross-department disputes (e.g., registrar staffing, assessor reassignment).

**Building administrator (principal or designee).** Receives referrals at the building level; ensures every referral, however received, reaches the registrar for date-stamping the same day. Convenes or designates a chair for the referral review meeting. Ensures general education teachers supply intervention data within the deadline in Section 6.3.3. Verifies the building referral log weekly against the timeline dashboard.

**Registrar (each building).** Date-stamps every referral document on the day of receipt. Enters the referral into the building referral log and forwards it to the records clerk and the assigned case manager within one business day. Date-stamps and routes all returned consent forms and parent correspondence on the day of receipt.

**Special education records clerk (district office).** Maintains the timeline dashboard and building referral log audit. Enters every referral within one business day of receipt from the registrar. Calculates and enters due dates. Sends automated and manual reminders at the intervals in Section 6.6.5. Prepares the draft monthly timeline report.

**School psychologist / SLP (evaluation case manager).** Coordinates assigned evaluations end to end: leads the existing-data review, drafts the assessment plan, obtains consent, schedules assessments, tracks component completion, drafts the evaluation report, schedules and facilitates the eligibility meeting, and enters all dates in the IEP system within one business day of each event.

**Assessors (psychologists, SLPs, OTs, PTs, nurses, special education teachers, others).** Complete assigned assessment components by the internal due date on the assessment plan (no later than Day 25 of the 35-day window) and submit written results to the case manager.

**General education teachers.** Provide classroom performance data, work samples, and intervention records within 5 school days of a case manager's request. Attend referral review and eligibility meetings when the student is or may be participating in general education.

**School counselors.** Forward any online counseling request that constitutes a referral to the registrar the same day it is read.

**Front office staff.** Treat any written or oral request for evaluation as a referral. Hand written referrals to the registrar the same day; document oral referrals on form SE-100 while the requester is present, when possible.

**Interpretation/translation coordinator (district office).** Arranges interpreters for meetings and translation of consent forms, PWNs, and evaluation summaries within the timelines in Section 6.10. Approximately 9 percent of referrals require language services; requests are flagged at referral entry using the Skyward home-language field.

**Special education teacher (IEP case manager).** Assigned at eligibility; schedules and drafts the initial IEP and ensures it is in effect within 30 calendar days of the eligibility determination.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Receipt and Dating of Referrals (Day 0)

**6.1.1** A referral may arrive by any of the following channels. All are valid referrals from the moment of receipt:

- **(a)** Form SE-100 delivered to any front office, mailed, faxed, or emailed to any district employee.
- **(b)** An online counseling request submitted through the district website that asks for, or reasonably suggests a need for, a special education evaluation.
- **(c)** A parent letter, email, or note in any language, addressed to any district employee.
- **(d)** An oral request from a parent, teacher, physician, agency, or other person. The receiving employee completes an SE-100 on the requester's behalf on the same day, records the requester's name and the date and time of the oral request, and writes "oral referral documented by [employee name]" on the form.
- **(e)** A written statement within a Section 504 referral, disciplinary record, counseling record, or other document indicating a suspected disability and possible need for special education. The employee who identifies it photocopies the relevant page, attaches an SE-100, and treats it as a referral dated the day it was identified.

**6.1.2** The employee who first receives a referral delivers it to the building registrar **the same day**. If the registrar is absent, the employee delivers it to the building administrator, who assumes the registrar's duties in this step. A referral is never held in a mailbox, inbox, or desk drawer overnight without being date-stamped.

**6.1.3** The registrar, on the day of receipt:

- **(a)** Physically date-stamps paper documents; for electronic documents, prints or saves the document with the received-date metadata visible and applies an electronic date stamp in the IEP system.
- **(b)** Verifies the student's enrollment and home language in Skyward. If the home language is not English, the registrar flags the referral "LANGUAGE SERVICES" and notes the language.
- **(c)** Enters the referral in the **building referral log** (mandatory at every building; see Section 6.1.5): student name, Skyward ID, referral date, source, channel, language flag, and date forwarded.
- **(d)** Scans and uploads the referral to the IEP system and emails the records clerk and the building administrator, subject line: "REFERRAL — [Student ID] — [Date]."

**6.1.4** The records clerk, within one business day of the registrar's email:

- **(a)** Creates a row in the timeline dashboard and calculates the **Day 25 decision due date** using the adopted school calendar.
- **(b)** Notifies the Director of Special Education, who assigns an evaluation case manager within **2 school days** of the referral date, balancing caseloads across the seven remaining school psychologists and, for communication-only referrals, the SLP staff. During the current staffing shortage the Director may assign contracted psychologists; the timeline obligations in this SOP apply to contractors identically.

**6.1.5** **Building referral log requirement.** Every building, including both middle schools cited in the April 2026 complaint decision, maintains the building referral log in the shared district template. The building administrator reviews and initials the log **every Friday**, reconciling it against the timeline dashboard. Discrepancies are reported to the records clerk the same day. The Director audits two building logs per month on a rotating schedule and reports results in the monthly OSPI report.

### 6.2 Screening Out What Is Not a Referral

**6.2.1** Only the evaluation case manager, in consultation with the Director, may determine that a document logged under Section 6.1 is not a referral (for example, a request solely for counseling services with no suspicion of disability). This determination is recorded on the dashboard with a one-sentence rationale. When in doubt, the document is treated as a referral. Front office staff, registrars, and building administrators may **never** screen out a referral.

### 6.3 Review of Existing Data and the 25-School-Day Decision

**6.3.1** Within **3 school days** of assignment, the case manager schedules the **referral review meeting** to occur no later than **Day 15** of the 25-school-day window. Required participants: case manager, building administrator or designee, at least one general education teacher of the student, and the parent (invited in writing, with interpretation arranged if flagged). A parent's inability or declination to attend does not delay the meeting; the case manager documents two contact attempts by two different methods.

**6.3.2** Before the meeting, the case manager assembles the **existing data packet**: Skyward attendance, grades, discipline, and assessment history; state and district assessment scores; MTSS/intervention records; health records; ELL records where applicable; and any information submitted with the referral.

**6.3.3** General education teachers provide requested intervention data, work samples, and a completed teacher input form within **5 school days** of the case manager's request. The building administrator resolves any non-response within 1 additional school day.

**6.3.4** At the referral review meeting, the group decides one of the following, no later than **Day 25**:

- **(a) Evaluate.** The group completes the review of existing data (WAC 392-172A-03020), identifies all areas of suspected disability, and drafts the assessment plan (Section 6.5).
- **(b) Decline to evaluate.** The group documents the specific data supporting the decision that no disability is suspected or that existing data are sufficient to determine the student is not eligible.

**6.3.5** Within **3 school days** of the decision, and in all cases by Day 25, the case manager issues **PWN** stating the decision, the reasons, the data relied upon, other options considered, and parent procedural safeguards, accompanied by the *Notice of Special Education Procedural Safeguards*. PWN is issued in the parent's native language per Section 6.10. **Every referral, including declined referrals, receives a PWN. No file is closed without a PWN in the IEP system.** The records clerk verifies PWN presence on the dashboard before marking the decision step complete; this control directly addresses the 22 missing-PWN findings in the April 2026 decision.

### 6.4 Consent for Initial Evaluation

**6.4.1** If the decision is to evaluate, the case manager sends the **consent for initial evaluation** form with the assessment plan and PWN, translated as needed, within **3 school days** of the decision, by two methods (e.g., mail plus email, or sent home with the student plus email), documenting both.

**6.4.2** If consent is not returned within **5 school days**, the case manager attempts contact by phone (with interpreter if flagged). If not returned within **10 school days**, the case manager makes a second documented attempt by a different method and offers to meet with the parent. All attempts are logged in the IEP system.

**6.4.3** When consent is received by any office, the receiving registrar date-stamps it the **same day** and forwards it to the case manager and records clerk. The records clerk enters the consent date and calculates the **Day 35 evaluation due date** within one business day.

**6.4.4** If the parent **refuses consent** or does not respond after the attempts in 6.4.2, the case manager refers the matter to the Director within 2 school days. The Director decides whether to (a) close the referral with PWN documenting the refusal and the district's decision not to pursue override, or (b) request mediation or file a due process hearing request to override the refusal (permitted but not required for initial evaluations, WAC 392-172A-03000). The decision and PWN are entered on the dashboard.

### 6.5 Evaluation Planning and Assignment of Assessors

**6.5.1** The assessment plan, drafted at the referral review meeting and finalized upon consent, lists every area of suspected disability and, for each: the assessment tools or procedures, the assigned assessor by name, and an **internal due date no later than Day 25** of the 35-school-day window, leaving 10 school days for report writing, review, and the eligibility meeting.

**6.5.2** Areas to consider in every plan: cognitive, academic, communication, social-emotional/behavioral, adaptive, motor, health/medical, vision/hearing, and, where relevant, assistive technology and vocational. The plan states in writing why any area is not assessed.

**6.5.3** The case manager distributes the plan to all assessors within **2 school days** of receiving consent and confirms each assessor's acceptance of the due date in writing (email suffices). If any assessor cannot meet the date, the case manager escalates to the Director the **same day**; the Director reassigns the component or authorizes contracted assessment within 2 school days.

**6.5.4** For students flagged for language services, assessments are conducted in the student's native language or mode of communication unless clearly not feasible (WAC 392-172A-03020(3)(a)(ii)); the case manager arranges an interpreter or bilingual assessor at the time the plan is distributed, not later.

### 6.6 The 35-School-Day Evaluation Window and Tracking

**6.6.1** The evaluation must be **completed** — meaning the evaluation group, including the parent, has met and determined eligibility, and the evaluation report is finalized — within **35 school days** of the consent date, unless an exception in Section 6.9 applies.

**6.6.2** Standard internal milestones (school days from consent):

| Milestone | Due |
|---|---|
| Assessment plan distributed; assessors confirmed | Day 2 |
| All assessments scheduled; parent notified of dates | Day 5 |
| All assessment components completed and submitted to case manager | Day 25 |
| Draft evaluation report complete; eligibility meeting invitation sent (minimum 5-school-day notice unless parent agrees to shorter) | Day 28 |
| Eligibility meeting held; report finalized; PWN issued | Day 33–35 |

**6.6.3** The case manager enters each milestone's completion date in the IEP system within one business day; the records clerk mirrors it on the dashboard.

**6.6.4** If a student is absent for a scheduled assessment, the assessor reschedules within **3 school days** and notifies the case manager. Two missed appointments trigger Section 6.9.2.

**6.6.5** **Automated reminders.** The records clerk sends dashboard-generated alerts to the case manager and building administrator at **Day 15** (status check), **Day 25** (component completion verification), and **Day 30** (final warning, copied to the Director). Any milestone missed by more than 2 school days is escalated to the Director the day it is detected; the Director takes corrective action (reassignment, contracted support, or extension request under Section 6.9.3) within 2 school days.

**6.6.6** **Weekly review.** The Director reviews the full dashboard every Monday, prioritizing any evaluation past Day 25.

### 6.7 Eligibility Meeting and Evaluation Report

**6.7.1** The case manager invites the parent in writing at least **5 school days** before the eligibility meeting (parent may agree in writing to shorter notice), offers at least two scheduling options, and arranges interpretation if flagged. Required participants: parent, case manager, each professional who assessed the student (or a qualified representative able to interpret the results), a general education teacher, a special education teacher, and a district representative (building administrator or Special Education Department administrator).

**6.7.2** At the meeting, the group reviews all results, determines whether the student meets criteria under one or more of the categories in WAC 392-172A-01035, whether the disability adversely affects educational performance, and whether the student needs specially designed instruction. Exclusionary factors (lack of appropriate instruction in reading or math; limited English proficiency) are documented.

**6.7.3** The evaluation report is finalized at or within **2 school days** after the meeting and in no case later than Day 35. Each assessor certifies agreement or attaches a dissent. The parent receives a copy of the report at no cost, translated or orally interpreted per Section 6.10.

**6.7.4** The case manager issues **PWN** of the eligibility decision (eligible or not eligible) within **3 school days** of the meeting and uploads meeting sign-in sheet, report, and PWN to the IEP system. The records clerk records the **eligibility date** and, if eligible, calculates the **30-calendar-day IEP due date**.

### 6.8 Initial IEP Within 30 Calendar Days

**6.8.1** At the eligibility meeting, the district representative names the **special education teacher who will serve as IEP case manager** and, where possible, sets the IEP meeting date before the group leaves the room.

**6.8.2** The IEP meeting is held, and the IEP completed, within **30 calendar days** of the eligibility determination. The 30 days include weekends and breaks; see Section 6.9.4 for summer handling. The IEP case manager sends written invitations at least 5 school days ahead, drafts proposed goals from the evaluation, and arranges interpretation as flagged.

**6.8.3** Following the IEP meeting, the IEP case manager: issues PWN of the proposed placement and services within 3 school days; obtains the parent's **consent for initial provision of services** (services do not begin without it, WAC 392-172A-03000(2)(b)); and, upon consent, ensures services begin **as soon as possible and no later than 5 school days** after consent. Copies of the IEP go to the parent and every service provider before the start date.

**6.8.4** If the parent refuses consent for initial services, the IEP case manager issues PWN documenting the refusal; the district may not use mediation or due process to override refusal of initial services and provides no special education services, but the student's eligibility record is retained.

**6.8.5** The records clerk enters the IEP meeting date, consent date, and service start date on the dashboard, closing the referral row as "compliant" or "late" with reason codes.

### 6.9 Exception Paths

All exceptions require a dashboard reason code, supporting documentation in the IEP system, and — except 6.9.1 pause events — Director approval.

**6.9.1 Student transfers into the district mid-evaluation.** When a student enrolls with an evaluation in progress in another district: the registrar flags enrollment records showing a pending evaluation and notifies the records clerk the same day. The case manager contacts the sending district within **2 school days** to obtain the referral date, consent date, completed components, and raw data. Northline and the parent then agree in writing on a specific date by which the evaluation will be completed; the original 35-day clock does not automatically restart, but the district must ensure prompt completion (WAC 392-172A-03005(4)). The agreed date is entered on the dashboard as the controlling due date. Sending-district assessment components may be adopted if current and technically adequate, as documented by the case manager. **Student transfers out of the district mid-evaluation:** the case manager forwards all evaluation materials to the receiving district within 2 school days of a records request and closes the dashboard row with code "TRANSFER-OUT."

**6.9.2 Parent repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the student.** After two missed assessment appointments (Section 6.6.4), the case manager sends the parent a written notice (translated as needed) listing the missed dates, offering three new dates including at least one outside standard hours, and stating that the timeline may be affected. If the student is still not produced after the third offered date, the Director either (a) documents an agreed written extension with the parent under 6.9.3, or (b) closes the referral with PWN explaining that the evaluation could not be completed due to the parent's repeated failure to produce the student, noting that WAC 392-172A-03005(2)(b) excuses the timeline in this circumstance and that the parent may re-refer at any time. All contact attempts are logged. **Parent revokes consent mid-evaluation:** revocation must be in writing; if received orally, the case manager confirms it in writing to the parent the same day. Upon revocation, all assessment activity stops immediately; the case manager issues PWN within 3 school days acknowledging the revocation and stating that the evaluation will not be completed; the dashboard row closes with code "CONSENT-REVOKED." Revocation is not retroactive and does not require the district to amend records of activity before revocation.

**6.9.3 Agreed written extensions.** The 35-day timeline may be extended **only** by written agreement between the parent and the district on form SE-115, stating the specific reason, the new completion date, and the parent's signature obtained after an oral explanation (interpreted as needed). Acceptable reasons include: student illness or extended absence, parent-requested delay, need for a low-incidence assessment (e.g., audiological, neuropsychological) not obtainable within the window, or mid-window enrollment gaps. **Staff shortage, workload, and scheduling convenience are not acceptable reasons.** Only the Director may approve an SE-115; approval or denial is issued within 2 school days of the request. The extension and reason code are entered on the dashboard, and all extensions are itemized in the monthly OSPI report.

**6.9.4 Referrals received in June and over summer.** Because school days pause over summer, timelines behave as follows, and the records clerk calculates due dates accordingly:

- A referral received in June with fewer than 25 school days remaining: the 25-day clock runs through the last school day, pauses over summer, and resumes on the first school day in September, **unless** the referral review can be completed before the last school day, which is the district's preferred practice. The case manager attempts to hold the referral review before the year ends whenever the referral is received at least 10 school days before the last day.
- Consent received with fewer than 35 school days remaining: the 35-day clock likewise pauses and resumes. The case manager notifies the parent in writing before the last school day of the projected completion date and offers, where assessor availability permits, to complete assessments during summer by mutual agreement (documented on SE-115 as a schedule agreement, not an extension).
- **The 30-calendar-day IEP timeline does not pause for summer.** If eligibility is determined in June, the initial IEP meeting is held within 30 calendar days even if that date falls in July; the Director maintains a summer duty roster of special education staff for this purpose.
- Referrals received during summer recess (July–August) are date-stamped by the district office receptionist, logged by the records clerk, and assigned a Day 0 of the date received; the 25-day clock begins on the first school day in September, but the Director may authorize the referral review during August to reduce fall backlog.

**6.9.5 Private school and out-of-district referrals.** For a student parentally placed in a private school located within Northline boundaries, or a home-based instruction student residing in-district, the referral follows this SOP in full; the district office serves as the "building," the records clerk performs registrar duties, and the case manager coordinates assessment locations with the private school and parent. For a student who **resides outside** Northline but attends a private school inside district boundaries, Northline is responsible for child find and evaluation under WAC 392-172A-04005; the case manager notifies the district of residence as a courtesy but does not transfer the obligation. For a Northline resident attending a private school in another district, the case manager forwards the referral to that district within 2 school days, confirms receipt in writing, and closes the row with code "OUT-OF-DISTRICT — FORWARDED." Part C toddlers transitioning at age three are referred through the district's early childhood coordinator and follow this SOP with the goal that an IEP is in effect by the child's third birthday.

**6.9.6 Suspected disability raised during a disciplinary proceeding.** If, during any suspension, expulsion, or emergency removal proceeding, a parent, teacher, or administrator expresses in writing (including in a hearing record) a suspicion that the student has a disability, that expression is a referral under Section 6.1.1(e), dated the day it is made. The building administrator delivers it to the registrar the same day. The evaluation is conducted on an **expedited** basis: the Director assigns the case manager within 1 school day, the referral review occurs within 10 school days, and the Director prioritizes assessor scheduling so that the evaluation is completed as far ahead of the 35-day maximum as staffing allows. While the evaluation is pending, the student remains in the placement determined by school authorities, and disciplinary protections under WAC 392-172A-05170 through -05175 (basis-of-knowledge provisions) are applied; the case manager confirms with the building administrator, in writing, whether the district had a basis of knowledge of the disability before the behavior occurred.

### 6.10 Language Access

**6.10.1** For every referral flagged "LANGUAGE SERVICES," the interpretation/translation coordinator: provides written translation of the consent form, assessment plan summary, PWNs, and meeting invitations in the parent's language within **3 school days** of request when the language is among the district's top nine; for other languages, provides certified oral interpretation of these documents by phone or in person within the same window, documented on a language-access log entered in the IEP system.

**6.10.2** Interpreters for referral review, eligibility, and IEP meetings are booked at the time the meeting invitation is sent, never later than 3 school days before the meeting. A meeting is not held without the arranged interpreter unless the parent declines interpretation in writing.

### 6.11 Monthly Timeline Reporting and Corrective Action Compliance

**6.11.1** By the **5th of each month** through August 2027, the records clerk prepares a draft report from the dashboard showing, for the prior month: referrals received by building and channel; referral decisions made, with days elapsed; consents received; evaluations completed, with days elapsed; evaluations open past Day 25 and past Day 35 with reason codes; extensions approved; PWN completeness audit results; and building referral log audit results.

**6.11.2** The Director reviews and finalizes the report by the **7th**, briefs the Deputy Superintendent by the **9th**, and submits to OSPI by the **10th**.

**6.11.3** **Compensatory services.** For each of the 38 students identified in the April 2026 decision, the Director convenes the student's IEP team (or, for students found not eligible, an evaluation group review) to determine whether compensatory services are owed and, if so, their type, amount, and schedule. Determinations are documented on PWN, tracked on a dedicated dashboard tab, and completed by the deadline in the corrective action order. Status is included in each monthly report.

**6.11.4** Any evaluation that exceeds Day 35 without an approved SE-115 triggers a written root-cause memo from the case manager to the Director within 5 school days, retained with the monthly report.
