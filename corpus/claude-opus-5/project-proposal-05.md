# Proposal to Establish a District-Operated Student Transportation Department

**To:** Board of Trustees, Tule Bend Unified School District
**From:** Guadalupe Serrato, Superintendent; Nathan Okonkwo, Chief Business Official
**Date:** March 25, 2026
**Board Meeting:** April 8, 2026 — Action Item
**Prepared with:** Wesley Frayne, Director of Operations

---

## 1. Summary of the Proposal

Administration recommends that the Board direct staff to terminate the District's pupil transportation services agreement with First Route Student Transportation at the conclusion of the 2025–26 term and authorize the establishment of a District-operated transportation department, phased across eighteen months beginning July 1, 2026.

The department would acquire forty-six school buses, lease and retrofit a corporation yard on the east side of Tule Bend, and employ fifty-eight drivers and six mechanics under a Director of Transportation reporting to the Director of Operations. Twenty-two of the District's forty-one home-to-school routes would convert to District operation in August 2026; the remaining nineteen routes would convert in January 2027, with First Route continuing under a transitional service agreement for the routes not yet converted.

Capital cost is $14.2 million. Annual operating cost at full conversion is $6.9 million, against a current contract cost of $7.6 million and a contractually available one-year extension priced at $8.66 million. Funding for the capital investment draws $9.1 million from the Fund 25 capital facilities balance, $3.4 million from an anticipated Clean School Bus Program allocation, and $1.7 million from the unrestricted general fund reserve, which would fall from 6.4 percent to 4.2 percent against a Board-adopted minimum of 3 percent.

The decision is time-bound. Section 14.2 of the current agreement requires written notice of non-renewal no later than May 15, 2026. Absent that notice, the agreement renews automatically for one year at a 14 percent increase, and the District forfeits the opportunity to bring service in-house before the 2027–28 school year.

The requested action on April 8 is set out in full in Section 11.

## 2. Why the Board Is Being Asked to Act Now

### 2.1 The service record

The District contracted with First Route Student Transportation in July 2021 under a five-year agreement covering home-to-school routes, special education transportation, and a limited allocation of field trip and athletics hours. Performance in the first two years was within tolerance. Performance in 2024–25 and in the current year has not been.

**Missed runs.** In the 2024–25 school year, First Route failed to operate 1,140 scheduled runs. A "run" is a single directional trip on a single route — a morning pick-up sequence or an afternoon drop-off sequence. With forty-one routes operating two runs per day across 180 instructional days, the District's baseline is 14,760 scheduled runs annually. A missed-run count of 1,140 represents a 7.7 percent failure rate. The contract sets a performance standard of 98 percent completion, or a permissible maximum of 295 missed runs. First Route exceeded the permissible maximum by a factor of 3.9.

Missed runs are not evenly distributed. Transportation office logs show that 61 percent of missed runs in 2024–25 fell on eleven routes, all of which serve the District's outlying attendance areas — the Cutler Road corridor, the Marchand Colony area, and the agricultural housing north of Highway 216. These are the routes with the longest deadhead distances and the least attractive shift structures for drivers, and they are the routes First Route has most consistently deprioritized when short-staffed.

**Late arrivals.** In January 2026 alone, the District logged twenty-two instances in which a bus arrived at its destination school more than forty-five minutes after the scheduled arrival time. A forty-five-minute late arrival means a student misses the entire first instructional period and, in most cases, the school breakfast service. Twenty-two such instances in a twenty-day instructional month means that, on average, more than one bus per day arrived so late that the students aboard lost a full period of instruction.

The forty-five-minute threshold is the District's internal reporting trigger, not a contractual one. The contract defines a late arrival as any arrival more than ten minutes past schedule. Under that definition, the January 2026 count was 187 late arrivals across 820 scheduled morning runs — a 22.8 percent on-time failure rate against a contractual standard of 95 percent on-time performance.

**Notification failures.** The contract obligates First Route to notify the District Transportation Coordinator of a cancellation no later than 5:00 a.m. on the day of service. In 2024–25, the District received advance notice for 402 of the 1,140 missed runs — 35 percent. In the remaining 738 cases, the District learned that a bus was not coming either from a school site reporting that students had not arrived or from a parent calling the District office. Families waiting at stops in the Marchand Colony area, where cellular coverage is intermittent, in several documented instances waited more than an hour before learning no bus was coming.

**Contract remedies exhausted.** The District has invoked the liquidated damages provision in Section 9.4 in each of the last six quarters, assessing $214,000 in credits against invoices. First Route has paid $138,000 and disputes the balance. The District issued a formal notice of material breach on October 14, 2025, initiating the sixty-day cure period under Section 13.1. First Route submitted a corrective action plan on December 2, 2025, committing to hire fourteen additional drivers by February 1, 2026. As of March 20, 2026, First Route reports a net gain of three drivers. The cure period has expired without cure.

### 2.2 The attendance consequence

Chronic absenteeism across the District is 34.1 percent for 2024–25 — meaning better than one student in three missed at least 10 percent of the days they were enrolled. The statewide rate is 20.0 percent. The District's rate in 2018–19 was 14.2 percent.

Transportation is not the sole driver of that figure, and this proposal does not claim it is. Post-pandemic attendance patterns, health-related absence, agricultural employment cycles affecting family mobility, and housing instability all contribute. But the transportation contribution is measurable and it is large.

The District's Student Information System permits disaggregation of absence by transportation status. Among the 3,860 students assigned to a bus route, chronic absenteeism is 41.7 percent. Among the 5,900 students not assigned to a bus route, it is 29.2 percent. The 12.5-point gap persists after controlling for socioeconomic status and English learner designation, both of which are independently associated with higher absence.

The relationship is sharpest on the eleven routes carrying the majority of missed runs. On those routes, serving 1,410 students, chronic absenteeism is 48.9 percent. Two of the three schools these routes feed — Marchand Colony Elementary and Tule Bend Continuation High — are in Differentiated Assistance under California's accountability system, with chronic absenteeism identified as a contributing indicator at both.

Staff conducted a single-day absence match for the twenty-two January 2026 dates on which a bus arrived more than forty-five minutes late. On those dates, same-day absence among students assigned to the affected routes averaged 24.3 percent, against a District-wide daily absence rate of 11.8 percent. The pattern is consistent with what site principals report anecdotally: when a bus is unreliable, a portion of families stop attempting the trip. A parent who has waited forty minutes at a stop three times in a month, and who has a shift start time of their own, makes a rational decision to keep the child home rather than risk a fourth.

**Fiscal consequence of absence.** Under the Local Control Funding Formula, the District's apportionment is driven by average daily attendance. At the District's 2025–26 LCFF entitlement, each percentage point of ADA is worth approximately $1.31 million annually. The District does not project that in-house transportation eliminates the transportation-attributable absence gap. But staff model a conservative recovery of 0.8 to 1.4 percentage points of ADA over three years as service reliability stabilizes — an annual revenue effect of $1.05 million to $1.83 million. That recovery is not included in the cost comparisons in Section 5, which are presented on expenditure alone.

### 2.3 The May 15 deadline

Section 14.2 of the current agreement provides that the term renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least seventy-five days before expiration. The agreement expires July 29, 2026. Notice is therefore due May 15, 2026.

First Route has provided its extension pricing for 2026–27: $8,664,000, a 14 percent increase over the current $7,600,000. The increase is attributed to driver wage pressure and insurance costs. First Route has offered no service level commitments beyond those in the existing agreement and has declined the District's request for enhanced liquidated damages.

The Board has two regular meetings before May 15: April 8 and May 13. Staff recommend action on April 8 rather than May 13. A May 13 decision leaves two days to issue notice, no margin for a continued item or a request for additional analysis, and — critically — compresses the driver recruitment timeline described in Section 7 past the point where an August start is achievable. The recruitment sequence requires ninety days minimum from authorization to first cohort licensure. April 8 authorization supports an August 17 start. May 13 authorization does not.

## 3. What the Department Would Look Like

### 3.1 Scope of service

The District-operated department would assume:

- **Home-to-school transportation** on forty-one routes serving fourteen schools and approximately 3,860 students
- **Special education transportation**, including eleven wheelchair-accessible routes and four routes with required aide staffing, serving 340 students under IEP transportation provisions
- **Mid-day kindergarten transportation** at six elementary sites
- **Athletics and activity transportation**, currently purchased separately at $310,000 annually and folded into the operating budget in Section 5
- **Field trip transportation**, currently rationed by contract hour allocation and expanded under District operation

Regional occupational program transportation and the two County Office of Education special education routes would remain with their current providers, as the District is not the responsible agency for those services.

### 3.2 Organizational structure

The department sits under the Director of Operations, Wesley Frayne, who reports to the Chief Business Official.

**Director of Transportation** (1.0 FTE, classified management, Range 42). Responsible for routing, compliance, safety programming, driver training and certification, and department budget. Must hold or obtain a California School Bus Driver Instructor certificate within twelve months. This position is recruited first, with a target start date of June 1, 2026.

**Transportation Supervisor** (1.0 FTE, classified, Range 34). Day-of-service dispatch, driver assignment, and substitute coverage. Serves as second certificated instructor.

**Lead Mechanic** (1.0 FTE, classified, Range 32) and **Mechanics** (5.0 FTE, Range 28). Preventive maintenance on a forty-six-bus fleet, California Highway Patrol inspection preparation, and roadside response. Staffing at one mechanic per 7.7 buses is consistent with California school district practice, which ranges from one per six to one per ten depending on fleet age.

**Transportation Clerk** (1.0 FTE, Range 22) and **Routing Technician** (1.0 FTE, Range 26). Student eligibility, route data maintenance, parent communication, and state reporting.

**Bus Drivers** (58.0 FTE-equivalent positions, Range 24, proposed $24.10 per hour). Staffing at fifty-eight drivers for forty-one routes reflects a 1.41 ratio accounting for split shifts, mid-day runs, athletics coverage, absence, and the fact that a portion of positions will be filled at less than full-time hours. Detail in Section 7.

**Bus Aides** (6.0 FTE, Range 18). Required on four special education routes; two floating positions.

Total department headcount at full conversion: seventy-four positions, of which sixty-four are bargaining unit positions under CSEA Chapter 619.

### 3.3 Facility

The proposal leases the former Valley Ag Equipment property at 1180 East Bardsley Avenue: 4.1 acres with a 6,200-square-foot shop building, a 1,900-square-foot office structure, and paved yard. The site is zoned M-1, is within the District's boundaries, and sits 2.4 miles from the geographic centroid of the route network.

Lease terms negotiated subject to Board approval: ten years at $16,800 per month, with two five-year renewal options and a purchase option at year five at appraised value. Annual lease cost of $201,600 is carried in the operating budget.

Retrofit requirements are itemized in Section 5.2 and include fueling infrastructure, a two-bay lift installation, wash rack with reclaim, security fencing and lighting, an electrical service upgrade to support future electric bus charging, and office and dispatch build-out. The District's architect has completed a feasibility review; a Division of the State Architect submission is not required, as the facility is not a school building under Field Act jurisdiction.

### 3.4 Fleet

Forty-six buses, acquired as follows:

- **Thirty-two Type C conventional buses**, 78-passenger, diesel. Standard route vehicles.
- **Eight Type C buses with wheelchair lift**, 48-passenger with four wheelchair positions. Special education routes.
- **Four Type A buses**, 24-passenger, for low-density routes and mid-day kindergarten.
- **Two Type D transit-style buses**, 84-passenger, for the two highest-ridership routes.

Fleet size of forty-six against forty-one routes provides a 12 percent spare ratio. California Department of Education guidance and industry practice support a 10 to 20 percent spare ratio; at 12 percent the District is at the lower end, adequate for a new fleet and sufficient to absorb routine maintenance downtime and CHP inspection cycles.

All units specified to 2027 model year, meeting current California Air Resources Board standards. The District has not specified electric buses for the initial acquisition. Electric units at current pricing would raise the capital cost by approximately $9.4 million net of available incentives, and the Bardsley Avenue site would require a service upgrade and charging infrastructure estimated at $2.1 million. The retrofit scope in Section 5.2 includes conduit and panel capacity sized for future charging installation, positioning the District to convert progressively as replacement cycles and state funding permit.

The comparison requires care and the Board should understand its limits.

Against the **current** contract year, District operation costs $350,600 more. Against the **extension** year — which is the actual alternative, since the current price is not available for 2026–27 — District operation costs $756,400 less. Over the ten-year lease term, assuming 3.5 percent annual escalation on contract pricing and 3.0 percent on District operating costs, cumulative savings against continued contracting are $11.4 million, which exceeds the capital investment in year eight.

Three cautions:

**Capital is not in the operating comparison.** The $14.2 million is a one-time investment funded from capital and reserve sources, not from ongoing operating dollars. But the fleet has a useful life of twelve to fifteen years, and the District must plan for replacement. Staff recommend a fleet replacement contribution of $560,000 annually beginning in 2028–29, deposited to Fund 17. That contribution is not in the Section 5.1 budget and represents a real future obligation. Including it, District operation costs $7,460,600 annually — still $196,400 below the extension-year contract price, and the gap widens as contract escalation compounds.

**The driver wage assumption is contested.** Section 8 addresses this directly. If the Board directs a wage above $24.10, each dollar per hour adds approximately $102,000 to line 1 and $29,000 to lines 10 through 13, for a total of $131,000 per dollar. At $26.75, the county average cited by CSEA, annual operating cost rises to $7,247,000 net — $410,000 below the extension price without the fleet replacement contribution, and $150,000 above it with the contribution included.

**Fuel is the largest volatile line.** Line 15 assumes $4.28 per gallon. A one-dollar move in diesel pricing is $412,000 annually. Under the contract, that risk sits with First Route, priced into the rate. Under District operation, the District bears it directly. Staff recommend the Board authorize participation in the Tulare County fuel purchasing consortium, which has historically achieved a $0.14 to $0.22 per gallon advantage and permits forward purchase of up to 60 percent of anticipated volume.

### 6.2 The vehicle delivery gap

Twenty-two routes convert in August; the first owned buses arrive in October. Staff propose an interim lease of twenty-four Type C units from a fleet leasing provider, at $2,850 per unit per month for five months — $342,000, carried in the 2026–27 operating budget. Two providers have quoted; both have available inventory of 2020–2022 model year units in CHP-certifiable condition. This is a real and unavoidable cost of an August start, and the Board should weigh it against the alternative of converting all forty-one routes in January 2027, which would eliminate the lease cost but leave the eleven worst-performing routes with First Route for an additional semester.

Staff recommend the August start. The eleven failure-concentrated routes serve 1,410 students with 48.9 percent chronic absenteeism, including both Differentiated Assistance schools. A semester of reliable service beginning in August, rather than January, is worth $342,000.

### 6.3 Transitional agreement risk

The schedule depends on First Route agreeing to operate nineteen routes from August through December on terms acceptable to the District. First Route is not obligated to do so; the contract terminates as a whole. Staff have opened informal discussion and believe agreement is achievable — the nineteen routes represent $3.1 million in revenue First Route would otherwise forgo entirely — but the Board should understand this as a negotiated outcome, not a certainty. Section 9.3 addresses the contingency.

## 7. Driver Recruitment

Recruitment of fifty-eight drivers in a labor market where the incumbent contractor could not net more than three is the single most demanding element of this proposal, and staff will not represent it as straightforward.

### 7.1 The requirement

California school bus drivers must hold a commercial driver's license with passenger and school bus endorsements, a Department of Motor Vehicles school bus driver certificate, a valid medical examiner's certificate, and clearance under the Department of Justice fingerprint process. Initial certification requires twenty hours of classroom instruction and twenty hours behind-the-wheel training delivered by a certified instructor, plus DMV written and skills examinations.

For a candidate without a CDL, the full sequence — CDL permit, entry-level driver training under federal rules, CDL road test, school bus certification, DOJ clearance — runs ninety to one hundred twenty days. This is why April authorization matters. It is also why the District must pay candidates during training, which staff propose at $18.00 per hour for up to 140 training hours, budgeted at $146,000 across three cohorts in 2026–27.

### 7.2 Recruitment strategy

**First Route incumbent drivers.** First Route currently employs approximately thirty-four drivers serving District routes, all already certified. This is the District's most important recruitment pool. The transition offers these drivers CalPERS membership, District health and welfare benefits, CSEA representation, and paid holidays — none of which they currently receive. Staff have conservatively modeled capture of eighteen to twenty-two incumbent drivers, which is 31 to 38 percent of the requirement. Note that the current agreement contains no non-solicitation clause; counsel has confirmed the District may recruit directly.

**Internal District candidates.** The District employs 214 classified staff in instructional aide, custodial, food service, and campus supervision roles, many at fewer than eight hours daily. Bus driving pairs naturally with a split-shift structure. Staff propose an internal-first recruitment window of three weeks with fully paid training and a guarantee of return to the prior classification if certification is not achieved. Target: eight to twelve positions.

**Parent and community recruitment.** The split-shift structure — morning run, mid-day gap, afternoon run — has historically attracted parents of school-age children. The District will offer a schedule that mirrors the instructional calendar and will conduct recruitment in Spanish and Mixtec through the Family Resource Center, parent liaisons, and the two community radio stations. Target: fifteen to twenty positions.

**Open recruitment.** Advertising through EDD, the Tulare County Workforce Investment Board, and commercial driver channels. Target: ten to fifteen positions.

**Retention structure.** Staff propose a $1,500 certification completion bonus paid at ninety days of service, a $750 referral bonus, and a $1,000 retention payment at twelve months. Total budgeted at $184,000 across the first two years, in line 24 of the operating budget.

### 7.3 The wage question

The proposed rate is $24.10 per hour, at Range 24 Step 1 of the classified salary schedule, placing bus driver at parity with the District's Instructional Assistant III classification. CSEA Chapter 619 President Dolores Ybarra has correctly noted that the Tulare County average school bus driver wage is $26.75, and that $24.10 places the District at approximately the tenth percentile among the county's ten districts operating their own fleets.

Staff take this seriously. It is not a technical objection; it is the central operational risk.

Three observations for the Board's consideration:

**Total compensation is not wage.** First Route drivers are paid $22.85 to $24.60 with a defined contribution retirement plan and an employee-only health plan with a $4,500 deductible. The District's offer at $24.10 includes CalPERS defined benefit membership, the District's family health plan, twelve paid holidays, and step advancement to $29.85 at Step 6. Staff estimate the total compensation package at approximately $38.40 per hour fully loaded, which is competitive within the county even where base wage is not.

**The rate is subject to negotiation.** Establishing this classification requires negotiation with CSEA under the Educational Employment Relations Act. The $24.10 figure is the District's opening position for costing purposes, not a determination. The Board is not being asked to set the wage on April 8.

**The Board should understand the cost of the alternative.** As noted in Section 5.2, moving to $26.75 costs $131,000 per dollar, or $347,000 annually. That expenditure remains within the envelope that keeps District operation below the contract extension price, though it consumes most of the margin once the fleet replacement contribution is included. Staff would rather present the Board with an honest recruitment risk at $24.10 and a costed path to $26.75 than represent that $24.10 will fill fifty-eight positions when the incumbent could not fill thirty-four at a comparable rate.

Staff recommend the Board authorize the CBO to negotiate within a range of $24.10 to $26.75, with any settlement above $25.40 returned to the Board for budget amendment.

## 8. Alternatives Considered

### 8.1 Exercise the one-year extension

**Cost:** $8,664,000, plus $353,000 athletics, net $7,657,000 after apportionment.

**Assessment:** This is the default outcome if the Board does not act by May 15. It costs $756,400 more than District operation in 2026–27 and preserves the service failures documented in Section 2.1 with no additional remedies. First Route has declined enhanced liquidated damages and has demonstrated over a sixty-day cure period that it cannot recruit the drivers required. The extension purchases one year of the same performance at a 14 percent premium. Staff do not recommend it.

The extension does have one merit worth stating plainly: it requires no capital, no reserve draw, and no execution risk. A Board that judges the recruitment risk in Section 7 to be unacceptable has a defensible reason to choose it. Staff's judgment is that a 34.1 percent chronic absenteeism rate, concentrated on the routes the contractor most consistently fails, makes another year of the same service the costlier choice.

### 8.2 Rebid the service

**Cost:** Unknown; estimated $8,200,000 to $9,400,000 based on regional awards.

**Assessment:** Staff conducted market sounding in January and February 2026, contacting six providers operating in the San Joaquin Valley. Four declined to express interest, citing driver availability. Two indicated conditional interest: one at a rate estimated 8 to 12 percent above current pricing, one requesting a five-year term with an annual escalator tied to the regional CPI plus two points.

The structural problem is that a contractor faces the same driver shortage the District faces, in the same labor market, but must additionally generate margin. The contractor's advantage — access to a regional driver pool that can be shifted between districts — is precisely what has failed here, because when the pool is short, the contractor allocates drivers to its most profitable or most contractually exposed accounts, and Tule Bend's outlying routes are neither.

Timing is also prohibitive. A compliant procurement under Public Contract Code Section 20111 requires approximately 120 days from specification development to award. Beginning April 8 yields award in early August, after school starts. The Board would have to exercise the extension to bridge, at $8,664,000, and would then be rebidding into 2027–28 with no better market. Staff do not recommend rebidding.

### 8.3 Joint powers arrangement with Kettleman Valley Unified

**Cost:** Estimated $6,400,000 to $7,100,000 District share; substantial governance and timing uncertainty.

**Assessment:** Kettleman Valley Unified, seventeen miles west, operates a District fleet of thirty-one buses from a yard on Avenue 320. Superintendent Serrato and Superintendent Aguilar-Reyes met February 19 and March 6, 2026, to explore a shared transportation arrangement.

The concept has genuine merit. A combined operation of seventy-seven to eighty-two buses achieves scale in maintenance, dispatch, and administration that neither district achieves alone. Kettleman Valley has an underutilized shop and an existing certified instructor. Preliminary modeling suggests a combined operation could deliver service to both districts at 6 to 9 percent below the sum of separate operations.

The obstacles are timing and governance. Formation of a joint powers authority under Government Code Section 6500 requires an agreement negotiated and approved by both boards, a governance structure resolving cost allocation and service priority, resolution of employer status for represented employees — Kettleman Valley's drivers are represented by a different CSEA chapter under a different agreement — and, in the view of both districts' counsel, a minimum of twelve to eighteen months. Kettleman Valley's board has not been briefed and has taken no position. Its own transportation contract does not expire until June 2028.

Kettleman Valley cannot absorb Tule Bend's forty-one routes in August 2026. Its fleet is at 94 percent utilization and it is itself six drivers short.

**Staff recommend the Board direct that this alternative remain open rather than be foreclosed.** The proposal before the Board builds a District department with a fleet, a facility, and a workforce — all of which are contributable assets to a future JPA. Staff recommend the Board direct the Superintendent to continue discussions with Kettleman Valley toward a shared services agreement covering maintenance, driver training, and fleet purchasing in 2027–28, as a bridge toward possible fuller integration in 2028–29 when Kettleman Valley's contract expires. This costs nothing now and preserves the option.

### 8.4 Partial in-house conversion

**Cost:** $3,900,000 District operation of fourteen routes plus $4,800,000 contracted for twenty-seven.

**Assessment:** Operating only the eleven failure-concentrated routes plus three adjacent routes reduces capital to $6,100,000 and requires only twenty-one drivers. It targets the worst service failures with the least exposure.

It also loses most of the economics. Fixed costs — facility, director, supervisor, shop, dispatch, software — are incurred nearly in full for a fourteen-bus fleet as for a forty-six-bus fleet. Per-route cost under partial conversion is $278,600 against $205,800 at full conversion. Combined cost of $8,700,000 exceeds both full District operation and the contract extension.

Staff considered whether partial conversion might serve as a lower-risk pilot. The difficulty is that it does not reduce the binding constraint. Recruiting twenty-one drivers is not meaningfully easier per position than recruiting fifty-eight; the same market, the same training pipeline, the same wage question. And it leaves the District paying a 14 percent premium for the twenty-seven contracted routes while carrying full departmental overhead. Staff do not recommend it.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

### 9.1 Driver recruitment shortfall

**Risk:** The District fills fewer than the forty-two drivers required for the August conversion of twenty-two routes.

**Likelihood:** Moderate to high. This is the principal risk in the proposal.

**Mitigation:** Four-channel recruitment (Section 7.2) with incumbent capture as the anchor. Paid training removes the primary barrier for career-change candidates. Phased conversion means August requires forty-two drivers, not fifty-eight. **Trigger and fallback:** If certified and in-training drivers total fewer than thirty-eight on July 1, staff will reduce the August conversion from twenty-two routes to fourteen — the eleven failure-concentrated routes plus three — and extend the transitional agreement accordingly. This decision point will be reported to the Board at the July 8 meeting.

### 9.2 Clean School Bus award does not materialize

**Risk:** The $3,400,000 CEC allocation is not awarded, or is awarded at less than the requested amount.

**Likelihood:** Moderate. The application scored within range in preliminary ranking, but awards have not issued and the program is oversubscribed.

**Mitigation:** Staff recommend the Board authorize the following contingency in the same motion. If no award letter is received by August 1, 2026, staff will (a) reduce the initial fleet order from forty-six to thirty-eight units, deferring eight units to a 2027–28 acquisition and extending the interim lease correspondingly, reducing capital by $1,504,000; (b) defer the wash rack and reclaim system, $412,000; (c) defer the electrical service upgrade beyond the minimum required for shop operation, $340,000; and (d) draw an additional $1,144,000 from Fund 25, reducing the remaining Fund 25 balance to $1,603,000. **No additional draw on the unrestricted reserve would be required.** The Board would be asked to ratify these adjustments at the August 12 meeting.

### 9.3 First Route declines the transitional agreement

**Risk:** First Route refuses to operate nineteen routes from August through December, or demands pricing the District cannot accept.

**Likelihood:** Low to moderate.

**Mitigation:** Two fallbacks. First, the District would extend the interim bus lease and accelerate conversion of all forty-one routes to District operation in August, which requires fifty-eight drivers by August 17 and is achievable only under favorable recruitment. Second, the District would procure short-term contracted service on the nineteen routes from a secondary provider; staff have identified two regional charter operators with capacity for partial-year work at an estimated premium of $410,000 over the transitional agreement price. This premium is not budgeted and would require a budget amendment.

### 9.4 Facility retrofit delay

**Risk:** Permitting or construction delays prevent yard readiness by August 17.

**Likelihood:** Low to moderate.

**Mitigation:** The critical path for August is fueling, parking, and minimal dispatch — not the shop, wash rack, or full office build-out, which can complete through the fall. Staff have confirmed with City of Tule Bend that the M-1 zoning permits the use by right and that no conditional use permit is required. **Fallback:** mobile fueling service and temporary office trailer, at $8,400 monthly, permitting operations from the paved yard alone.

### 9.5 Reserve falls below acceptable level from other pressures

**Risk:** The reserve at 4.2 percent leaves insufficient margin for a mid-year revenue reduction or unanticipated expenditure.

**Likelihood:** Moderate. The District faces enrollment decline of 11 percent over five years, which is a structural revenue pressure independent of this proposal.

**Mitigation:** The Section 5.4 projection shows the reserve recovering to 4.9 percent by 2027–28. The margin above the 3 percent minimum is $1,071,000 in the lowest year. Staff will bring a reserve replenishment plan with the 2026–27 First Interim report. Staff further note that continued declining enrollment argues for, not against, the fixed-cost control that District operation provides: contracted per-route pricing does not decline with enrollment, while a District department can consolidate routes and reduce its own driver complement as ridership falls.

### 9.6 Operating costs exceed projection

**Risk:** Actual costs exceed the $6,900,600 projection.

**Likelihood:** Moderate. First-year costs in new operations commonly exceed projection.

**Mitigation:** The largest volatile line is fuel; consortium participation and forward purchase are recommended. Staff have budgeted 2026–27 as a transition year with $412,000 in one-time costs — interim lease, training wages, recruitment incentives — identified separately so that the underlying operating cost is visible. Staff commit to quarterly transportation budget reporting to the Board beginning October 2026, with variance explanation on any line exceeding 5 percent.

### 9.7 Labor relations

**Risk:** Negotiation with CSEA on classification and wage does not conclude before drivers must be hired.

**Likelihood:** Moderate.

**Mitigation:** Staff have opened informal discussion with Chapter President Ybarra and will formally notice the classification proposal in May. The District may hire into a proposed classification pending agreement, with retroactive adjustment. Staff regard the CSEA relationship as an asset in this transition rather than an obstacle: the chapter has a direct interest in sixty-four new bargaining unit positions, and Ms. Ybarra's wage concern is a concern about the sustainability of the operation, which the District shares.

## 10. Performance Accountability

If the Board approves, staff commit to the following reporting:

- **Monthly** during 2026–27: missed runs, on-time performance, driver headcount against target, reported to the Board in the Superintendent's report.
- **Quarterly:** transportation budget variance against the adopted budget.
- **Semi-annually:** chronic absenteeism disaggregated by transportation status, reported alongside LCAP progress.
- **Annually:** cost per route and cost per rider, benchmarked against the seven comparable Tulare and Kings County districts operating their own fleets.

Staff propose the following first-full-year targets for 2027–28: missed runs below 150 annually, a 99 percent completion rate; on-time performance above 96 percent at the ten-minute threshold; zero arrivals more than forty-five minutes late; and chronic absenteeism among transported students reduced from 41.7 percent to below 36 percent.
