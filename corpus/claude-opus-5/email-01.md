Marisol,

Let me be precise about what the grant does and does not do, because I already have two board members asking me whether it "solves" anything and it does not.

The $2.3 million is capital. Our $8.4 million shortfall is operating. Oregon does not let us move capital grant funds into the general fund, and this award is categorical besides. Not one dollar of it closes the budget gap.

What it does is change the value of keeping Sawyer Creek open. Sawyer Creek's $759,000 annual operating cost is high because the building leaks. Post-modernization, my estimate is that number falls to about $580,000 — call it $179,000 a year of avoided operating cost. Over ten years, undiscounted, that is $1.79 million.

So the real comparison is this. Closing Sawyer Creek yields $759,000 of avoided operations annually and forfeits a $2.3 million capital award. Keeping Sawyer Creek open costs $580,000 annually post-upgrade but captures $2.3 million of building value we would otherwise have to fund ourselves eventually or continue deferring.

Here is the part that is uncomfortable. Sawyer Creek is on our deferred maintenance list at $3.1 million. The grant covers $2.3 million of that. If we close the building, we still own it. We do not stop owning a building by closing it. Mothballed, Sawyer Creek costs roughly $95,000 a year in minimum heat, insurance, security, and grounds. Sold, it fetches maybe $1.4 million in this market for a 1962 school on 9 acres, and the sale takes 18 to 30 months and requires a public process.

So Package A's $1.9 million savings should really be stated as $1.9 million in year one, less $95,000 mothball cost, and against a forfeited $2.3 million capital award and a retained $3.1 million deferred maintenance liability we would have to address if we ever want to sell or reuse.
