## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the standardized process by which Front Range Workforce Collaborative ("FRWC" or "the Collaborative") identifies, assesses, monitors, and closes out relationships with subrecipient organizations receiving federal and state pass-through funds. It is issued in direct response to the material weakness identified in the FY2025 single audit, which found that FRWC lacked a consistent, documented approach to pre-award risk assessment, ongoing monitoring, audit report collection, and corrective action tracking across its 31 subrecipients. This procedure is required as a condition of continued federal funding, including the $12.8 million award subject to an October 1 compliance deadline, and is designed to bring FRWC into conformance with 2 CFR Part 200, Subpart D (Uniform Guidance) subrecipient monitoring and audit requirements.

The procedure is intended to be sufficiently detailed that any staff member newly assigned to a grants compliance, program, contracting, fiscal monitoring, or finance role can execute their responsibilities without requiring additional verbal instruction.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all FRWC subawards funded in whole or in part by federal financial assistance passed through FRWC to subrecipient organizations, and to state pass-through funds subject to equivalent monitoring expectations. It covers the full subaward lifecycle: determination of subrecipient versus contractor status, pre-award risk assessment and eligibility screening, subaward issuance, risk tiering, ongoing desk and on-site monitoring, audit report collection and review, findings resolution and corrective action tracking, disallowed cost recovery, and closeout and record retention.

This procedure applies to all 31 current subrecipients delivering job training services across the 14-county service area, and to any new subrecipient added during the life of this procedure. It does not apply to procurement contracts for goods and services governed under FRWC's separate procurement policy, except to the extent that a contractor-versus-subrecipient determination must first be made under Section 6.1.

## 3. Definitions

**Subrecipient** — A non-federal entity that receives a subaward from FRWC to carry out part of a federal award and, in doing so, has performance measured against whether the objectives of the federal program are met; is subject to the terms and conditions of the federal award; and uses federal funds to carry out a program, as opposed to providing goods or services for FRWC's own benefit.

**Contractor** — An entity that receives a contract from FRWC to obtain goods or services for FRWC's own use, operating in a competitive environment, providing similar goods or services to many purchasers, and not subject to program compliance requirements.

**Subaward** — A legal instrument (grant agreement, subgrant, or subcontract under a grant) by which FRWC provides a portion of a federal award to a subrecipient to carry out part of the award.

**Pass-Through Entity** — FRWC, in its role as recipient of federal and state funds that in turn makes subawards to subrecipients.

**Risk Assessment Worksheet** — The standardized FRWC tool used to score a subrecipient's risk across financial, programmatic, and organizational dimensions prior to award and annually thereafter.

**Risk Tier** — The classification (Low, Moderate, High) assigned to a subrecipient following risk assessment, which determines the frequency and intensity of monitoring activities.

**Single Audit** — An organization-wide financial statement and federal awards audit required under 2 CFR Part 200, Subpart F, of any non-federal entity that expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in its fiscal year.

**Questioned Cost** — A cost that is questioned by FRWC or an auditor because of a violation of a statute, regulation, or the terms of the subaward; is not supported by adequate documentation; or appears unreasonable.

**Corrective Action Plan (CAP)** — A written plan submitted by a subrecipient (or by FRWC to its awarding agency) describing the steps, responsible parties, and timeline for resolving an audit finding, monitoring finding, or material weakness.

**Desk Review** — A remote review of subrecipient documentation, reports, and expenditure data conducted without an on-site visit.

**Site Visit** — An in-person monitoring visit to a subrecipient's location involving review of source documentation, staff interviews, and observation of program delivery.

**Excluded Party** — An individual or entity listed in the federal System for Award Management (SAM.gov) exclusions database as debarred, suspended, or otherwise ineligible to receive federal funds.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Director of Grants Compliance (Solomon Kirui)** — Owns this procedure; approves risk assessments and monitoring plans; reviews and signs off on all corrective action plans before submission to the awarding agency; escalates material weaknesses, suspected fraud, and unresolved findings to the CEO; maintains the master monitoring calendar; ensures the procedure is reviewed annually.

**Chief Executive Officer (Dr. Tanya Ruelas)** — Approves this procedure and any material revisions; approves final corrective action plans submitted to federal and state awarding agencies; approves subrecipient suspension or termination decisions; receives quarterly monitoring status reports from the Director of Grants Compliance.

**Program Officers** — Serve as the primary relationship contact with assigned subrecipients; conduct programmatic desk reviews; participate in site visits; communicate performance expectations; escalate programmatic concerns to the Director of Grants Compliance.

**Contract Analysts** — Draft and issue subaward agreements; ensure all required Uniform Guidance elements are included in each subaward; verify indirect cost rate documentation; process subaward amendments; maintain the subaward document repository in the grants management system.

**Fiscal Monitors** — Conduct pre-award risk assessments; perform expenditure sampling during desk reviews and site visits; test compliance with approved budgets; identify and document questioned costs; verify subrecipient audit report submission and review findings for fiscal impact.

**Finance Team** — Processes subrecipient reimbursement requests; verifies expenditures against approved budget line items prior to payment; flags payment requests inconsistent with the approved subaward budget for fiscal monitor review; tracks disallowed cost recovery and repayment.

**Grants Management System Administrator** — Maintains subrecipient records, monitoring schedules, and document repositories within the grants management system; generates automated alerts for upcoming site visits, audit report due dates, and risk assessment renewals.

## 5. Governing Citation

This procedure implements the requirements of 2 CFR Part 200, Subpart D, §§200.331–200.333 (subrecipient monitoring), Subpart F (audit requirements, including the $1,000,000 single audit expenditure threshold at §200.501), and the required elements of subaward agreements at §200.332. Where state pass-through requirements exceed these federal standards, the more stringent requirement governs.

## 6. Procedural Steps

### 6.1 Contractor vs. Subrecipient Determination

**Step 6.1.1** — Before any new funding relationship is established, the assigned Program Officer completes the Contractor/Subrecipient Determination Form in the grants management system, addressing the substance of the relationship rather than its form, per 2 CFR 200.331.

**Step 6.1.2** — The determination must document: (a) whether the entity's performance will be measured against meeting the objectives of the federal program; (b) whether the entity must comply with federal program requirements as a condition of receiving funds; (c) whether the entity uses the funds to carry out a program rather than to provide goods or services to FRWC; and (d) whether the entity has responsibility for programmatic decision-making.

**Step 6.1.3** — The Director of Grants Compliance reviews and countersigns every determination form within five business days of submission. Determinations classifying an entity as a subrecipient trigger Section 6.2; determinations classifying an entity as a contractor route to FRWC's procurement procedure instead.

**Step 6.1.4** — Determination forms are re-executed whenever the scope of an existing relationship changes materially (e.g., a contractor begins exercising programmatic discretion).

### 6.2 Pre-Award Risk Assessment and Eligibility Screening

**Step 6.2.1** — No subaward may be issued, and no existing subaward may be renewed, until a current Risk Assessment Worksheet is completed and approved. This closes the FY2025 gap in which 9 of 31 subrecipients had no documented pre-award risk assessment.

**Step 6.2.2** — The assigned Fiscal Monitor completes the Risk Assessment Worksheet, scoring the prospective or renewing subrecipient across the following domains: (a) prior single audit results and any unresolved findings; (b) financial condition, including reserves and going-concern indicators; (c) staff turnover and organizational stability; (d) internal control environment, including segregation of duties; (e) programmatic experience with the funded activity; (f) size and complexity of the subaward relative to the subrecipient's total budget; and (g) results of federal exclusion checks.

**Step 6.2.3** — The Fiscal Monitor runs an exclusion check in SAM.gov for the subrecipient organization and each individual identified as an authorized signatory, executive director, or fiscal officer. The screenshot or export confirming the check date and result is uploaded to the grants management system as a permanent attachment to the subaward file. This check must be repeated annually and immediately prior to any subaward amendment increasing funding.

**Step 6.2.4** — Each risk domain is scored 1 (low risk) to 3 (high risk). The composite score determines the Risk Tier: 7–11 points = Low; 12–16 points = Moderate; 17–21 points = High. Any single domain scored a 3 automatically elevates the subrecipient to at least Moderate regardless of composite score.

**Step 6.2.5** — The completed Risk Assessment Worksheet is submitted to the Director of Grants Compliance for approval within 10 business days of subaward negotiation commencing. No subaward may be executed without this written approval on file.

**Step 6.2.6** — Risk assessments are refreshed annually for all active subrecipients no later than 60 days before the anniversary of the subaward effective date, and immediately upon any trigger event listed in Section 6.11.

### 6.3 Subaward Issuance and Required Terms

**Step 6.3.1** — Upon approval of the risk assessment, the Contract Analyst drafts the subaward agreement using the FRWC master subaward template maintained in the grants management system.

**Step 6.3.2** — Every subaward agreement must include, at minimum, the following elements required under 2 CFR 200.332: (a) subrecipient name, DUNS/UEI, and federal award identification number; (b) federal award date and period of performance; (c) amount of federal funds obligated and total amount of the federal award; (d) federal award project description; (e) name of the federal awarding agency and pass-through entity contacts; (f) applicable CFDA/Assistance Listing number and title; (g) all compliance requirements imposed on the subrecipient, including which requirements from the federal award flow down; (h) an approved line-item budget; (i) indirect cost rate treatment per Step 6.3.3; (j) monitoring and reporting requirements, including required data elements and due dates; (k) record retention requirements; (l) access to records provisions for FRWC, the federal awarding agency, and the Comptroller General; and (m) the closeout requirements described in Section 6.10.

**Step 6.3.3** — Indirect cost treatment must be explicitly stated: the subrecipient either applies its federally negotiated indirect cost rate (a copy of the current negotiated rate agreement must be on file), elects the de minimis rate of 10% of modified total direct costs, or negotiates a rate with FRWC as pass-through entity following the process in 2 CFR 200.414. The Contract Analyst confirms which option applies and attaches supporting documentation before the subaward is finalized.

**Step 6.3.4** — The Contract Analyst routes the completed draft to the Director of Grants Compliance for compliance review, then to the assigned Program Officer for programmatic review, before it is sent for subrecipient and FRWC signature. No agreement is executed without both sign-offs documented in the grants management system.

**Step 6.3.5** — Executed subaward agreements, budgets, and all attachments are uploaded to the grants management system within five business days of full execution.

### 6.4 Risk Tiering and the Monitoring Plan

**Step 6.4.1** — The Risk Tier assigned in Section 6.2 drives the minimum monitoring activity for the subaward period, as follows:

| Risk Tier | Site Visits | Desk Reviews | Expenditure Sample Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Once every 12 months | Quarterly | 10% of transactions, minimum 15 |
| Moderate | Once every 6 months | Quarterly | 20% of transactions, minimum 25 |
| High | Once every 3 months | Monthly | 30% of transactions, minimum 35 |

**Step 6.4.2** — The Director of Grants Compliance builds and maintains a master Monitoring Plan in the grants management system at the start of each program year, listing every subrecipient, its risk tier, and the scheduled dates for each required monitoring activity. This plan directly remedies the FY2025 finding that 4 subrecipients had received no site visit in three years.

**Step 6.4.3** — The grants management system generates automated reminders to the assigned Fiscal Monitor and Program Officer 30 days and 10 days before each scheduled monitoring activity.

**Step 6.4.4** — Any missed monitoring activity must be rescheduled within 15 calendar days and the delay, reason, and new date logged in the Monitoring Tool. Two consecutive missed activities for the same subrecipient trigger mandatory review by the Director of Grants Compliance and may result in risk tier escalation.

### 6.5 Desk Reviews

**Step 6.5.1** — The assigned Program Officer and Fiscal Monitor jointly conduct each scheduled desk review using the standardized Desk Review Checklist in the Monitoring Tool.

**Step 6.5.2** — The desk review covers: performance report timeliness and content against contracted outcomes; expenditure report reconciliation against the approved budget; verification that reimbursement requests match supporting invoices submitted; review of any subcontractor or vendor relationships the subrecipient itself maintains; and confirmation that required subaward reporting data elements have been submitted.

**Step 6.5.3** — Findings from the desk review are logged in the Monitoring Tool within five business days of completion, with each finding categorized as an Observation, a Finding, or a Material Finding, and routed per Section 6.8 if a Finding or Material Finding is identified.

### 6.6 On-Site Monitoring and Expenditure Sampling

**Step 6.6.1** — The Fiscal Monitor schedules each site visit at least 15 business days in advance with the subrecipient's fiscal contact, confirming the documentation to be made available: general ledger detail, payroll records and timesheets, vehicle and equipment lease or usage records, procurement files for expenditures above FRWC's threshold, and personnel files for staff charged to the award.

**Step 6.6.2** — Using the risk-tier sample size from Step 6.4.1, the Fiscal Monitor selects a random sample of expenditure transactions from the subrecipient's general ledger for the period since the last review.

**Step 6.6.3** — For each sampled transaction, the Fiscal Monitor verifies: the expenditure is included in the approved budget line item; supporting documentation exists (invoice, receipt, timesheet, or lease agreement); the expenditure falls within the subaward period of performance; and, for personnel costs, time and effort is documented consistent with the approved level of effort in the budget. This step is a direct response to the $412,000 in questioned costs identified in FY2025 for staff time and vehicle leases charged outside the approved budget.

**Step 6.6.4** — Any sampled transaction that fails verification is documented on the Questioned Cost Log with the amount, budget line affected, and reason for the discrepancy, and is routed to Section 6.9 for resolution.

**Step 6.6.5** — The Fiscal Monitor conducts brief interviews with at least two program staff and the fiscal officer during each site visit to assess understanding of budget restrictions and internal controls, documenting responses in the Monitoring Tool.

**Step 6.6.6** — A written Site Visit Report is completed within 10 business days of the visit, reviewed by the Program Officer, and approved by the Director of Grants Compliance before being shared with the subrecipient.

### 6.7 Collection and Review of Audit Reports

**Step 6.7.1** — At the start of each program year, the Fiscal Monitor confirms whether each subrecipient expended $1,000,000 or more in federal awards during its most recently completed fiscal year, using subrecipient expenditure certifications and prior audit data.

**Step 6.7.2** — Subrecipients meeting or exceeding the $1,000,000 threshold are required, per subaward terms, to submit their single audit reporting package (audited financial statements, Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards, auditor's reports, and summary schedule of prior audit findings) no later than the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt from their auditor or nine months after the subrecipient's fiscal year end.

**Step 6.7.3** — The Fiscal Monitor logs the due date and receipt status for every subrecipient audit report in the Corrective Action Log at the start of the program year, and this closes the FY2025 gap in which required audit reports were missing for 6 subrecipients.

**Step 6.7.4** — If an audit report is not received by its due date, the Fiscal Monitor issues a written notice to the subrecipient within five business days and copies the Director of Grants Compliance. If the report is not received within 30 days of the notice, the Director of Grants Compliance may withhold further reimbursements pending receipt, subject to CEO approval.

**Step 6.7.5** — Upon receipt, the Fiscal Monitor reviews the audit report within 20 business days for: any material weaknesses or significant deficiencies; any questioned costs related to the FRWC award; and the status of any prior-year findings. Findings are logged in the Corrective Action Log and routed per Section 6.8.

### 6.8 Findings and Corrective Action Tracking

**Step 6.8.1** — Every finding generated by a desk review, site visit, or audit report review is entered into the Corrective Action Log by the identifying staff member within five business days, including: finding description, source, date identified, responsible subrecipient contact, and required response date.

**Step 6.8.2** — The subrecipient is notified in writing within 10 business days of a finding being logged, with a required corrective action plan due back to FRWC within 30 calendar days, unless the finding involves suspected fraud (see Section 6.11.4), in which case notification and escalation procedures under Section 6.11.4 apply instead.

**Step 6.8.3** — The Program Officer and Fiscal Monitor jointly review the subrecipient's proposed corrective action plan for adequacy, confirming it identifies root cause, corrective steps, responsible party, and completion date. Inadequate plans are returned to the subrecipient with specific written feedback within 10 business days.

**Step 6.8.4** — Approved corrective action plans are logged with target completion dates, and the Fiscal Monitor verifies implementation through a follow-up desk review or site visit no later than the target completion date.

**Step 6.8.5** — The Director of Grants Compliance compiles a quarterly Corrective Action status report for the CEO, summarizing open findings, overdue items, and any findings unresolved for more than 90 days.

**Step 6.8.6** — Any material weakness identified at FRWC itself (as opposed to a subrecipient) is escalated immediately to the CEO, and a corrective action plan for submission to the federal awarding agency and state pass-through is drafted by the Director of Grants Compliance and approved by the CEO before submission, consistent with the October 1 deadline condition on the $12.8 million award.

### 6.9 Disallowed Cost Recovery

**Step 6.9.1** — When a questioned cost identified under Step 6.6.4 or 6.7.5 is confirmed as a disallowed cost following subrecipient response and Fiscal Monitor review, the Director of Grants Compliance issues a formal Disallowed Cost Notice to the subrecipient within 15 business days, stating the amount, basis, and repayment terms.

**Step 6.9.2** — The Finance Team establishes a receivable for the disallowed amount and, where future reimbursements are due to the subrecipient, may offset the disallowed amount against future payments upon Director of Grants Compliance approval, or establish a repayment schedule not to exceed 12 months.

**Step 6.9.3** — Disallowed costs that a subrecipient disputes are resolved through the appeal process stated in the subaward agreement; unresolved disputes are escalated to the CEO and, where required, reported to the federal awarding agency.

**Step 6.9.4** — All disallowed cost determinations, repayments, and outstanding balances are tracked in the Corrective Action Log and reconciled monthly by the Finance Team against the accounting system.

### 6.10 Closeout and Record Retention

**Step 6.10.1** — No later than 90 calendar days before a subaward's period of performance ends, the Contract Analyst notifies the subrecipient of upcoming closeout requirements and required final reports.

**Step 6.10.2** — Final closeout requires: submission of the final performance and expenditure report; resolution of all open findings and disallowed cost balances; return of any unobligated federal funds; and completion of a final desk review confirming all deliverables were met.

**Step 6.10.3** — The Fiscal Monitor completes a Closeout Certification confirming the above items, approved by the Director of Grants Compliance, before the subaward file is marked closed in the grants management system.

**Step 6.10.4** — All subaward records, including risk assessments, monitoring reports, audit reports, correspondence, and financial documentation, are retained for a minimum of three years from the date of submission of the final expenditure report to the federal awarding agency, or longer if required by an unresolved audit finding, litigation, or claim, consistent with 2 CFR 200.334.

### 6.11 Exceptions

**Step 6.11.1 — Subrecipient Below the Single Audit Threshold.** A subrecipient expending less than $1,000,000 in federal awards annually is not required to submit a single audit report but must submit a signed Expenditure Certification annually, and remains subject to the full pre-award risk assessment, risk tiering, and monitoring plan requirements in Sections 6.2 through 6.6.

**Step 6.11.2 — Going-Concern Doubt.** If a subrecipient's audit report or financial statements include a going-concern qualification, the Fiscal Monitor immediately elevates the subrecipient to High risk tier regardless of composite score, notifies the Director of Grants Compliance within two business days, and schedules an ad hoc site visit within 30 calendar days to assess continued capacity to perform. Reimbursements may be moved to a cost-reimbursement-only basis with documentation review prior to payment, pending CEO approval.

**Step 6.11.3 — Mid-Year Change in Key Personnel.** When a subrecipient reports a change in executive director, fiscal officer, or program director, the assigned Program Officer notifies the Fiscal Monitor within five business days, an updated exclusion check is run on the new individual per Step 6.2.3, and an interim risk assessment update is completed within 30 calendar days to determine whether a risk tier change or additional monitoring is warranted.

**Step 6.11.4 — Suspected Fraud.** Any staff member who suspects fraud, waste, or abuse involving a subrecipient must report it immediately, and no later than 24 hours, to the Director of Grants Compliance, who notifies the CEO within one business day. The Director of Grants Compliance suspends further reimbursement to the affected subrecipient pending investigation, subject to CEO approval, and reports the matter to the federal awarding agency's Office of Inspector General and the state pass-through entity within the timeframe required by the subaward terms, typically no later than 30 calendar days from initial suspicion. Normal corrective action plan timelines under Section 6.8 do not apply; the investigation and reporting process governs instead.

## Appendix A: Risk Assessment Worksheet — Scoring Detail

The Risk Assessment Worksheet referenced in Step 6.2.2 assigns a numeric score of 1, 2, or 3 to each of the seven risk domains. This appendix defines the scoring criteria the Fiscal Monitor must apply to ensure consistent, defensible scoring across all 31 subrecipients and any new entities added during the year.

**Domain 1 — Prior Single Audit Results**
- Score 1: No findings in most recent single audit, or subrecipient below threshold with clean expenditure certification history.
- Score 2: One or more findings in most recent single audit, none rising to material weakness, all prior findings resolved within 12 months.
- Score 3: Material weakness or significant deficiency in most recent single audit, or any unresolved finding older than 12 months.

**Domain 2 — Financial Condition**
- Score 1: Positive net assets, at least 60 days operating reserve, no going-concern language in financial statements.
- Score 2: Positive net assets but fewer than 60 days reserve, or a qualified opinion unrelated to going concern.
- Score 3: Negative net assets, going-concern qualification, or declined bond/insurance coverage in the past 12 months.

**Domain 3 — Staff Turnover and Organizational Stability**
- Score 1: No turnover in executive director, fiscal officer, or program director role in the past 12 months.
- Score 2: One key personnel change in the past 12 months with a documented transition plan.
- Score 3: Two or more key personnel changes in the past 12 months, or any vacancy in the fiscal officer role exceeding 60 days.

**Domain 4 — Internal Control Environment**
- Score 1: Documented segregation of duties for cash receipts, disbursements, and payroll authorization; written financial policies less than three years old.
- Score 2: Partial segregation of duties with documented compensating controls; financial policies more than three years old but under revision.
- Score 3: No documented segregation of duties, or no written financial policies on file.

**Domain 5 — Programmatic Experience**
- Score 1: Three or more years delivering the funded service type, with performance meeting or exceeding contracted outcomes in the prior period.
- Score 2: One to three years of experience, or performance meeting outcomes with minor deviations.
- Score 3: First-time delivery of this service type, or performance below 80% of contracted outcomes in the prior period.

**Domain 6 — Size and Complexity of Subaward**
- Score 1: Subaward represents less than 25% of the subrecipient's total annual operating budget.
- Score 2: Subaward represents 25%–50% of total annual operating budget.
- Score 3: Subaward represents more than 50% of total annual operating budget, indicating high dependency and limited diversification.

**Domain 7 — Exclusion Check Results**
- Score 1: No matches for the organization or any listed individual in the SAM.gov exclusions database.
- Score 2: Not applicable — this domain is binary and does not have a middle value; any partial match requiring manual resolution is treated as Score 3 pending clearance.
- Score 3: Confirmed match, or unresolved potential match, for the organization or any listed individual.

The Fiscal Monitor totals the seven domain scores to produce the composite used in Step 6.2.4. Worksheets must include a narrative justification for any domain scored a 3, and the Director of Grants Compliance may not approve a worksheet with an unexplained Score 3.

## Appendix D: Corrective Action Plan Submission Checklist (FRWC to Awarding Agency)

In connection with the October 1 deadline conditioning continuation of the $12.8 million award, the Director of Grants Compliance uses this checklist before submitting FRWC's own corrective action plan to the federal awarding agency and state pass-through entity:

1. Root cause analysis addresses each of the four FY2025 findings individually: missing pre-award risk assessments, lapsed site visits, missing audit reports, and questioned costs from budget non-compliance.
2. Corrective steps reference the specific section of this procedure that closes each gap (Sections 6.2, 6.4, 6.7, and 6.6/6.9, respectively).
3. Responsible parties and target completion dates are named for each corrective step.
4. Evidence of procedure approval by the CEO is attached.
5. A summary of the Master Monitoring Plan status as of the submission date is attached, showing current risk tiers and scheduled activities for all 31 subrecipients.
6. The plan is reviewed by the CEO and signed before transmittal.
7. A copy of the submitted plan and any agency response is filed in the Corrective Action Log and retained per Step 6.10.4.
