### 4. Bid Opening — Baghouse and Cupola Control Replacement

Chair Katsaros stated that three sealed bids had been received by the 10:00 a.m. deadline this date in response to Request for Proposal 2026-ENV-01, issued January 30, 2026. Ms. Oyelaran and Mr. Pfannenstiel had jointly witnessed the opening at 10:15 a.m. in the Controller's office. The bids were read into the record:

| Bidder | Base Bid | Lead Time (primary equipment) | Bid Bond | Alternates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vandermolen Industrial Systems, Neenah, WI | $8,640,000 | 38 weeks | 10% | Two, priced |
| Corcoran Air Technologies, Elkhart, IN | $9,875,000 | 38 weeks | 10% | Two, priced |
| Thibodeaux–Reinholt Environmental, Peoria, IL | $11,200,000 | 34 weeks | 10% | One, priced; one no-bid |

Mr. Wojciechowski of Bergstrom Hollis Engineering presented a preliminary technical comparison. He advised that all three bidders had proposed pulse-jet fabric filtration sized at 185,000 ACFM for the melt deck and shakeout streams combined, and that all three proposals met the specified outlet grain loading of 0.005 gr/dscf. He noted three differences of consequence.

First, the Vandermolen proposal substituted a membrane-laminated PTFE filter media for the specified PPS media. Mr. Wojciechowski said the substitution was technically defensible and probably superior on filtration efficiency, but that it changed the cleaning air demand and had not been priced with a corresponding compressor upgrade.

Second, Corcoran had proposed a modular pre-assembled arrangement that reduced field labor by an estimated 4,200 hours but required a 60-foot-by-90-foot laydown area adjacent to the melt building, which the site does not presently have.

Third, Thibodeaux–Reinholt's shorter lead time was achieved by allocating an existing production slot; the proposal conditioned the 34-week figure on notice to proceed no later than April 15, 2026, and reverted to 41 weeks thereafter.

Mr. Balogun asked whether any bidder had addressed the cupola takeoff hood, which he considered the actual source of the October exceedances rather than the baghouses themselves. Mr. Wojciechowski replied that all three had included the takeoff modification in base scope but that only Thibodeaux–Reinholt had modeled the capture velocity at the charge door, and that the other two had assumed the existing hood geometry was adequate. Mr. Balogun said flatly that it was not, and that he had said so in the December scoping meeting.

Ms. Fasthorse concurred with Mr. Balogun. She stated that the October 3 and October 19, 2025 exceedances both occurred during charging cycles and that the compliance case for the project rested on capture, not filtration.

Ms. Stavrou raised the financing question. She reported that the company's revolving credit facility with Marshall Bay Commercial Bank carried a fixed charge coverage covenant of 1.20 to 1.00, measured quarterly, and that a cash purchase of equipment at the $8.6 million level would bring the December 31, 2026 measurement to approximately 1.24 on current forecasts. She said she would not recommend a cash purchase and had opened discussions with two equipment finance lessors.

Chair Katsaros stated that no award would be made at this meeting. She directed that Bergstrom Hollis complete a full technical evaluation with a scored matrix, and that the committee would take up award at the April meeting.

**ACTION ITEM 26-03-03:** Mr. Wojciechowski to deliver a scored technical evaluation of all three bids, including a capture-velocity assessment of each bidder's cupola takeoff scope, to Mr. Trombley and Ms. Fasthorse. **Due: April 1, 2026.**

**ACTION ITEM 26-03-04:** Mr. Trombley to obtain written clarification from Vandermolen on compressor capacity implications of the PTFE media substitution and from Corcoran on laydown area requirements. **Due: March 31, 2026.**

**ACTION ITEM 26-03-05:** Ms. Stavrou to present at least two financing structures, including a capital lease and a term loan, with covenant impact modeled through Q4 2027. **Due: April 8, 2026 (April meeting).**

### 4. Fourth Exceedance — April 3, 2026 — and Penalty Accrual

Ms. Fasthorse reported that at 04:12 on Friday, April 3, 2026, the continuous opacity monitor on BH-7 recorded a six-minute average of 27.4 percent, exceeding the 20 percent standard, with the condition persisting for four consecutive averaging periods before the shakeout was isolated at 04:38.

She stated that this was the fourth exceedance at the facility since the beginning of the enforcement period and the first since entry of the consent order. Root cause was determined to be a ruptured bag in Compartment 3 of BH-7, discovered on inspection the same morning, together with a failure of the differential pressure alarm to annunciate on the melt deck HMI. She reported that the alarm setpoint had been placed in a maintenance-suppressed state on March 30 during unrelated instrument work and had not been restored.

Mr. Trombley accepted responsibility for the suppressed alarm. He stated the suppression had been entered by a contract instrument technician and that the company's management-of-change procedure had not been followed because the work was classified as routine calibration.

Ms. Oyelaran and Mr. Ingebretsen advised on the legal consequence. Mr. Ingebretsen stated that under Paragraph 33 of the consent order, the exceedance triggered renewed penalty accrual at $2,500 per day beginning April 3, 2026 and continuing until the company demonstrates compliance through a passing performance stack test under Paragraph 29. He stated that on a literal reading, accrual continues until the December 2026 stack test, which would produce an exposure on the order of $665,000. He advised that Paragraph 35 contained a force-and-effect provision permitting the agency to suspend accrual upon a showing of prompt corrective action, and that the company should seek suspension immediately.

Ms. Stavrou asked whether an accrual of that magnitude required disclosure. Mr. Pfannenstiel replied that under the company's accounting policy a loss contingency becomes probable and estimable at the point the agency declines suspension, and that he would consult the company's auditors at Deloitte within the week.

Ms. Fasthorse reported the corrective actions already taken: all 1,440 bags in BH-7 inspected April 4–5 with 11 additional bags replaced; alarm suppression list audited across all seven baghouses with two other suppressed points discovered and restored; and a revision to the management-of-change procedure drafted to require EHS countersignature on any suppression of an environmental-critical alarm.

Mr. Balogun stated that the company had now had a compliance failure caused by an administrative lapse rather than by the capital deficiency the project was designed to cure, and that this would be difficult to explain to the agency.

Chair Katsaros agreed. She directed that the corrective action report be treated as the company's most consequential submittal of the year.

**MOTION:** Ms. Fasthorse moved that the committee authorize immediate submission to WDNR of a corrective action report on the April 3 exceedance together with a formal request for suspension of penalty accrual under Paragraph 35, in a form approved by Ms. Oyelaran and Mr. Ingebretsen. Mr. Nambiar seconded.

**DISCUSSION:** Mr. Trombley asked whether the request should await completion of the alarm audit documentation. Ms. Oyelaran advised that promptness weighed more heavily than completeness and that supplemental material could follow.

**VOTE:** Carried 6–0.

**ACTION ITEM 26-04-01:** Ms. Fasthorse, with Ms. Oyelaran, to submit the corrective action report and Paragraph 35 suspension request to WDNR. **Due: April 17, 2026.**

### 5. Bid Protest and Contractor Selection

Chair Katsaros reported that on April 6, 2026, the company received a written protest from Thibodeaux–Reinholt Environmental, the high bidder, delivered by counsel.

Mr. Ingebretsen summarized the protest. Thibodeaux–Reinholt alleged that the specification's cupola takeoff capture-velocity requirement had been communicated orally to Vandermolen and Corcoran at a January 20 pre-bid site walk that Thibodeaux–Reinholt's representative did not attend, and that the addendum memorializing the walk, Addendum 2, issued February 6, omitted the capture-velocity discussion. The protest asserted that Thibodeaux–Reinholt priced the capture work to a stricter standard than its competitors and sought either rebidding or a price adjustment opportunity.

Mr. Ingebretsen advised that the company is a private purchaser and is not bound by public procurement law; the protest created no legal right to relief. He advised, however, that the factual allegation was potentially accurate and that awarding over an unresolved allegation of unequal information would expose the company to a claim of bad faith and, more practically, would give a disappointed bidder a narrative to carry to the agency.

Mr. Wojciechowski confirmed that capture velocity at the charge door was discussed at the January 20 walk and that Addendum 2 did not record it. He characterized the omission as his firm's error.

Mr. Wojciechowski then presented the scored technical evaluation required by Action Item 26-03-03:

| Criterion (weight) | Vandermolen | Corcoran | Thibodeaux–Reinholt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical compliance (30) | 24 | 27 | 29 |
| Capture design adequacy (25) | 14 | 16 | 23 |
| Schedule (20) | 13 | 13 | 17 |
| Constructability at site (15) | 12 | 7 | 13 |
| Price (10) | 10 | 8 | 5 |
| **Total (100)** | **73** | **71** | **87** |

Mr. Wojciechowski stated that on technical merit Thibodeaux–Reinholt was the superior proposal, that its price premium of $2.56 million over Vandermolen was substantially attributable to capture scope the other two bidders had not priced, and that a corrected comparison would narrow but not close the gap.

Ms. Stavrou objected to any process that treated an $11.2 million bid as comparable to an $8.6 million bid on the strength of a scoring matrix. She said the company's borrowing capacity was finite and the difference was not a rounding error.

Mr. Balogun said he would rather build the right hood once than build the wrong one and rebuild it under a second consent order.

Extended discussion followed. Chair Katsaros observed that the committee could not responsibly award this date: the technical basis of comparison was defective by the engineer's own admission, the protest was unresolved, and Ms. Stavrou's financing work was not complete.

**MOTION:** Ms. Stavrou moved to table contractor selection to the May 13, 2026 regular meeting, and to direct Bergstrom Hollis to issue a corrective addendum stating the capture-velocity requirement explicitly and to solicit revised pricing on that element only from all three bidders. Mr. Trombley seconded.

**DISCUSSION:** Ms. Fasthorse expressed concern that a rebid of any element would push award past the May 1 milestone. Mr. Ingebretsen responded that the company's position with the agency was stronger if it could show a documented, fair procurement than if it showed a fast one. Mr. Nambiar asked whether the three bidders would honor their base pricing through May 13; Mr. Wojciechowski said all three bid validity periods ran to June 30.

**VOTE:** Carried 6–0.

**ACTION ITEM 26-04-05:** Mr. Wojciechowski to issue Addendum 4 to RFP 2026-ENV-01 stating the cupola charge-door capture-velocity requirement of 250 feet per minute and requesting revised pricing on the capture element only. **Due: April 15, 2026.**

**ACTION ITEM 26-04-06:** All three bidders to return revised capture pricing; Mr. Trombley to compile. **Due: May 6, 2026.**

### 7. Near-Miss Investigation — Pouring Floor, April 30, 2026

Mr. Wamalwa presented the investigation report on the April 30 ladle contact event.

He reported that at 14:52 on April 30, 2026, a 2,000-pound-capacity bottom-pour ladle carrying approximately 1,800 pounds of iron at 2,590 degrees Fahrenheit, suspended from the No. 2 pouring monorail, swung laterally and struck a structural stanchion at the east end of the transfer aisle. Contact velocity was estimated at 2.4 feet per second. The ladle did not overturn and no iron was spilled. Two employees were within 15 feet; neither was injured.

He identified four causal factors:

1. **Monorail switch alignment.** The switch at the transfer aisle entry was out of alignment by approximately 3/8 inch, producing a lateral impulse to the trolley on transit. The condition had been reported on a work order dated February 9, 2026 and remained open at the time of the event, 80 days later.

2. **Absence of anti-sway control.** The monorail was installed in 1987 without pendant anti-sway damping. Nothing in the design prevented accumulation of lateral oscillation.

3. **Aisle width.** The transfer aisle measured 42 inches clear at the stanchion against a company standard of 60 inches for molten metal transport aisles. The stanchion was added during a 2011 roof reinforcement without an aisle clearance review.

4. **Production pressure.** The event occurred during the recovery schedule following the Aurelian sort. The pouring crew was on its fourth consecutive Saturday and the pour rate on April 30 was 118 percent of standard.

Mr. Wamalwa stated that the event was survivable only by chance and that a spill of 1,800 pounds of iron in a 42-inch aisle with two employees present would very likely have been fatal.

The committee received the report in silence.

Chair Katsaros asked Mr. Trombley to explain the 80-day-old work order. Mr. Trombley said the work order had been classified priority 3 and that the maintenance backlog for priority 3 items on the pouring floor stood at 61 open items with a median age of 44 days. He said this was a resourcing failure and that he would not characterize it otherwise.

Mr. Balogun said the fourth causal factor was his responsibility and that he had authorized the pour rate.

**MOTION:** Ms. Fasthorse moved that the committee (a) accept the investigation report, (b) direct that no molten metal be transported through the east transfer aisle until the stanchion is relocated or the aisle is rerouted, effective immediately, (c) direct that all monorail switch and pouring-floor work orders be reclassified to priority 1 regardless of prior classification, and (d) authorize an unbudgeted capital expenditure of up to $340,000 for anti-sway control on the No. 2 and No. 3 pouring monorails and for aisle reconfiguration. Mr. Balogun seconded.

**DISCUSSION:** Ms. Stavrou asked whether the $340,000 could be absorbed within the reforecast just approved. Mr. Pfannenstiel said it could be taken from the routine and maintenance capital line with a reduction elsewhere, or added. Ms. Stavrou said she would add it rather than trade it, and that she did not want a safety expenditure competing against a pump replacement.

Mr. Nambiar asked how long the aisle closure would affect production. Mr. Balogun estimated a 9 percent reduction in pouring throughput for approximately six weeks and said the company would take it.

**VOTE:** Carried 6–0.
