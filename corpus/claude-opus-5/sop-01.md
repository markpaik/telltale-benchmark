## 1. PURPOSE

This procedure establishes the required method for the City of Harlow Springs Water Division to:

1. Build and maintain a compliant pool of lead and copper tap sampling sites, classified by tier;
2. Distribute sample kits, instruct residents, and collect first-draw samples within the regulatory monitoring window;
3. Maintain unbroken chain of custody from kit issue through laboratory receipt;
4. Review, report, and retain analytical results;
5. Invalidate and substitute samples when a site becomes unusable;
6. Verify service line material and retire unknown records from the service line inventory; and
7. Execute the required response when the 90th percentile result exceeds the lead action level.

This procedure exists in direct response to the Oregon Health Authority sanitary survey findings of January 2026, which identified (a) 11 of 30 tap samples in the 2025 monitoring period drawn from sites not meeting highest-tier criteria, (b) two missing chain-of-custody forms, and (c) 1,240 of 9,800 service lines carrying an unknown material classification. It is the written procedure required by the OHA compliance advisory to be in place by September 1, 2026.

---

## 2. SCOPE

**Applies to:** All lead and copper tap monitoring conducted for PWS ID OR41‑00218, all service line material verification and inventory maintenance, and all public notification and corrosion control follow-up triggered by an action level exceedance. Applies to City staff in the Water Division, Meter Services, and Records; and to the contract laboratory when performing analyses under City purchase order.

**Does not apply to:** Distribution system water quality parameter (WQP) sampling at entry points and distribution sites, source water sampling, bacteriological (coliform) sampling, disinfection byproduct monitoring, or customer-requested courtesy lead tests conducted outside the compliance monitoring period. Courtesy tests are handled under WD‑SOP‑2021‑006 and are never reported as compliance samples.

**System profile governing sample counts.** Harlow Springs serves 28,400 residents through 9,800 connections and is classified as a system serving 10,001–100,000 people for lead and copper monitoring purposes. Standard monitoring requires **30 tap samples** per monitoring period, with a reduced-monitoring count of 15 available only upon written OHA approval. Until OHA lifts the January 2026 compliance advisory, the City shall collect 30 samples regardless of eligibility for reduced monitoring.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**90th percentile.** The concentration value below which 90 percent of results fall, computed per Section 9.3. Compared against the lead action level of 0.010 mg/L and the copper action level of 1.3 mg/L.

**Action level (AL).** Lead: 0.010 mg/L. Copper: 1.3 mg/L. An action level is not a maximum contaminant level; exceeding it triggers treatment and public education obligations, not an automatic violation.

**Chain of custody (COC).** The continuous written record documenting possession of a sample from container issue through laboratory analysis. A sample without a complete COC is not a valid compliance sample.

**First draw.** A one-liter sample of cold water that has stood motionless in the plumbing at least 6 hours and no more than 12 hours, collected from a kitchen or bathroom cold-water tap without prior flushing, aerator removal, or temperature adjustment.

**Galvanized requiring replacement (GRR).** Galvanized iron or steel service line that is or ever was downstream of a lead service line or a lead connector, or whose upstream material cannot be documented as never having been lead.

**Lead connector / gooseneck.** A short flexible lead fitting joining the main to the service line, typically 12 to 24 inches. A connector alone does not make the line a lead service line but does classify it as a lead connector line requiring the same treatment as lead for inventory purposes.

**Monitoring period.** June 1 through September 30 of the applicable year, the summer window required for lead and copper tap monitoring.

**Service line.** The pipe from the water main to the building inlet, including both the City-owned portion (main to meter) and the customer-owned portion (meter to building).

**Site tier.** Classification of a sampling location by plumbing material and installation date, per Section 5.2. Tier 1 is highest priority.

**Unknown.** A service line whose material has not been documented by an acceptable verification method for either the City-owned or customer-owned portion. A line is unknown if *either* portion is undocumented.

**Verified.** A service line material determination supported by at least one method listed in Section 11.2 with documentation filed per Section 11.6.

## 5. PROCEDURE — SITE TIER CLASSIFICATION AND POOL MAINTENANCE

### 5.1 Purpose of the pool

The City maintains a standing pool of qualified sampling sites so that 30 valid samples can be collected each monitoring period without last-minute site hunting. The pool consists of a **primary list of 30 sites** and an **alternate list of at least 15 sites**, all tier-classified.

### 5.2 Tier criteria

Classify every candidate site into exactly one tier using the following hierarchy. Assign the highest tier for which the site qualifies.

**Tier 1 (highest priority).** Single-family residences that meet at least one of:
- Served by a lead service line (City-owned portion, customer-owned portion, or both);
- Served by a galvanized service line meeting the GRR definition;
- Served by a lead connector or gooseneck;
- Contain copper pipe with lead solder installed after 1982 and before the effective date of the Oregon lead solder ban (June 19, 1986);
- Contain interior lead pipe.

**Tier 2.** Buildings, including multi-family residences, meeting the same material criteria as Tier 1 but not single-family residences.

**Tier 3.** Single-family residences containing copper pipe with lead solder installed before 1983.

**Tier 4.** Sites representative of the distribution system plumbing materials, used only when Tiers 1–3 are exhausted and documented as such.

### 5.3 Building and correcting the pool

**Step 5.3.1** — Not later than **February 1** preceding each monitoring year, the Water Quality Supervisor shall export the current service line inventory from Cityworks and identify all addresses classified lead, GRR, lead connector, or unknown.

**Step 5.3.2** — Cross-reference the export against the Building Division permit database for construction dates. Flag all single-family residences constructed between January 1, 1983 and June 19, 1986 as candidate Tier 1 on the lead-solder criterion.

**Step 5.3.3** — Rank candidates: all Tier 1 sites first, ordered by (a) confirmed lead service line, (b) GRR, (c) lead connector, (d) lead solder date range. Fill the primary list of 30 from the top of the ranked list.

**Step 5.3.4** — **Correction of the 2025 deficiency.** The 11 sites identified by OHA as not meeting Tier 1 criteria in the 2025 period are removed from the pool effective with this SOP. Each is listed in Attachment C with the specific reason for disqualification. None may be reinstated without written re-verification of plumbing material and the Water Quality Supervisor's signed determination.

**Step 5.3.5** — If fewer than 30 Tier 1 sites can be identified, the Water Quality Supervisor shall prepare a **Tier Exhaustion Memorandum** documenting the number of Tier 1 sites available, the search method used, and the date. Only after this memorandum is signed may Tier 2 sites be added, then Tier 3, then Tier 4. File the memorandum with the monitoring period record. **Never fill a sample slot with a lower-tier site without this memorandum.**

**Step 5.3.6** — Because 1,240 lines remain unknown, the Water Quality Supervisor shall prioritize verification (Section 11) at candidate sampling addresses. Any unknown-line address that returns a lead, GRR, or lead-connector result is added to the pool immediately as Tier 1.

**Step 5.3.7** — Record for each pool site in the Cityworks Sampling Site Register: address, account number, tier, the specific criterion met, the documentary basis, date classified, classifying staff member, resident contact name and telephone, and participation history for the last three monitoring periods.

### 5.4 Site retention and consent

**Step 5.4.1** — Obtain a signed **Sampling Site Participation Agreement** (Attachment A) from each resident on the primary list. The agreement records consent, preferred contact method, and acknowledgment that results will be provided to the resident and reported to the State.

**Step 5.4.2** — Sites that have participated in the two most recent monitoring periods shall be retained if still qualified and still willing. Regulatory preference is for consistency across periods.

**Step 5.4.3** — Review the pool annually by **March 15**. Remove sites where the residence has been demolished, converted to non-residential use, replumbed such that the tier criterion no longer applies, or where the service line has been replaced. Document each removal with reason and date.

## 6. PROCEDURE — KIT DISTRIBUTION AND RESIDENT INSTRUCTION

### 6.1 Kit assembly

**Step 6.1.1** — Order 45 sample kits from Rogue Basin Analytical Labs by **May 1** (30 primary plus 15 alternate). Each kit contains one wide-mouth one-liter polyethylene bottle, unpreserved, laboratory-cleaned and lot-labeled.

**Step 6.1.2** — Assemble each kit in a sealable bag containing: the sample bottle; a pre-printed bottle label; the **Resident Sampling Instructions** sheet (Attachment B, English and Spanish); a **Resident Sample Data Card**; the partially completed COC form; and a Water Division contact card.

**Step 6.1.3** — Pre-print each bottle label with: PWS ID OR41‑00218, kit number, service address, account number, tier, and the monitoring period. Leave collection date, collection time, and stagnation start time blank for resident completion.

**Step 6.1.4** — Log every kit in the Cityworks **Kit Register** at assembly: kit number, bottle lot number, assigned address, assembly date, and assembling operator. Kit numbers are sequential and never reused within a monitoring year.

### 6.2 Distribution

**Step 6.2.1** — Distribute kits between **May 15 and May 31**, ahead of the June 1 window opening. Do not distribute before May 15; residents hold kits too long and stagnation discipline degrades.

**Step 6.2.2** — Deliver in person wherever possible. In-person delivery is required for all first-time participating sites. Mailed delivery is permitted only for returning sites that have submitted valid samples in each of the two preceding periods.

**Step 6.2.3** — At delivery, the operator shall walk the resident through all of the following and initial each line on the Kit Register:
- Which tap to use — a kitchen or bathroom **cold-water** tap that is regularly used for drinking or cooking. Never an outside spigot, laundry tub, water softener outlet, or a tap fitted with a point-of-use filter.
- The stagnation requirement — water must sit **at least 6 hours and no more than 12 hours** with no use anywhere in the house, including toilets, ice makers, dishwashers, and irrigation on the same service.
- The most reliable schedule: use water normally in the evening, stop by 10:00 p.m., collect first thing the next morning before any other water use.
- Fill the bottle **first draw** — do not flush, do not remove the aerator, do not run to get cold, do not adjust the temperature.
- Open the tap to a **moderate steady flow**, not a trickle and not full blast, and fill to the neck line in one continuous pour.
- Cap tightly, complete the label and data card, and return within **14 days** of collection.
- Where and how to return the kit — Water Division front counter at 400 Cascade Street, weekdays 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or scheduled pickup by calling the number on the contact card.

**Step 6.2.4** — Where the resident is not home, leave the kit at the door with instructions and place a follow-up telephone call within two business days to confirm receipt and complete the verbal instruction. Record the call date and outcome in the Kit Register. Do not count a door-drop as instruction delivered until the call is completed.

**Step 6.2.5** — If a resident declines participation at distribution, record the refusal and proceed under Section 10.

### 6.3 Reminders

**Step 6.3.1** — On **June 15**, the Records Coordinator generates a Kit Register status report and issues reminder calls to all sites with kits outstanding.

**Step 6.3.2** — Repeat reminders on **July 15** and **August 15**.

**Step 6.3.3** — Any kit still outstanding on **August 20** is escalated to the Water Quality Supervisor for a substitution decision under Section 10. Do not wait until September; substitution needs collection time inside the window.

## 7. PROCEDURE — COLLECTION WINDOW AND FIRST-DRAW CONDITIONS

### 7.1 Window

**Step 7.1.1** — All compliance samples shall be collected between **June 1 and September 30** of the monitoring year. A sample collected outside this window is invalid for compliance and shall not be submitted as a compliance sample under any circumstance.

**Step 7.1.2** — Target completion of all 30 samples by **August 31**, reserving September for substitutions, resample of invalidated samples, and laboratory turnaround problems.

### 7.2 Conditions required for validity

Every compliance sample shall satisfy all of the following. Any failure invalidates the sample.

1. Collected at a site on the approved primary or alternate list;
2. Cold-water tap used for drinking or cooking;
3. Stagnation of at least 6 and not more than 12 hours;
4. First draw, no pre-flush, aerator in place;
5. One liter in a single unpreserved wide-mouth container;
6. Collection date, collection time, and stagnation start time recorded;
7. Collected within the June 1 – September 30 window;
8. Returned to the City or laboratory within 14 days of collection;
9. Complete, signed chain of custody.

### 7.3 Receipt inspection

**Step 7.3.1** — At kit return, the receiving operator shall inspect and initial each of the following on the COC form: bottle intact and sealed; volume at or above the neck line; label complete and legible; collection date within the window; stagnation interval between 6 and 12 hours computed from the recorded times; and resident data card signed.

**Step 7.3.2** — If any check fails, the operator marks the sample **HOLD** and notifies the Water Quality Supervisor the same business day. Do not send held samples to the laboratory. Do not discard them either; retain pending the Supervisor's determination under Section 10.

**Step 7.3.3** — Store accepted samples in the Water Division sample refrigerator at the Operations Office. Lead and copper samples have no thermal preservation requirement, but secure storage is required to maintain custody. The refrigerator remains locked; keys are held by utility operators on shift and the Records Coordinator.

## 8. PROCEDURE — CHAIN OF CUSTODY AND LABORATORY SUBMISSION

> **This section addresses the two missing chain-of-custody forms cited in the January 2026 sanitary survey. Every control below is mandatory, and none may be waived by field staff.**

### 8.1 Form structure

**Step 8.1.1** — Use the Rogue Basin Analytical Labs COC form, City-modified, four blocks:
- **Block A — Kit issue:** kit number, address, account, tier, bottle lot, issuing operator signature, issue date.
- **Block B — Resident collection:** collection date, collection time, stagnation start date and time, resident printed name and signature.
- **Block C — City receipt and transfer:** receiving operator signature and date/time; Records Coordinator verification signature; relinquishing signature and date/time at transfer to laboratory.
- **Block D — Laboratory receipt:** laboratory receiver signature, date/time, condition-upon-receipt notation, laboratory sample ID.

**Step 8.1.2** — The COC travels in the kit bag with its bottle from issue to laboratory. One COC per sample. **Never** use a single COC to cover multiple bottles.

### 8.2 Verification before shipment

**Step 8.2.1** — The Records Coordinator shall verify every COC against the Kit Register before release to the laboratory, confirming: kit number matches; Blocks A, B, and C complete and signed; no blank fields; no correction fluid; any correction made by single strikethrough, initial, and date.

**Step 8.2.2** — **Reconciliation control.** The Kit Register is the master count. The Records Coordinator shall reconcile weekly during the collection window: kits issued = kits outstanding + kits returned + kits voided. Every returned kit must have a COC in hand. **Any discrepancy is reported to the Water Quality Supervisor the same business day and is not closed until the COC is located or a written loss memorandum is signed by the Supervisor.**

**Step 8.2.3** — Scan every COC to PDF at the point of Records verification, before the paper leaves City custody. Store scans in Cityworks under the monitoring period record, filename `LCR-[year]-[kit number]-COC.pdf`. **A sample may not be released to the laboratory until its COC scan exists.** This control alone would have prevented the 2025 loss.

### 8.3 Transfer to laboratory

**Step 8.3.1** — Batch samples for delivery no less than weekly during the collection window. Do not accumulate more than 10 samples awaiting transfer.

**Step 8.3.2** — Prepare a **Sample Transmittal Sheet** listing each kit number, address, collection date, and analysis requested (total lead and total copper, EPA Method 200.8 or 200.9). Attach copies of all COCs.

**Step 8.3.3** — Transfer by City vehicle to Rogue Basin Analytical Labs in Medford, or by tracked courier. The relinquishing operator signs Block C at transfer. The laboratory signs Block D at receipt. For courier transfer, retain the tracking record with the transmittal sheet as the intervening custody link.

**Step 8.3.4** — Confirm laboratory receipt within one business day of transfer. The laboratory returns a receipt acknowledgment listing every sample and its condition. The Records Coordinator matches the acknowledgment against the transmittal sheet and resolves discrepancies before proceeding.

**Step 8.3.5** — Holding time for lead and copper is **6 months** from collection to analysis. Deliver samples to the laboratory within 14 days of collection notwithstanding the generous holding time; this preserves schedule margin for reanalysis.

### 8.4 Custody file

**Step 8.4.1** — The Records Coordinator maintains the chain-of-custody file, organized by monitoring period, then by kit number. The file holds, for each sample: the original signed COC, the scan, the resident data card, the transmittal sheet, the laboratory receipt acknowledgment, and the countersigned COC returned with results.

**Step 8.4.2** — Conduct a **file completeness audit** within 30 days of the close of each monitoring period. The audit confirms one complete custody package per compliance sample submitted. The Water Quality Supervisor signs the audit result. Retain the signed audit with the monitoring period record and make it available to OHA on request.

## 9. PROCEDURE — RESULT REVIEW, CALCULATION, AND REPORTING

### 9.1 Result receipt

**Step 9.1.1** — Results arrive electronically from the laboratory system and as a signed PDF report. The Records Coordinator logs receipt date and files the package.

**Step 9.1.2** — The Water Quality Supervisor reviews each package within **three business days** of receipt for: sample count matching submittal; laboratory sample ID matching COC; analytical method identified; reporting limit at or below 0.001 mg/L for lead and 0.05 mg/L for copper; quality control summary present and acceptable; countersigned COC attached; and analyst signature or authorized electronic signature present.

**Step 9.1.3** — Reject and return any package failing review. Document the rejection, the deficiency, and the laboratory's response in the monitoring period record.

### 9.2 Entry

**Step 9.2.1** — Enter each result in the Cityworks Sampling Site Register against its kit number and address: lead result, copper result, reporting limits, analysis date, and laboratory sample ID.

**Step 9.2.2** — Results below the reporting limit are entered as the reporting limit value with a `<` qualifier and treated as zero for percentile ranking purposes, consistent with laboratory reporting convention.

### 9.3 90th percentile calculation

**Step 9.3.1** — Use only valid compliance samples. Invalidated samples are excluded and are not replaced in the ranking; the substitute sample takes the slot.

**Step 9.3.2** — Sort all results in ascending order and number them 1 through *n*.

**Step 9.3.3** — Multiply *n* by 0.9. With *n* = 30, this yields 27. The **27th value in ascending order is the 90th percentile**.

**Step 9.3.4** — If *n* is such that *n* × 0.9 is not a whole number, round to the nearest whole number and take that ranked value. For *n* = 15 (reduced monitoring), *n* × 0.9 = 13.5, rounded to 14; take the 14th value. For fewer than 5 samples, average the two highest results.

**Step 9.3.5** — Compare the lead 90th percentile against **0.010 mg/L** and the copper 90th percentile against **1.3 mg/L**. A result **greater than** the action level is an exceedance. A result exactly equal to the action level is not an exceedance.

**Step 9.3.6** — A second staff member independently recomputes the 90th percentile from the raw result list. Both calculations are documented on the **90th Percentile Calculation Worksheet** and signed by both parties. Discrepancies are resolved before reporting.

### 9.4 Reporting to the State

**Step 9.4.1** — Submit results to OHA through **Drinking Water Data Online** within **10 days after the end of the monitoring period** — that is, by **October 10**.

**Step 9.4.2** — The submission includes: every individual sample result with address and collection date; the 90th percentile for lead and for copper; the sample count; the tier distribution of sampled sites; and any Tier Exhaustion Memorandum applicable to the period.

**Step 9.4.3** — Print and retain the DWDO submission confirmation. File it in the monitoring period record. A submission without a retained confirmation is treated as unsubmitted.

**Step 9.4.4** — Report any invalidated sample to OHA with the reason and the substitute site used, per Section 10.5.

### 9.5 Reporting to residents

**Step 9.5.1** — Provide individual results to each participating resident within **30 days of receiving the results from the laboratory**, in writing, including the result, the action level, and an explanation of what the result means.

**Step 9.5.2** — For any individual result above 0.010 mg/L, the notice shall additionally include flushing guidance, filter information certified to NSF/ANSI 53 for lead reduction, information on blood lead testing through Jackson County Public Health, and the Water Division contact for a follow-up site visit. Deliver these notices within **3 business days** of result receipt, ahead of the 30-day general requirement.

## 10. PROCEDURE — SAMPLE INVALIDATION AND SITE SUBSTITUTION

### 10.1 Grounds for invalidation

A sample may be invalidated only for:
- Laboratory error, documented by the laboratory in writing;
- Container damage, leakage, or contamination in transit;
- Sample not meeting first-draw or stagnation conditions per Section 7.2;
- Sample collected from a tap other than the designated qualifying tap;
- Collection outside the monitoring window;
- Incomplete or missing chain of custody;
- Resident-reported deviation from instructions.

**A sample shall never be invalidated because the result is high.** Any invalidation request originating in response to a result value shall be refused and the request documented.

### 10.2 Invalidation process

**Step 10.2.1** — The operator or Records Coordinator identifying grounds completes a **Sample Invalidation Request** with the kit number, address, grounds, supporting documentation, and date.

**Step 10.2.2** — The Water Quality Supervisor reviews within two business days and either approves with written justification or denies with reason.

**Step 10.2.3** — Approved invalidations are logged in the Sampling Site Register with an `INVALID` status and the justification. The result, if any, is retained in the record but excluded from the 90th percentile calculation.

**Step 10.2.4** — Invalidations exceeding 4 in a single monitoring period trigger a written root cause review by the Water Quality Supervisor, submitted to the Director of Public Works within 15 days.

### 10.3 Site refusal, vacancy, and access failure

**Step 10.3.1** — **Refusal.** If a resident declines, make one documented follow-up contact explaining the purpose and offering a scheduled operator-assisted collection. If the resident declines again, record `REFUSED` with date and proceed to substitution. Do not contact a refusing resident a third time within the same period.

**Step 10.3.2** — **Vacancy.** Confirm vacancy through utility billing records showing no consumption for 60 days, or by field observation documented with date and photograph. Record `VACANT`. A vacant house cannot produce a valid first-draw sample and shall not be sampled by running water artificially to create stagnation.

**Step 10.3.3** — **Non-response.** After three documented reminder contacts (Section 6.3) with no return by August 20, record `NON-RESPONSE` and substitute.

**Step 10.3.4** — **Access failure.** Where the qualifying tap is inaccessible — remodel in progress, plumbing replaced, point-of-use filter installed and not removable — record `ACCESS FAILURE` with the specific condition. Where plumbing has been replaced, reassess tier under Section 5 before returning the site to the pool.

### 10.4 Substitution

**Step 10.4.1** — Draw the substitute from the alternate list, selecting **the highest-tier available alternate**. A Tier 1 site is always replaced by a Tier 1 site if any alternate Tier 1 site remains.

**Step 10.4.2** — If no same-tier alternate remains, prepare a Tier Exhaustion Memorandum (Step 5.3.5) before descending a tier.

**Step 10.4.3** — Issue a kit to the substitute site under Section 6, with in-person instruction required regardless of the site's participation history.

**Step 10.4.4** — Record the substitution in the Sampling Site Register: original site, reason, substitute site, substitute tier, date of substitution, and the approving Supervisor.

**Step 10.4.5** — If the alternate list falls below 5 available sites during a monitoring period, the Water Quality Supervisor shall replenish it within 10 business days using the Section 5.3 ranking method.

### 10.5 Reporting substitutions

Report all invalidations and substitutions to OHA with the monitoring period submission, identifying each original site, the reason, and the substitute. Where more than 20 percent of the primary list required substitution, include a narrative explaining the cause and corrective measures.

## 11. PROCEDURE — SERVICE LINE MATERIAL VERIFICATION AND RETIREMENT OF UNKNOWNS

> **Target:** Reduce unknown classifications from **1,240** to **fewer than 400** by **June 30, 2027**, per the OHA compliance advisory. This requires verification of at least **841 lines**, a pace of approximately **65 lines per month** over 13 months.

### 11.1 Inventory structure

**Step 11.1.1** — The inventory of record resides in Cityworks, maintained by the Records Coordinator. Each service line record carries: address, account number, install year, **City-side material**, **customer-side material**, verification method, verification date, verifying staff, documentation reference, and overall classification.

**Step 11.1.2** — Overall classification follows the more restrictive of the two portions. A line with copper on the City side and unknown on the customer side is classified **unknown**. A line with lead on either side is classified **lead**.

**Step 11.1.3** — Permitted classifications: `Lead`, `Galvanized Requiring Replacement`, `Non-Lead`, `Lead Status Unknown`. Sub-material detail (copper, plastic, ductile iron) is recorded in a separate field for planning use.

### 11.2 Acceptable verification methods

| Method | Description | Acceptable for |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual inspection at meter box** | Direct observation of pipe entering and leaving the meter setting; scratch test and magnet test | Both sides, where both are visible |
| **Pothole excavation** | Small excavation exposing the line at a documented location | The side exposed |
| **Hydrant-vacuum excavation** | Vacuum excavation, preferred near utilities and in paved areas | The side exposed |
| **Records review** | Tap cards, service permits, as-builts, plumbing permits, meter installation records | Either side, where the record identifies material unambiguously |
| **Install-date determination** | Documented installation after the Oregon lead ban (June 19, 1986) with no subsequent lead-permitting record | Non-lead determination only |
| **Interior inspection** | Observation of the line at the building entry point, inside the structure | Customer side |
| **Water-loss / CCTV inspection** | Camera inspection of the interior of the line | The side inspected |

**Predictive statistical modeling is not accepted** by this procedure for retiring an unknown to non-lead. It may be used to prioritize field work only.

### 11.3 Prioritization

Verify in this order:
1. Addresses on the sampling site primary or alternate lists;
2. Addresses in service areas with pre-1950 construction;
3. Addresses where a lead connector was recorded historically anywhere on the block;
4. Addresses served by mains scheduled for replacement or repair in the next 24 months, so verification pairs with open trench;
5. Facilities serving sensitive populations — schools, child care, medical clinics;
6. All remaining unknowns, geographically batched to minimize mobilization.

### 11.4 Excavation work orders

**Step 11.4.1** — The Water Quality Supervisor generates a **Material Verification Work Order** in Cityworks, batching 8 to 12 addresses in a geographic cluster.

**Step 11.4.2** — The work order includes for each address: service address, account, meter location, known or suspected material, records reviewed, locate ticket number, and traffic control requirement.

**Step 11.4.3** — Call in utility locates through the Oregon Utility Notification Center not less than 2 business days before excavation. Record the locate ticket number on the work order. **Do not excavate without a valid locate.**

**Step 11.4.4** — Notify affected residents by door hanger not less than 2 business days before excavation, describing the work, duration, and restoration commitment.

### 11.5 Field verification

**Step 11.5.1** — **Meter box inspection first.** Open the meter box, clear debris, and observe the pipe on both the inlet and outlet side. Where both sides are visible and identifiable, no excavation is needed.

**Step 11.5.2** — **Material identification tests.** For each pipe observed, perform and record all three:
- **Visual:** color and surface. Lead is dull gray, soft, often with a swelled wiped joint. Galvanized is silver-gray with threaded fittings and often heavy corrosion. Copper is orange-brown or green-patinated.
- **Scratch:** scrape with a key or coin. Lead reveals a bright shiny silver streak and marks easily. Galvanized resists scratching and shows gray. Copper reveals bright copper color.
- **Magnet:** a magnet adheres to galvanized steel. It does not adhere to lead, copper, or plastic.

Lead is confirmed by soft scratch to bright silver **plus** non-magnetic. Both conditions must be met.

**Step 11.5.3** — **Excavation.** Where the meter box is inconclusive, excavate. Use hydrant-vacuum excavation in paved areas, near known utilities, and within 24 inches of any located facility. Use pothole excavation elsewhere. Expose not less than 12 inches of pipe. Where City-side and customer-side materials may differ, expose both sides of the meter setting.

**Step 11.5.4** — **Documentation at each verification.** Record: address; date; crew members; method; City-side material; customer-side material; all three test results per side; and **not fewer than three photographs** — one wide showing address context, one of the exposed pipe with a scale reference, one of the scratch test surface.

**Step 11.5.5** — **Restoration.** Backfill in lifts, compact, and restore surface to pre-existing condition within 5 business days. Note restoration completion on the work order.

**Step 11.5.6** — **If lead or GRR is found**, flag the record immediately, notify the Water Quality Supervisor within one business day, and issue the resident notice required under Section 11.7.

### 11.6 Retiring an unknown

**Step 11.6.1** — Meter Services submits completed verification records to the Records Coordinator within **two business days** of field work.

**Step 11.6.2** — The Records Coordinator confirms the record contains: address matching the work order; method identified; material determination for **both** sides; all three test results; required photographs; and verifying staff signature.

**Step 11.6.3** — Only when both sides carry a documented determination may the classification change from `Lead Status Unknown`. A single-side determination updates that field only; the overall classification remains unknown.

**Step 11.6.4** — The Records Coordinator updates the inventory, attaches documentation to the Cityworks record, and logs the retirement in the **Unknown Retirement Log** with date, address, prior classification, new classification, method, and documentation reference.

**Step 11.6.5** — The Water Quality Supervisor audits a **10 percent random sample** of retirements monthly, confirming documentation supports the classification. Audit results are recorded and retained. Any retirement failing audit reverts to unknown pending re-verification.

**Step 11.6.6** — **Monthly progress reporting.** By the 10th of each month, the Records Coordinator issues to the Water Quality Supervisor and Director of Public Works a report showing: unknowns at the start of the prior month, retirements during the month by method, unknowns remaining, and pace against the 65-per-month target. Where cumulative pace falls more than 10 percent behind, the Water Quality Supervisor submits a written recovery plan to the Director within 10 business days.

### 11.7 Resident notification of lead or GRR findings

Where verification identifies lead, a lead connector, or GRR serving a residence, deliver written notice to the resident **within 30 days** of the determination. The notice shall state the material found, which portion of the line, health effects of lead, flushing and filtration guidance, blood lead testing information, and the City's replacement program and schedule. Deliver by mail and door hanger. Record delivery date in the service line record.

## 12. PROCEDURE — EXCEEDANCE RESPONSE

### 12.1 Trigger

The lead 90th percentile exceeds 0.010 mg/L, or the copper 90th percentile exceeds 1.3 mg/L.

### 12.2 Immediate actions — hour 0 to hour 24

**Step 12.2.1** — Upon determining an exceedance, the Water Quality Supervisor notifies the Director of Public Works **immediately by telephone**, followed by written confirmation the same day. Record the notification time. **The 24-hour clock starts at the moment of determination.**

**Step 12.2.2** — The Director notifies the City Manager and the OHA Drinking Water Services regional office within 4 hours.

**Step 12.2.3** — Draft public notification using the Tier 2 template (Attachment D). Required content: what happened, when, the 90th percentile value, the action level, health effects of lead using the mandatory OHA health effects language verbatim, what the City is doing, what residents should do, whether alternate water is needed, and Water Division contact information. **Do not paraphrase the mandatory health effects language.**

**Step 12.2.4** — The Director of Public Works approves the notification content. OHA review is requested concurrently, but **approval by OHA is not a precondition to issuing within 24 hours**.

**Step 12.2.5** — **Issue public notification within 24 hours of the exceedance determination** by all of: posting on the City website home page; direct mail or hand delivery to all 9,800 connections initiated the same day; press release to the *Harlow Springs Sentinel* and KRVM radio; posting at City Hall, the Public Library, and the Community Center; and Spanish-language versions of all of the above.

**Step 12.2.6** — Deliver a certification of public notification, with a copy of the notice and the distribution method, to OHA within **10 days** of issuance.

### 12.3 Public education — first 60 days

**Step 12.3.1** — Deliver full lead public education materials to all bill-paying customers within **60 days** of the end of the monitoring period in which the exceedance occurred.

**Step 12.3.2** — Deliver directly to: all schools and child care facilities in the service area; Harlow Springs Family Clinic and all OB/GYN and pediatric practices; the Jackson County WIC office; and all multi-family property managers for posting in common areas.

**Step 12.3.3** — Issue public service announcements to local radio and television serving the area within 60 days.

**Step 12.3.4** — Repeat public education **every 12 months** for as long as the system exceeds the action level. Retain proof of delivery for every distribution.

### 12.4 Water quality parameter monitoring and corrosion control

**Step 12.4.1** — Initiate water quality parameter monitoring at entry points and distribution sites within **6 months** of the end of the exceedance monitoring period: pH, alkalinity, calcium, conductivity, orthophosphate or silica as applicable, and water temperature.

**Step 12.4.2** — Return to **standard 6-month tap monitoring** at 30 sites in the next monitoring period. Reduced monitoring is forfeited.

**Step 12.4.3** — Conduct a corrosion control treatment study if required by OHA, and submit a treatment recommendation on the schedule OHA establishes. Budget authorization for the study is requested by the Director of Public Works through the City Manager within 30 days of the exceedance.

### 12.5 Service line replacement

**Step 12.5.1** — Where an exceedance continues after installation of optimal corrosion control treatment, the City shall conduct **lead service line replacement at not less than 7 percent of the initial number of lead service lines annually**, on the schedule OHA establishes.

**Step 12.5.2** — Replacement shall be **full** — City-side and customer-side portions together. **Partial replacement is prohibited under this procedure** except where the customer refuses in writing after two documented offers, or where the customer-side portion is documented non-lead. Partial replacement disturbs scale and can raise lead concentrations at the tap.

**Step 12.5.3** — At every replacement: notify the resident not less than 45 days before work; provide a pitcher filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53 with 6 months of replacement cartridges; flush the service line and interior plumbing following reconnection per the flushing protocol in Attachment E; and collect a follow-up tap sample **3 to 6 months after replacement**, with results provided to the resident within 30 days.

**Step 12.5.4** — Update the service line inventory within 5 business days of each replacement.

**Step 12.5.5** — Report replacement progress to OHA annually, identifying lines replaced, lines remaining, and lines where the customer refused, with documentation of each refusal.

### 12.6 Return to compliance

Two consecutive 6-month monitoring periods at or below the action level permit a request to OHA for reduced monitoring. Public education continues until OHA confirms in writing that it may cease.

## 13. TRAINING

**13.1** — All utility operators, meter services crew members, and the Records Coordinator shall complete training on this SOP before performing any task under it, and annually thereafter by **April 30**.

**13.2** — Training covers: tier criteria; resident instruction script; first-draw and stagnation conditions; COC completion and the reconciliation control; material identification tests; and documentation standards.

**13.3** — Training includes a practical exercise: each participant identifies pipe material on a sample board containing lead, galvanized, copper, and plastic specimens, and completes a COC form for a mock sample.

**13.4** — The Water Quality Supervisor maintains training records with participant name, date, topics, and instructor. Records are available to OHA on request.
