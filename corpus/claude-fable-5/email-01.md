Quick report from tonight while it's fresh. About 130 attendees, which for a school of 289 is enormous.

It was hard, and it was better than I feared. Having the draft maps in the room changed everything — people spent the evening standing at the boundary table finding their street instead of lined up at a microphone. The sibling guarantee got an actual round of applause. The sharpest anger was about the timeline ("you decided this in a spreadsheet in February"), and Colleen took that question straight on and walked through why September needs a spring decision, which didn't make people happy but visibly made them less suspicious.

Three commitments I made on the district's behalf that need to be real: assignment letters to every family by May 22, the family liaison named before the end of this school year, and Sawyer Creek's May carnival happens as scheduled with district support — the PTA president asked and I was not going to say no.

Also, Terrance came, unannounced, on his own time, and talked with families about what it's like to fight for a school. Several Sawyer Creek parents mentioned it to me on the way out. It mattered.

Members of the Board,

As committed, my recommendation for the April 20 meeting. The full packet publishes Wednesday; this is the substance.

I recommend the board approve the consolidation of Sawyer Creek Elementary effective at the close of this school year, with students reassigned to Juniper Flat and Deschutes View under the draft boundaries presented to Sawyer Creek families on April 8, incorporating the sibling guarantee and a 30-minute ride-time cap. Recurring savings, net of transportation: $1.9 million. One-time transition costs of $600,000 from the capital fund.

I recommend the board accept the $2.3 million ODE facilities modernization grant and direct its application to the Ponderosa Elementary building, committing that facility to continued service. Ponderosa remains open. I want the record to reflect both reasons: the financial case for its closure weakened materially with the grant award and revised enrollment projections, and this community — 480 attendees across four sessions — made a substantive case about the concentration of harm that this administration heard and found persuasive. Ponderosa will carry its proportional share of district-wide staffing reductions, at its principal's own insistence.

I recommend the board close the remaining $6.1 million gap through the bridge plan in CFO Hobbes's April 6 memo, authorize a RIF ceiling of 14 licensed FTE with stated intent to rescind toward a projected 9-11 actual notices, and include in the motion a directive that staff deliver a comprehensive facilities and boundary study to the board no later than January 31, 2027.

I will not dress this up. We are closing a school that children love, reducing staff, and drawing down reserves, and the study directive exists because this may not be the last such recommendation I bring you. But it is a plan that is honest about its numbers, keeps faith with what families told us, meets the May 15 contractual deadline, and leaves the district standing on firmer ground than the one I inherited in March. I ask for your support on April 20.

With respect and gratitude to the team on this thread, who argued with each other in the open and changed their minds in public when the facts changed. That is rarer than it should be.

Dr. Marisol Quintanilla
Superintendent, Cascade Ridge School District
