## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the method by which the City of Harlow Springs Water Division collects, handles, analyzes, reports, and responds to lead and copper tap monitoring results, and maintains and reduces the unknown-material count in its service line inventory. It is issued in response to the Oregon Health Authority (OHA) compliance advisory dated January 2026, which identified nonconforming tier classification on 11 of 30 tap samples in the 2025 monitoring period, two missing chain-of-custody forms, and 1,240 of 9,800 service lines recorded as unknown material. This procedure is intended to bring the Water Division into sustained compliance with OHA's Lead and Copper Rule requirements and to satisfy the advisory's two deadlines: a written, approved procedure in place by September 1, 2026, and a reduction of unknown service lines below 400 by June 30, 2027.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all lead and copper tap sampling events conducted by the City of Harlow Springs Water Division during the June 1–September 30 monitoring window, to all chain-of-custody and laboratory submission activity associated with those samples, to all service line material verification and inventory maintenance activity conducted by the meter services crew and records coordinator, and to all public notification and remediation activity triggered by a 90th percentile action level exceedance. It applies to city staff, contracted excavation crews performing potholing or hydrant-vac work on the city's behalf, and Rogue Basin Analytical Labs to the extent their laboratory reporting practices affect chain-of-custody integrity and result turnaround. It does not cover routine bacteriological or disinfection byproduct sampling, which are addressed in separate SOPs.

## 3. Definitions

**Action Level (AL)** — 0.010 mg/L for lead, calculated as the 90th percentile of all valid first-draw tap results collected in a monitoring period.

**Chain of Custody (COC) Form** — The document that records sample collector, collection date/time, site ID, sample type, and every transfer of custody from collection through laboratory receipt.

**First-Draw Sample** — Water that has stood motionless in the plumbing for at least six hours, collected as the first water to come from the tap without flushing.

**Monitoring Period** — The June 1–September 30 sampling window designated by OHA for this system's standard lead and copper monitoring.

**Sample Site Pool** — The fixed roster of 30 sampling locations (Tier 1 through Tier 3, as applicable) approved by OHA from which tap samples are drawn each monitoring period.

**Tier 1 Site** — A single-family residence with lead service line, lead-lined interior plumbing, or lead solder installed after 1982 and before the effective date of the state lead ban, representing the highest-risk sampling criteria.

**Tier 2 / Tier 3 Sites** — Lower-risk site categories used only when an insufficient number of Tier 1 sites exists to fill the 30-site pool, per OHA-approved site selection hierarchy.

**Unknown Service Line** — A service line, on either the utility-owned or customer-owned side, for which material has not been field-verified or documented through a reliable record (permit, as-built, or direct visual/physical confirmation).

**Material Verification** — Confirmation of pipe material through permitted records review, visual inspection at an accessible point, or physical verification via pothole excavation or hydrant-vac exposure.

**DWDO** — Drinking Water Data Online, the state's electronic reporting system for public water system compliance data.

**Cityworks** — The city's work order and asset management system, used here to generate, track, and close out sampling, potholing, and inventory verification work orders.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Site Tier Classification and Sample Pool Maintenance

5.1.1 By March 1 each year, the Water Quality Supervisor pulls the current 30-site sample pool roster from the inventory database and cross-references each site against the service line material inventory and any building permit or plumbing record on file.

5.1.2 Each site is assigned to Tier 1, Tier 2, or Tier 3 using the OHA site selection hierarchy: Tier 1 requires a documented lead service line, lead interior plumbing, or lead solder installed within the eligible date range; sites lacking documentation to support Tier 1 status are downgraded and flagged for verification.

5.1.3 Any site whose classification cannot be confirmed through inventory records is placed on the Material Verification work queue (Section 5.7) before it is confirmed into the pool. A site may not be counted toward the Tier 1 minimum until its classification is verified.

5.1.4 The Water Quality Supervisor documents the pool composition (site ID, address, tier, verification basis) on the Sample Pool Roster form and reviews it against OHA's minimum Tier 1 site count for the system's population category.

5.1.5 If the number of verified Tier 1 sites falls short of the required minimum, the Water Quality Supervisor identifies replacement candidates from the next tier down using the same verification process, prioritizing sites with the least monitoring history in the past three years.

5.1.6 The completed roster is submitted to OHA for pool approval no later than April 15, prior to the June 1 collection window opening. A copy of the approved roster and OHA's confirmation is filed in the Sample Pool file maintained by the Records Coordinator.

5.1.7 The roster is reviewed at every monitoring period, even years when no pool changes are anticipated, to confirm no site has been sold, demolished, converted to non-residential use, or otherwise disqualified since the prior period.

### 5.2 Kit Distribution and Resident Instruction

5.2.1 No later than May 15, the Water Quality Supervisor generates a Cityworks work order for kit assembly and distribution, referencing the approved 30-site roster.

5.2.2 Each sample kit contains: one labeled sample bottle (site ID pre-printed), a pre-addressed COC form with site ID and tier pre-populated, written first-draw collection instructions in English and Spanish, a postage-paid return mailer or a designated operator pickup date, and a City contact card for questions.

5.2.3 Utility operators hand-deliver kits to occupied Tier 1 and Tier 2 sites between May 15 and May 31, obtaining a signature or door-tag confirmation of delivery. Kits for non-owner-occupied or hard-to-reach sites are mailed with delivery confirmation.

5.2.4 At delivery, the operator verbally reviews the instructions with the resident when present: do not run any water, including toilets and dishwashers, for at least six hours before sampling; collect the very first water from the designated cold-water kitchen or bathroom tap; fill the bottle to the marked line; record the date and time of collection on the COC form; and return the sample by the specified date.

5.2.5 The operator logs the delivery date, method, and recipient (or "no contact — door tag left") in Cityworks against the site's work order record. Any site where delivery cannot be confirmed after two attempts is flagged for follow-up per Section 5.6.

### 5.3 Collection Window and First-Draw Conditions

5.3.1 Samples are collected only between June 1 and September 30. No sample collected outside this window is submitted for compliance calculation without prior written concurrence from OHA.

5.3.2 Residents self-collecting samples are instructed to complete collection between designated weekday mornings to maximize the likelihood of a genuine six-hour-plus stagnation period following overnight non-use.

5.3.3 For sites where the utility collects on the resident's behalf, the operator confirms verbally or via posted note that no water use has occurred at the site for a minimum of six hours prior to arrival. If use cannot be ruled out, the visit is rescheduled rather than the sample collected under uncertain conditions.

5.3.4 The sample is drawn as the first water from the designated cold-water tap, with no pre-flushing, no aerator removal, and no faucet disassembly. The bottle is filled to the fill line, capped immediately, and labeled with the site ID matching the pre-printed COC form.

5.3.5 The collector records on the COC form: site ID, exact date and time of collection, collector name (resident or operator), and confirmation of the stagnation period ("resident confirmed no use since [time]" or "operator confirmed no use since [time]").

5.3.6 Completed self-collected samples are returned via the postage-paid mailer or held for operator pickup on the scheduled date; operator-collected samples are placed directly into the transport cooler with an ice pack and delivered to the pickup point the same day.

### 5.4 Chain of Custody and Lab Submission

5.4.1 Every sample bottle must have a COC form completed in full before it leaves the collection site. No sample is accepted at the pickup point without a matching COC form; incomplete forms are corrected on the spot with the resident or collecting operator whenever possible.

5.4.2 The Records Coordinator collects all COC forms and samples from the designated pickup point each Monday, Wednesday, and Friday during the monitoring window and logs each site ID, collection date, and receipt date into the COC Tracking Log (a running spreadsheet cross-referenced to the sample pool roster).

5.4.3 The Records Coordinator reconciles the day's incoming samples against the roster of expected sites still outstanding. Any discrepancy (sample received without a matching roster entry, or roster entry with no corresponding sample past its expected date) is logged as an open item and followed up within two business days.

5.4.4 Samples are packed in a cooler with ice packs, and the original COC form for the batch is signed by the Records Coordinator relinquishing custody, dated and timed, then transported or couriered to Rogue Basin Analytical Labs in Medford. Transport must occur within 24 hours of the Records Coordinator's receipt to preserve holding time.

5.4.5 Upon delivery, laboratory staff sign the COC form accepting custody, noting receipt date, time, and sample condition (temperature on arrival, cap integrity, label legibility). The lab returns a signed copy of the COC form to the Records Coordinator, either at drop-off or by scanned email within one business day.

5.4.6 The Records Coordinator files the fully executed COC form (collection through lab receipt) in the master Chain of Custody binder, organized by monitoring period and site ID, and scans a digital copy into the shared compliance drive within five business days of receipt. No COC form is considered complete, and no associated result may be submitted to DWDO, until both signature stages are on file.

5.4.7 The Records Coordinator runs a completeness check at the close of each collection week: every sample logged as received must have a corresponding fully signed COC form on file. Any gap is escalated to the Water Quality Supervisor immediately rather than at monitoring period close.

### 5.5 Result Review and Reporting

5.5.1 Rogue Basin Analytical Labs returns signed laboratory reports within 10 business days of sample receipt via the lab's electronic reporting system. The Water Quality Supervisor is the designated recipient and downloads each report upon notification.

5.5.2 Upon receipt of each result, the Water Quality Supervisor cross-checks the site ID and collection date on the lab report against the COC Tracking Log to confirm they match. Any mismatch is resolved with the lab before the result is accepted into the compliance dataset.

5.5.3 The Water Quality Supervisor enters each accepted result into the Monitoring Period Results Log, which tracks site ID, tier, collection date, lead concentration, copper concentration, and COC completeness status.

5.5.4 Once all 30 results for the monitoring period are received and logged, or the period closes on September 30 with any outstanding results documented as invalidated per Section 5.6, the Water Quality Supervisor calculates the 90th percentile for lead by ranking all valid results and identifying the value at the 90th percentile position per the standard EPA/OHA calculation method for the applicable sample count.

5.5.5 The 90th percentile value is compared to the 0.010 mg/L action level. The result of this comparison, along with the full results dataset, is documented on the Monitoring Period Summary and reviewed by the Water Quality Supervisor before submission.

5.5.6 The Water Quality Supervisor submits the complete results, the 90th percentile calculation, and the Monitoring Period Summary to OHA through Drinking Water Data Online no later than 10 days after the end of the monitoring period, or immediately upon the Water Quality Supervisor's confirmation of an exceedance, whichever is sooner.

5.5.7 The Public Works Director is briefed on the 90th percentile result within one business day of the calculation being finalized, regardless of outcome, and receives a copy of the DWDO submission confirmation.

### 5.6 Sample Invalidation and Substitution

5.6.1 A scheduled sample site may be invalidated and require substitution under the following conditions: the resident refuses access after two documented contact attempts, the property is confirmed vacant or unoccupied for the full monitoring window, the sample was collected outside the required stagnation conditions and this is discovered before lab submission, the sample bottle was damaged, contaminated, or lost in transit, or the COC form cannot be completed or reconstructed.

5.6.2 Refusal is documented only after two separate contact attempts by different methods (in-person visit plus phone call, or two in-person visits on different days) at least three business days apart, each logged in Cityworks with date, method, and outcome.

5.6.3 Vacancy is confirmed through the utility billing system (no active account or confirmed disconnect) or direct observation by an operator, documented with a photo of the property and a note in Cityworks.

5.6.4 When a site is invalidated, the Water Quality Supervisor selects a substitute site from the OHA-approved alternate pool, maintained as a standing list of five backup sites per tier established during the annual roster approval (Section 5.1.6). The substitute must match the tier of the invalidated site.

5.6.5 A new kit is prepared and delivered to the substitute site as soon as the invalidation is confirmed, provided sufficient time remains in the monitoring window (delivery no later than August 15 to allow for the six-hour stagnation collection and return before September 30).

5.6.6 If no substitute can be delivered and collected within the monitoring window, the Water Quality Supervisor documents the shortfall in the Monitoring Period Summary and notifies OHA of the reduced sample count before the 90th percentile calculation, per OHA guidance on invalidation reporting.

5.6.7 All invalidation determinations, contact logs, and substitution actions are filed with the monitoring period's records and referenced in the DWDO submission narrative.

### 5.7 Material Verification Methods and Unknown Retirement

5.7.1 The Records Coordinator maintains the master Service Line Material Inventory, a database record for every one of the 9,800 connections listing utility-side material, customer-side material, verification method, verification date, and verifier.

5.7.2 Verification is achieved through one of three methods, ranked by preference: (a) documentary verification — permit records, as-built drawings, or prior inspection records clearly identifying material and date of installation; (b) visual verification — direct visual confirmation of an exposed portion of the line, such as at a meter pit, basement entry point, or curb stop, documented with a dated photograph; (c) physical verification — excavation via pothole or hydrant-vac exposure at a point along the line where no exposed segment exists.

5.7.3 Each month, the Records Coordinator generates a prioritized Unknown Line Verification List in Cityworks, ranked first by any line serving a Tier 1 or Tier 2 sample site, second by lines in service areas with pre-1988 construction dates, and third by remaining unknowns geographically clustered to minimize excavation mobilization.

5.7.4 The meter services crew receives a standing monthly work order target, calibrated to retire enough unknowns each month to bring the total under 400 by June 30, 2027 — a minimum of 75 verifications per month based on the current 1,240 count and the compliance deadline.

5.7.5 For documentary and visual verification, the assigned operator or crew member completes the Material Verification Field Form, records the method, attaches a photograph where applicable, and submits it to the Records Coordinator within two business days.

5.7.6 For physical verification, the meter services crew schedules a pothole or hydrant-vac excavation via Cityworks, exposes a minimum 12-inch segment of the service line at the curb stop or nearest accessible point, visually and, where ambiguous, magnetically or chemically confirms material (copper, lead, galvanized, plastic), photographs the exposed segment with a scale reference and address marker, backfills and restores the surface, and closes the work order with the completed Material Verification Field Form attached.

5.7.7 The Records Coordinator updates the Service Line Material Inventory within three business days of receiving a completed verification form, changing the line's status from "unknown" to the confirmed material, and logs the verification method and date.

5.7.8 The Records Coordinator produces a monthly Unknown Line Reduction Report showing the running total of unknowns, verifications completed that month, and progress against the 400-line target by June 2027, submitted to the Water Quality Supervisor and Public Works Director by the fifth business day of the following month.

5.7.9 If any newly discovered lead or galvanized-requiring-replacement service line is identified during verification, it is immediately flagged in the inventory and referred to the Public Works Director for inclusion in the service line replacement capital plan, regardless of its relationship to the sample site pool.

### 5.8 Exceedance Response

5.8.1 If the 90th percentile lead result calculated under Section 5.5 exceeds 0.010 mg/L, the Water Quality Supervisor declares an action level exceedance and immediately notifies the Public Works Director, verbally within the hour and in writing within four hours.

5.8.2 Public notification of the exceedance is issued within 24 hours of the Water Quality Supervisor's confirmation of the exceedance calculation, consistent with the compliance advisory requirement. The Public Works Director approves the notification language before release.

5.8.3 The notification is distributed through all of the following channels within the 24-hour window: direct mailing or utility bill insert to all 9,800 connections, posting on the city website home page and the Water Division page, a press release to local media serving Harlow Springs, and posting at City Hall and the Public Works office.

5.8.4 The notification includes the required public education content: an explanation of health effects of lead exposure, the action level and the system's exceedance value, sources of lead in drinking water, recommended steps residents can take (flushing lines, using cold water for cooking and drinking, considering filtration certified for lead removal), and information on requesting a free water test through the Water Division.

5.8.5 Within 30 days of the exceedance notification, the Water Quality Supervisor initiates a Corrective Action Plan review, which includes: examination of whether corrosion control treatment is optimized, review of the service line material inventory for lead lines serving the sample pool or broader system, and evaluation of whether an accelerated service line replacement schedule is warranted for identified lead lines.

5.8.6 Any confirmed lead service line — utility-owned or customer-owned — serving a sample site involved in the exceedance is added to the priority replacement queue maintained by the Public Works Director, and replacement is scheduled within the timeframe set by OHA's corrective action requirements, not to exceed the state-mandated replacement schedule for systems under an exceedance-driven mandate.

5.8.7 The Water Quality Supervisor documents all exceedance response actions, notification proof (mailing records, website screenshots, media confirmation), and the Corrective Action Plan in the Exceedance Response File, and reports the response status to OHA through DWDO and, where required, through direct correspondence with the OHA drinking water program contact assigned to the system.

5.8.8 Follow-up monitoring for the next standard period proceeds under the same procedure described in Sections 5.1 through 5.5, with the sample pool re-verified per Section 5.1 rather than assumed carried over, given the compliance status change.

## Appendix A — WD-FORM-014A: Sample Kit Resident Instruction Sheet

**City of Harlow Springs Water Division — Lead and Copper Tap Sampling**

Thank you for helping the City monitor drinking water quality. Your home has been selected as part of the required sampling pool for this monitoring period. Please follow these steps exactly so your sample can be used.

**Before you collect your sample:**

1. Do not run any water in your home for at least six hours before you collect the sample. This includes the kitchen and bathroom faucets, the dishwasher, the washing machine, and toilets. The best time to collect is first thing in the morning, before anyone in the household uses water.
2. Do not remove the aerator (the small screen at the tip of the faucet) and do not run water to "clear the line" before collecting. The first water out of the tap is the sample we need.

**Collecting your sample:**

1. Choose the cold-water tap identified on your kit label (kitchen or bathroom, as marked).
2. Remove the cap from the sample bottle. Do not rinse the bottle.
3. Turn the cold water on and immediately begin filling the bottle with the very first water from the tap.
4. Fill to the line marked on the bottle, then turn off the water.
5. Recap the bottle tightly.

**After you collect your sample:**

1. On the attached Chain of Custody form, write the date and the exact time you collected the sample, and sign your name where indicated.
2. Place the bottle and the signed form in the return bag provided.
3. Return the sample using the postage-paid mailer by the date shown on your kit, or leave it out for City pickup on the date your operator gave you.

**Questions?** Call the Harlow Springs Water Division at the number on your kit card. There is no cost to you for this sampling.

## Appendix E — Public Education and Notification Template, Lead Action Level Exceedance

**[CITY OF HARLOW SPRINGS LETTERHEAD]**

**IMPORTANT INFORMATION ABOUT YOUR DRINKING WATER**

**The City of Harlow Springs Water Division exceeded the federal action level for lead**

Date of Notice: __________________

Our water system recently monitored for lead at customer taps. The results showed a 90th percentile level of ____ mg/L, which is above the federal and state action level of 0.010 mg/L. This is not a violation of a health-based standard, but it requires us to take the actions described below and to inform you about steps you can take to protect your health.

**What does this mean?**

Lead can enter drinking water primarily through the corrosion of materials in the water distribution system and in household plumbing — including lead service lines, lead solder, and brass fixtures containing lead. The City's source water does not contain lead; the exceedance reflects lead picked up from plumbing materials between the water main and your tap.

**Health Effects of Lead**

Infants and children who drink water containing lead in excess of the action level could experience delays in physical or mental development. Children could show slight deficits in attention span and learning abilities. Adults who drink this water over many years could develop kidney problems or high blood pressure. Lead is stored in the body and can be released over time, affecting the brain, red blood cells, and kidneys. Pregnant women and infants fed formula reconstituted with tap water are at particular risk.

**What Is Being Done**

The City has initiated a Corrective Action Plan that includes review of corrosion control treatment, expedited verification and replacement scheduling for any confirmed lead service lines connected to the affected sample sites, and continued reduction of unknown service line materials system-wide. The City will report progress to the Oregon Health Authority and will notify customers of significant developments.

**Steps You Can Take Now**

1. **Run your water before using it for drinking or cooking.** Run the cold water for 30 seconds to 2 minutes, or until it becomes noticeably colder, if the water in a faucet has not been used for six hours or more.
2. **Use only cold water for cooking, drinking, and preparing baby formula.** Hot water dissolves lead more quickly than cold water. Boiling water does not remove lead.
3. **Have your water tested.** The City offers free lead testing for residents. Call the Water Division at the number below to request a test kit.
4. **Consider a water filter.** If you choose to use a filter, use one certified by NSF/ANSI Standard 53 for lead reduction, and maintain and replace filter cartridges according to the manufacturer's instructions.
5. **Identify your service line material.** Contact the Water Division to find out whether records identify a lead service line at your address, or to request a free verification.
6. **Clean faucet aerators regularly.** Sediment containing lead particles can collect in aerator screens.

**For More Information**

Contact the Harlow Springs Water Division at [phone number] or [email], Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Additional information about lead in drinking water is available from the U.S. EPA Safe Drinking Water Hotline at 1-800-426-4791 or at epa.gov/lead, and from the Oregon Health Authority Drinking Water Services program at [phone number].

This notice is sent to you by the City of Harlow Springs Water Division. State Water System ID #: __________________. Please share this information with all people who drink this water, including renters, family members, and anyone in your household.

## Appendix G — New Employee Quick-Reference Checklist

This checklist is provided to orient new Water Division staff to their role under WD-SOP-014. It does not replace the full procedure and does not authorize deviation from any numbered step above.

**If you are a Utility Operator:**
- [ ] Confirm you have the current 30-site roster with tier classification before distributing kits.
- [ ] Never leave a kit without reviewing first-draw instructions verbally when a resident is present.
- [ ] Log every delivery attempt in Cityworks the same day, including "no contact" outcomes.
- [ ] If collecting on a resident's behalf, verbally confirm six hours of no water use before drawing the sample — reschedule if you cannot confirm this.
- [ ] Same-day delivery to pickup point or cooler for any sample you personally collect.

**If you are on the Meter Services Crew:**
- [ ] Check the monthly Unknown Line Verification List in Cityworks before starting excavation work.
- [ ] Expose a minimum 12-inch segment at the curb stop or nearest accessible point — no shortcuts on segment length.
- [ ] Photograph every verification with a scale reference and visible address marker.
- [ ] Any lead or galvanized find gets flagged the same day, not batched for later.
- [ ] Complete the Material Verification Field Form before closing the work order — the work order is not complete without it.

**If you are the Records Coordinator:**
- [ ] Reconcile incoming samples against the roster three times per week during the monitoring window — do not wait until period close.
- [ ] No result goes to the Water Quality Supervisor for compliance calculation without a fully executed, two-signature chain of custody form on file.
- [ ] Update the Service Line Material Inventory within three business days of receiving a completed field form.
- [ ] Produce the Unknown Line Reduction Report by the fifth business day of each month without exception — this is the document OHA will ask for first.

**If you are new to any role and are unsure whether a situation is covered:**
Stop and contact the Water Quality Supervisor before improvising. Undocumented deviations from this procedure are the specific failure pattern that produced the January 2026 compliance advisory.
